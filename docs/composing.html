Composing a tile

Composition is how looks share one tile. Rocks sit bedded in the ground, grass grows right up to them and parts around them, bare earth shows between, and every tile meets its neighbour at the same edge level. The approved example is the meadow: rocks, grass and bare earth on one tile, printed as one piece with no supports.

What it looks like

The rocks belong to the ground. Each one is bedded into the soil on every side, so on a slope the uphill side is buried and the downhill side shows no gap. Every rock's top stands clearly above the grass around it, so no rock is swallowed.

The grass treats a rock as an obstacle. Its flow parts upstream of the rock and joins again behind it, the way water runs round a stone. Most rocks have grass right up to them, pressed against the stone and a little lower at its foot. About one rock in three has a patch of bare earth on one side: usually a wake downstream, sometimes a scour to the side. No rock sits in a ring of bare ground.

Bare earth covers about a fifth of the tile, counting the ground under the rocks. It comes as a few natural-size patches in the low ground, not as one big bald area. Grass stays the main look.

At the tile edge the ground comes down to the standard edge level, so a meadow tile meets any other ground or grass tile with no step.

Using it in a tile

In the Rust engine a tile is a short KDL document that lists looks in a stack. Solids (rocks, trees, walls, cliffs) are planned before anything grows, so grass and dirt grow round every solid wherever it is listed. A meadow in the as-built vocabulary looks like this:

use "parts/grass" lawn

tile meadow size="2x2" seed=1 {
    stack {
        dirt                                         // the ground, and the bare earth between the grass
        scatter per-square=2 {                       // about 8 rocks on a 2×2
            rock size="2..10" shape=angular buried="30%"
        }
        lawn cover="80%"                             // grass over 80% of the ground; it grows round the rocks
    }
}

This document was written for this page; the library has no meadow tile yet. The library tile library/tiles/ground/2x2-grass-tree.dtile (branch rust/p1) shows the same rule with a tree: its grass is listed before the tree and still grows round the trunk. A tile divided by a path or a shore uses a line and a region, and each look acts in= its region; see the path example on the language page.

The meadow reaches the Rust engine in phase P4 (see the plan). Until then the lab builds it: python meadowlab/meadowlab.py --seed 1 writes meadowlab/out/2x2-meadow-s1.stl with a DungeonBlocks base. In the Python library, a tile spec lists layers per region, and order matters there: put Rocks and Tree before Grass so the blades steer round them (see CLAUDE.md, "Placement Layer Ordering").

Specification

Controls

Composition has no look of its own in the tile language. It is controlled by the words every look shares. These are the ones that decide how looks meet.

ControlValuesDefaultEffect
stack orderthe order looks are listed—Free for solids: they are always planned first. For surfaces, a later surface paints over an earlier one.
in=a region namewhole tileThe look acts only inside that region.
along=an edge, edge path or drawn line—The look is built along it (a path, a wall, a cliff).
at=a point or range, in squaresanywhereWhere one thing stands. Footprints are kept inside the tile and clear of other solids.
against=cliff, wall, rock, tree—Scatters and dirt banks gather at the foot of that kind of solid.
grass coverpercent100%Share of the ground grassed over. A meadow uses about 80%.
scatter count / per-squarenumber—How many solids. The meadow uses 6–9 rocks on a 2×2.
rock buriedpercent—How much of each rock is under the ground.
regionbounded-by lines, fill points—Divides the tile, like a paint bucket: name the lines, drop fill points.

The meadow's own rules are fixed at the approved values and have no control: rock tops stand at least max(1.5 mm, 0.45 × rock height) above the grass; grass is half height at a rock's face, back to full height 1.2 mm out; about 35% of rocks get an apron; bare earth is filled to 20% with natural-size patches. The full language is on the language page.

Edges and joining tiles

Every tile obeys one edge contract, so any two tiles butt with no step:

  • Ground meets every tile edge at 3.4 mm and rises from there; nothing inside dips below it.
  • Water meets the edge at a calm, fixed profile (from phase P3).
  • Lines such as paths and shores leave the tile only at fixed anchor positions.
  • Cliffs end in a standard end section; walls end in a clean vertical cut.
  • Solids never cross the tile edge: they are nudged or shrunk inward, never clipped.

The full contract is on the tile system page. In the Rust engine it is one file, library/parts/edges.dtile, that every tile uses.

Printing and painting

  • No supports. Grass blades, rocks and bare earth all print base down at 0.2 mm layers. Each look keeps its own printability rules; see the grass and rocks pages.
  • No floating rocks. Each rock stands on a plinth of its own section that reaches below the lowest ground under it, so there is no gap or overhang on its downhill side.
  • One piece. Grass, rocks and base are joined into one watertight body that survives a float32 STL reload.
  • Painting. Rocks stand clear of the grass, so a rock colour and a grass colour can be drybrushed separately. Bare earth sits in the low ground and takes a wash.

The full printing rules are on the printing page.

How it is made

The meadow generator is meadowlab/meadowlab.py (266 lines). It writes almost no geometry of its own: it calls grasslab for the ground and grass and rocklab for the rocks, and owns only how they meet. The key idea is plan, then build: rocks claim their footprints before the grass grows, and are seated on the result after.

  1. Two random streams. The grass uses the seed, the rocks use seed + 1,000,003, so a change to the rocks does not reshuffle the grass (main, line 207).
  2. Ground and flow. grasslab builds the hills and a wandering flow direction for the blades.
  3. Rocks first. rocklab scatters and poses 6–9 rocks, fixing each rock's turn and so its real footprint. footprint_masks (line 62) slices each rock 0.45 mm above its base and rasterises the outline onto the 0.12 mm grass grid.
  4. Bare earth. apron_shapes (line 86) gives 35% of rocks a one-sided lens of bare earth, downstream 60% of the time. fill_bare_earth (line 115) then adds grasslab's soil patches, best first and at natural size, until bare earth plus rock footprints reach 20% of the tile.
  5. Flow wraps. rock_circles (line 129) adds a ring 0.6 mm wider than each rock to the obstacles the flow parts around, alongside the soil patches.
  6. Grass. grasslab grows the blades with the footprints as blockers (grasslab/grasslab.py:951): no blade roots inside a footprint, blades heading into a rock stop 0.5 mm inside its face, and grass is halved in height at the face.
  7. Seat the rocks. seat_rocks (line 138) bases each rock at the 35th percentile of the ground under it, less 0.3 mm; lifts it up to 1.5 mm if its top does not clear the grass; and adds a plinth down to below the lowest ground.
  8. Join. attach_base (line 181) unions grass, rocks and the DungeonBlocks base and keeps the one body; stl_safe checks the float32 reload before export.

The Python library composes differently. A tile spec divides the tile into regions by boundary curves (flood fill, core/region.py), blends region heights across boundaries, and runs each region's layers in list order; Rocks and Tree stamp an obstacle mask that a later Grass layer steers round. The Rust engine keeps the regions but drops the order rule: it plans every solid first, as meadowlab does.

Requirements

The lab's requirement IDs (revision 3, 28 Oct 2026), with later amendments marked inline. The meadow inherits every grasslab and rocklab requirement and adds only the interactions. [S] is checked by a script or statistic, [R] by eye. The original page is in the archive.

  • REQ-M-1 [S] A run writes meadowlab/out/<cols>x<rows>-meadow-s<seed>.stl: one watertight DungeonBlocks tile with grasslab hills, grass and soil, and rocklab rocks. Deterministic per seed. Amended 29 Oct: the grass is grasslab v3 stroke blades.
  • REQ-M-2 [R] Rocks are part of the ground: seated at a low quantile of the ground under them, with a plinth to just below the lowest ground. No rock floats or perches (M1).
  • REQ-M-3 [R] Grass flows around rocks: the flow parts upstream and rejoins behind. Grass never runs into a rock head-on (M4).
  • REQ-M-4 [R] Most rocks have no margin. About 35% have bare earth on one side (a wake or a scour), never a ring (M2).
  • REQ-M-5 [R] Rocks are obstacles, not soil: no grass roots under a footprint; blades stop pressed against the face, tucked 0.5 mm in; grass is lower beside a rock; rocks don't trigger soil-bank behaviours.
  • REQ-M-6 [S] Bare earth is 20% of the tile including ground under rocks, met by adding natural-size patches, never by scaling them (M3).
  • REQ-M-7 [S] A handful of rocks: 6–9 placements on a 2×2. Grass stays dominant.
  • REQ-M-8 [S] The grass and rock rules still hold, and the audit reports them. Amended 29 Oct: the FDM backstop of the old height-field grass is retired; the overhang audit and the float32 reload check remain.
  • REQ-M-9 [R] Rocks read as rocks: every rock top stands at least max(1.5 mm, 0.45 × its height) above the grass, lifted by up to 1.5 mm if needed (M5).

Audit numbers

By construction: no grass roots inside a footprint; the plinth reaches below the lowest ground, so no rock floats; rock tops clear the grass by the REQ-M-9 margin; bare earth is filled until the 20% target is met.

Checked: each run reports every rock's top above the grass, its lift and the deepest plinth, the bare-earth share, and the overhang area (gl.audit_overhang). stl_safe refuses any export that is not one watertight body after a float32 reload.

Seed 1 with the current stroke-blade grass: 2,905 blades, 1.58 M faces, 79 MB, about 85 s; bare earth 20.8% (15.7% visible); overhang 13.0 mm². Seeds 1–6 all export as one watertight body.

Rust port

Phase P4, “Grass, meadow, shore and reeds, the first real test of plan-then-build”. It ports footprint_masks, apron_shapes, fill_bare_earth and seat_rocks, and rocks gain footprints planned before the grass and seats built after it. Exit gate: bare earth at 20% ± 3 points, rocks above the grass top, the meadow built in 15 s or less on 8 cores (Python: 85 s), Shawn's sign-off and a test print with clean blade tips. It retires risk R8, that plan-then-build misses a case. Status: not started. The stack-order rule and the place words exist in dt-doc; the library has no meadow tile and no parity contact sheet yet.

Open issues

  • The tile language has no control for bare earth counted under rocks. cover is the share grassed over; whether it counts ground under rocks, as REQ-M-6 does, is still to decide in P4.
  • Aprons, clearings and spill appeared in the plan's P4 scope under the old look name meadow (renamed grass by decision D8). The as-built vocabulary has none of them; they stay hidden at the approved values unless P4 exposes them.
  • The meadow builds in about 85 s and writes a 79 MB STL. P4 targets 15 s.
  • The rock-interaction numbers were measured with the retired height-field grass; only seed 1 has been measured with stroke blades.
  • The Python library depends on layer order (Rocks before Grass); the Rust engine does not. Tile twins converted in P4 must not rely on order for solids.
  • Where meadow meets water is the shore, part of the water look (shorelab).

History

meadowlab started on 27 Oct 2026 as the first lab to put two approved looks on one tile. It went through five versions in two days, then took grasslab's stroke blades on 29 Oct. It showed the order the Rust engine is built on: plan the solids first, grow the surfaces round them, then seat the solids. The full texts are archived: the lab's original requirements and design and iteration log, and the Rust study's lab analysis and notes.
