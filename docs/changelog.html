Unreleased

In the source, not yet in a numbered version.

  • The film's grain stands over its skin, and its edge meets the stone at the skin: holding the grain a skin over the stone's top flattened the film (the pits had no room, so it read as a smooth slab with a wall round it). The film's face now stands the grain's depth (0.6 mm at the 35 mm square) over its band, so the pits reach down to the skin and no further, and both that lift and the band's width fade in over 2.5 mm from the film's outline: the edge is a step of the skin (0.6 mm) and the face climbs from it. Measured over the flat-facing film at every vertex the thinnest is still 0.6 mm; the middle eight tenths spread 0.4 to 0.55 mm. A film of the library's cliffs changes; nothing else does.
  • The film keeps its skin over the stone: the grain was held off the core under the stones, half a millimetre below their tops, so its pits could take a film of 0.6 mm down to 0.2 mm or less over the stone (median 0.5 mm, under the band's low). The grain of a film is now held a skin (3 layers, 0.6 mm) over the stone's top: measured over the flat-facing film at every vertex, the thinnest is 0.6 mm and the median 0.8 to 1.05 mm, with the grain still there. A film of the library's cliffs changes; nothing else does.
  • The cliff's pile engine is its own module: planning the pile and dressing its stones (cliff/pile.rs) reads a band, a seed and a foot and knows no scene or stack entry; the body adapter (cliff/mod.rs) lays the core, the film's tops and the stones. Its grids are the kit's Field directly (the cliff's own alias is gone). Nothing a cliff builds changes: every cliff tile's triangles are identical.
  • Floors are laid before the lift: flagstones and planks stand on the land the surfaces laid and need no lifted ground, so the plan puts them after the surfaces and before the lift (a new Write::Laid), where the cliff's stones already are, and the lift sees them. Every floor tile's geometry is unchanged (the union and export hashes are identical); only the build order, and so the plan hash and the order of the stages, moved.
  • The preview's value helper asks the program: the check under a typed value (limits, percent, a range where one number is taken, a whole count) is the reader's own, answered by the server from dt_doc::decode::check_text, so the page re-implements nothing and its messages are the program's (tree height is 20 mm to 120 mm, not 150 mm). The page keeps only what it needs to draw: the words to offer and the one-number or range buttons. The dead level completion (no control takes a named level any more) is gone from the page.
  • Settings are typed once: an element reads its controls by name through one reader (Settings: a number, a Span for a number or a range, counts, a word, a length kept as written) instead of looping over the controls matching names and value shapes; ranges and the tree's branching angle are typed values (Span, Degrees). Documents and every hash are unchanged. Axis::Level is gone from the schema (no control used it; a line's wobble still names a let, now by an explicit flag, and its error says "a length").
  • The rare crash in the wall and floor builds is fixed: Manifold's level set read one element before a buffer when its hash table filled before any vertex had moved, and sized the table from what it found there; the table could come out empty and the next insert faulted (an intermittent SIGSEGV, about one build in thirteen of the eight wall and floor tiles at once). Found with a sanitised build; the build now carries a one-guard patch to Manifold (third_party/manifold-csg-sys), and the golden hashes are unchanged.
  • The cliff's film has a smoother outline: a gap too narrow for the lattice between two places a film may not lie is closed, and the outline of every patch is rounded over a few cells, so it no longer follows the lattice as a staircase or a one-cell comb (measured on the library's cliffs: single-cell teeth in the outline fall by about three quarters, its length by about an eighth). The film's insets (1.5 mm from a lip, 1.0 mm lip depth) are sizes at the 35 mm square and scale with the system; the 35° is an angle and does not. Cliff tiles on DungeonBlocks change only in the film's outline.
  • The OpenLOCK lotus keeps clear of the pockets: the logo is as large as fits at the middle without touching a pocket (22 mm at most) and is left out where that is under 6 mm: a 1×1 and a 2×1 have none now (the pockets leave only a few millimetres at the middle, and the logo used to run across them); 2×2, 3×3 and 4×2 keep their 22 mm lotus where it was.
  • Small tidy-ups: the cliff's pile is planned once per build (it was planned for the foot and again for the build); the pile's ghost FOOT constant and its f64::MAX distance are gone; the cliff no longer silences the lints that hid loop and name smells; one stamp_heights takes how the ground under it is painted (Under::Bedding or Under::Earth); the element table's entries take ..Element::BASE instead of repeating eight default fields; a wall's Mason::thickness is across (the same number is a wall's thickness standing and a slab's depth laid); Frame::node_range serves the film and the fitter; a draft's cliff stones may stand a draft's carving grid (0.3 mm) over the band, as a draft carves coarser (a migrated escarpment failed to draft).
  • New guards: goldens for the stone floor beside a wall and for the cove's water; tests for the minimum wall between an OpenLOCK pocket and the tile's faces, the logo clear of every pocket on a 1×1, 2×1, 2×2, 3×3 and 4×2, the core inside its rims, film outlines and insets, a side's grain by the spread of its depths, a film's thickness against its band, no repeated stones on the spire, and that each migrated cliff form builds (on the quick setting) as well as checks.
  • The grain's set-in reach scales with the system: the reach (6 mm) and smoothing (3 mm) it looks for the grain's highest point over are sizes at the 35 mm square like the rest, so an OpenLOCK tile is its DungeonBlocks tile scaled (they were true mm, a workaround from before the cliff was rebuilt). DungeonBlocks is unchanged; every OpenLOCK library tile still passes the gate (tree tiles aside). cargo xtask sheet takes --system ol.
  • OpenLOCK sockets sit on the grid lines: along a side n squares long a socket stands at each of the n − 1 interior grid lines, or at the middle when n is 1, instead of at the centre of every perimeter square. This is where OpenSCAD-OpenLock's parts E, R, S, SA and SB put them (checked in its code and by slicing its STLs): a 2×2 has one per side, a 2×1 one on each long side and one on each short, a 4×2 three along the long sides. Clip pockets no longer crowd each other at the corners of larger tiles. OpenLOCK tiles of 2 squares or more change; DungeonBlocks and 1×1 tiles do not.
  • The OpenLOCK socket is corrected: the two retention slots beside the T (4.7 mm wide, at 8.35 mm either side, 9 mm deep, closed below) were a misreading of the reference and are gone. Behind each T slot is now one pocket centred on it, 18 mm along the edge and 4.7 mm deep (6.0 to 10.7 mm in from the face), open through the bottom of the base up to the slot’s top (5.6 mm), with its middle 14 mm reaching 0.3 mm higher, so its roof bridges. The T profile and its 7 mm depth are unchanged; no printed support is needed (the overhang gate reads 0.00 mm²). A cut may not reach a pocket.
  • The cliff's film is read from the finished stones: rays are dropped on the stones as they stand, not on the plan they were made from, so the film lies only where a stone's top is, under 35°, 1.5 mm or more from any lip, face or slit. The core stands under the stones as they are, and its edge is drawn back from a face in one place. The preview paints earth under a cliff as earth, not as the flat bedding brown of a floor, so the foot of a cliff and its film read as grained dirt.
  • Every shipped cliff checks clean: the library's cliffs hold the dirt at the standard edge height where they meet the tile's edge (pin top=dirtEdgeHeight at=edges), so the tile's side under the stones carries the dirt's grain. dt::edge no longer fires for a tile pin that holds the library's height, and a default pin the tile covers with its own is not reported as shadowed. The mound, spire, massif and needles are drawn by hand in the library as lumps with wide, overlapping bands (rough masses again, not concentric terraces), so a stone over two of them has room; a few seeds are chosen so each tile passes the gate (the mesh kernel's occasional hairline pinch fails a tile at some seeds, as before).
  • migrate carries an old cliff form over as one region and one pin, no longer owning a catalogue of rings.
  • A pieces body is a flag in the element schema (pieces), not a list of names in the solver, and takes a slope to ease between its pins. The rock weights (rock columns=…) are no longer marked planned: they are live.
  • A stone the band holds down no longer repeats: it adds no height at its site, so the stack could draw the same one there again and again (dozens of coincident solids that a union never finished; reproduced on the previous commit, a spire at one seed). The cliff keeps only the first of stones that coincide, and a rock thinner than the seat's bevel is no longer seated (it panicked).
  • Cliff internals and tests: one fitter for cliff stones and floors (Pieces::plane_or_flat), the core's constants and phases in core.rs, shared constants (Z_UP, crack sizes), and new tests (tests/cliffs.rs) for stone tops inside the band, film only on standing stones, grain on the tile's outer sides and the migration of every old cliff form.
  • The cliff is a pieces body: cliff top=… at=REGION with pins, like a floor. Its region is where the stones stand and its band, solved with its pins (cliffs step, nothing eases), is how high their tops are: one height is a plane every stone top touches, a wide band a jagged crest, nested pinned discs a mound or a spire. A stone is made to fit the band as it is stacked (the nearest top plane inside the band over every node it stands on; a stone stays where the band holds its height), never trimmed after, so there is no hollow behind or under one; a stone over the band is a bug (dt::band), and the grain is held under the band's high. form=, height, depth, along=, top dirt|bare, the old planned bank-*, scree, boulders and grit controls and the dirt's bank are gone: dharmatiles migrate writes the region (a within "[10 .. 20]" strip whose depth is drawn along the edge, or nested discs; needles are three sets of nested discs, the rings of every needle laid outer first), the band, the pins, the bank as pins on the dirt and the film. ledges (default 1) steps the outer strip of most masses down in shelves, below the band.
  • Dirt on stone is a film: a dirt laid on a cliff's tops (dirt top="bottom + [0.6 .. 1.2]" at=crag) follows the stones' own tops, on rock under 35° and clear of every lip, face and of the core's edge. The cliff's core is no longer a carved, grained mesh of millions of triangles but a rock layer of the terrain, set in from the faces and kept inside and clear of the finished stones; with the stones' fits this takes a cliff tile from 9-59 s to 4-26 s (the library's total from 324 s to 202 s, its repairs a fifth fewer). A range in within (within "[10 .. 20]" of=northEdge) draws a depth that wanders along the place in masses of 6 to 15 mm, the standard end section at both ends.
  • Floors are two pieces bodies, flagstones and planks: floor is gone, and dharmatiles migrate writes it as flagstones (its level goes; a level other than floorHeight becomes top= and pin top=… at=edges). Both take a band like dirt and a pin at the edges, and step instead of easing. Each slab or board is made to fit the band as it is made: its height and tilt come from a heaved surface inside the band (one height is a flat floor, a wide band a heaved one), then are reduced until its top plane is inside the band over every node it stands on, and the finished piece is checked against the band, a miss being a bug the build refuses (dt::band). The slabs' own random settle and tilt are gone (a single height is flat); the grain is set in so its highest points reach the band's top and no higher. A floor takes its own defaults wherever it is laid, stands on the land under it (or on the base with no dirt: a floor no longer needs a dirt written before it), and meets the tile edge flush at floorEdgeHeight with no edge-round (except weathered flagstones, texture=worn, which keep a worn round), its faces at the plane keeping the material's grain: slabs and boards stand 0.05 to 0.1 mm past the plane and are cut there, so the top edge is square and the chipped relief lies at or just behind the plane; a drystone flag's upper flank is drawn to the plane; a board's end grain carries the wood's pattern set back from it. planks is new: boards across the tile in mixed lengths or half-square lengths, with grain and checks, in the wood material. Dirt laid on a floor does not follow it as a film yet. The library gains floors/1x1-floor-planks and the tests a heaved-flagstones and a planks tile.
  • The base is its own line, and has the terrain's corners: the connector is built only when the stack has a base line (first, or dt::base-order); a tile with no base line has no base, no connector on either system, and is printed flat on the bed from zero up (the library's test/1x1-no-base tile shows it). The base's four vertical corners take the same round as the terrain's (0.5 mm at the 35 mm square, 0.36 mm on OpenLOCK), so the terrain sits flush on the base where it had a small upward ledge; both connectors are built from the tile's footprint at zero, and the build keeps, as data, the parts of each connector a cut may not touch. The gate's see-through-hole count treats the rounded corners as outside the tile, as it must now that the base is round there too.
  • Terrain clean-up before pieces: the tile's corner round scales with the system like every size (0.5 mm at the 35 mm square, 0.36 mm on OpenLOCK; DungeonBlocks is unchanged), and so does the coarse lattice the dirt's hills are smoothed on. The terrain's lift takes its layers (each with where it exists, its relief and the land as it stood) instead of recomputing them, the stack mesher takes a layer's presence as a flag instead of reading it from a gap, the flat-faced path is gone (flat faces are a flat Sides), and the edge-round and the plan's corner round are one profile. A dirt whose smoothing does not settle notes relax_unconverged in the build record. New tests cover the smoother, the grain never rising above the shape, the edge height as a ceiling, the set-in's reach and the corner as one watertight surface.
  • Rocks and reeds on the land: the kit has a Land view over the laid terrain (grain included): top, seat and things_at. Rocks are seated by Land::seat, and each build notes how far any foot hovers over the land (seat_gap_max, seat_gap_stones). Reeds root by a height window on the land (1.1 mm under to 0.2 mm over the calm level) and no longer read the water; they are built before it. The water's ripples come from things_at its calm level, the real sections of the rocks and reeds that reach its surface, so reeds now ring the water like rocks and a thing under the surface makes no ring.
  • The heights language, read and solved by check: a tile can write how high each body is. dirt, water, cliff and floor take top= (a height or a range such as "[groundHeight .. groundHeight + 1]", or bottom + … for the surface under it), and pin top=… at=… lines in their block hold the top to a height at a place; slope 30 sets how steeply dirt lies. check works the bands out (no meshes) and refuses what cannot be solved (dt::empty, dt::thin) and notes what looks wrong (dt::steep, dt::shadowed, and dt::edge where a tile's own pin moves a body off the standard edge height). build builds a dirt's and a water's bands and says which lines it does not build yet (a cliff's or a thing's top, a thing at a line or region).
  • Named heights, let and override: the library's heights (groundHeight, dirtEdgeHeight, waterBedHeight, waterSurfaceHeight, waterEdgeHeight, floorHeight, wallHeight and the rest) are let constants in library/parts/heights.dtile; a tile adds its own and replaces a library one with override. Names are camelCase and declared once, of any kind (dt::name); heights are checked for measure (dt::type, so groundHeight + x is refused). Expressions take ranges in brackets [a .. b] and sin cos abs min max clamp smooth, computed in the program so every platform gives the same bits.
  • Defaults with provenance: the tops and pins every body starts with are a defaults block in library/parts/edges.dtile; check --explain lists the ones applied and where they came from, each override, and each pin's share of its place.
  • Places by name: nine built-in points (northWestCorner … center … southEastCorner), the four edge lines and edges, and tile; point NAME "(x, y)"; closed lines (closed=#true); regions with within MM of=PLACE and except; and one place property, at=, that takes a name or "(x, y)" (a body, grass or scatter takes a region, a pin or a thing any place). Points may be written "(x, y)"; "x,y" still reads.
  • Stack order checked: in a tile that uses the new words, base must be the first line (dt::base-order) and nothing may be written after water (dt::fluid-order).
  • The selection is outlined: what you select in the editor's 3D view has an accent-coloured outline round its whole shape, drawn through whatever stands in front of it, so a rock behind a tree still shows where it is. It follows the view as you orbit.
  • The build target: one build is one target, the base system and a printer profile. --printer NAME sits beside --system on build, watch and preview; a profile is a file in library/printers/ (the library now has default and fine) or in a printers/ folder beside your tiles. Output goes to out/<system>/, or out/<system>-<printer>/ for a printer that is not the default, and the build record, the STL header and the 3MF name the target. The default target builds byte for byte what it did.
  • A chip for the build target in the editor: the title bar shows the base system and the printer; click it to change either. Every built tile builds again for the new target, the gate uses the chosen printer's angles, and the choice is remembered in your home folder. The Export card's system switch is gone: Export builds for the chip's target.
  • No --system both: it is removed. Build once for each system.
  • Water is a layer of the terrain: the water and the ground are one closed surface, cut exactly where the water's top meets the ground, so a shore is a clean line instead of a stepped one and the water skin is gone. Where water meets a tile's side, the side shows the water's whole depth in water colour (before, a line of water 0.3 mm deep showed over the earth). Water tiles have fewer triangles and build faster, with fewer repairs; the terrain's corners are rounded to float32 once, so the ground alone survives the STL reload as it was built.
  • Dirt and water are built from their bands: a tile's top= and pin lines now build. dirt draws broad hills in its band (the smoothest surface the band allows, with hills spread over as much of the band as there is room for), water draws its waves inside its band, scaled to fit it, and both are laid on the terrain as layers. A bank is a wide band you draw between the bed and the land, no longer inferred. The grain of a dirt (clods, grit, pebbles) fades out where water covers it. A cliff's or a thing's top=, and a thing at a line or region, are still checked but not built.
  • ground and the old surface forms are gone: the ground element, level= and bumps on dirt and water, the bank rule between areas painted at different levels, and the edges block of library/parts/edges.dtile (its defaults are the edge contract now). dharmatiles migrate FILE… carries a tile written in them to the new words in place: levels and bumps become a top, the bed under water becomes pins, and the stack gains base.
  • The whole library is written in the heights language: every tile starts with base; pools, channels and coves are dirt pinned down to the bed, with a strip a bank's width (7 mm) round the shore as the bank's wide band, and water written last. Looks stay close to before; water is drawn inside its band, so the shore moves with the waves.
  • The levels block is gone: named heights are only let and override. a line's wobble reads a tile's lets (level=groundHeight, level="groundHeight - 1"), library/parts/levels.dtile and its compiled-in copy are deleted, and the editor offers the library's and the tile's heights as completions.
  • One edge height, and one solve: the height a tile meets its edge at is read from the solved pins, with no setter and no fallback, and water's calm level is the library's waterSurfaceHeight. A body is matched to its stack entry by index, with its role (land or fluid) from what its element writes, not by name. check and build share one solve on the build's own lattice, so they cannot disagree.
  • Clean-up of the terrain code: the stack mesher refuses more than 255 layers instead of saturating; the cliff's relief cutters and seats use the stack lift with one layer (the same mesh, byte for byte) and the separate adaptive lift is gone; the ground's words become terrain and land in the code (Kind::Terrain, Scene::land, KitError::Lift); one FNV helper serves the evaluator.
  • Pins reach out from where they win: a pin covered all over by a later pin no longer pulls the surface beside it toward its height.
  • Banks and water look as before again: the library's water band is [waterSurfaceHeight - 1.9 .. waterSurfaceHeight + 3], wide enough that waves, rock ripples and the splash round a rock keep their old height instead of being scaled down or going flat. A dirt's smoothest surface in its band is now the one of least bending (a thin plate, not a membrane), so a bank leaves the land and the bed with no crease; the hills grow away from a pin over a smoothed distance, so there is no crease half way across a bank either. Pebbles are left off ground steeper than about 22 to 34 degrees and a pebble's mound no longer reaches a pinned rim, so a rim is exact (a test checks it) with no special case.
  • Plain dirt: clods=none stones=none is dirt with no texture at all, its hills alone (flat ground is about 660 triangles again). The tiles that were plain ground before (a pond's bed, the ground under a tree) are written so, and migrate writes it for a ground.
  • Editing the water refreshes the dirt under it: the cache key of a dirt step includes the water laid over it.
  • The library's heights are read from disk: library/parts/heights.dtile is no longer compiled into the program.
  • Grain is set into the surface, rim included: a dirt's clods, grit and pebbles no longer rise above the surface its band holds. The kit moves the surface down by the greatest height the grain stands to nearby (within 6 mm, smoothed) and adds the grain back, capped at half the thickness and by the printer's skin and kept off water. The grain runs right to the rim and over the edge-round, with no fade, frame or bare strip: the edge height (3.4 mm) is the most a rim reaches, its high points touching it and its low points up to the grain's depth under it. Bedded stones sit as far lower; a floor over dirt reaches down to the earth's lowest point.
  • Dirt has an edge-round: a 0.2 mm drop of the top along the rim, cut from the sharp corner at the edge height (so the ground on the edge is at most 3.2 mm, and two dirt tiles meet in a faint groove), mitred at the corners and tapering to none on dirt thinner than the printer's skin. The tile's four vertical corners are rounded 0.5 mm in plan on the terrain. The tile's sides are built as panels on columns shared with the top's rim, still flat. Water and floors have no round.
  • Textured sides: dirt's four sides are height fields over their planes carrying the top's grain as low relief, up to 0.6 mm deep (never under the printer's one line), continuous with the top over the edge-round and round the tile's rounded vertical corners (a face there is set back behind both planes, a little less deeply), fading over two layers at the base, under the edge-round and at layer boundaries, and held under the overhang by construction (the one pass down each column). At least 15% of a face stays within 0.1 mm of the plane. No grass blade crosses a plane.
  • A library wall seed changed: 1x1-wall-fieldstone-corner uses seed=15; the lower land left a 0.008 mm³ void between two stones of its old draw.

0.9.0 7 November 2026

The property sheet edits values properly: a helper that says what each control takes, checks as you type and completes level names. Each control's limits are said once and checked when a tile is read. Underneath, the program is rebuilt round a few plain parts, and the editor keeps up with the program across restarts.

  • Drafts keep coming: a fault while showing a draft no longer stops drafts for the rest of the session; it is reported in the terminal.
  • Clearer messages: a line's wobble written with an unknown level says so; a feature not built yet says only that, without the old development phase numbers (a scatter of something it cannot scatter now says what a scatter takes). Every element now words a value it refuses the same way: the control, what it may be, what was written, and what to write instead.
  • What a control takes, said once: each control's numbers, and whether it takes a range, are in the element schema and nowhere else. They are checked when the tile is read, so a value that only comes to a number through level names (level="ground - 50") is refused by check and the editor, not halfway through a build, and a range written for a control that takes one number (cliff height="30..40") is refused the same way. dharmatiles elements, the element pages and the editor's hints show what each control takes: "mm, 20 to 120, or a range".
  • A value editor in the property sheet: clicking a value opens a helper under it that says what the control takes, checks what you type as you type it, switches between one number and a range, and completes at the caret: a level's named levels (with their heights, anywhere in an expression such as ground - 1) and the control's words. A number that also has words, such as a wall's height (full, low or mm), can now be typed as a number in the sheet. Values are typed without quotes; the sheet adds them when the text needs them.
  • A faster gate: the printability check takes about half as long on big tiles (3.5 s to 1.7 s on the 2×2 grass and tree), with the same verdicts.
  • Under the hood: two reviews simplified the program throughout: one set of vector helpers, one request router, fewer copies of the same logic, kinds of solid and materials as types rather than words and numbers, the wall, tree and editor page split into parts that each do one job, and the editor page talking to the program through named calls; one grid type for every lattice of values (heights, fields, flags; the cliff's own copy is gone); and the scene split into the ground surface and the list of parts standing on it, with one question, "what stands here", that rocks and grass ask; the build order worked out as one graph, with the lift a step like any other and every library tile's order pinned by a test; and the overhang test available while an element builds, so a part can be checked in its final pose and shrunk or dropped there. Tiles build exactly as before, except for the two changes below.
  • Grass, re-seeded: each stage of the grass (the flow, the swaths, the clearings, the fronds) draws from its own seeded stream, so a change to one stage no longer reshuffles the others. Every grass tile is laid out afresh at the same seed; it looks as it did.
  • Water as its own part: the water's surface is a thin layer of its own over the ground, not faces of the ground marked as water, so every triangle's material comes from the part that made it. Water tiles print and paint as before; where water meets a tile's side, a line of water 0.3 mm deep shows above the earth.
  • Under the hood, the editor and the program: a tile is named by an id (not its place in the list), so a tile that is deleted leaves no empty slot and a page that reconnects finds its tile again by name; a draft is one request answered with its mesh, with no event, second route or waiting handshake; one build queue holds drafts, tiles asked for and tiles that changed, and a newer draft of a tile cancels the older; the event stream numbers its changes and resumes after a drop instead of starting over; and request reading is separate from routing, so routes are tested without a socket. In the page, three small stores (the server's word, the view, the text) replace one shared bag of state, each part draws by subscribing to them, and the selection is one shape.

0.8.0 7 November 2026

The editor feels alive: selection and hover fade and ease, work in progress breathes, the page rides out the program stopping, and the colours and the grass look truer.

  • The fade eases: selecting something fades the rest in about a tenth of a second; clicking from one thing to another cross-fades them, the new selection rising to solid as the old one sinks into the rest; deselecting brings the rest back, the old selection staying solid until it has caught up. Reduced motion keeps it instant.
  • Work in progress breathes: while a tile builds, a cliff's rough blocks, and the old tile until the first new part arrives, pulse gently in transparency.
  • Opening a tile mid-build now shows the parts it has already made; before, nothing showed until the next stage finished.
  • Pick a tile's size from a grid: click the size in the tile's sheet and a grid of squares opens by the field; point at a size and the field shows it, click to take it, or type as before.
  • The view is in place before the first build: it frames a 1×1 tile at once, then the tile's own footprint, follows the parts as they stream in, and flies to the finished tile.
  • No tint on a faded selection: the fade marks it; the tint comes back with the fade off, or while a rebuild runs.
  • Hover shows through the fade too: with nothing selected, the rest dims a little round what is under the pointer; with a selection, what is under the pointer lifts part way out of the fade, and clicking it rises the rest of the way. Moving between parts cross-fades them.
  • The page waits for the program and reconnects: when dharmatiles preview stops, the gate chip pulses reconnecting… and the tile fades and breathes, as for a build; when it starts again, however long after, the page carries on with the same tile, rebuilding it if needed and saving an edit typed meanwhile. Before, the page could stay stuck: waiting to build forever, showing an old mesh, or ignoring drafts after a restart.
  • New colours: rock and cliffs are a bluer slate grey, dirt a redder brown, bark a greyer brown, grass a yellower green, leaves a purer green and water a greener blue, each still within its real range. They are the default palette's, so they reach the preview, the PNG renders and the 3MF alike.
  • The sidebar no longer sticks half-slid, with the tile list and the tile's page both cut off.
  • Grass stays thick among stones: a blade rooted under a stone, boulder, trunk or flower now grows from its edge instead of not at all, and stones narrower than a blade no longer turn the grass into eddies that stall it. A cliff tile scattered with stones went from about 490 blades a square to about 600; the grass's own bare earth is unchanged.

0.6.0 7 November 2026

Faster cliffs, and the tile shows while it builds.

0.2.0 6 November 2026

The editor, and tiles named by their files.

  • An editor in the preview. Click anything on the tile to select it and see the text that made it; change it in the property sheet or the text. Place and draw rocks, trees, scatters, surfaces, lines and regions straight onto the tile. Drafts show while you drag; the full build follows.
  • Your work is kept. Changes save as you go once they read cleanly; a change that can't be saved is never thrown away without asking. New tile starts a printable flat tile; Make it mine copies a library tile into My tiles.

  • This site, rewritten for tile makers: a page and a picture for every element, and the same look as the editor.
  • The language server is gone; the test suite runs in under three minutes.

  • 3MF painted by material, and an MCP server for agents.
  • The library: ground, water, wall and floor tiles, ready to build.
  • macOS on Apple silicon, through Homebrew.
