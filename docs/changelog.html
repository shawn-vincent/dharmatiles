Unreleased

  • One panel, many levels: the sidebar's source and property sheet are one panel that shows one level of the tile at a time, full height: the tile (its own controls, the stack in build order with a dot on a row that has messages, then its lines and regions), one thing of it (its sheet), one control of that (a range's odds, a height's names, the value in a wide field the program checks as you type). Choosing a row slides to its level (about 200 ms, an instant swap for reduced motion); ‹, a crumb, Esc or ⌘[ slides back to where you left it, ⌘] goes down again, ↑ ↓ Enter move through the rows. One set of crumbs over it (‹ home › meadow › grass in bank › length) and a line or two of context under it (3rd of 5 in the stack · at=bank).
  • Tile | Source in one column: a switch beside the crumbs turns the panel into the source and back, keeping the selection: the cursor goes to the level's line, and an entry the cursor is on is the level when you flip back. Clicking a thing in the view slides the panel to it; the level in the panel is what the view lights.
  • The level is in the address: #tile=…&at=tile/rock/2&control=size (and face=source), so a reload lands on the same level and the browser's Back and Forward step through the levels. The Layout menu is Panel and Look (the source-over-properties and three-pane layouts are gone with the panes); \ hides the panel, and [ and ] no longer hide panes.
  • One stream of what happened: every check, build (with its timing), gate verdict (with its numbers), export and failure of the program's own is one typed message (kind, severity, time, tile, stack entry and span, code, text, help, data, open or cleared) with one text form and one JSON form, kept per tile: a new check or build of the same kind clears what it no longer reports. It travels as message events on GET /api/events and is read at GET /api/messages?tile=&since=&state=&format=text|json, so the notes the checker makes (dt::steep, dt::edge), which never reached the editor, are shown, and an assistant reads what the page reads. Failures the page meets (showError) join it (POST /api/messages).
  • A status line and a messages panel: a 26 px status line under the view says the verdict, the counts (1 error · 1 note), what the save is doing and the build target, with a thin progress bar on its top edge; pressing it, or ⌃J, opens a panel with the build (running, then its record with the gate's numbers), and a row for every open message with Go to line, Show on tile and a copy icon, filters and Copy all. While a build runs the last build's rows stay dimmed, then what it fixed goes (1 cleared by this build). The floating error card, the gate record card, the progress strip and the title bar's verdict chip are gone; notes have their own yellow (--caution) and their own mark in the source's gutter.
  • The top camera is called Top: the view button that looked straight down was labelled Map in the shortcut row and Top in the Display menu; it is Top in both, and the word Map is kept for a map document.
  • The first change to a library tile copies it, and keeps the change: there is no question, no strip over the source and no banner in the sheet. The key you typed, the value you changed or the gesture you made is made in the copy in My tiles (typing 7 after seed=1 gives seed=17, not a lost key), and one line over the view says so, with Undo, which returns to the library tile and deletes the copy (POST /api/tiles/ID/delete, only for a tile in a folder the preview lists, never the library). A small library chip beside the name says what the tile is.
  • The source pane is all source: the 39 px source bar and the 32 px instruction line are gone (the bar remains only while a part of the tile is shown, with Back to the tile); saved, saving and not saved are in the title bar. At 1147 by 650 the source gets 72 px back.
  • The ⋯ menu is Display, and Layout has its own: paint, overhang, wireframe, grid, projection and the views are in Display; where the panel is (The panel on the right) and Look are in Layout, beside it.
  • A build's mistake is marked at its line: dt::look and dt::build get the gutter mark and the wavy underline the check's mistakes always had.
  • The property sheet reads right: a range written with its unit (3.5dmm..6.5dmm) is not given a second one, a region's boundedBy group is headed paint bucket instead of repeating its own row's name, and a sheet that goes on below the pane says more below.
  • A tree with nothing written builds: a bare tree (no top=, no trunk) was refused every time, its 30dmm default trunk leaving a 40dmm tree's crown 0.2 mm under its clumps. The default trunk is now 18dmm, the library oak's.
  • An element's refusal is a mistake in the tile, not a bug: when an element can only tell at build time that a control cannot be built (a top too low for the crown, a trunk too tall), it is dt::look, shown at the entry with its help like any mistake. dt::build (“a bug, not a mistake in your tile”) is kept for failures inside the program.
  • Every entry in the error panel copies its details: a tile's mistakes and the bugs met building it now have a copy icon, as the program's own failures have Copy details. It copies the code, the file, line and column, the words and help, the source lines round it and the environment (version, target, printer, tile, browser, time).
  • A format 0 document is refused, with the command that fixes it: the program reads format 1 only. A document with no header, or version=0, stops with dt::format-0 (“run dharmatiles migrate FILE”) wherever it is read: check, build, watch, the preview and the editor. It used to load migrated in memory with a dt::migrated warning; that warning, the editor’s Update this file and the read_from_format field of the build’s JSON record are gone. dharmatiles --version now says (tile format 1.x).
  • dharmatiles migrate lives in its own crate: dt-migrate holds the format 0 decoder, the older-language step and the move to format 1, and nothing else reads format 0. Each migrated tile is checked as the program now reads it, against the library as it is, and one that does not read stops the run with nothing written; the hash guard that built each tile before and after is gone with the format 0 builder, and --no-guard with it.
  • Two ranges are two rolls in a top= too: top="$groundHeight + [1dmm .. 2dmm] - [1dmm .. 2dmm]" is a band from -1dmm to 1dmm whose heights come up as two dice do, most often near the middle, as the same text in a control value always did (it was one uniform roll over the whole band). A sum or difference of two ranges, with odds or without, no longer has to be one range written round the result: low(0dmm .. 2dmm) + [0dmm .. 2dmm] builds (it was dt::cloud). Still refused: a cloud multiplied or divided by a range, a range divided by a range, dividing by a cloud, sin, cos or abs of a cloud. A tile whose top adds or subtracts two ranges builds differently; every other tile is unchanged.
  • One evaluation of every value: a control's value, a body's top, a pin, a tree's crown and the checks of a number all walk an expression the same way, in one place, with one table of functions; the rule for ranges is stated once in the language guide.
  • A body's top is a control of its element: dirt, water, flagstones, planks and cliff list top among their controls, and so do rock, stone, boulder, pebble and tree (a height worked out where the thing stands). It shows on the element's page, in dharmatiles elements and as a faint row in the property sheet with its help before it is written.
  • The tile, lines and regions are described by the program: the property sheet of the tile, a line, a region and a region's within and except clauses is drawn from the same description of controls the elements use (name, unit, default, help, link to its page), served by the program, and the editor's own copies of those words, of the unit names and of the elements' colours are gone.
  • Names typed a moment ago complete: the editor asks the program to read the text for its definitions, regions, lines and points a moment after each change, so a define typed seconds ago is offered at once and one inside a comment or a string is not.
  • A selected cloud is easier to read: the span drawn at each tree tip or stone, and a sheet's contour, are lines a few pixels wide instead of one, and are drawn in a colour of their own (--cloud, a blue) instead of the teal of the selection's outline, which a rock's cloud used to share.
  • The debug layer no longer rebuilds the tile, and clouds do not need it: the clouds a selected top= or pin is drawn from now travel in a section of their own, collected for every editor build and every draft, and the debug layer's own marks (the grass starts and spines) are collected only while the layer is on, asked for by the page with the build or draft. Turning the layer off rebuilds nothing, and a scene kept by the build cache is no longer keyed on it: one kept without the marks is not given to a build that asks for them, one kept with them serves any build.
  • Clouds are found by the program, not by looking at the text: the program tells the editor where each entry's top= and each pin is written, so one inside a // or /* */ comment, a hidden /- node or a raw string is no cloud, and the top= on a call of a definition is the cloud of the tree inside it. A body's top is now a row in the property sheet, so focusing it selects its cloud as a tree's already did.
  • A body in a definition builds: a dirt, water, cliff, flagstones or planks written inside a define used to pass check and then fail build with “this dirt has no solved top”; its top, pins and slope are now read where the definition is called, like those of the tile's own entries, and it builds. Two calls of one definition are two bodies. A top= on a thing that does not take one, inside a definition, is now refused as not built yet (it was ignored there).
  • The editor follows an entry by its identity, not its place in the list: every stack entry has a key (its seed path, tile/grass/0, or its id=) and the mesh the program sends carries the stack it was built from, so the view decodes hover, click, the tint and the pieces against the mesh on screen and the sheet and the text against the text now. Writing a line above an entry, or moving one, no longer points the selection, the hover or the tint at another entry while the previous mesh is still shown.
  • One error panel, from the server to the screen: every failure in the editor, whether it began in the page or in the program's Rust, now shows the same way: a headline (the program's own words, or Something went wrong inside dharmatiles for a bug), a Details disclosure into the page's error and the server's causes and backtrace, and a Copy details button that copies a report with the version, build target, tile, browser and time. The server answers every failure with one error shape, and a panic carries its own stack.
  • Smoother orbiting with the map shown: the editor no longer re-reads its colours for every region on every frame; they are read once per theme.
  • A rebuild no longer wipes a field you are typing in: while a value in the property sheet is open for typing, the sheet is held as it is and redrawn when you close the field.
  • The drawn cloud follows the caret: typing inside a top= or moving the caret there by any means now moves the highlighted cloud at once, and a cloud no longer stays drawn from the previous tile after you open another.
  • No stale draft after a drag: a quick preview that came back late, for a value you had already moved past or after the real build had landed, no longer replaces the newer mesh; the last step of a drag now asks for a preview too.
  • Page errors are shown, not swallowed: a bug in one part of the editor page used to be taken for a lost connection, or stopped the other parts from following a change, with nothing on screen; it now lands in the error panel and the rest of the page carries on.
  • The sheet and the map wait for a clean check: a change made from the property sheet (a control, Hide, Delete), a handle dragged on the map or a node placed from the view is refused, with a note saying why, while the source has an edit not yet checked or a mistake in it; before, it could write at the wrong place in the source.
  • Clouds on every top: a body's top= and a pin's top may be a weighted range, a low surface, a high surface and one weighting (dirt top="$groundHeight + low(0dmm .. 3dmm)"), instead of dt::weighting. Dirt and water roll one smooth surface whose heights come up as often as the weighting says (the roll is read by rank); flagstones, planks and cliffs take their heaved target from it. A pin replaces the cloud where it holds and the body eases back as it does today. Arithmetic: + - * / by numbers, min max clamp smooth keep the weighting; a negative factor swaps low and high with the odds turned round; two clouds, dividing by one and sin, cos, abs of one are dt::cloud errors with the rewrite in the help. A plain range builds as before: no existing hash moves. Trees and rocks follow in later steps.
  • Tree crowns roll inside their cloud: a tree's top= is a cloud like every top, worked out at each branch tip instead of once per tree. A tree reads $x $y $d $a $bottom $region: its own region is a disc round the trunk ($d 0% at the trunk, 100% at the crown's rim, $a round it) and parent($region) the region it stands in, so a grove taller in the middle is top="$bottom + 36dmm + 20dmm * (1 - d(parent($region), %))" (new test tile 2x2-grove). The tips roll smoothly inside the cloud (neighbouring tips alike, ranked through the weighting) and the crown is stretched or squeezed to meet them. The crown overshoot goes: the clumps and leaves stood about 9 mm above the height asked for; the tips now stand a clump's depth under the cloud, so the whole tree stays at or under its high, and a top that leaves no room above the bare trunk is refused where it is low. Your own trees come out about 9 mm shorter for the same top=, since it is now the height of the whole tree; add about 9dmm to keep one as it was. Tree tiles change and are re-baselined (every other tile's hash is unchanged): the library and test trees' tops are raised by the overshoot they used to have (35dmm to 44dmm, 40dmm to 49dmm, the tree row [35dmm .. 41dmm], the glade [39dmm .. 45dmm], the oak 59dmm) so they keep their look.
  • Rocks take a cloud top: rock, stone, boulder and pebble accept top= (alone, in a scatter or in a part), a cloud worked out at each stone's middle with $x $y $d $a $bottom $region ($region the stone's own disc, parent($region) the region it stands in). Each stone rolls one number in it, from one smooth field over the tile ranked through the weighting (a lone stone draws its own), and its peak stands there: the stone keeps its width and facets and is stretched or squeezed upward. A peak under 1 mm or over 120 mm above the foot is refused. Rocks without a top= build exactly as before. Two test tiles (2x2-rock-clouds, 1x1-rock-tops).
  • A cloud is drawn as part of the selection: put the caret in a top= value or a pin, or use the top field in the property sheet, and that entry's cloud is drawn in the accent, as a selected region is: a surface's low and high sheets shaded node by node by how often each height comes up (the weighting's density at that end; a plain range is an even half), and a tree's branch tips and a rock's stones as short spans from low to high with a marker at the height the roll gave, shaded the same way. Pointing at a top's line, or the sheet's top row, draws it quietly. It is marks only: nothing reaches an STL or the gate and no hash moves. The debug layer keeps grass starts and spines.
  • A calm level that cannot be read is an error: a tile with water whose calm level ($waterSurfaceHeight) fails to evaluate is now a dt::calm error at the water, with help, instead of water that quietly behaves as if uncalmed. A tile with no water is unaffected, and no library hash moves.
  • A region that cannot be made is an error: regions that name each other round a loop (or name a place that does not exist) are now a dt::region error at the region, whether or not an element uses it, instead of an empty region.
  • Fallbacks are counted: when the kernel refuses a finishing step on a cliff stone (a chip cut, relief, a top crack, grain, or an outline for the stone's prism) the stone goes on without it as before, and the build now says so: cliff finishing steps skipped in the summary's left-out line, and tree.leaves_lying_undraped in a tree's stats. Nothing built changes, and no hash moves.
  • check takes the build target: dharmatiles check accepts --system, --printer and --seed as build does, so a tile that fails only on OpenLOCK (its units and sizes follow the system) is caught without building.
  • A broken library printer is an error, not a panic: the command line (build, check, audit) reads the default printer with Options::new(), which reports why it cannot (a broken $DT_LIBRARY) instead of panicking.

1.0.0 11 November 2026

Format 1: units with dmm, points from the north-west, $ names and the context, regions with parents, dharmatiles migrate and migrate-on-load

  • Format 0 leftovers: dharmatiles elements shows units as dmm and deg with format 1 defaults and a weighting example with units; the help in error messages (a missing top=, a tree's top, a line with too few points, a fill, a point, within, a ^ in a range) writes $ names, units and points from the north-west corner; the hints for words from before format 0 give their examples in format 1; a control's limits are given in dmm. The editor's place tool writes one point for a thing's at= whatever the drag (a thing's at= takes one point, never a range), and the library's comments are in format 1.
  • Tiles are format 1: every library tile, test tile, part, printer and palette, and the element pages' demo tiles, are now written in format 1 (a dharmatiles version="…" header, $ names, units on lengths and angles, points counted from the north-west corner; see the language guide and the element pages), and the builds are bit for bit what they were (the baseline hashes did not move). The program writes format 1: dharmatiles migrate FILE|FOLDER… carries your own documents over in place (each tile built before and after, and a mesh that moves stops it), and a document in format 0 is migrated in memory as it loads, with a dt::migrated warning, the file left as it is (the editor's Update this file writes the new text). The development switch DT_FORMAT_PREVIEW is gone; dharmatiles --version and the About box say 0.x, 1.x, and the editor's New tile writes the header. The editor's weighting gallery is not yet available for a format 1 value (type the weighting). The site is written in format 1 to match: the language guide and every element page state the format 1 rules and show format 1 source (each example is checked with dharmatiles check), the generated controls tables show defaults as format 1 values (5dmm, 34deg, ranges with a unit at both ends), and the code colouring marks the header line, $ names and units.
  • Format 1 in development: $ names read from the context (the hidden first argument of every formula and definition call; the library's heights are $groundHeight, $waterSurfaceHeight, … installed at the root, and an element sets $x, $y and $bottom where it works a height out at each point), let and override in any block (a tile, a stack, a scatter, a definition), a let read where it is used so it follows an override made below it, and reserved words that a plain let or param may not take. check refuses a read nothing installed, or a position where a size is drawn, at the spot. Read a format 1 document with DT_FORMAT_PREVIEW=1; without it the dt::format-newer refusal stands. Format 0 tiles read and build as before; no hash moved. The language guide moves with the release.
  • Format 1 in development, step 2, regions and their parents: every thing makes its region and regions nest. $tile is the root (its parent is none); a named region's parent is the smallest region holding its fill point, else the tile, and regions that only share a line (a pool and its bank) are siblings; a tree makes a disc of its crown width, a rock or flower a disc of its size, a wall a strip of its thickness along its line; a body or a scatter acts in its at= region, else the tile; each thing a scatter lists has its own region under the scatter's. $region, $tile and parent(…) are a new kind of value, a region: used as a number it is a dt::type error, and parent($tile) says the tile has no parent. check works out how many parents each placement has and refuses a parent(…) that reaches past the tile at its spot, before any build; check --explain lists the region tree. Regions are rasters on the build's lattice, held with their bounding boxes. Format 0 is untouched; no hash moved.
  • Format 1 in development, step 3a, coordinates and units: a scatter is a region of its own (its at= region, the strip of its line, else the whole tile) under the region it is written in, and each thing it places sits in it: a tree in a tile-level scatter has the scatter for its parent and the tile for its grandparent. Rows and columns: x and y start at a frame's north-west corner, x east along a row and y south down a column; geometry keeps its own frame and the reader converts at the language's edge (y becomes rows − y), so a tile written as the mirror of a format 0 one builds the same mesh; north, south-west and the other named edges and corners keep their places. Points are written in parentheses inside quotes, "(0.65sq, 1sq)", each half a length, a % in one a share of the frame's box; point($tile, 50%, 0) and polar($tile, 40%, 90deg) name the frame; a point without parentheses is an error whose help shows the tuple; a point takes plain names (a let of the tile), never $x $y $d $a $bottom; one off the tile is refused at check. Coordinates: x(region, unit), y(…), d(region, %) (0% deepest, 100% at the edge, scaled by the deepest part of each connected piece, a region thinner than a cell one cell deep), d(region, mm) (millimetres in from the edge), a(region, %|deg|rad) (round the deepest point or ridge middle, along a line's strip from its first through point to its last) and the shorthands $x $y (x($tile, sq), y($tile, sq), plain numbers) and $d $a (the thing's own region, fractions); check refuses $a in a region with a hole. Each element names what it sets: a body (dirt, water, floors, a cliff) sets all of them at each node, a tree only $bottom and $region. Units: sq, mm and in (printed lengths that do not scale with the system), dmm (a design millimetre, 1/35 of a square, which scales: what every size written today is), layer (the printer's), deg, rad, and pi a plain number; a unit follows its number (8dmm, 4.5 mm, 2 pi rad, (2 * pi) rad) and binds tighter than * and /; a value with a unit is a quoted string; % is a plain fraction. No unit is implied: a bare non-zero number where a length or an angle goes is an error (“write 8dmm or 8mm”), and lengths, angles, plain numbers and regions are checked apart before any build ($bottom + 20%, 2mm * 3mm, cos(720) and 1/2rad are refused). Angle controls, a slope and the printer's overhang take deg; counts stay plain. Read with DT_FORMAT_PREVIEW=1; format 0 reads and builds as before and no hash moved.
  • Format 1 in development, step 3a continued, points in a region's frame and the editor: a point written inside a body that acts in a named region (a pin) is read in that region's frame, a % a share of its bounding box and the origin its north-west corner; point(parent($region), 25%, 75%) and polar(parent($region), 40%, 90deg) (out from the deepest point of the region's largest piece, a share of its depth) name another frame, and a part's points are worked out at each call site. The decoder runs before the region tree exists, so it records such a point as written and check works it out once the tree is built. A pin's point outside the region its body acts in is a dt::point error at check, pointing at it. The editor and preview read and edit format 1 under DT_FORMAT_PREVIEW=1: a tile opens, builds, shows its errors, and what the sheet and the place tools write carries units ("8dmm", "(0.5sq, 1.75sq)" counted from the north-west corner); the typed-value check, the completion of $ names, functions and units, the map's markers and a placed thing's handle read the format of the document being edited. The weighting gallery is withheld in format 1 (type the value). Format 0 reads and builds as before; no hash moved.
  • Format 1 in development, step 3b, migrate 0 to 1 (nothing in the library is converted yet): DT_FORMAT_PREVIEW=1 dharmatiles migrate FILE|FOLDER… carries documents to format 1 in place: the header line first (dharmatiles version="1.0.0+5.gabc1234": the program's build details under the format's own major, until 1.0.0 ships), names gain their $ (x, y, bottom and the library's heights; override groundHeight 6 is override $groundHeight "6dmm"), points become "(Xsq, Ysq)" counted down from the north-west corner (a decimal comes back as itself: the reader works the flip to twelve decimals), a point in a pin of a body at a region or a line becomes point($tile, …) so its frame cannot move, every length gets dmm (a size, a top, a pin, a let, both ends of a range, (mm)4.5 as 4.5dmm), angles get deg (split="34deg", the argument of sin and cos), weightings keep their extra plain numbers, and counts, shares and densities stay plain; the tide is cos(720deg * ($x - $y)) and a tile's own read of y is (rows - $y). A param or let takes its unit from where it is used (through nested definitions and the parts a tile uses); one used both as a length and as a count, or never used, stops that file, naming the line. A let named for a word of the language is renamed; an override of a plain let, a point in a part, and a y in a part outside the tide are for a hand. An unstamped tile with format 1 forms is refused where they are (dt::migrate-header if it only forgot its line, dt::migrate-mixed if it has both); a stamped one at format 1 is left alone, whatever its minor or build part. Migrating a tile takes the parts it uses along (a library part is checked in memory and says so). The hash guard: each tile is built before and after, the library's heights and parts migrated with it, and the meshes' hashes compared; a mesh that moves names its file and writes nothing (--no-guard skips the builds, --check says what would change and exits 1 if anything would). Migrated as it loads: a program whose current format is 1 (Options::current_format; today 0) migrates an older document in memory as it reads it, builds the result with a dt::migrated warning (the CLI, check, the preview's problems list), writes read from format 0 in the build record (its document hash is the file as written), and leaves the file as it is until migrate, or the editor's Update this file; what migrate refuses, loading refuses with the same error. Every library file migrates (109 tiles and test tiles, the heights, the edge defaults, the grass and tree parts, the two printers and the palette), and all 109 tiles build the same mesh, hash for hash, on DungeonBlocks (cargo test --release -p dt-eval --test migrate -- --ignored builds each before and after); the library's heights and edge defaults are read in either format.
  • Versions: a document of any kind (tile, parts, printer, palette) may start with a header line, dharmatiles version=0 (or version="0.9.0+120.gf9481c8"); only the major part is the format, none is format 0, and a format newer than the program reads is refused (dt::format-newer). A header out of place, twice or malformed is a dt::header… error with help; editing leaves it as written. Nothing writes it yet and no tile changed. The program's version is drawn from git: 0.9.0 at the tag, 0.9.0+120.gf9481c8 on main N commits past it, 0.9.0+branch.120.gf9481c8 on a branch, and .dirty (it was -dirty, after the hash) added for uncommitted changes to the engine's files. dharmatiles --version adds the tile formats it reads, and the editor has an About box in the view's ⋯ menu. cargo xtask release refuses a dirty tree and, unless --no-tests, rebuilds the baseline and the element pictures from the committed tree and refuses if any differs.
  • Water after the rocks, before the grass: the ten library tiles with water and grass, the shore and stream test tiles and the stack and reeds demos list rocks (and reeds) before the water and grass after it, so the water rings each rock with chop again and the grass still stops at the waterline. The water fills over a rock listed before it to its level, so a low rock may only just show. The water, stack and composing pages and the language guide say so.
  • A tree's height is its top=: written like every top, in mm above zero, with bottom the ground at the tree's foot. top="bottom + 40" is a 40 mm tree wherever it stands; top="groundHeight + 40" reaches 40 mm above zero, so the tree is shorter on a mound. Each tree draws its own top, so it takes a weighting (bottom + low(30..40, 4)). A tree is 20 to 120 mm over its foot, or the build stops at the top. A tree's size= is gone: it is an error whose help gives the top=, and dharmatiles migrate rewrites size=40 to top="bottom + 40". A call's top= is the tree's inside it: oak top="bottom + 40" (the oak's size parameter is gone). Rocks and flowers keep size=, their width. The eight library and test tiles with trees migrated; no hash moved.
  • The debug layer: ⌃⌥D (or Debug layer in the view's ⋯ menu) draws how the build made the tile over the editor's view: every grass blade start coloured by its fate (grew, cut short, outside, too short at a line or the tile edge, crowded, under a stone, bare soil, cover), each blade's spine, the grass flow, and the low and high of every band a tile writes as translucent sheets.
