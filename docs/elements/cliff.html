Element · run

cliff

A storey of stacked, broken rock: big blocks resting on each other under gravity, cleaved into columns, cracked across their joints and grained, with dirt on the top and scree at the foot. Along one tile edge it is a run, along two a corner, and the free-standing forms make plateaus, mounds, spires and needles.

Scree is not part of the cliff: it is stones and boulders in a scatter against=cliff, and the earth heaped at its foot is a dirt bank.

What it looks like

The cliff is built from broad, interlocking masses of stone of very different widths, not a pile of round boulders. Joints run mostly vertical, broken by short ledges. Columns of stone step forward and back, so the face casts deep shadows. About a third of the face is broken into clusters of smaller stones and cracks; the rest is quiet, flat rock. Edges carry a few crisp chips. Dirt lodges on the top and in hollows, and angular scree of the same stone lies at the foot.

No two seeds make the same cliff, but every seed reads as the same stone.

Using it in a tile

A cliff on a tile edge stands inside the tile, its face toward the open ground. This is the library's run, with its scree and its dirt bank:

tile "2x1-cliff" size="2x1" seed=1 {
    stack {
        dirt level=edge {
            bank against=cliff height="1.5..3.5" reach="3..6.5"
            scatter per-square=45 { pebble size="1..2.2" buried="20%..50%" }
        }
        cliff along=north height=35 {   // 35 mm above its foot
            rock mixed                  // or: columns, blocks, slabs
            stone-size massive          // or: rubble, broken
            top dirt                    // or: bare
            shattered patches=3 width="5..10"
        }
        scatter against=cliff per-square=55 reach=4 { stone size="1..6" mostly=small }
        scatter against=cliff per-square=2.5 { boulder size="4.5..8" }
    }
}

Two edges make a corner: cliff along="north west". The other forms are named with form=; the free-standing ones need no along=:

cliff form=canyon                  // bands on the west and east edges
cliff along=north form=escarpment  // height ramps from 10 to 40 mm along the edge
cliff form=spire height=50         // a tall tapering stack, standing free

Both ends of a run finish in the standard end section, so runs butt end to end. Write dirt level=edge under a cliff so the ground at its foot meets the next tile flush.

Sizes and levels

  • Tile. Any size from 1×1 squares (35 mm each). A run fits on a 1×1.
  • Height. The cliff top is 35 mm above the foot by default, and flat: the top edge is straight, with dirt on it.
  • Foot. The ground in front of the cliff sits at the standard 3.4 mm edge level, so cliff tiles meet other ground tiles flush (see edges).
  • Stones. Widths vary at least four times over. Scree at the foot is 1–6 mm, with a few fallen blocks of 4.5–8 mm.

Edges and joining tiles

  • Every cliff ends in the same full-height end section, 4 mm long and 11 mm deep, at both tile ends. Two cliff tiles of different seeds butt into one long cliff; the seam reads as another joint.
  • Rock faces on the tile boundary touch it at their high points and never cross it. No stone is cut flat at the edge; stones that would cross are shrunk to fit.
  • Tile sizes, edge names and the edge contract are on the tile system page.

Printing and painting

  • No supports. Print base down at 0.2 mm layers with a 0.4 mm nozzle. Every overhang is aimed within 55° of vertical (65° at most, in small orange patches), and no layer reaches more than 0.6 mm past the one below.
  • Cracks print as dark slots. They are 0.4–0.5 mm wide and cut straight in, so their tops bridge cleanly.
  • No fragile bits. Nothing is thinner than one 0.45 mm line; there are no floating islands; every stone rests on the pile.
  • Painting. Prime dark and drybrush: faces carry a fine grain (0.25 mm deep) that catches the brush, while joints and cracks stay dark. Joints read darker than cracks.
  • Checked on every build. Each STL is one watertight body that survives a float32 reload, and every render paints any face past the overhang limit red.

The full printing rules are on the printing page.

How it is made

Step by step

A cliff is stacked, not carved. A form gives the height the cliff should reach; stones are dropped into the lowest pocket first, each resting on what is under it, until the pile fills the form. Because every stone is seated on its bed at the overhang limit, a stone that rests on rock has no flat ceiling to print.

Front elevation of a 70 mm run. The stacking step animates.

    The animation is a simplified stand-in for the idea, drawn in your browser; it is not the program's output. What the program does, in order:

    1. Form: the form (run, corner, spire, …) gives a target height field: bands grown out from the tile edges, or free-standing rings.
    2. Stack: stones are placed lowest pocket first, each resting on the highest supports under it; neighbouring tops never line up, which is what makes courses read.
    3. Columns: stones that share a footprint stand in one column, cleaved by whole planes planned once on the column's hull.
    4. Cracks: each open face of the pile gets one crack network, planned once so a crack runs on across the joints between stones.
    5. Each stone: cut to its column's planes, grooved under the stone it carries, seated on its bed at the overhang limit, chipped, carved with the face relief and cracks, fitted to the tile (scaled back, never clipped) and grained.
    6. Core: a hidden core behind the stones makes every gap between them a dark recess, never a hole through the cliff.
    7. Scale: the masses scale with the square; cracks, joints, chips and grain are printability sizes and do not.

    The code: crates/dt-looks/src/cliff/.

    The generator is clifflab/rockpile.py (line numbers at commit 31f7c5d). It stacks stones under gravity, then dresses them. Four stages:

    1. Form. make_form (line 413) turns the chosen form into contact planes (for a run: the back edge, both ends and the top) and a target profile the pile grows toward: stepped tiers, gullies cut back, buttresses standing out.
    2. Stack. stack (line 575) fills the lowest spot still short of the target with a stone: it picks a kind (column, block, slab, odd, monolith), grows a footprint, and rests the stone on its highest supports. Its underside fills down to what is below, so there are no gaps. Stones are bigger low down and in quiet zones, and stone tops are kept from lining up like masonry.
    3. Finish. Stones sharing a footprint are dressed as one column whose faces run across the joints (_plan_column, line 1035); columns stand flush or 2.5–4.5 mm back. Each resting stone gets a sloped seat instead of a flat underside (_seat, line 1674). A few chips are knocked off sharp edges (chip_edges, line 1600). Sparse straight cracks grow edge to edge and wrap round corners (_crack_net, line 1098), then each face is cut into slightly tilted sub-faces and the cracks are sunk in (_relief, line 1341).
    4. Assembly. The stones are joined with a hidden core, so a gap between stones is a dark recess, never a hole. The foot ground, dirt bank, scree and top dirt are added, and the result is fused to the base.

    Design record

    For the people developing the look: Shawn's rulings, the requirements, audit numbers, the round history and open issues. Owned by the labs (clifflab). Approved: round 17, accepted 2 Nov 2026.

    Rulings (21)

    Shawn's decisions, oldest first. Each holds unless a later line replaces it.

    DateRulingShawn's words
    30 OctThe design must reach every form on the sheet, not just a straight run.“I love those photos, especially the range. We should not build something that can't meet those needs.”
    30 OctThe depth-map cliffs (rev 3) set the bar for the look.“These are the best cliffs I've ever seen. I love that you already cover all of the different use cases, including spires and stuff, and outcroppings, and all of this.”
    30 OctCliffs must fit on a 1×1 tile: a thin band along the edge.“these cliffs should be supportable in a 1x1 tile, and the thickness of the cliff should respect that.”
    30 OctOnly the rim of a face on the tile boundary is low relief; the middle can be deep.“It's just the edge of the face that is against the tile edge that needs to be low relief… the middle of the tile can have big cuts and rocky construction in it.”
    30 OctFaces on the tile edge are textured to the edge, not cut flat.“you're clipping adjacent edges flat. They should be regular textured surfaces whose outside limits of texture touch the edge.”
    30 OctThe depth map's stacked-block version regressed. This led to rockpile.“the aesthetics have regressed… the kind of the sense of big piled up slabs, like, it doesn't really fit.”
    30 OctRocks fill the space between contact planes (back, ends, top); the front is open. The top is a contact plane, with dirt on it.“I think the top is a contact plane as well. The rock should end up be to touch the top.”
    30 OctThe straight top edge is intended. Flat ceilings must be fixed by construction.“top edge is intended to be a straight line. The flat ceilings should just be fixed.”
    30 OctEnd-to-end matching: a standard end section is enough. A few mm of mismatch reads as another joint.“That's perfect. It's fine.” (on the end-to-end test, after asking “I wonder if we're being too strict.”)
    30 OctSome forms need massive blocks.“I'd like to see more massive blocks in some.”
    31 OctAfter a test print: fine texture must read at print scale; the back wall is too flat. Edge stones touch the boundary plane; inner stones may sit back.“Biggest problem: the fine surface texture does not read at printing scale. The fine texture is important for easy painting.”
    31 OctVoronoi “flake” chips on every face: rejected and reverted.“I hate it. Sorry, undo this texture.”
    31 OctMake the existing grain stronger instead.“so there is a fine texture on this. Can we just make it stronger?”
    31 OctNever cut a stone at the tile edge; shrink it back to touch.“Clipping is bad. Whenever we do clipping, could we scale the thing back away from the clipped barrier instead?”
    31 OctBuild resting stones with sloped bottoms that sink into the stone below, instead of adding keels.“Could we, instead, construct the rocks in the first place with 45 degree bottoms? That embed into the rock beneath them?”
    31 OctSeams between stacked stones must read as grooves.“still lots of stones that merge without meaningful grooves.”
    4 NovThe 55° ruling is now a target with a 65° hard limit, for every look: faces between 55° and 65° are the band (orange, budgeted), over 65° fails (red).“the 65 degree exception that we had for trees, let's generalize it. Anything can be up to 65 degrees. It's useful to know. The two colors is useful, separating them is useful, because one is conservative, one is more extreme. But cliffs should be allowed to have that 65 degree overhang as well.”
    31 OctThe overhang limit is 55° from vertical, not 45°.“45 is too conservative”
    1 NovCracks must be thin, like the reference.“Still gaping… We need the cracks to be thin, like in the reference photos.”
    1 NovEvery crack runs its full width and depth to an edge, joint or crack.“cracks should go to the end, not stop in the middle of a stone.”
    1 NovA crack reaching a convex corner carries on round it onto the next visible face.“cracks don't go to an end and stop if there's another visible side.”
    2 NovRound 17 accepted.“ok. I'm good with cliff round 17.”

    The target is Shawn's heroic cliff spec of 30 Oct 2026, kept in the archive. Its summary line: “That is where the heroic quality lives: broad, powerful masses of stone interrupted by deep, expressive fractures, rather than simply making everything jagged and rough.”

    • REQ-OUT-1 [S] A build writes an STL to clifflab/out/. Amended 30 Oct (rockpile): <N>x<N>-pile-<form>-s<seed>.stl.
    • REQ-OUT-2 [S] One watertight body that survives a float32 STL reload, fused to the DungeonBlocks base.
    • REQ-OUT-3 [S] The foot ground sits in the DungeonBlocks dirt band, 4.0 mm above the base. A run's top is 35 mm above the foot.
    • REQ-OUT-4 [S] Deterministic per seed.
    • REQ-FDM-1 [S] No downward face steeper than 45° from vertical. Amended 31 Oct: 55°, checked as layer reach (no layer more than 0.6 mm past the one below, no run of layers past the 55° cone).
    • REQ-FDM-2 [S] No islands at 0.2 mm layers.
    • REQ-FDM-3 [S] Fissures are wedges, never slots. Amended 1 Nov: cracks are thin slots, 0.4–0.5 mm wide, cut straight in, with ceilings that bridge.
    • REQ-FDM-4 [S] No feature narrower than one line (0.45 mm); no relief under about 0.15 mm.
    • REQ-FDM-5 [S] The top and the foot are solid down to the base.
    • REQ-EDGE-1 [S] Edge cliffs are thin bands, 8–20 mm deep, so a run works on a 1×1 tile.
    • REQ-RUN-1 [R] The face line wanders in plan: buttresses stand out, gullies cut back.
    • REQ-RUN-3 [R] Rock all round; faces on the tile boundary touch it and never cross it; each run ends in the standard end section. Amended 31 Oct: stones that would cross the boundary are shrunk, never cut.
    • REQ-RUN-4 [R] The top edge is broken and irregular. Amended 30 Oct: the top edge of a run is straight; the top is a contact plane with dirt on it. Free-standing forms may have broken tops.
    • REQ-ROCK-1 [R] Interlocking masses, not stacked boulders; widths vary at least 4×. Amended 30 Oct: some forms use massive blocks.
    • REQ-ROCK-2 [R] Mostly vertical and steep joints, broken by ledges.

    • REQ-ROCK-7 [R] No pattern, no repetition.
    • REQ-TOP-1 [R] Dirt on the rock tops, in joints and hollows, thinning to nothing at lips.
    • REQ-FOOT-1 [R] Dirt with angular scree of the same stone, mixed sizes, densest at the face.

    Open issues

    • Thin-wall area is still about twice what it should be, mostly where cracks meet sharp edges.
    • The massif reports holes in its gullies (110 in the review measure); runs and corners report none.
    • The foot sits at 4.0 mm; it moves to the standard 3.4 mm edge level before P7.
    • dt-doc defaults stone-size to rubble, but rockpile's run uses massive.
    • The thin-wall and hole checks live in a review script outside the repo; P7's parity harness needs them in code.
    • Only the run is tuned; the other eight forms share its finish but have not been reviewed round by round.
