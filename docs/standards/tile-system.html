Tile system

How dharmatiles tiles are sized, what they clip into, and how they meet each other. Every tile is built twice: once for DungeonBlocks (35 mm squares) and once for OpenLOCK (1-inch squares). Tiles of the same system butt edge to edge with no step.

Tile, square, cell

Three sizes, three names, used everywhere on this site.

NameWhat it isSize
tileThe whole printed piece.cols × rows squares, e.g. 2×2
squareOne grid unit of the game board.35 × 35 mm (DungeonBlocks); 25.4 × 25.4 mm (OpenLOCK)
cellOne step of the internal height grid.about 0.27 mm (128 per square)

A 35 mm unit is a square, never a “tile”. A 2×2 tile has four squares. Densities are always per square (“45 pebbles per square”), so they mean the same on a 1×1 and a 3×3 tile.

Two base systems

A system is the underside a tile clips into: DungeonBlocks or OpenLOCK. Every tile is built for both. The OpenLOCK version is not a shrunk copy: it is a second build of the same layout at 25.4 mm per square, so it has the same number of squares and detail suited to the smaller size.

Both bases end at the same plane, the datum (z = 0). The base is below it; the terrain is above it. All heights on this page are measured from the datum.

DungeonBlocks base

DungeonBlocks is a commercial terrain system on a 35 mm grid. Each square gets one peg: a column that drops into the socket of the piece below. The geometry follows the published DungeonBlocks blank floor tile, so dharmatiles tiles mix with commercial pieces.

OpenLOCK base

OpenLOCK, by Printable Scenery, is a 1-inch (25.4 mm) grid. Tiles join side by side with separate clips that slide into T-shaped slots in the base walls. There is one clip slot per square on every edge, centred on the square. dharmatiles builds only the 25.4 mm version; the 25.0 mm metric variant does not tessellate with it.

File names

A tile's file name says its size and what is on it:

stl/<db|ol>/<group>/<NxM>-<name>-<db|ol>.stl

For example stl/db/ground/1x1-soil+grass-db.stl. NxM is cols × rows; the group is ground, water, walls or floors; db or ol is the base system.

Terrain order

A mixed tile names its terrains from low to high, joined by +:

water < soil < grass < floors < walls / cliffs

1x1-soil+grass is soil and grass on one tile; 1x1-grass is grass alone. Words after a terrain describe content: 1x1-grass-tree, 1x1-wall-brick-corner. Rotations are not separate files: each shape has one standard orientation, and you turn the printed tile by hand.

Shape suffixes

A shape suffix says which terrain has the shape, by where it sits: before the + for the lower terrain (water-corridor+grass, a water corridor through grass), after it for the higher one (soil+grass-corner, a grass corner on soil).

Edges and orientation

A boundary between two terrains, such as a river bank, leaves the tile only at fixed points on its edges called anchors, so it lines up with the next tile. Anchors sit at 0.5 (the middle of an edge) for half-tile and corner shapes, and at 0.33 and 0.67 for corridors. Positions are measured from the south-west end of each edge: left to right along the bottom and top, bottom to top along the sides.

Inside the tile a boundary may wander, but it passes exactly through its anchors. x runs east (right), y north (up), z up from the datum, with the origin at the bottom-left corner. The Rust tile language names edges by compass (north, south, east, west) and writes positions in squares from the south-west corner.

How tiles meet

The edge contract is what every tile does at its border so that two tiles side by side meet with no step:

  • Ground meets the tile edge at 3.4 mm all round and rises from there. Nothing dips below the edge level.
  • Water meets the edge at a calm, consistent level, so two water tiles continue each other.
  • Boundaries (banks, paths) leave the tile only at the anchor points above.
  • Cliffs end in a standard end section, so cliffs of different seeds join into one run. No stone is cut at the tile edge.
  • Walls butt with a clean vertical cut at the tile edge, as DungeonBlocks walls do; courses need not line up.

  • OpenLOCK. src/dharmatiles/bases/openlock.py: square :20, box :23, slot :24–:31, :48, :88, retention :34–:35, slot positions :96, :115, logo :137. Dimensions ported from Caitlyn Byrne's OpenSCAD-OpenLock; the OpenLOCK developer page's rule is that “the footprint tessellation of each piece must never change, and the location of the OpenLOCK ports must never alter.” The Rust plan makes “an OpenLOCK clip fits” part of the P5 exit.
  • Heights. Library water 3.0 (layers/water.py:49), grass 5.0 (layers/grass_carpet.py:71), floor 7.4 (walls/floor.py:56), wall 33.1 (walls/masonry.py:550). Rust levels in library/parts/heights.kdl: edge 3.4, ground 5.0, water 2.6, wall 33.1.
  • Names. Tiles live at src/tiles/<group>/<NxM>-<name>.tile.py; dharmatiles-gen builds every one under src/tiles/ (src/dharmatiles/terrains/tile.py:1289) and writes the path above (:1083). Experiment tiles live in src/experiments/tiles/, outside the batch glob.
  • Edges. Anchors: src/dharmatiles/core/region.py:23 (_anchor_to_mm). FloodFill(x, y) takes 0–1 coordinates (spec.py:131); edges are Edge.TOP, BOTTOM, LEFT, RIGHT (spec.py:108). Rust places: crates/dt-doc/src/place.rs:1 on rust/p1-doc (decisions V4, V5). Water-to-land strips on the corridor tiles use width_mm=2.5, amplitude_mm=4.0, wavelength_mm=12.0; new shorelines use these unless a shape needs a tighter turn.
  • Edge contract. Ground: grasslab REQ-HILL-8, dirtlab REQ-H-3; dirtlab adds a 0.4 mm flat band at the edge. Water: waterlab REQ-W-9; the library's water lays a sine along the edge with a wavelength of half a square, fading over 5 mm (src/dharmatiles/layers/water.py:153); the Rust contract keeps it: crests at corners and square midpoints, 0.6 mm amplitude, calm within 4 mm. In Rust the contract is one file, library/parts/edges.kdl, delivered in P3: the edge profile must match within 0.05 mm on all four edges and two printed water tiles must butt with no step.
Rulings
  • 4 Jul 2026, walls. DungeonBlocks-compatible by default, every dimension configurable. Walls butt with a clean vertical cut; courses need not match (walls interview, paraphrased in the commercial sets analysis).
  • 5 Jul 2026. Ground bands measured from the commercial STLs as upward-face area histograms, minus the 10.9 mm short base (commercial sets analysis, §1b). The wall default was lowered from 49.7 mm (a whole-piece height) to the visible wall top, 33.1 mm.
  • 30 Oct 2026, cliffs. A standard end section is enough; a few millimetres of mismatch reads as just another joint. Stones are shrunk back to the tile edge, never clipped.
  • 1 Nov 2026. Ground at the edge is 3.4 mm (confirmed default 12); the datum belongs to the connector (confirmed default 6).

Open conflicts

  • The Python config.py docstring says 256 cells per square; the code default is 128.
  • dirtlab's REQ-H-1 puts the dirt edge at 4.0 mm and clifflab's rockpile puts the cliff foot at 4.0 mm. Both are to move to 3.4 mm (rockpile before the P7 freeze, decision R6.6); 3.4 mm is to be confirmed by a test print in P3.
  • Library water sits at 3.0 mm, above the commercial 1.9–2.4 band; the labs and Rust use 2.6 mm.
  • One file breaks the terrain order: src/tiles/water/1x1-grass-tree+water.tile.py names grass before water.
  • Older notes give the output path without the group folder (stl/db/1x1-…-db.stl); the code includes it.

History

This page replaces three earlier documents: the tile design guide (filenames, terrain order, edge coordinates, shapes), the tile naming convention, and the OpenLOCK specification. Where they disagreed with the code, the code won: the output path includes the group folder; the OpenLOCK retention slot is cut on both sides of each socket, 9.0 mm deep, and the clip slot spans z −6.6 to −2.4 mm.
