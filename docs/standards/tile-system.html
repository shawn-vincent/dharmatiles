Tile system

1. Tile, square, cell

Three sizes, three names. These names are used everywhere: in code, in docs and in conversation.

NameWhat it isSizeKey symbols
tileThe whole printed piece.cols × rows squaresTileScene, build_tile_from_spec(), stl/
squareOne grid unit of the game board.35 × 35 mm (DungeonBlocks); 25.4 × 25.4 mm (OpenLOCK)cols, rows, square_mm, cells_per_square
cellOne subdivision of the height grid.square_mm / cells_per_squarecell_w, grid_w, grid_h

Never call a 35 mm unit a “tile”. A 2×2 tile has four squares. Density parameters always end in _per_square (rocks_per_square, groups_per_square), so a density means the same thing on a 1×1 and a 3×3 tile.

The Python defaults are in src/dharmatiles/core/config.py:103: square_mm = 35.0 and cells_per_square = 128, so a cell is 35 / 128 ≈ 0.27 mm. (The docstring above it still says 256; the code says 128.) An OpenLOCK build caps the grid at 64 cells per square, about 0.40 mm, close to one printed line (src/dharmatiles/systems.py:107).

The Rust port keeps the same three words. Its Frame holds cols, rows, square_mm and cells_per_square, defaulting to 35 mm and 128 cells (crates/dt-kit/src/scene.rs:11 on branch rust/p1-skel). In the KDL tile language, positions are written in squares and sizes in millimetres at the 35 mm reference square; an OpenLOCK build scales every written size like a photocopy (decision R6.5, see the Rust plan).

2. The datum and the two systems

A system is the underside a tile clips into: DungeonBlocks or OpenLOCK. Every tile is built for both by default (src/dharmatiles/spec.py:243). The OpenLOCK build is not a shrunk copy of the DungeonBlocks mesh. It is a second build of the same tile at 25.4 mm per square, so the layout, measured in squares, is the same and the grid resolution suits the smaller square.

The datum is z = 0. It is the top of the base (the connector) and the bottom of the terrain. The connector owns everything below z = 0; looks own everything above it. This holds for both systems (confirmed default 6, 1 Nov 2026). All heights on this page are above the datum unless they say otherwise.

3. DungeonBlocks base

DungeonBlocks is a commercial terrain system on a 35 mm grid. Each square gets one peg: a column that drops into the socket of the piece below. The geometry follows the published DungeonBlocks blank floor tile and is built in src/dharmatiles/bases/dungeonblocks.py:80 (make_base) from the constants in src/dharmatiles/core/config.py:321 (BaseConfig).

These match the measured commercial pieces: ground pieces use the short base, wall pieces the tall one (commercial sets analysis, §1b). The peg choice looks at every mesh on the tile, trees and walls included, not just the terrain. The Rust connector carries the same numbers (crates/dt-looks/src/connector.rs:43 on rust/p1-skel) and is checked against the Python volume.

4. OpenLOCK base

OpenLOCK, by Printable Scenery, is a 1-inch grid (25.4 mm). Tiles join side by side with separate clips that slide into T-shaped slots in the base walls. The base is a solid box with the slots cut out by boolean CSG (src/dharmatiles/bases/openlock.py:60). Dimensions are ported from Caitlyn Byrne's OpenSCAD-OpenLock; the rule from the OpenLOCK developer page is that “the footprint tessellation of each piece must never change, and the location of the OpenLOCK ports must never alter.”

There is one clip slot per square on every edge, centred at (i + 0.5) × 25.4 mm (bases/openlock.py:96 and :115). A 25.0 mm metric variant exists in the community; it does not tessellate with 25.4 mm tiles, and dharmatiles builds only 25.4 mm. Standard OpenLOCK wall heights are 25.4 mm (half) and 50.8 mm (full).

The OpenLOCK base is built and exported today but has not yet been test-printed against a real clip; the Rust plan makes “an OpenLOCK clip fits” part of the P5 exit.

5. Standard heights

Two places still disagree with the 3.4 mm edge level: dirtlab's REQ-H-1 puts the dirt edge at 4.0 mm, and clifflab's rockpile puts the cliff foot at 4.0 mm. Both are to move to 3.4 mm (rockpile before the P7 freeze, decision R6.6). The 3.4 mm level is to be confirmed with a test print in P3.

6. Names and output paths

A Python tile lives at src/tiles/<group>/<NxM>-<name>.tile.py, where the group is ground, water, walls or floors and NxM is cols × rows (1x1, 2x2, 3x3). dharmatiles-gen builds every *.tile.py under src/tiles/ (src/dharmatiles/terrains/tile.py:1289) and writes

stl/<db|ol>/<group>/<NxM>-<name>-<db|ol>.stl

(terrains/tile.py:1083). The group folder is part of the path; the shorter form stl/db/1x1-…-db.stl found in older notes is out of step with the code. Experiment tiles that are not part of the set live in src/experiments/tiles/, outside the batch glob.

Terrain order

A mixed tile names its terrains from low to high:

water < soil < grass < floors < walls / cliffs

A + separates the terrains: 1x1-soil+grass is soil and grass on one tile. A single-terrain tile has no +: 1x1-grass, 1x1-water. Rotations are not separate files; each shape has one canonical orientation and the printed tile is turned by hand.

Shape suffixes

A shape suffix says which terrain has the shape, by where it sits: before the + for the lower terrain (water-corridor+grass, a water corridor through grass), after it for the higher one (soil+grass-corner, a grass corner on soil).

Other words after the terrain name describe content, not shape: 1x1-grass-tree, 1x1-grass-flowers, 1x1-wall-brick-corner, 1x1-floor-stone. One file breaks the terrain order: src/tiles/water/1x1-grass-tree+water.tile.py names grass before water.

7. Edges, anchors and orientation

A boundary between two terrains starts and ends on the tile edge. Its end points are anchors: an edge plus a position t from 0 to 1 along it. t = 0 and t = 1 are corners; 0.5 is the middle. t runs from the south-west end of each edge: left to right along the bottom and top edges, bottom to top along the left and right edges (src/dharmatiles/core/region.py:23, _anchor_to_mm).

The anchors are the compatibility points between neighbouring tiles. An organic boundary may wander inside the tile, but it must pass exactly through its anchors. Waypoints route a path through the tile without moving its anchors. A water-to-land boundary is a strip, not a line: the corridor water tiles use width_mm=2.5, amplitude_mm=4.0 and wavelength_mm=12.0, and new shorelines use these unless a shape needs a tighter turn.

Coordinates: x runs east (right), y runs north (up), z runs up from the datum. FloodFill(x, y) takes 0–1 coordinates with (0, 0) at the bottom-left (src/dharmatiles/spec.py:131). Python names the edges Edge.TOP, BOTTOM, LEFT, RIGHT (spec.py:108). The Rust tile language uses compass words for the same edges (north, south, east, west, and corners like north-west) and writes positions in squares from the south-west corner (crates/dt-doc/src/place.rs:1 on rust/p1-doc; decisions V4 and V5).

The canonical orientation in the table above is only a convention for writing specs. Printed tiles can be turned freely.

8. The edge contract

The edge contract is what every tile does at its border so that two tiles placed side by side meet with no step. It has four parts.

  • Ground. Ground meets the tile edge at one level, 3.4 mm, all round, and rises from there; nothing inside dips below the edge (grasslab REQ-HILL-8, dirtlab REQ-H-3). dirtlab adds a 0.4 mm flat band at the edge.
  • Water. Water meets the edge at a calm, consistent level (waterlab REQ-W-9). The library's water already lays a sine along the tile edge with a wavelength of half a square, fading over 5 mm (src/dharmatiles/layers/water.py:153). The Rust contract keeps that sine: crests at corners and square midpoints, 0.6 mm amplitude, calm within 4 mm of the edge.
  • Lines. Boundaries leave the tile only at the anchor positions in §7.
  • Cliffs. A cliff that runs off the tile ends in the standard end section, so runs of different seeds join. Shawn, 30 Oct 2026: a standard end section is enough; a few millimetres of mismatch reads as just another joint. Stones are never clipped at the tile edge; they are shrunk back to touch it.

Walls butt without matching courses: a clean vertical cut at the tile edge is correct, the same way DungeonBlocks pieces butt (Shawn, walls interview, 4 Jul 2026).

In the Rust port the contract is one file, library/parts/edges.kdl, delivered in P3, whose exit criterion is that the edge profile matches within 0.05 mm on all four edges and two printed water tiles butt with no step.

History

This page replaces three earlier documents: the tile design guide (filenames, terrain order, edge coordinates, shapes), the tile naming convention, and the OpenLOCK specification. Where they disagreed with the code, the code won: the output path includes the group folder; the OpenLOCK retention slot is cut on both sides of each socket, 9.0 mm deep, and the clip slot spans z −6.6 to −2.4 mm. The ground heights were measured on 5 Jul 2026, and the wall default was lowered from 49.7 mm (a whole-piece height) to the official visible wall top the same day. The 3.4 mm edge level was confirmed on 1 Nov 2026. The originals are in the archive: tile design guide, tile naming convention, OpenLOCK specification.
