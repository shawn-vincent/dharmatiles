The printability gate

Every build is checked before it is written, and a tile that fails is reported as failed (exit code 3) even though its files are still written for inspection.

  • Watertight after the mesh is reloaded at float32, the precision an STL stores.
  • Nothing lost: every part is joined to the tile. Sealed voids are filled and reported, not counted as lost.
  • Overhang: 55° from vertical is the target for every element. Faces between 55° and 65° are the band: allowed in small patches, up to 0.1% of the tile's downward-facing area, and painted orange. Faces past 65° are overhang, painted red, and allowed only up to another 0.1%. More than either budget fails. Both angles are the printer profile's (overhang 55, steepest 65 in the printer); 65° is where a 0.2 mm layer steps out 0.43 mm, just under one 0.45 mm line.
  • A gap narrower than the printer's gap (0.2 mm) counts as solid, so the lip over a fine joint is not an overhang.
  • The gate is never loosened, and geometry is never culled to pass it. A failure is fixed where the geometry is made.
  • An element meets 55° by construction. Where it cannot be sure (a part on a curved surface, parts lying over each other), it asks the gate's own test of the part in its final pose, on what it rests on, and shrinks or drops the part then (dt_kit::hang and fit).
  • Tiles print base down, with no supports. See printing.

Tiles

  • A tile is the whole printed piece; a square is one grid unit (35 mm for DungeonBlocks, 25.4 mm for OpenLOCK); a cell is one subdivision of the heightmap. A square is never called a tile, and densities are per square.
  • DungeonBlocks is the default base; OpenLOCK is the other. One document builds for either, and every size it writes scales with the square.
  • Dirt reaches no higher than $dirtEdgeHeight, 3.4dmm, at any tile edge (its grain is set into that height, so its rim's high points reach it and its low points lie up to the grain's depth under it) and water meets every tile edge at $waterEdgeHeight, so any two tiles sit side by side: the library pins both at edges (library/parts/edges.dtile), and a tile's own pin wins where it writes one. A built floor (flagstones, planks) meets every tile edge flush at $floorEdgeHeight, 7.4dmm, with no edge-round and its faces at the plane carrying the material's grain (never past it, at least 15% of each face within 0.1 mm of it), so floor tiles meet with no step and an outdoor tile beside one is a 4 mm step down. The standard heights are in library/parts/heights.dtile.
  • A pieces body (the floors and the cliff) is made to fit its band: each slab, board or stone is drawn inside the band as it is made and never trimmed after, and one that still misses is a bug the build refuses (dt::band). Its grain never rises above the band's top. A field body (dirt, water) is clamped point by point and cannot miss.
  • The stack is built in the order it is listed: each entry stands on the terrain laid so far, and what is listed after it is laid over or beside it (a dirt after a tree banks against the trunk; grass after the water keeps off the wet ground; a dirt after the water lies over it; water is built where it is listed, with no rule that it comes last). The plan never reorders: it refuses an order it cannot build (dt::order). Only the lift, which comes after the last surface, and base, built last, are placed by what they are.
  • Dirt behaves like dirt: it lies in its region on whatever surface is under it (ground, a floor, a cliff's stones, the tops of loose rocks listed before it), measures its band from that surface, and stays only where the surface is flatter than its maxSlope (35°) and is not laid at a drop (the high side of a step deeper than 1 mm, or within 1.5 mm of one); a rise is no drop, so dirt banks up to the foot of a stone. It slides off steeper faces, so a cliff's faces are clean, and it never bridges a slit between stones. Nothing in it knows about cliffs.
  • Dirt's edge-round is the material's own, 0.2 mm at the 35 mm square on every printer: a drop of the top at the rim, cut from the sharp corner at the edge height, mitred at the tile's corners, tapering to none on dirt thinner than the printer's skin. Water has none, and floors have none except weathered flagstones (texture=worn), whose worn edges take a round of their own. Edge height less the round is never under the tide's crest. The tile's four vertical corners are rounded 0.5 mm in plan at the 35 mm square, scaled with the system like every size (0.36 mm on OpenLOCK), and the base's four corners take the same round, so the terrain sits flush on it.
  • The tile's four sides are height fields over their vertical planes: a face is only ever set back behind its plane, never past it, so tiles butt and slide into clips and onto boards. Dirt's sides carry the top's grain as low relief, up to 0.6 mm deep at the 35 mm square (never under the printer's one line, 0.45 mm), continuous over the edge-round and round the rounded corners (where a face is set back behind both planes), with at least 15% of a face within 0.1 mm of the plane, fading to zero over two layers (0.4 mm) at the base, below the edge-round and at each layer boundary. No face leans out past the overhang angle, by construction. Water's side is smooth; where no dirt reaches an edge there is no relief on it. No grass blade crosses the plane.
  • z = 0 is the top of the base; the base owns everything below it. A tile with no base line has no base: it prints flat on the bed from zero up, and the connector rules do not apply to it.
  • Nothing is clipped at the tile edge: a thing near the edge is moved or shrunk back until it fits inside.
  • A tile is named by its file and nothing else: the tile node takes no name, and seeds are keyed by the seed and the stack, never the file name, so renaming a tile never changes it.
  • File names and edges follow the tile system.

Builds

  • Deterministic: the same document, seed and system give the same bytes on one machine, at any thread count, and the same look on any machine.
  • The library is files on disk, read from the checkout's library/ when run inside it, else $DT_LIBRARY, else the copy installed beside the program.
  • Documents are .dtile files, KDL v2 underneath. Each starts with dharmatiles version=…, whose major number is the format; this program reads format 1, carries a format 0 document to it in memory as it loads (with a dt::migrated warning), and refuses a newer one. dharmatiles migrate rewrites a file in place, only after building the tile before and after and finding the mesh identical.
  • Every length is written with its unit (8dmm) and every angle with deg or rad; dmm is 1/35 of a square and scales with the system, mm, in and layer are printed lengths and never do. Kinds are checked before any build. See the language guide.
  • Positions count from the north-west corner, across then down, in parentheses with units ("(0.65sq, 1sq)"). A $ name is read from the context a formula is called with; a plain name is declared once where it is written; check refuses a read of a name an element does not set before any build. Every frame is a region and regions nest; the tile has no parent.

Pictures

  • Every render paints overhang red and the band orange. On a model those colours mean overhang and nothing else; the interface's own red (--err) marks errors and destructive actions.
  • Tiles are shown on the plate (#D9E0EB), whole, never cropped.
  • The site and the preview share one visual system.

The project

  • Nothing is swallowed and nothing is hidden: your mistakes are shown where you made them, the program's are shown loudly, and no fallback quietly changes what is built. See errors.
  • dharmatiles is a Rust program. The only other code is the paint organizer and two STL helpers in extras/, which are not part of the program.
  • This site is the reference for the tile language: the guide, and one page per element. Each element page's controls table is generated from the program (cargo xtask docs), and a new element needs its page before it ships.
  • The site describes the program as it is. How it got here lives in git history (the tag before-rust-only holds the old design notes and the Python library).
  • Tests run locally: cargo test --release --workspace, with clippy and rustfmt clean.
