Printing rules

Every tile must print on a home FDM printer, base down, with no supports. This page says what printer we design for, which shapes it can lay, how texture must be sized to survive, how renders show failures, and how an STL is checked before anyone slices it.

1. The printer profile

A printer profile is the small set of numbers that every look reads in order to make printable geometry. There is one profile today:

SettingValueWhere it is written
ProcessFDM, base down, no supportsclifflab and dirtlab requirements
Nozzle0.4 mmclifflab, dirtlab, rocklab, waterlab requirements
Line width (one extrusion line)0.45 mmgrasslab/grasslab.py:130
Layer height0.2 mmgrasslab/grasslab.py:131, clifflab/rockpile.py:172
Overhang limit, from vertical55°clifflab/rockpile.py:171
Reach of one layer past the layer below0.6 mmclifflab/rockpile.py:173

Shawn slices in Bambu Studio and inspects meshes in MeshLab. Resin is a second target for grass: the same blade shape is smoothed so the layer steps melt, with surfaces within about 20 µm of the stepped shape (grasslab REQ-OUT-6).

The rule behind the profile is “model only what prints”. Shawn, 29 Oct 2026, on grass blades: “make it more honest about the shape, so that we can refine THAT rather than a theoretical ideal.” A grass blade is built directly as a stack of 0.2 mm layers, each a whole number of 0.45 mm lines wide, and only then smoothed (grasslab requirements §1.2).

In the Rust port the profile is the Printer struct: 55°, 0.2 mm layers, 0.6 mm reach over a 3 mm span, a 0.1 mm ray grid and an overhang budget of 0 mm² (crates/dt-kit/src/audit.rs:20 on branch rust/p1-skel). It is meant to move into a library file, library/printers/default. Looks read it; tiles do not write printer numbers.

2. The 55° overhang limit

An overhang is material printed over air: a downward-facing surface. The printer can lay a layer slightly wider than the one below, and a run of layers can lean out gradually. Past a certain lean the plastic droops.

The limit is 55° from vertical. No downward face may lean further from vertical than that. Shawn raised it from 45° on 31 Oct 2026: “45 is too conservative.”

How overhang is measured

Two tests exist. The face-angle test flags a face whose normal points within the limit of straight down: n_z < −sin(OVERHANG_DEG + 3°), above 0.05 mm (for example dirtlab/dirtlab.py:561). The layer-reach test slices the mesh every 0.2 mm with vertical rays and flags material that reaches more than 0.6 mm past the layer below, or past the 55° cone of any layer up to 3 mm below (clifflab/rockpile.py:2155, overhang_faces). It is closer to what a slicer does: a 0.4–0.5 mm crack with a short ceiling passes, a long shelf does not. clifflab has used the layer-reach test since 1 Nov 2026 (commit 049038d), and the Rust audit ports it as the one rule.

Not every lab has moved to 55° yet. rocklab (rocklab/rocklab.py:89), grasslab (grasslab/grasslab.py:160) and dirtlab (dirtlab/dirtlab.py:128) still audit at 45° with the face-angle test, and clifflab's own requirements page still says 45° in REQ-FDM-1. The ruling is 55°; those numbers are stricter than it, never looser.

How much overhang is allowed

  • Cliffs: at most 2 mm² per tile, and no see-through holes (confirmed default 14, 1 Nov 2026).
  • Rust gate: the default budget is 0 mm²; a look that needs more must say so.
  • Trees measure branch angle the other way round, as elevation above the horizon. The skeleton tries to keep every branch above 35° (target_fdm_angle_deg), which is the same 55°-from-vertical limit. A branch below 26° (strict_fdm_angle_deg) is a print failure and raises a RuntimeWarning (src/dharmatiles/trees/layer.py:51, src/dharmatiles/trees/mesh.py:1264). Between 26° and 35° is tolerated: round tubes bridge better than flat shelves.

3. Feature sizes

  • No sub-line features. Free ridges, chips, plates and blades are at least one line (0.45 mm) wide. A slicer drops anything thinner, or its thin-wall detection mangles it.
  • No hidden relief. Relief smaller than about 0.15 mm is not modelled: it would not print, so it would only mislead the viewer (clifflab REQ-FDM-4).
  • No islands. Every 0.2 mm layer is supported by the one below. Nothing floats; a crumb that separates from the main body is dropped at export and reported (§6).
  • Cracks open to the paint brush. A crack must be wide enough to keep a dark wash and not fuse in the slicer. Since 1 Nov 2026 clifflab's cracks are 0.4–0.5 mm wide and 0.6–0.9 mm deep, straight in; the layer-reach audit allows their short ceilings.
  • Ceilings by construction. A stone resting on another is seated with its underside shaped to its bed, 3° inside the overhang limit (clifflab/rockpile.py:177), so there are no flat ceilings to audit away.

4. The printable-texture rule

Fine surface texture must be at least one layer deep, or the printer turns it into a smooth plain.

Within any 1.5 mm window, local relief (highest minus lowest point) is at least 0.2 mm, over at least 97% of the tile's interior.

The rule was added to dirtlab on 29 Oct 2026 after Shawn sliced a test tile: version v1g failed it on 33% of the tile, and its flat, “tamped” areas printed as smooth plains (dirtlab REQ-D-TEX). The failing area fell from 32.8% to 1.9%; the Python dirt now passes on 98.1% of the tile. It applies to every ground texture and is the first exit criterion of Rust phase P2 (Rust plan).

The rule is measured by a check script that lived in a session scratchpad (texcheck.py) and is not in the repository. The Rust parity harness is where it becomes a committed check.

5. Red overhang in every render

Every render paints the faces that fail the overhang audit red: diagnostic close-ups and the final painted contact sheets.

“painting the flagged faces as red — that's a good idea in general. let's use that as our policy in the future, including on the final painted renders.”

Shawn, 31 Oct 2026

An overhang number in mm² says nothing about where. Red faces show it at a glance: the first time, they showed that the remaining overhang was thin strips along the bottom edge of every stone, which pointed straight at the cause.

The red uses the same test as the audit, so the picture and the number agree. In clifflab that is the layer-reach test: paint() in clifflab/iterate.py:44 colours the faces overhang_faces flags. The Rust renderer does the same per face (crates/dt-kit/src/render.rs:97 on rust/p1-skel) and counts red pixels so a test can fail on them. Any new render script must do this too.

6. The export audit: float32 reload and watertight

An STL file stores each vertex as three 32-bit floats, and programs that read STL merge vertices that land at the same position. A mesh that is a clean closed solid in 64-bit memory can therefore open up when it is reloaded: a sliver triangle collapses, or two surfaces that nearly touch fuse into a non-manifold pinch. The slicer then sees holes. Watertight means the mesh is closed with consistent normals, so it has a well-defined inside.

The shared answers live in src/dharmatiles/stone/solidify.py:

  • separate_pinches (:42) nudges apart vertex pairs that are within 0.2 µm of each other without being connected, by 0.15 and 0.30 µm along their own normals: well past the float32 grid, invisible in print.
  • survives_stl32 (:30) rounds the vertices to float32, merges by position, and reports whether the result is still watertight.
  • clip_to_box (:18) clips with a real boolean, never a plane cap, and leaves the mesh unclipped with a warning if the clip would open it.

Every lab export goes through stl_safe (grasslab/grasslab.py:1060, dirtlab/dirtlab.py:529): separate pinches, round to float32 and merge, keep the largest body and drop the crumbs, and refuse to write anything that is not then a single watertight body. The Rust audit gate does the same steps in the same order, then measures overhang and see-through holes on exactly what is written (crates/dt-kit/src/audit.rs:122 on rust/p1-skel).

The Python library is weaker. Stones and crack engraving reject any result that fails survives_stl32 (src/dharmatiles/scatter/stones.py:490, src/dharmatiles/stone/cracks.py:270), walls separate pinches (src/dharmatiles/walls/masonry.py:197), and the exporter separates pinches on the whole tile before writing (src/dharmatiles/systems.py:47). But a library STL is the base and every layer's parts concatenated, not one unioned body, so the export does not check watertightness (src/dharmatiles/terrains/tile.py:583). When library STLs are regenerated, the vertex and face counts and the watertight status of each file are reported.

For stones there is one more check, src/extras/stone_audit.py, run by hand on an STL. It finds two faults that pass a watertight test: knife edges, convex edges folding back by more than 100°; and layered skin, unconnected vertices closer than 0.08 mm, which means the surface has folded over itself.

7. By construction, or by audit

The aim is printability by construction: each look builds only shapes that print, so the audit should find nothing. The audit is the gate that catches what construction missed.

RuleGuaranteed by constructionChecked by
Overhang ≤ 55°cliff seats 3° inside the limit; convex cuts; grass blades raked layer by layer; tree skeleton steers above 35°layer-reach audit (clifflab, Rust); face-angle audit (other labs); tree strict-angle warning

The final check is always a print. Each look's requirements include a pre-paint test: the bare grey print must read as the thing it is before any paint goes on.

History

The labs started with a 45° overhang limit and voxel FDM rules. On 29 Oct 2026 grass moved to blades built in printer units, and dirt gained the printable-texture rule after a slicing test. On 31 Oct 2026 Shawn raised the limit to 55° and made red overhang faces the policy for every render. On 1 Nov 2026 clifflab replaced the face-angle audit with the layer-reach audit so that thin cracks pass, and the Rust plan confirmed the 2 mm² cliff budget. The float32 export guarantees come from the July 2026 stone and wall work (fieldstone experiments E23 and E25). Earlier notes are in the archive.
