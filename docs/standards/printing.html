Printing

Every dharmatiles tile prints on a home FDM printer, base down, with no supports. This page gives the settings the tiles are designed for, what you can expect from a print, and how to paint one. Below that, the design record explains how printability is guaranteed and checked.

No supports: a 55° target, small patches past it

An overhang is material printed over air. A printer can lay each layer slightly wider than the one below, so a surface can lean out gradually, but past a certain lean the plastic droops. Every dharmatiles tile aims to keep every downward-facing surface within 55° of vertical, and no layer reaches more than 0.6 mm past the layer below. That is why no supports are needed. Thin cracks have short ceilings that bridge cleanly.

Every look may lean up to 65° (Shawn, 4 Nov 2026, generalising the tree exception of 3 Nov: “the 65 degree exception that we had for trees, let's generalize it. Anything can be up to 65 degrees. It's useful to know. The two colors is useful, separating them is useful, because one is conservative, one is more extreme. But cliffs should be allowed to have that 65 degree overhang as well.”) Everything still aims for 55° by construction, and the gate reports two numbers. Band is the area of downward faces between 55° and 65°, the conservative target and the extreme: it is painted orange and allowed up to 0.1% of the tile's downward-facing area (the share trees measured, 0–0.015%). Overhang is anything over 65°: painted red, and allowed only in small patches too, up to another 0.1% of the downward-facing area; more than that fails (Shawn, 4 Nov 2026: “allow small patches over 65°, like the band”). 65° is the physical edge on the default printer: each 0.2 mm layer steps out 0.2 × tan 65° ≈ 0.43 mm, just under one 0.45 mm line.

What prints, and what to expect

  • Nothing too thin. Ridges, chips, plates and grass blades are at least one line (0.45 mm) wide, so the slicer keeps them.
  • Nothing floating. Every layer rests on the one below; every stone sits on what is under it.
  • Cracks stay open. Cracks are 0.4–0.5 mm wide, wide enough to hold a dark wash and not fuse in the slicer.
  • No flat ceilings. Stones resting on other stones have sloped undersides, not flat shelves.
  • Reads before paint. A bare grey print already reads as grass, rock or water before any paint goes on.

Texture you can paint

Fine surface texture is cut at least one layer deep, or the printer would smooth it away. The rule: within any 1.5 mm window, the surface rises and falls by at least 0.2 mm over at least 97% of the tile. That is what gives a drybrush something to catch.

Painting

  • Prime dark, then drybrush light. Raised texture catches the brush; cracks, joints and the gaps between blades stay dark.
  • On rock, joints between stones are cut deeper than cracks, so they read darker.
  • A dark wash settles in the 0.4–0.5 mm cracks without filling them.

Red in renders

In every render on this site, faces past the overhang limit are painted red. A render with no red needs no supports. A small red spot is a small overhang that a printer usually bridges.

Every file is checked

An STL file stores positions as 32-bit numbers, and a mesh that is closed in memory can open up when it is saved and reloaded. Before a tile is written, it is rounded exactly as an STL reader would round it, checked to be one closed, watertight solid (it has a well-defined inside), and refused if it is not. Loose crumbs are dropped and reported. The final check is always a test print.

The printer profile in code

Line width grasslab/grasslab.py:130; layer grasslab/grasslab.py:131, clifflab/rockpile.py:172; 55° clifflab/rockpile.py:171; reach clifflab/rockpile.py:173. Nozzle and process are in the clifflab, dirtlab, rocklab and waterlab requirements. Resin smoothing keeps surfaces within about 20 µm of the stepped shape (grasslab REQ-OUT-6). In the Rust port the profile is the Printer struct: 55°, 0.2 mm layers, 0.6 mm reach over a 3 mm span, a 0.1 mm ray grid and an overhang budget of 0 mm² (crates/dt-kit/src/audit.rs:20 on branch rust/p1-skel), moving into library/printers/default. Looks read it; tiles do not write printer numbers.

The rule behind the profile is “model only what prints”. Shawn, 29 Oct 2026, on grass blades: “make it more honest about the shape, so that we can refine THAT rather than a theoretical ideal.” A grass blade is built directly as a stack of 0.2 mm layers, each a whole number of 0.45 mm lines wide, and only then smoothed (grasslab requirements §1.2).

How overhang is measured

Face angle and layer reach

Two tests exist. The face-angle test flags a face whose normal points within the limit of straight down: n_z < −sin(OVERHANG_DEG + 3°), above 0.05 mm (for example dirtlab/dirtlab.py:561). The layer-reach test slices the mesh every 0.2 mm with vertical rays and flags material that reaches more than 0.6 mm past the layer below, or past the 55° cone of any layer up to 3 mm below (clifflab/rockpile.py:2155, overhang_faces). It is closer to what a slicer does: a 0.4–0.5 mm crack with a short ceiling passes, a long shelf does not. clifflab has used it since 1 Nov 2026 (commit 049038d), and the Rust audit ports it as the one rule.

How much overhang is allowed

  • Cliffs: at most 2 mm² per tile, and no see-through holes (confirmed default 14, 1 Nov 2026).
  • Rust gate: the default budget is 0 mm²; a look that needs more must say so.
  • Trees measure branch angle as elevation above the horizon. The skeleton tries to keep every branch above 35° (target_fdm_angle_deg), the same 55°-from-vertical limit. A branch below 26° (strict_fdm_angle_deg) is a print failure and raises a RuntimeWarning (src/dharmatiles/trees/layer.py:51, src/dharmatiles/trees/mesh.py:1264). Between 26° and 35° is tolerated: round tubes bridge better than flat shelves.

Other feature rules: relief under about 0.15 mm is not modelled (clifflab REQ-FDM-4); cliff cracks are 0.4–0.5 mm wide and 0.6–0.9 mm deep since 1 Nov 2026; cliff seats sit 3° inside the overhang limit (clifflab/rockpile.py:177).

The printable-texture rule

Added to dirtlab on 29 Oct 2026 after Shawn sliced a test tile: version v1g failed it on 33% of the tile, and its flat, “tamped” areas printed as smooth plains (dirtlab REQ-D-TEX). The failing area fell from 32.8% to 1.9%; the Python dirt now passes on 98.1% of the tile. It applies to every ground texture and is the first exit criterion of Rust phase P2 (Rust plan).

Red overhang: the policy and the code

“painting the flagged faces as red — that's a good idea in general. let's use that as our policy in the future, including on the final painted renders.”

Shawn, 31 Oct 2026

An overhang number in mm² says nothing about where. The first red render showed the remaining overhang was thin strips along the bottom of every stone, which pointed straight at the cause. The red uses the same test as the audit, so picture and number agree: paint() in clifflab/iterate.py:44 colours the faces overhang_faces flags. The Rust renderer does the same per face (crates/dt-kit/src/render.rs:97 on rust/p1-skel) and counts red pixels so a test can fail on them. Any new render script must do this too.

The export audit in code

The shared answers live in src/dharmatiles/stone/solidify.py:

  • separate_pinches (:42) nudges apart vertex pairs within 0.2 µm of each other that are not connected, by 0.15 and 0.30 µm along their normals: past the float32 grid, invisible in print.
  • survives_stl32 (:30) rounds to float32, merges by position, and reports whether the result is still watertight.
  • clip_to_box (:18) clips with a real boolean, never a plane cap, and leaves the mesh unclipped with a warning if the clip would open it.

Every lab export goes through stl_safe (grasslab/grasslab.py:1060, dirtlab/dirtlab.py:529): separate pinches, round to float32 and merge, keep the largest body, and refuse anything that is not then a single watertight body. The Rust audit gate does the same steps in the same order, then measures overhang and see-through holes on exactly what is written (crates/dt-kit/src/audit.rs:122 on rust/p1-skel).

For stones there is one more check, src/extras/stone_audit.py, run by hand. It finds two faults that pass a watertight test: knife edges, convex edges folding back by more than 100°; and layered skin, unconnected vertices closer than 0.08 mm.

Open conflicts

  • The Python library is weaker at export. Stones and crack engraving reject results that fail survives_stl32 (src/dharmatiles/scatter/stones.py:490, src/dharmatiles/stone/cracks.py:270), walls separate pinches (src/dharmatiles/walls/masonry.py:197), and the exporter separates pinches on the whole tile (src/dharmatiles/systems.py:47). But a library STL is the base and every layer's parts concatenated, not one unioned body, so its export does not check watertightness (src/dharmatiles/terrains/tile.py:583). Watertight status is reported when library STLs are regenerated.
  • The texture check (texcheck.py) lived in a session scratchpad and is not in the repository. The Rust parity harness is where it becomes a committed check.

History

The labs started with a 45° overhang limit and voxel FDM rules. On 29 Oct 2026 grass moved to blades built in printer units, and dirt gained the printable-texture rule after a slicing test. On 31 Oct 2026 Shawn raised the limit to 55° (“45 is too conservative”) and made red overhang faces the policy for every render. On 1 Nov 2026 clifflab replaced the face-angle audit with the layer-reach audit so that thin cracks pass, and the Rust plan confirmed the 2 mm² cliff budget. On 2 Nov 2026 the rocklab, grasslab and dirtlab face-angle audits moved from 45° to 55° (OVERHANG_DEG). The float32 export guarantees come from the July 2026 stone and wall work (fieldstone experiments E23 and E25). Earlier notes are in the archive.
