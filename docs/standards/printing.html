Printing

Every dharmatiles tile prints on a home FDM printer, base down, with no supports. This page gives the settings the tiles are designed for, what you can expect from a print, and how to paint one. Every file is checked by the printability gate before it is written.

Printer settings

The tiles are designed for one printer profile, default. Any FDM printer that can hold these settings will do.

SettingValue
ProcessFDM, base down, no supports
Nozzle0.4 mm
Line width0.45 mm
Layer height0.2 mm
Overhang the tiles stay within55° from vertical as the target; up to 65° in small patches; past 65° also in small patches; more than that fails

A profile is a short document, library/printers/default.dtile: line, layer and gap in printed millimetres ("0.45mm") and overhang and steepest in degrees ("55deg"). It is the printer's floors and the gate's angles, and nothing else: the base system (DungeonBlocks or OpenLOCK) is chosen beside it, and edge and corner rounding belong to materials, not to printers. For another printer, name another profile: library/printers/ also holds fine (a 0.25 mm nozzle, 0.12 mm layers), and your own go in a printers/ folder beside your tiles ($DT_HOME, else ~/dharmatiles), one .dtile file each, named like the file. dharmatiles build my-tile.dtile --printer fine builds for it, and the preview's title bar has a chip for the choice. One build is one target, the base system and the printer: its files go to out/<system>/, or out/<system>-<printer>/ for a printer that is not the default, and its build record names both.

A profile can also describe the machine (its bed, nozzle and filaments), by brand and model: see Printers.

The library is sliced in Bambu Studio. Grass can also be printed in resin: the same blade shapes are smoothed so the layer steps melt away.

No supports: a 55° target, small patches past it

An overhang is material printed over air. A printer can lay each layer slightly wider than the one below, so a surface can lean out gradually, but past a certain lean the plastic droops. Every dharmatiles tile aims to keep every downward-facing surface within 55° of vertical, and no layer reaches more than 0.6 mm past the layer below. That is why no supports are needed. Thin cracks have short ceilings that bridge cleanly.

Every element may lean up to 65° in small patches. Everything aims for 55° by construction, and the gate reports two numbers. Band is the area of downward faces between 55° and 65°: it is painted orange and allowed up to 0.1% of the tile's downward-facing area. Overhang is anything over 65°: painted red, and allowed only up to another 0.1% of the downward-facing area. More than either fails. 65° is the physical edge on the default printer: each 0.2 mm layer steps out 0.2 × tan 65° ≈ 0.43 mm, just under one 0.45 mm line.

What prints, and what to expect

  • Nothing too thin. Ridges, chips, plates and grass blades are at least one line (0.45 mm) wide, so the slicer keeps them.
  • Nothing floating. Every layer rests on the one below; every stone sits on what is under it.
  • Cracks stay open. Cracks are 0.4–0.5 mm wide, wide enough to hold a dark wash and not fuse in the slicer.
  • No flat ceilings. Stones resting on other stones have sloped undersides, not flat shelves.
  • Reads before paint. A bare grey print already reads as grass, rock or water before any paint goes on.

Texture you can paint

Fine surface texture is cut at least one layer deep, or the printer would smooth it away. The rule: within any 1.5 mm window, the surface rises and falls by at least 0.2 mm over at least 97% of the tile. That is what gives a drybrush something to catch.

Painting

  • Prime dark, then drybrush light. Raised texture catches the brush; cracks, joints and the gaps between blades stay dark.
  • On rock, joints between stones are cut deeper than cracks, so they read darker.
  • A dark wash settles in the 0.4–0.5 mm cracks without filling them.

Printing in colour

dharmatiles build my-tile.dtile --3mf also writes a 3MF in which every triangle is painted with its material's filament, so a multi-colour printer prints the earth, rock, water and grass in their own colours with no painting in the slicer.

  • Filaments are numbered by the materials the tile uses, earth first, then rock, water, grass, flowers, masonry, wood and leaves. The connector prints in earth's filament. The 3MF's description lists the order.
  • Bambu Studio: save any project from Bambu Studio, with your printer and process, as ~/dharmatiles/bambu.3mf (or in $DT_HOME), or name one with --bambu-template. The 3MF is then a Bambu Studio project with exactly one slot per material, each coloured from the palette, standing on the middle of the bed. Every slot uses the template's first filament profile; change them in Bambu Studio.
  • Without a template the 3MF is a plain one with the same painting. Bambu Studio 2.9.1 or later and OrcaSlicer keep the painting; give the slots their colours yourself.
  • Colours come from library/palettes/default.dtile, or your own with --palette.
  • Bambu Studio saves a project over the file it opened. Open a new build only after closing the last one without saving, or build into another folder.

Red in renders

In every render on this site, faces past the overhang limit are painted red. A render with no red needs no supports. A small red spot is a small overhang that a printer usually bridges.

Every file is checked

An STL file stores positions as 32-bit numbers, and a mesh that is closed in memory can open up when it is saved and reloaded. Before a tile is written, it is rounded exactly as an STL reader would round it, checked to be one closed, watertight solid (it has a well-defined inside), and refused if it is not. Loose crumbs are dropped and reported. The final check is always a test print.
