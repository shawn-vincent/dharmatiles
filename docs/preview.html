dharmatiles preview

Preview

A live, painted 3D view of your tiles in the browser, and their editor. Click anything on the tile to see the line and editable properties that made it, or type in the document beside it. It rebuilds a tile every time you save it, paints overhang the way the printability gate sees it, and shows a mistake at its line. It is part of the program and works offline.

Run it

dharmatiles                                   # your tiles and the library, in the browser
dharmatiles preview my-tile.dtile --open      # one document
dharmatiles preview a.dtile b.dtile walls/    # several files and folders
dharmatiles preview lib:tiles/cliffs          # one library group
dharmatiles preview my-tile.dtile --system ol # start on OpenLOCK (the title bar's chip changes it)
dharmatiles preview my-tile.dtile --printer fine # start on another printer profile
dharmatiles preview slicer-export.stl         # any binary STL, through the same gate

The sidebar is two pages. The first is the tile browser: where the tiles come from at the top, a filter and New tile, then the list. Choose a tile and the editor slides in beside it; ‹ in the editor's header (‹ My tiles › 2x2-meadow) slides back to the list, and the tile's name at the top right of the list, or Esc, returns to the tile. With nothing named, the browser has three sources: My tiles ($DT_HOME, else ~/dharmatiles), the Library, and Tests, the tiles the program's own tests build (odd shapes and edge cases among them; open one to see what a test sees, or make it yours like a library tile). Folders are watched: a new tile appears in the list within a second, and a deleted one leaves it.

It serves http://127.0.0.1:7878/ (the first free port from 7878, or --port) on the loopback address only, and refuses a request whose Host is not 127.0.0.1, localhost or [::1]. Ctrl-C stops it.

What it shows

  • The title bar: the tile's size, undo and redo, the build target, the gate's verdict and Export. Click the verdict for the record, which drops down over the view: why a tile fails, the audit's numbers, and an action that flies to the largest red face (or the largest orange face when there is no red).
  • The editor page of the sidebar: a header naming the tile and where it comes from (‹ goes back to the tile browser; click the name for the tile's own properties), then the document, as written, coloured by kind, and editable (see Editing).
  • The property sheet under it: the selected entry's editable controls, a Help link to its element page, and Hide and Delete at the foot. With nothing selected it shows the tile itself: its size (click it to type a size or pick one from a grid of squares, as a word processor offers for a table: the field follows the pointer, a click takes it) and seed, its description (about), Rename is at the foot. A selected line shows its name, wobble, width and the points it runs through; a region its name and what its source says: the lines it is boundedBy (each one a toggle) and its fill point, and each within clause (its distance or range, and the place it is round, chosen from the tile's edges, lines, regions and points) and except clause (the regions taken out), with a line saying how they combine (the clauses add together, then the excepted regions are taken away). Each clause has a × that takes it out of the source (the boundedBy takes its fill points with it), and add clause at the foot of the region's sheet writes a new within (a small disc round the centre), except (another region) or boundedBy (a line, with a fill point if the region has none); one that cannot be written, such as an except with no other region, says why. A region made only of places shows no boundedBy or fill. Renaming either renames every boundedBy or at= that names it.
  • The tile on the plate, painted like the PNG renders, with its squares ruled against an open blue sky. The ⋯ button holds paint, overhang, wireframe, grid, projection and all nine views. Before a tile has been built, the view frames its footprint, widens to take in each part as the build sends it, and settles on the finished tile; move the view yourself and it stays where you put it.
  • Overhang from the gate itself: faces between 55° and 65° are orange, faces past 65° red. The colours come from the gate's own flags, so the view and build cannot disagree.
  • Progress while a tile builds: the stage, an element's own count (grass blades, wall stones, tree leaves) and the time left, estimated from the last build of the same tile.

The debug layer

When a tile does not come out as you expect, the debug layer shows how the build made it. It is off whenever the editor opens; ⌃⌥D (Ctrl-Alt-D) or Debug layer in the ⋯ menu turns it on and off, and a legend at the top left names what is drawn, with counts. It reads in every view, Map included, and changes nothing that is built or printed.

  • Grass: every blade start as a dot coloured by what became of it: grew; grew but cut short; outside the grass's area; too short at a line; too short at the tile edge; too short on bare soil, at a rock or where the flow folds; crowded by a blade already there; under a stone; on bare soil; left out by cover. Each grown blade's spine is drawn on the ground: blue when it grew to its full length, yellow when it was cut short (yellow, not orange: orange and red always mean overhang), pink for a blade started along the area's edge. Thin dark arrows show the flow the blades follow.
  • Height bands: a surface whose tile writes its band (top= or a pin) shows the band's low and high as translucent sheets over its region, a hue for each, with a bright edge.

A tile with a great many marks has them thinned evenly, and the legend says so. More elements will add what they do.

Panes

The document and the property sheet share one sidebar by default, the source above the properties: the Edit layout. Every edge between panes is a sash: drag it to resize, double-click it to reset. Each pane's × hides it, and a button on the view brings it back.

  • Edit: the source over the properties, in one sidebar.
  • Inspect: the source, the view and the properties side by side.
  • Look: only the view.

The three are in the ⋯ menu, with Panes on the right to put the sidebar on the other side. [ hides or shows the source, ] the properties, \ both. The layout and its sizes are remembered in the browser. On a narrow screen the panes stack under the view instead.

Selecting

Every triangle of a built tile knows the entry that made it, so the view, the property sheet and the document share one selection: the rest of the tile fades behind it. What is under the pointer shows the same way, lighter: with nothing selected the rest dims a little round it; with a selection, it lifts part way out of the fade. With the fade off, the selection and the pointer's thing are tinted with the theme's accent instead.

  • Hover a thing on the tile and it lights up and is named beside the pointer: rocks in pool · 2 of 3.
  • Click to select it. A thing a scatter placed is selected on its own; click it again to climb to the whole scatter, then to the region it is at=, then the tile. Clicking the plate or Esc clears.
  • The property sheet and document follow: the entry's controls open on the right and its lines light up. An entry written in a file the tile uses (grass from lib:parts/grass.dtile) opens that file; Back to the tile returns.
  • Click a line in the document to select its entry in the view; hover it to light its geometry. A line or region node selects that line or region on the map.
  • Double-click, or f, flies to the selection.
  • The rest fades. With something selected, everything else eases to translucent in about a tenth of a second; select something else and the two trade places the same way, the new selection rising to solid as the old one sinks back; deselect and the rest comes back (at once if your system asks for reduced motion). The selection stands out by the fade alone and can be seen through what is in front of it. Fade the rest in the ⋯ menu, or x, turns it off, and the selection is tinted instead.

Water owns the surface it fills, so clicking the pool selects the water; put the cursor in the ground's line to reach the ground beneath it.

Editing

Every change is a change to the document's source, and the source is the tile: what you change in the view you can read, and keep, in the file.

  • Start fresh. New tile makes printable flat ground in My tiles, in a file called what you name it, at 1×1, 2×1, 1×2 or 2×2 squares, ready to change in the same editor.
  • The property sheet. Selecting an entry opens its controls in the whole right sidebar: the ones written, then the rest at their defaults, faint. Help is always at the top right; Hide and Delete stay at the bottom. Drag a number sideways to change it (Shift for fine steps), or click it to type. A short word control shows its choices directly; larger sets use a dropdown so the sheet stays aligned. A switch flips; × takes a control out, back to its default. Every seed has a die beside it: press it for another arrangement. A scatter's sheet holds its things' controls too. Only the value changes in the text: the layout and comments stay.
  • Weighting a range. Clicking a value that draws (a rock's size, a scatter's count) opens a pop-up with what the control takes and three forms: one number, a range and weighted. Over a range it also shows the weighting functions as small curve thumbnails: flat, low, high, bell, peak, ends, steps, list and mix, each a curve of how likely the values along that range are, with a dotted line for a plain range; a list is a bar for each value. Pick one and the source is rewritten (size="1..6" becomes size="low(1..6, 4)"); the gallery is withheld for a format 1 value (every library tile) until it writes units on both ends, so there you type the weighting, size="low(1dmm .. 6dmm, 4)", and the pop-up checks it; weighted on a plain range picks low. Under the thumbnails, the picked function's curve is drawn larger with a slider for each number it takes (the ratio of low and high, the spread and centre of bell and peak, the power of ends), kept inside its limits; dragging one rewrites the source and the 3D draft as it goes, and Enter keeps the change as one step of history while Esc puts the source back. steps, list and mix have no slider: their numbers are written in the field. The curves and the written text come from the program (GET /api/weightings); the page draws what it is sent, and a number past its limits shows the program's own message (dt::range) in the pop-up's check line.
  • Typing. The document is checked as you type, without building: a mistake is underlined at its line, marked in the gutter (hover the mark for the message) and said in words under the source. Long lines wrap, each continuation indented under its line; ⌘F finds and replaces; a { block folds from the arrow beside its line; brackets are matched and closed; ⌥-drag or ⌘-click makes several cursors. Completion offers element names at the start of a node, an element's controls on its line, and after name= the control's words, or the document's regions and lines. Ctrl-Space asks for it.
  • Drafts while you drag. As a value is dragged, the view shows quick drafts of the tile: fewer cells, the parts not joined, and overhang judged face by face. A cliff's draft carves its faces on a coarser grid and leaves the stone's grain off, so it answers in about two seconds. A draft of more than 400,000 triangles is thinned for the view. The verdict says draft beside it, since a draft is never judged by the gate. Let go and the full build follows.
  • Saved as you go. A change is written to the file half a second after it reads cleanly, and the tile rebuilds. A source with a mistake is kept in the page, unsaved, until it is fixed. If you leave while it cannot be saved, the editor makes discarding explicit. If the file changes on disk under unsaved changes, you choose which to keep.
  • One history. ⌘Z (Ctrl-Z) undoes typing and changes made in the property sheet alike; ⌘⇧Z redoes. Undo and Redo also live in the title bar: press either for one step, or open its arrow to inspect the action history and jump several versions at once. Each action shows a short monospace excerpt of what it added and, when applicable, what it removed.
  • Rename. A tile's file name is its name. Double-click the name in the editor's header, or press F2, type the new name and press Enter (Esc cancels). The file is renamed in its folder, never over another tile, and the undo history comes with it. Library tiles are made yours first.
  • Make it mine. Library tiles are never written. Typing in one, or changing it in the property sheet, offers a copy in My tiles, its uses pointing back at the library; the copy opens, ready to change.

Only a page served by this run of the program can change a file: each change carries a secret the page reads from the program, and a page from any other site is refused.

Placing and drawing

The bar under the view puts new things on the tile. Type an element, a definition of the document, line or region (A goes there), choose with the arrows and press Enter; anything after the name, such as rock size=12, is written with it. Each gesture writes one node into the source, saves it, and selects what it made. Esc puts the tool down.

  • Things (rock, boulder, tree, a definition): its footprint follows the pointer with the point in squares from the north-west corner; a click writes rock at="(0.64sq, 0.58sq)" at the end of the stack. Click again to place another.
  • Surfaces (dirt, flagstones, planks, water, grass): the region under the pointer lights up; a click writes the entry at= it, or for the whole tile outside every region.
  • Scatter: click or drag in a region to write scatter at=pool count=3 { rock }, with its property sheet open on the count. scatter boulder scatters boulders.
  • Walls: click a line, or near an edge, to write at= it.
  • Lines: click points on the plate; Enter or a double-click ends the line, Backspace takes back the last point. A point by the middle of an edge or a corner snaps and is written as its word (north, south-east); one close to an edge goes onto it.
  • Regions: click inside the area the lines enclose; it writes region area-1 { boundedBy …; fill "(0.5sq, 0.75sq)" } (a point counted from the north-west corner) naming every line, for you to rename and trim.
  • Handles. A selected line shows its points, a region its fill points, a placed thing its at=: drag one to rewrite that point.
  • The map. Only what you are working on is drawn over the tile: a selected line, drawn as a labelled ribbon at zero with its wobble's band, or a selected region. A selected region is the selected item: it is drawn opaque in the accent, flat at level zero (where the base meets the tile body), with its name, and the rest of the tile fades so you see down through it to the region, from any angle. The places it is made from are drawn and named beside it at zero, in periwinkle: a line or tile edge as a line and another region as an outline with a faint fill (the lines a bucket region is boundedBy too). While a tool is held every line and region shows faintly (regions at zero), so you can aim at them. With nothing selected the tile is shown bare.
  • Control points. A selected line or region marks every point it refers to: each through point of a line; each fill point of a region and each within … of= centre that is a point. Every one is a plum dot at zero with its label beside it: a literal shows its coordinates as written (0,0.38, (1.5, 0.7)), a named point or corner or edge word shows its name. A point drawn from a range or written as an expression is labelled with its source text, as a ring at the middle of where it may land. Positions and spans come from the program's own reading of the source.
  • Hide and delete. The property sheet's Hide writes /- before the selected node, so the tile builds without it and the document says so. Delete takes the node out of the text. Both offer an immediate Undo.

Moving round a tile

Orbit with the left button, pan with the right button or Shift, zoom with the wheel or a pinch, toward the point under the pointer. A fling keeps turning and settles. Picking a view turns the tile there rather than cutting; moving the camera clears that preset. The shortcut row always holds ISO, Map and the two most recently used other views; all nine remain in ⋯.

The build target

The chip at the right of the title bar names what every build is for: the base system (DungeonBlocks or OpenLOCK) and the printer profile, such as DungeonBlocks · default. Click it to change either. The tile you are looking at, and every other tile you have built, builds again for the new target; the gate's angles and the numbers in the record are the chosen printer's. The choice is remembered in $DT_HOME (else ~/dharmatiles) for the next run; --system and --printer on the command line start the run on another one. The printers are the library's (library/printers/) and your own: a printers/ folder beside your tiles, one .dtile file each, named like the file (see Printing). There is no both: one build is one target.

Export

Export, beside the verdict, runs the same build as dharmatiles build on the saved file and writes it to out/: the project's (beside the nearest dharmatiles.dtile above the tile), else one beside the tile, as out/db/my-tile-db.stl. A library tile is never written into the library: it goes to out/ in My tiles (~/dharmatiles/out/db/ground/1x1-grass-db.stl). The card says where before you export. It builds for the build target in the title bar, written to out/<target>/ (out/ol-fine/my-tile-ol-fine.stl for OpenLOCK and the printer fine); choose Also a painted 3MF for a 3MF painted by material (a Bambu Studio project when ~/dharmatiles/bambu.3mf exists; see Printing). Reveal in Finder shows what was written. Export waits while the tile builds or has unsaved changes, and is refused, with the reason, while the tile fails the printability gate.

Live updates

Save the document, or any file it uses, and the tile rebuilds. Your camera stays; the old tile fades at once, so it never passes for the new one. As the build goes, each stage's parts take its place, faded too: the ground within a moment, then each element as it finishes. A cliff shows its rough blocks within a second or so, long before its stones are cut. What stands for work still going breathes, its transparency rising and falling: the rough blocks, and the old tile until the first new part arrives (not with reduced motion). Opening a tile mid-build shows what it has made so far. The finished tile replaces them all, solid, with its verdict. What shows while a tile builds is never judged by the gate, and a large stage is sent thinned. A rebuild starts from the first step your edit changes: the program keeps the tile as it stood after each step of the last build (in the order the plan runs them, surfaces and ground first), so changing the boulders by a cliff rebuilds the boulders and joins and checks the tile again, in about two seconds, without carving the cliff again. Changing a surface, such as the dirt's level, rebuilds everything after it. The last few tiles you worked on keep theirs; drafts keep their own. A mistake shows over the view as file:line:column with a hint, in front of the last good build; the next good save clears it. One tile builds at a time: tiles named on the command line are built at the start, and a tile picked from a folder is built when you pick it.

If the program stops, the page says so the way it says a build is running: the gate chip pulses reconnecting…, the strip over the view reads Waiting for dharmatiles preview…, and the tile fades and breathes. Start the program again and the page carries on by itself, whenever that is: it finds your tile again by its file, rebuilds it if it needs to, and keeps your camera. An edit typed while it was away is kept and saved once it is back (the source bar says not saved yet until then).

Paint

Every material is painted like a quick acrylic job: a dark basecoat, mid tones, and pale highlights drybrushed on whatever stands proud, so joints and cracks stay dark and edges catch the light. Each material's ramp is a line in library/palettes/default.dtile. Give your own copy to dharmatiles preview --palette my.dtile, which repaints when you save it, or to build --png --palette my.dtile.

Limits

  • Seeds, sweeps and the deferred assistant are designed but not built: see the editor. The map shows a region's area as the last good check made it; a line moved by its handle redraws when the text next checks.
  • Made for your own machine: a finished tile's mesh is sent whole (about 94 bytes a triangle), fine on the loopback, not meant for a network.
  • A build reuses only the steps before the first one an edit changes: a change to a surface or an early element rebuilds a tile with a tree or a cliff in full, in seconds, not at once. Joining the parts and the gate always run on the whole tile.
  • No section cuts or measuring.
