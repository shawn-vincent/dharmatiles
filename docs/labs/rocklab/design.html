1. Core idea: lumpy convex body, then planes

Every rock is built in manifold3d from half-space cuts of a convex body, so it stays convex, grounded and printable at every step:

  1. Body (make_body): convex hull of 75–145 points on a non-increasing profile above a flat base, with angle-only lumpiness.
  2. Facets (cut_facets, scale 1): blue-noise seeds with mixed radii on the body; each seed's tilted tangent plane is one facet; the rock is the intersection of those half-spaces with a box on z = 0.
  3. Chips (chip_corners_edges, scale 2): more, smaller half-space cuts at corners and along sharp edges.
  4. Grain (texture, scale 3): fine displacement along vertex normals, faded at the base and halved at sharp edges.

Guarantees:

  • Convex, so grounded (REQ-ROCK-3): an intersection of half-spaces is convex; the planes come from a body whose cross-sections never grow upward, and no plane with n_z < −0.5 is used, so there is no mushroom.
  • Printable (REQ-FDM-1): no face points down past 30°; grain is zero on downward faces. Measured overhang 0.00–0.02 mm².
  • Crisp edges (REQ-ROCK-2): planes meet at sharp edges; grain is halved within 0.5 mm of them.
  • Mixed facet sizes by construction: one facet per seed, size from the seed spacing.
  • Clusters are unions of convex rocks; a union's surface is a subset of its parts', so the guarantees carry over.

2. Stages

2.1 Rock mass (make_body)

  • Width D = 6 + 9·U^1.4 mm (skewed small); footprint semi-axes a = D/2, b = a / U(1, 2), random yaw.

  • Height h = min(D·U(0.45, 0.85), 9 mm); slabs × 0.6; at least 2.5 mm.

  • Profile family (profile, radius scale r(t), t = z/h, always non-increasing):

    Family r(t) Weight
    dome (1 − t^2.2)^0.5 0.35
    slab (1 − t^2.8)^0.45, h × 0.6 0.2
    block (1 − t^3.5)^0.4, superellipse (p = 3) footprint 0.2
    wedge (1 − t)^0.8, centre shifts by U(0.2, 0.45) of the footprint toward a random side as t grows 0.25

  • Points: base ring of 14–22 at z = 0 (radius × U(0.94, 1.0)); 60–120 body points at t ∈ U(0.05, 0.97) (× U(0.93, 1.0)); 1–3 top points at t ∈ U(0.95, 1.0) (× U(0, 0.4)). Jitter only shrinks, so the profile stays non-increasing.

  • Lumpiness: radius × (1 + Σ_{k=2..5} a_k cos(kφ + φ_k)), a_k ~ N(0, 1)· 0.08/2. It depends on angle only, so every vertical line is scaled by a constant and the profile stays non-increasing. Why: flat body regions (old block sides, slab tops) gave all their seeds the same tangent plane, which merged them into one oversized face (R9).

  • Hull: Manifold.hull_points.

2.2 Facets (cut_facets, scale 1)

  • Seeds: dart-throw over 4,000 area-weighted surface samples of the body (seeded from the rock RNG; trimesh's own sampler would use the unseeded global RNG). Each candidate draws r = r_min + (r_max − r_min)· U^1.6 and is accepted when ≥ (r + r_j)/2 from every accepted seed. Radii 0.9–2.5 mm × √(D/15): small rocks get fewer, proportionally coarser facets. Base samples (n_z < −0.3) are skipped: the flat base is its own facet.
  • Plane: the sample normal tilted by U(0, 14°) about a random tangent axis; planes with n_z < −0.5 are skipped; offset jitter U(−0.15, +0.1) mm along the normal.
  • Result: 30–165 facets per rock; the facetted rock keeps about 70–90% of the body's volume.

2.3 Chips (chip_corners_edges, scale 2)

  • Corners: every polytope vertex above z = 0.6 mm, with probability 0.45. Normal = sum of incident face normals; depth U(0.3, 0.9)·√(D/15).
  • Edges: every sharp edge (dihedral > 18°) with both ends above 0.6 mm, with probability 0.35, at a random point 20–80% along it. Normal = the two faces' normal sum; depth U(0.2, 0.55)·√(D/15).
  • Candidates are shuffled; each plane is tilted by U(0, 25°). A cut is skipped if n_z < −0.5 or it would remove more than 3% of the volume. At most U{8…16} chips per rock. After chips the rock keeps about 60–80% of the body volume.

2.4 Grain (texture, scale 3)

  • Remesh: Manifold.refine_to_length(0.25 mm) (trimesh's subdivide_to_size left T-junctions, which made the mesh non-manifold).
  • Grain: dharmatiles.stone.noise.fbm, 4 octaves at 0.45 mm, amplitude 0.12 mm, along vertex normals.
  • Edge keep: × (0.5 + 0.5·smoothstep(dist to the nearest sharp edge / 0.5 mm)).
  • Base fade: × smoothstep((z − 0.15)/0.8 mm), and zero where the vertex normal has n_z < −0.3. The result is trimmed at z = 0.

2.5 Clusters (make_item)

  • Probability 0.25 per placement; k = 2–4 stones.
  • Satellite widths max(D·U(0.45, 0.9), 4 mm), at evenly spaced angles (± 0.5 rad jitter) around the main stone, at (D/2 + d/2)·U(0.35, 0.6) from its centre so they overlap. Each stone gets its own family and texture.
  • Boolean union; the placement radius R covers the whole cluster.

2.6 Scatter (scatter, pose)

  • N = round(U(9, 12) × area / 4 squares) items are built first, then placed largest first by dart throwing: ≤ 400 darts each, accepted when ≥ 1 mm + R from the rim and ≥ 1.5 mm + R_i + R_j from every placed item. An item that finds no spot is dropped.
  • pose gives each placement a random yaw. (meadowlab reuses scatter(…, placements) and pose.)

2.7 Tile (build_tile)

  • Slab [0, W] × [0, H] × [0, 4.0] (z = 0 is the terrain datum, as in dharmatiles); rocks translated to z₀ = 4.0 − 0.3 mm.
  • Slab ∪ rocks ∪ DB base (make_base, peg height from select_peg_height on the tallest point), exported via trimesh.
  • Audit: watertight, body count, overhang area (n_z < −sin 48°, z > 0.05), per-rock facet and chip ranges, timing.
  • Each rock is evaluated (num_tri) as soon as it is made. manifold3d is lazy; deferring rocks into the tile union gives the same geometry with a different triangle order, so this keeps STLs byte-stable.

Export guarantee (2026-10-27)

Before export, the mesh is merged exactly as a slicer does (float32 positions, merge by position) and the run raises unless it is watertight. Seeds 1–4 pass with no repair needed.

Exposed ground = dirt (2026-10-29)

Shawn: dirt should be the texture wherever ground is exposed, across all labs, and mud and other dirt textures are eliminated. This lab now uses dirtlab.dirt_relief(seed, W, H): dirtlab's soil roughness, clods, grit and pebbles, without its hills or big stones, as a relief offset sampled onto this lab's grid.

  • The flat 4.0 mm slab is replaced by a dirt heightfield (4.0 mm + dirt) plus an exact 1 mm box. Rocks sink an extra 0.25 mm (DIRT_SINK_MM), so dirt dips never expose a rock's base.
