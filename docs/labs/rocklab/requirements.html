Revision 2, 2026-10-27 (matches code v1f). Visual target: reference/approved-reference.png, a grid of about 60 painted, chiselled miniature rocks. A standalone experiment built like grasslab/: it may import dharmatiles primitives, never modifies src/dharmatiles/, and writes to rocklab/out/.

1. What this is

One STL: a standard DungeonBlocks tile with a flat surface and a handful of faceted rocks scattered on it. No grass, soil texture or hills; the purpose is to get the rocks themselves right. (meadowlab/ puts them on grassy hills.)

2. What the reference shows

Property Observation
Silhouette A broad, flat base with a domed-to-blocky body, widest at or near the ground, never mushrooming above it.
Faceting Planar facets of mixed sizes over the whole surface: a few large faces define the mass, many small ones break up the corners. Chiselled or knapped, not a smooth boulder or a noise blob.
Edges Crisp facet ridges that catch the drybrush highlight.
Shape variety Domes, low slabs, wedges or pyramids, blocky cuboids. Footprints round to elongated (up to about 2:1). No two alike.
Clusters About a quarter of the items are 2–4 stones of different sizes leaning together, fused.
Size Largest about 3× the smallest; small rocks carry proportionally coarser facets.
Colour Blue-grey with lighter edge highlights: paint, out of scope.

3. Requirements

3.1 Output and tile

  • REQ-OUT-1 [S] A run writes rocklab/out/<cols>x<rows>-rocks-s<seed>.stl, defaulting to 2 × 2 DB squares.
  • REQ-OUT-2 [S] One watertight manifold body: rocks fused to the slab, slab fused to the DB socket base (dharmatiles.bases.dungeonblocks).
  • REQ-OUT-3 [S] The surface is flat at 4.0 mm above the terrain datum, inside the official DB dirt band (14.8–15.3 mm above piece bottom).
  • REQ-OUT-4 [S] Deterministic per seed (byte-identical STL), builds in under 30 s, STL under 25 MB.

3.2 Rock form

  • REQ-ROCK-1 [R] Faceted. Every rock is a polyhedron of planar facets (roughly 30–165 per rock), sized from about 40% of the rock's width down to about 8%.

  • REQ-ROCK-2 [R] Crisp edges. Facet edges are sharp ridges, never blurred or rounded away.

  • REQ-ROCK-3 [R] Grounded silhouette. Widest at the base; cross- sections never grow going up.

  • REQ-ROCK-4 [R] Variety. Each rock draws its own footprint (aspect 1–2, any orientation), height-to-width ratio (0.45–0.85; slabs × 0.6), profile family (dome, slab, wedge, block), lumpiness, facets and chips. No two rocks on a tile are near-copies.

  • REQ-ROCK-5 [R] Clusters. About 25% of placements are clusters of 2–4 overlapping, fused stones, each 45–90% of the main stone's width.

  • REQ-ROCK-6 [S] Sizes. Width 6–15 mm skewed small; cluster stones ≥ 4 mm; height 2.5–9 mm.

  • REQ-ROCK-7 [R] Three scales of texture. (Shawn: "different degrees of texture… the surfaces should be rough and the corners of these big things should be chipped.")

    Scale Size What it is
    1. Facets 2–7 mm the planar faces that define the mass
    2. Chips 0.2–0.9 mm deep small planar chips off corners and bevels along sharp edges
    3. Grain ~0.45 mm, ±0.12 mm fine, clearly visible roughness everywhere

    Scale 1 and 2 ridges stay crisp (grain is halved near them). Texture never blurs the facets into a potato (R1) or crumpled foil (R3).

3.3 Scatter

  • REQ-SC-1 [S] A handful. A 2 × 2 tile asks for 9–12 placements (rocks or clusters); one that finds no free spot is dropped.
  • REQ-SC-2 [R] Natural spacing. Not on a grid; no unintended overlap (≥ 1.5 mm clearance); open floor left for miniature bases.
  • REQ-SC-3 [S] Rim. Every rock lies inside the tile, ≥ 1 mm from the wall; nothing is sliced by the edge.
  • REQ-SC-4 [S] Seated. Rocks sink 0.3 mm into the slab: fused, no floating gap.

3.4 FDM (0.4 mm nozzle, base-down print)

  • REQ-FDM-1 [S] No downward face steeper than 45°. Guaranteed by REQ-ROCK-3 plus facet and chip planes that never point down past 30° (n_z ≥ −0.5).
  • REQ-FDM-2 [S] Chip depths scale with √(D/15), so small rocks get proportionally small chips; grain is small (±0.12 mm) and fades to zero at the base and on any downward face, so it cannot create overhangs.
  • REQ-FDM-3 [S] An audit reports watertightness, body count and overhang area (faces with n_z < −sin 48°); any violation is reported.

4. Failure modes (named)

# Failure Looks like
R1 Potato a smooth, rounded blob with no facets
R2 Gem / dice regular, symmetric or uniformly sized faceting
R3 Crumpled foil noisy micro-facets everywhere, no large faces
R4 Clones rocks of the same shape at different scales
R5 Mushroom wider above the ground than at it (and unprintable)
R6 Pebble dust rocks too small to read at tabletop distance
R7 Floating a visible gap between a rock and the floor
R8 Scallops mid-scale fracture bowls (Worley, ~1.5 mm): "horrible" (v1d)
R9 Big flat faces a few oversized facets instead of a mosaic (v1c, fixed in v1f)

5. Acceptance

  • S1: the 2 × 2 tile at seed 1. Rocks read as the reference's chiselled stones; none of R1–R9 is visible.
  • S2: seeds 1–6. Each looks different.
  • S3: a print, primed and drybrushed. Facet edges catch highlights.

6. Rejected approaches (keep the lessons)

  • Shave-the-extreme-vertex chip cuts (v1a/b): converge to uniformly sized "dice" facets (R2) and removed 50–68% of the volume. → One tangent-plane facet per blue-noise seed.
  • Fracture scallops (v1d): Shawn: "horrible". Don't reintroduce. → Turn up the fine grain instead.
  • Harder facet tilt (22–28°, v1f trial): removed 40–60% of the volume and still left the big faces. → Smaller facet spacing plus angle-only body lumpiness.
  • Flake scars: proposed and declined by Shawn.
  • Uncapped chip rate: with smaller facets there are many more corners; uncapped chips eroded rocks to about 60% of their volume. → At most 8–16 chips per rock, each removing ≤ 3%.

7. Out of scope (later)

Integration as a dharmatiles layer (scatter/stones.py is a separate, smoother "aged" family), colour, stacked or overhanging boulder piles. Rocks on hills and among grass: see meadowlab/.
