1. Core idea: the face is a depth map on a footprint outline, printability is one pass

1.1 Bodies and forms

A cliff is a set of bodies. Each has a plan footprint (polygon, may extend past the tile), a face line along its edge (an open line for a run's face, the closed ring for a free-standing body), a top height, and an optional taper (inset per mm of height, for spires). For every (x, y) of the mesh grid: d = signed distance inside the footprint (shapely, exact) and s = arc position of the nearest face-line point (line_locate_point). The face texture is a depth map D(s, z) per body (below, with "y" read as "inset along the outward normal"): rock where d ≥ D(s, z). Because d is a true distance, the upward clamp on D bounds the overhang angle exactly as on a plane. Closed face lines wrap in s (noise, Voronoi and fissure distances are periodic).

Form Bodies Reference
run (gen-1) one footprint behind a line across the tile B, H, N
corner an L-shaped footprint: an inside corner C
canyon two runs facing across a 20 mm gorge, different heights I, K
outcrop one lumpy closed blob, 14 mm high A
spire three stacked blobs (18, 32, 46 mm), tapered, the top one bare E, P, F

Still to come from the sheet: escarpments (D/O: a sloping top), stacked overhanging slabs (L: limited to 45° undersides), pinnacle pairs (J), ruins on top (G). The machinery covers them; they need their footprints and top shapes.

1.2 The face of one body

Tile coordinates: x along the run (0…70 mm), y into the tile (front y = 0, back y = 70), z up. The cliff face is a depth map F(x, z): the y of the face at each point of the vertical plane. Rock is behind it (y ≥ F). Every face feature is a 2-D operation on F:

 base: face line y₀ + plan wobble(x) + batter·z
   + LARGE masses   (anisotropic warped Voronoi, 8–35 mm): per-mass plane (offset, tilt)
   + MEDIUM plates  (same generator, 3–9 mm), scaled by the activity field
   + LEDGES / SHELVES (short horizontal steps) + long DIAGONAL fractures
   + V-groove FISSURES at every cell boundary (depth by level, fading along the joint)
   → weathering: round convex edges only (F ← max(F, blur F)); recesses stay sharp
   + FINE: shattered plates and hairlines in hot spots, grain everywhere (≥ 0.15 mm)
   → END SECTION blend (x within 5 mm of either end → the fixed standard section)
   → 45° CLAMP, bottom-up per column: F(z) ≥ F(z − dz) − dz·tan 42°

Why a depth map. The face's whole vocabulary (planes, steps, fissures, ledges, undercuts) is relief across the face, and a depth map makes it cheap 2-D work on a 0.18 mm grid. It can't fold back on itself, and that is exactly the FDM rule: the only overhang a depth map can make is the face advancing as z rises (∂F/∂z < 0), and the upward clamp limits that to 42° (3° margin). So REQ-FDM-1 holds by construction, and every undercut shelf comes out as a 45°-ish wedge. Islands can't form: every row of the face is attached to the row below.

Solid. With G(x, y) the foot ground and P(x, y) the plateau top:

f(x, y, z) = max( G − z,  min( y − F(x, z),  P − z ) )      solid where f > 0

The terms are (near-)distances in mm, so marching cubes interpolates them cleanly; y − F is linear in y, so the face is placed exactly in depth. The solid is meshed on a 0.18 mm grid from just below the foot to above the plateau, clipped to the tile box, decimated, then unioned with the slab under it, the scree rocks and the DB base.

2. Stages

2.1 Frame

  • Foot level z_f = 4.0 mm (DB dirt band). Plateau level z_p = z_f + 35 mm (REQ-OUT-3; fixed, so the end sections match).
  • Face line y₀ = 28 mm (40% of 70). Batter: the face leans back 4° (y grows 0.07 mm per mm of height).
  • Plan wobble: 1-D smooth noise along x (σ 9 mm), scaled to ±3 mm; the large masses add ±2 mm more (REQ-RUN-1: about ±5 mm overall).

2.2 Activity field (REQ-ROCK-3: 60 / 30 / 10)

A smooth noise field on the face (σ 7 mm) plus a bump near major fissures (fractures cluster at big joints), rank-transformed to a uniform u ∈ [0, 1]. Then u < 0.6 is quiet (medium cracks at 15% strength, no fine shatter), 0.6–0.9 is fractured (medium at full strength, fine at 30%), and u > 0.9 is shattered (everything at full strength). Ranking makes the split exact by area on every seed, and clustered rather than sprinkled.

2.3 Masses: anisotropic warped Voronoi at three levels

One generator, used at three sizes (the self-similar family, §1.1):

  • Seeds: dart-thrown, each with a size s_i (large: 9–22 mm, skewed small, so widths vary ≥ 4×), a joint direction θ_i, and an aspect (large 2.4: tall). θ_i: 80% within ±20° of vertical, 20% at 35–55° (diagonal masses). Medium seeds inherit their parent mass's θ ± 10°.
  • Ownership: argmin_i ‖(Δ·a_i, Δ·b_i / aspect)‖ + warp(s, z)·φ_i (an unweighted anisotropic Voronoi), with a_i ⊥ θ_i, b_i ∥ θ_i, and warp a smooth noise (σ 3 mm, ±0.07 × the level's mean cell size). Size variety comes from the variable-radius seed spacing; the anisotropy (large 3.0, medium 2.2) gives vertical-dominant joints; the per-seed warp phase bends them. Triple junctions are the branching. (Rev 1 divided the distance by each seed's size: an Apollonius diagram, whose curved boundaries read as cobbles, C1/C6.)
  • Plane per cell: offset (large N(0, 2.4) mm clipped ±5, medium N(0, 0.45)), tilt across (large ±28°, medium ±9°) and in height (large −1.5…+9°, medium −1.5…+6°: they barely lean forward; forward-leaning masses made big undersides, which B/H/N don't have). Offset differences between neighbours are the "pronounced changes of plane"; proud cells are buttresses and shoulders.
  • Fissures: V-grooves along every cell boundary, from the EDT of the boundary mask: depth·(1 − d/w)₊. Large: depth 2–5 mm, half-width 0.9 mm (mouth ≈ 1.8 mm). Medium: 0.6–1.4 mm deep, half-width 0.45. Fine: 0.25–0.45 mm deep, half-width 0.3 (mouth 0.6, REQ-FDM-3). Depth is modulated along each joint by a smooth noise, so joints fade out and reappear (REQ-ROCK-2); medium and fine are also scaled by activity.

2.4 Ledges, shelves and long diagonals

  • Ledges (4–8 per run): a short near-horizontal line (4–14 mm, ±10°) across which the face above steps back 0.6–1.6 mm, giving a lit shelf top. 35% are undercut shelves instead: the face below steps back, and the 45° clamp turns the step into a wedge underside.
  • Long diagonals (1–3 per run): bent polylines at 30–60°, 15–40 mm long, carved as medium-depth V-grooves across several masses.

2.5 Weathering and fine detail (REQ-ROCK-5, REQ-FDM-4)

  • Convex-only rounding: F ← max(F, G_σ(F)), σ 0.45 mm. Behind-the- face is +y, so proud edges are local minima of F and rise to the blur, rounding them; fissures and inside corners are maxima and stay sharp.
  • Fine shatter: the fine level (1.2–3 mm cells) in active areas only.
  • Grain: noise at σ 0.5 mm, amplitude 0.16 mm (≥ the printable threshold), halved on proud edges; a few pits (r 0.3–0.6 mm, 0.2 mm deep) in shattered zones. Then a light second rounding (σ 0.15 mm).

2.6 Ends, clamp, top edge

  • Standard end section (REQ-RUN-3): within 5 mm of x = 0 and x = W, F blends (smoothstep) to F_end(z) = y₀ + batter + a fixed ledge profile (built from a fixed RNG seed, identical for every tile seed), and the top edge blends to z_p. Any two tiles therefore meet with the same section. Deviation from REQ-RUN-3: like every DB tile, the solid is cut at the tile boundary, so a lone tile's end shows the section as a flat cut; in a run it is hidden.
  • Clamp: bottom-up per column, D(z) ≥ D(z − dz) − dz·tan 42°. Then the undersides (where the clamp acted, mask blurred 0.6 mm) are replaced by the blur of the clamped surface (σ 1.0 mm along s, 0.35 mm in z) and clamped again. Averaging can't steepen a slope, so the rule still holds; without it each underside was the edge profile above extruded down as vertical streaks (a "comb").
  • Top edge (REQ-RUN-4): each large mass that reaches the top gets a top height z_p + U(−4, +1.5) mm, each medium plate adds U(−1.4, +0.7). Major fissures notch the top as V-dips. The rock top runs back 2–7 mm as a gently tilted surface, then dips at 45° under the plateau dirt.

2.7 Ground

  • Plateau P = max(cap, rock top). The dirt cap follows the rock's top line near the edge and eases to the plateau level over 6 mm behind the rock band, so lowered blocks and notches show in the silhouette (a fixed cap level hid them: a ruled edge, C11). The rock top shows through near the edge (REQ-TOP-1).
  • Foot G = z_f + dirt + talus: a low apron against the face base, up to 0.6–2.2 mm high, decaying over 3–7 mm, higher below major fissures.
  • Dirt = dirtlab.dirt_relief, seeded from the tile seed.

2.8 Scree (REQ-FOOT-1)

25–45 rocklab faceted rocks per 70 mm of face, D = 2.5–8 mm skewed small (no single size dominates: no scale marker), drawn low and blocky (block, slab and wedge families, height 0.3–0.55 × width; rocklab's small domes read as spikes). Positions: face-line points weighted toward major-fissure mouths, pushed out along the outward normal by 0.3·D + Exp(3.5 mm), kept 3 mm off the tile rim and outside every body; dart-thrown with clearance. Seated 0.3 mm into the ground at their spot.

2.9 Mesh, base, audits

  • Marching cubes (skimage) on f, 0.18 mm grid, padded with air; trimesh; quadric decimation to MESH_FACES (1.6M); manifold ∩ tile box ∪ slab box ∪ scree ∪ DB base; keep the largest body; stl_safe (grasslab).
  • Audits printed: heights, overhang area past 48°, slicer islands at 0.2 mm layers (shapely, as used for the shore), the 60/30/10 split measured on the face, face-line range, timing.

3. Painting (contact sheet only; REQ-DIAG-2)

A simulated cheap-acrylic job, per vertex, then quantised to a palette:

  • Stone: base coat dark warm grey; a black-brown wash in recesses (cavity = how far the vertex sits below its Laplacian-smoothed position after many iterations: ambient-occlusion-like); then drybrush layers by edge convexity (a few iterations) plus a bonus for upward facing, the way a dry brush catches raised edges and top surfaces; very occasional ochre staining (a low-frequency mask).
  • Dirt (plateau and foot, up-facing ground): brown base, light drybrush on bumps. Tile walls and base: flat dark.

4. Parameters

All module constants in clifflab.py, grouped by stage as above. The contact sheet varies seeds and a few presets (quiet, deep fissures, big masses, diagonal) through a preset override dict.

Rev 3: thin edge bands, low-profile ends (2026-10-30)

Shawn: "spectacular… the best cliffs I've ever seen", then three tweaks. (1) Edge cliffs should take a small share of the tile (1 × 1-friendly), hugging the back edge, depth going forward, a little lean out, a ground top. (2) Ends: he looked at the Majestic Highlands cliff STLs (MH-117 High Wall 1, MH-120 High Angle, MH-123 High End: a lens-shaped band on one edge of a 3 × 3, ≈ 17 mm deep at most, tapering to exactly zero at both ends, so two at right angles join into one curve). He likes the narrow band and the right-angle behaviour, not the zero: ends should be low-profile natural rock, a consistent profile so they match, not flat, no repeating pattern. (3) Try a few things; show end and top views.

  • Edge bodies (Body.backs = the tile edge(s) behind the face): the face line sits EDGE_T_MM 8 mm in from the back edge. Masses are biased 1 mm forward and recesses soft-limited (softplus knee 0.4 mm) so the wall to the tile edge is never thinner than 1.5 mm: depth goes out, not in. Batter −2° (leans out). Rock top band 0.6–2 mm, cap ease 3 mm, so the top is mostly dirt and reaches plateau level at the back edge.
  • Ends: within END_RAMP_MM 8 mm of each tile crossing, the face line curves (smoothstep) back to END_T_MM 3.2 mm from the back edge, so the face turns the corner as textured rock instead of a right angle. At the crossing D blends to end_profile(z): a fixed-seed (identical on every tile) smooth wander + five irregular ledges + two notches. No sine.
  • End relief (end_cuts): each exposed end is recessed from the tile edge by a shallow noise relief (0.3–0.9 mm, σ 0.7 mm, per-tile seed), from just above the foot to the top (fading it out at the top made the end step outward), clamped at 45° in z.
  • Support backstop in mesh_solid: every voxel needs rock below it within one voxel (4-neighbour, ≈ 45°). It only removes specks where two surfaces meet (a face and its recessed end: up to three 0.001–0.023 mm² islands per tile before it), so every audited tile has 0 islands.
  • Presets end_thin (2.0 mm, ramp 6) and end_thick (4.6 mm, ramp 10) are the "few things" to try; the contact sheet shows each as a butted pair and an outside corner, plus run → corner tile → rotated run (an L).
  • Corner and canyon are thin bands too (an L band, a corridor); outcrops and spires scale with the tile. --cols/--rows default to 1 × 1.
  • Contact sheet views: painted oblique, top-down, end view (from −x), painted close-up of an end or seam.
  • Back edge flush: within 2.5 mm of an edge band's back edge the top eases to exactly plateau level (z_p − 0.4 mm + dirt, the dirt being 0 at the rim), so raised ground set behind the cliff merges with it. Without it a thin band's cap followed the rock's top line all the way back. Verified: back-edge top 38.60 mm along the whole edge (run, corner, canyon).
  • Crowned free-standing tops: outcrop and spire caps rise 1.8 mm toward the middle; a flat cap below a rock rim read as a crater from above (the new top-down view caught it).

Rev 4: ends are faces (2026-10-30)

Shawn, seeing the textured standard ends: drop the matching profile; the ends get the same texture as the faces ("like the stuff we're doing with spires… can go right around the edge"); the only constraint is that where an end meets the back wall it comes within a millimetre or two of the tile's side, so neighbours read as a crevice. No repeating anything. And thumbnails: whole tiles only, from several sides; no crops.

  • Bands are closed bodies inside the tile (_band): a box from END_GAP_MM (1.2) in from each side to 2 mm past the back edge, convex corners rounded 3 mm, concave (corner form) 4 mm; its outline is the face line, wrapping in s. Ends are faces like any other. Removed: the standard end section, end_profile, the end-relief cuts, the end_thin/end_thick presets, Body.ends.
  • Per face-line sample (face_map): position, outward normal, and wf (1 on fronts — facing away from the back wall — 0 on ends).
    • Fronts: plan wander, 1 mm forward bias, −2° lean out, the band-wall limit (inward ray to the tile boundary − 1.5 mm, soft).
    • Ends: no plan wander (it was z-constant: an end pushed out by it sat against the limit full height, a ruled surface), relief × 0.6, 1.5 mm recess bias, +4° lean back (leaning out ran ends into the limit and left long streaky undersides).
    • Back taper: relief × smoothstep(distance to the back wall / 1.5 mm), and the top edge → plateau level there.
    • Tile-edge limit for every body: soft max(D, −(outward ray to the tile boundary − clearance)), knee 0.7 mm, where the clearance is itself a rock relief (0.3–1.1 mm): rock pressed against the edge stays textured. Applied again after the underside smoothing (σ 2.0 mm along s now), whose blur had carried a front's proud corner mass past the tile edge into the tile clip, slicing it flat; then re-clamped.
  • Contact sheet: six whole-tile views per row (oblique left/right, front, left end, right end, top-down); assemblies: two butted pairs, an outside corner, run + corner tile + run.

Rev 5: rock bodies textured all round; contact rims seal (2026-10-30)

Shawn on rev 4: the ends weren't cohesive with the front ("a big mass in front with a horizontal crevice above it should curl around to the side"). Make the cliff not a special case: a big rock wall textured on all four sides with dirt on top; the only constraint is that faces against the tile edge are low relief at their rims (they must fit tight against neighbouring cliffs and the ground), while their middles can have big cuts.

  • One texture, no front/end special cases. Removed the per-face bias / scale / lean / wander rules of rev 4. Bodies: run = a slab box(0.6, H − 8, W − 0.6, H) against the back edge; wall (new) = an 11 mm free-standing wall across the middle; corner, canyon likewise. Convex corners rounded 3 mm, concave 8 mm (4 mm streaked: points just inside a tight concave corner flip between the two faces' textures).
  • Contact faces (c → 1 when the outward ray to the tile boundary is under 1–3 mm): no plan wander, no batter (upright on the boundary), and the texture shifted back per column (by its smoothed minimum) so its proudest point just touches the boundary: it cuts inward with its whole relief (× 0.6), never bulges past. Clipping the bulges instead left flat panels and a foil-like surface. Eased to flush over 2.5 mm at the top and bottom rims; top edge at plateau level. Open faces (c → 0): full relief, 4° batter. Blended by c along the outline, so the texture carries round the corners.
  • Cuts never meet: each face recesses at most half the chord through the body (ray within the footprint), less 0.75 mm (soft). Large masses sink at most 2 mm (they stand proud up to 5): deeper ones hit that limit and went flat.
  • Tile-edge clearance relief reduced to 0.3–0.65 mm.
  • Contact sheet: seven whole-tile views (adds the back); wall rows.
  • Audits: all forms watertight, micro-lips ≤ 1.7 mm², 0 islands except one 0.002 mm² speck near a back corner on run s2 (sub-nozzle).

Rev 6: the face is stacked blocks (2026-10-30)

Shawn: "the aesthetics have regressed… the sense of big piled-up slabs doesn't fit." Diagnosis: (1) the thin-slab and contact-face limits had flattened the masses into a crack network on a flat wall; (2) more fundamentally, a Voronoi cell network with V-grooves can never make the references' stacked prismatic blocks with flat chipped tops. Rewrite of the large scale (build_blocks), everything else kept:

  • Columns along the face line: widths 3–12 mm (skewed narrow), joints within ±7° of vertical (12% at 18–32°), wobbly; 25% of joints stop for one 5–12 mm stretch, so one block bridges two columns. (Bug found on the way: numbering columns by counting active joints renumbered every column to the right of a gap — horizontal stripes across the whole face.)
  • Blocks: each column split by near-horizontal joints (±5°) every 7–24 mm; 55% of columns snap a joint to one of 2–3 shared strata (subtle layering). Row joints shallow (0.45–1.0 mm) and fading, column joints deep (1.5–3.2 mm): vertical dominance.
  • Offsets: masses of 1–2 columns step forward 0–8 mm, columns ± 1.8, blocks ± 0.25; a block above steps back 0.6–2 mm with p 0.18 (lit block tops; every-row steps made a layer cake); 8% of blocks recessed (voids).
  • Faces: per block, a front plane (±7° across, lean back ≤ 7°) maxed with 4 chisel facets (18–40°, starting 0.45–0.9 of the half-extent out; top chamfer on 35% of blocks only; no downward facets) and a 1 mm 45° chamfer at every block edge: convex, crisp, chipped.
  • Top edge per column, −5…+3 mm (blocks stand proud of the top line).
  • Medium plates now only score blocks (plane offsets × 0.25); ledges removed (blocks make them); diagonals 0–2; slab 13 mm.

Butted tiles touch (2026-10-30)

Shawn: "significant light between adjacent cliff tiles viewed head on… there should be a fair bit of touching." Measured (rays along x onto each end face): 0% within 0.5 mm, median gap 1.4–2.2 mm per tile. Causes, all stacking: footprints inset 0.6 mm; every corner rounded 3 mm, including those on the tile boundary; a 0.3–0.65 mm clearance relief; softplus knees (0.4–0.7 mm) adding k·ln 2 of setback even at a face's proudest point; contact faces referenced to their single proudest point. Fixes: footprints run to the tile edge; only free (front) corners are rounded, corners on the boundary stay square (_slab_round_front); the tile-edge limit sits 0.15 mm past the edge with a 0.05 mm knee, so flush rock is clipped exactly on the boundary; contact faces are referenced to their 60th-percentile level (CONTACT_FLUSH), so ~60% lies flush and the joints and recesses stay as cuts, knee 0.12 mm, offset removed. Result: 64–81% of each end face touches the boundary (< 0.1 mm), 83–93% within 0.5 mm, median 0.0–0.07 mm; all forms watertight, 0 islands.

  • Correction (Shawn): "you're clipping adjacent edges flat. They should be regular textured surfaces whose outside limits of texture touch the edge." The 60th-percentile flush and the past-the-edge limit are gone: contact faces are referenced to their proudest points again (a running minimum along s, so smoothing never lifts a peak past it), no clearance, 0.04 mm knee. Two meshing faults surfaced: a grid row exactly on x = 0 meshed the whole left end flat (≈ 300 mm²; the right end, with no row on x = W, was fine) — the grid is now cell-centred and symmetric; and the constant air pad pulled every surface ≈ 0.1 mm inside the tile — the x/y pad is now the field's own continuation, then air, so a peak that reaches the edge meshes exactly to it. Result: 0 mm² flat in the side planes; 5–28% of each end within 0.1 mm of the edge (the texture's peaks), 16–54% within 0.5 mm. One 0.008 mm² speck at a slab's front corner on some seeds (sub-nozzle).
