Visual targets: docs/labs/clifflab/heroic-cliff-spec.html (Shawn's "Universal Heroic Cliff Face" spec, the authority on surface character) and reference/reference-sheet.png (16 painted cliff miniatures; B, H and N are this lab's form). A standalone experiment built like the other labs: it may import dharmatiles primitives and the other labs, never modifies src/dharmatiles/, and writes to clifflab/out/.

Shawn's choices for gen-1 (2026-10-30): a straight cliff run, FDM without supports, about one storey (30–40 mm), dirt top and scree foot, no vegetation.

1. What this is

One STL: a standard 2 × 2 DungeonBlocks tile crossed by a cliff. Low ground (the foot) runs along the front, a plateau (the top) along the back, and between them a monumental, weathered, fractured rock face about one storey high.

Heroic without being eccentric. Broad, powerful, quiet stone faces interrupted by deep, expressive fractures, mostly vertical and steeply diagonal, with broken ledges. The face must read as a big cliff and as a low ledge alike: no scale markers.

1.1 Visual scales (nested, self-similar)

Scale Size on the tile What it does
Large forms masses 8–35 mm wide; major fissures 2–5 mm deep Buttresses, recesses and broad faces changing plane; the silhouette and the big shadows.
Medium forms 3–10 mm Broken plates, shallow shelves, overlapping faces, chipped corners, secondary fractures.
Fine detail 0.5–3 mm Chips, hairline cracks, pits, grain, erosion channels. Printable sizes only (§2.2).

A small crack and a major fissure belong to the same family: the same directions, the same wedge profile, the same branching, at different sizes.

1.2 What it is not (gen-1 scope)

Out of scope for acceptance: corners, spires, outcrops, escarpments, canyons (later forms from the sheet); grass and other vegetation (grasslab can add them later); colour and paint; resin-specific detail; OpenLOCK.

  • REQ-RANGE-1 (Shawn, 2026-10-30: "I love those photos, especially the range. We should not build something that can't meet those needs.") The design must be able to reach every form on the reference sheet (subject to the FDM rules): nothing in it may assume a single flat face. Corner, canyon, outcrop and spire previews are built alongside the run.

2. Requirements

Each requirement is checkable from a viewer or render ([R]) or from a script or statistic ([S]).

2.1 Output and tile

  • REQ-OUT-1 [S] A run writes clifflab/out/2x2-cliff-s<seed>.stl.
  • REQ-OUT-2 [S] One watertight manifold body (passes the slicer-style reload check stl_safe), fused to the standard DB socket-peg base (dharmatiles.bases.dungeonblocks).
  • REQ-OUT-3 [S] The foot ground sits in the DB dirt band (4.0 mm above the terrain datum, as rocklab and dirtlab). The plateau top is 30–40 mm above the foot.
  • REQ-OUT-4 [S] Deterministic per seed; builds in under 2 minutes; STL under 100 MB.

2.2 FDM rules (hard; 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, base-down, no supports)

Lesson from grasslab v3: model only what prints.

  • REQ-FDM-1 [S] No downward-facing surface steeper than 45° from vertical. Overhanging shelves and undercuts are wedges with 45° undersides.
  • REQ-FDM-2 [S] No islands at 0.2 mm layers: every layer is supported by the one below.
  • REQ-FDM-3 [S] Fissures are wedges, never slots. Every fracture opens outward; its mouth is ≥ 0.6 mm wide wherever it is deeper than 0.5 mm, so paint can reach and slicers don't fuse it.
  • REQ-FDM-4 [S] No sub-line features. Free ridges, chips and plates are ≥ 0.45 mm (one line) wide; relief smaller than ≈ 0.15 mm is not modelled (it would not print, so it would only mislead the viewer).
  • REQ-FDM-5 [S] The plateau and the foot are solid ground to the base.

2.3 Layout of the run

  • REQ-RUN-1 [R] The cliff crosses the tile from the left edge to the right edge, its face toward the front. The face line wanders in plan by about ±5 mm: buttresses stand out, recesses and gullies cut in. It is never a straight wall.
  • REQ-RUN-2 [R] The face is near-vertical overall (mean batter 0–10° back from vertical), steeper and overhanging locally (within REQ-FDM-1).
  • REQ-RUN-3 [R] Rock all round; contact rims seal. (Shawn, 2026-10-30, rev 3 of this requirement: "a big wall made of rock, textured on all four sides with dirt on the top… the edge of the face that is against the tile edge needs to be low relief… the middle can have big cuts and rocky construction.") A cliff is a rock body textured on every side (front, ends and back), the texture continuous round its corners (a front mass and the crevice above it curl round onto the end). Faces lying along the tile boundary may cut inward freely in their middle but never cross the boundary, and at their rims (top, bottom) they come flush with it, so the piece fits tight against neighbouring cliffs and the ground beside it. No matching profiles, nothing repeating.
  • REQ-WALL-1 [R] A free-standing rock wall (wall form) across the tile, both faces fully textured: both sides of it are useful.
  • REQ-EDGE-1 [S] Edge cliffs are thin bands. (Shawn, 2026-10-30.) Cliffs that run to the tile edges (run, corner, canyon) hug the back edge(s) as a band ≈ 8 mm deep, a small share of a 1 × 1 tile (they must work on 1 × 1), with lots of depth projecting forward from it and a little lean out. The top is ground (dirt), at plateau level at the back edge, so raised ground set behind the cliff merges with it; it need not be wide enough to stand a miniature on. Free-standing forms (outcrops, spires) have no such limit.
  • REQ-RUN-4 [R] The top edge is broken and irregular: blocks standing proud, notches, and a few step-backs, never a ruled line.

2.4 Rock masses and fractures (the face)

  • REQ-ROCK-1 [R] Interlocking masses, not stacked boulders. Broad, angular, somewhat flattened masses that appear naturally joined. Some faces are flat and imposing, others project as chunky broken shoulders. Mass widths vary by at least 4×.
  • REQ-ROCK-2 [R] Vertical character, broken horizontals. Joints run mostly vertical and steeply diagonal (most within ±25° of vertical, a minority at 30–60°). They are interrupted by short irregular horizontal ledges, diagonal fractures that cross several faces, occasional projecting shelves and undercuts, and subtle discontinuous layering. Joints bend, branch, stop and reappear.
  • REQ-ROCK-3 [S] Controlled irregularity. By face area: about 60% broad quiet faces, 30% substantial fractures, offsets, ridges and broken edges, 10% intensely detailed crevices and shattered zones, in clusters rather than spread evenly.
  • REQ-ROCK-4 [R] Planes, not noise. Faces are planar or gently curved facets meeting at edges; the rock is never a noise blob or a melted surface. Fine detail decorates the planes; it doesn't replace them.
  • REQ-ROCK-5 [R] Age. Exposed convex edges are softened by weathering (rounded about 0.3–0.8 mm) with small missing chips and shallow erosion channels. Deep recesses stay sharp.
  • REQ-ROCK-6 [R] Heroic depth. A little deeper than realistic: major fissures 2–5 mm deep, pronounced changes of plane, strong projecting edges, so the face carries dramatic shadow. Not cartoonish.
  • REQ-ROCK-7 [R] No pattern, no repetition. No regular blocks or courses, no repeated column widths, no regular joint spacing. Two seeds look like the same stone, never the same cliff.

2.5 Top and foot

  • REQ-TOP-1 [R] The plateau is ground with the shared dirt texture (dirtlab.dirt_relief). It meets the cliff edge as rock: the tops of the cliff's masses show through near the edge.
  • REQ-FOOT-1 [R] The foot is dirt ground with scree: fallen blocks of the same stone (rocklab-style faceted rocks, 1–8 mm, mixed sizes), denser at the cliff base and below fissure mouths, thinning out toward the front edge. The scree never forms a uniform carpet of same-size pebbles (a scale marker).
  • REQ-FOOT-2 [R] No vegetation anywhere.

2.6 Parameters and diagnostics

  • REQ-PAR-1 Named, bounded module constants grouped by stage, with a seed.
  • REQ-DIAG-1 [S] Each run prints mesh, heights, overhang and island audits, the quiet/fracture/detail area split (REQ-ROCK-3) and timing.
  • REQ-DIAG-2 Before handing over an STL, Claude checks it with quick renders (oblique, face-on and raking light) as an internal filter only. Shawn's eye in a viewer and slicer is the judge.

3. Failure modes (named anti-goals)

# Failure Looks like
C1 Stacked boulders rounded lumps piled on each other; no structural masses
C2 Organ pipes regular vertical columns (basalt)
C3 Layer cake continuous even horizontal bedding (sandstone)
C4 Masonry blocks in courses, right angles everywhere: a wall, not a cliff
C5 Gravel every square millimetre fractured; no quiet faces; no sense of scale
C6 Noise blob a melted, lumpy surface with no planes or edges
C7 Wallpaper a visible repeat or a tiled texture
C8 Paint-on relief too shallow to cast shadow; a flat wall with scratches
C9 Cartoon absurdly deep, jagged or spiky
C10 Scale marker uniform pebbles, regular bedding or same-size blocks that announce a size
C11 Ruled edge a straight top edge or a straight foot line
C12 Sawn end a flat cut face at the tile edge
C13 Unprintable shelves overhanging past 45°, slot cracks, needles, islands

Pre-paint test: the bare grey print reads as a heroic cliff.

4. Acceptance scenes

Scene Content Passes when
S1: face study the cliff face only, on a flat base §2.4 holds; judged first, before top, foot or sweeps.
S2: full tile 2 × 2, seed 1, with top and foot All of §2 holds; no C-mode is visible.
S3: seed sweep S2 at seeds 1–6 At least 5 of 6 pass; none looks like another.
S4: long run 3 tiles of different seeds side by side One continuous cliff; no visible joints (REQ-RUN-3).
S5: print S2 on FDM, primed, drybrushed Fissures stay dark, faces catch the highlight, nothing broke or needed support.

5. Open questions

  1. Where the face stands on the tile. Proposed: the face line at about 40% of the depth from the front, giving a foot strip of ≈ 25 mm and a plateau of ≈ 40 mm. A narrower foot reads more like a wall section; a wider one gives more room for scree.
  2. Plateau shape. Flat with the dirt texture, or gently rolling (a little of grasslab's hills)?
  3. Standard end section (REQ-RUN-3). A fixed section for every seed means the ends always match; the price is that every tile's ends look alike for the last ≈ 5 mm.
  4. Reuse. The face could be built from the shared stone primitives (src/dharmatiles/stone/, as the fieldstone walls do) or from scratch. That is a design decision, settled in design.html.
