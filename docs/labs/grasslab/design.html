1. Core idea: separate blades on dirt, each built the way a printer draws

 1 H₀ hills (rises only) ──► 2 flow θ: regional angle + in-phase meanders + downhill pull
                                   │
          3 SWATH cells (warped anisotropic Voronoi) → strand lengths along each cell
                                   │
 4 soil LENSES (flow-aligned eyes) → flow wraps each lens → rim steering → final flow
                                   │
 5 STRANDS of roots on long streamlines → each FROND traced along the flow,
       drawn and shortened (rim, soil, obstacles)
                                   │
 5c each BLADE: a stack of 0.2 mm layers, each a stroke along its spine a whole
       number of 0.45 mm lines wide (blade_layers) → smoothed: a loft through
       the layer outlines in height-above-ground, marching cubes at 0.05 mm,
       seated on H₀, quadric-decimated (blade_mesh)
                                   │
 6 dirt ground solid (H₀ + dirt relief, from z = 0) ∪ every blade (batch union)
                                   │
 7 ∪ DB socket base (manifold3d) → simplify 0.005 mm → keep largest body → STL

Three guarantees drive the layout:

  • The model is what prints. A blade is defined in the printer's own units: whole 0.2 mm layers and whole 0.45 mm extrusion lines. Nothing is narrower than one line, so nothing the slicer would drop or mangle ("detect thin walls") is ever modelled. Overhangs are counted in layers: a layer reaches at most ½ line past the one below (≈ 45°). The smooth version (for resin) keeps that shape and only melts the steps.
  • Blades on dirt, no turf mass. (Shawn, 2026-10-29.) Every bit of grass is a blade; between blades is the dirt ground. No pad, skirt or envelope layer fills between them (that mass is what buried the undercuts).
  • Undercuts by construction. Each blade stands on a keel (its bottom layer) and its upper layers reach further toward the tip, so every tip overhangs open air, to the last layer.

Grids: ground, flow and swaths on a 0.25 mm grid extended 12 mm (ground) or 10 mm (swaths) past the tile; the ground solid and the grass-top map on a 0.12 mm grid (0.1 mm at 1 × 1); each blade's distance field on its own 0.05 mm grid.

2. Stages

2.1 Hills H₀ (make_ground)

  • Noise: unit-variance Gaussian-filtered noise at σ 10 mm plus 0.35 × σ 5 mm.
  • Rises only: shift so the interior's 40th percentile is 0, soft-floor at 0 with smoothmax (k = 0.25·std, offset so smoothmax(0, 0) = 0), clamp ≥ 0, and scale so the interior max is 7 mm.
  • Cosine feather over 9 mm to the 3.4 mm edge level: h = 3.4 + w(dist)·raw.
  • Slope cap: if any slope exceeds 34°, the relief above the edge level is scaled down uniformly. On seed 1 the cap binds, leaving 3.40–6.58 mm (≈ 3.2 mm of rise); 78% of the area is ≤ 12° (90% on seed 2).
  • --flat returns the constant edge level; the hill noise is still drawn so the rest of the RNG stream is unchanged.

2.2 Flow field (make_direction, finish_flow, steer_rim)

All steps before 4 operate on a scalar angle, which cannot form singularities (REQ-FL-5).

  1. Regional angle: θ_r = θ₀ + 60°·n(σ 15 mm), θ₀ uniform.
  2. Meander: θ = θ_r + A·sin(2π·s/15 mm + φ).
    • s is the least-squares along-flow coordinate, ∇s ≈ (cos θ_r, sin θ_r), solved by FFT Poisson on a mirror-extended grid. Lines of constant s cross the flow, so neighbouring streamlines wave in phase.
    • A = 10°–30° from smoothstep(0.5 + 0.35·n(σ 12 mm)); φ = π·n(σ 14 mm).
  3. Downhill pull: vector blend toward −∇H₀ with weight 0.3·clip(slope / tan 34°, 0, 1).
  4. Lens wrap (on the vector field), per lens with its own band U(1.5, 4.5) mm and strength U(0.55, 1.0):
    • d = lens-metric distance (§2.4), n̂ = normalised metric gradient, w = strength·(1 − smoothstep(d / band)).
    • Remove the inward component: v ← v − w·min(v·n̂, 0)·n̂.
    • Parting at the upstream tip: v ← v − 0.6·w·min(v·n̂, 0)·tanh(b / 0.8 mm)·p̂, with b the across-lens coordinate and p̂ the lens perpendicular. The smooth tanh side choice avoids a herringbone seam upstream.
    • Renormalise. Lens partings and rejoins are the only singularities.
  5. Rim steering (steer_rim, rev v2g). Three smooth knob maps (smoothstep(0.5 + 0.35·n(σ 8 mm))): ramp distance 9–15 mm, allowed angle φ_ok 70–88°, pull 0.15–0.45. φ is the heading measured from an edge's outward normal, w = smoothstep(1 − dist/ramp).
    • Turn side, once per stretch of rim (_rim_sides). Per perimeter sample, the w-weighted mean tangential lean sin φ across the steering band, laid out as one counter-clockwise loop (side +1 = turning CCW on every edge), smoothed at σ 20 mm along the loop, across corners. Its sign is the side: one per long stretch, and a corner continues the adjacent edge. hard = that sign smoothed at 2.5 mm (which way inward headings turn), soft = smoothed at 8 mm (≈ 0 around a flip).
    • Per edge, a rotation Δ_e of the input angle:
      • Inward headings (|φ| > φ_ok) are compressed toward ±φ_ok on the rim side; the two candidate rotations (turn +, turn −) are blended by λ = ½(1 + hard), except that headings within 30° of parallel keep their own side (this also masks each candidate's unwrap cut at 90°).
      • Pull outward (|φ| ← |φ|·(1 − pull·w)) only for headings already going outward, faded over ±0.35 rad about 90°. Pulling turned-in headings too gave an S-bend that read as a herringbone spine parallel to the wall.
      • Square up around a flip: there the pull rises to 0.85·(1 − |soft|) and its ramp reaches up to 2× further, so where two stretches meet the flow comes square to the wall and ends there.
      • Run alongside within 3 mm: blend toward sign(soft)·φ_ok with smoothstep(1 − dist/3 mm)·|soft|, i.e. faded out around a flip. (Without this every rim frond pointed into the wall and was cut short, leaving a bare "picture frame".)
    • Corners: Δ = Σ P_e Δ_e with a nearest-edge partition of unity P_e ∝ exp(−(d_e − d_min)/2 mm) (far edges have Δ_e = 0).
    • Applied as a smoothed scalar rotation: θ + G_σ1.5mm(Δ). A continuous Δ cannot create a singularity.
    • Before v2g the side came from a pointwise smoothed preference (σ 14 mm, 2-D) and edges were applied in a fixed order. Where the preference flipped, the run-alongside band forced opposite headings side by side: long feathered V seams and corner starbursts (meadow seeds 2, 4, 5).

2.3 Swaths (Swaths)

Swaths no longer carry height (there is no turf mass); they group strand roots and scale frond length along each cell.

  • Sites: Poisson-disk at 11 mm over the tile + 10 mm margin. Axis dᵢ = the pre-wrap flow (step 3) at the site.
  • Ownership: argmin of ‖(Δ·dᵢ), (Δ·d⊥ᵢ)/0.65‖ + 2.5 mm·warp(x, y)·φᵢ, with warp smooth noise at σ 3 mm and a per-site phase φᵢ ∈ U(−1, 1).
  • Cell coordinates s = (a − a_min)/(a_max − a_min) ∈ [0, 1], l = b/|b|_max ∈ [−1, 1], normalised per cell on the 0.25 mm grid; s at a root lengthens fronds toward the cell's downstream crest (§2.6).

2.4 Soil lenses and soil relief (place_lenses, Lenses)

  • Placement: N = round(U(1.3, 1.8) × squares), at least 1. 80 candidates inside a 7 mm rim inset, scored by −z-score(ground height) + N(0, 0.8), accepted best-first ≥ 13 mm apart. Typical coverage ≈ 10%.
  • Shape: axis = pre-wrap flow at the centre; half-length U(5, 9) mm, half-width × U(0.45, 0.65). Lens metric f = |a|/ℓ + (b/w)², which gives pointed ends (an eye); approximate distance d = (f − 1)·w/2 plus a shared ±0.8 mm border warp (σ 1.2 mm). Mask = smoothstep(0.5 − d/0.5 mm).
  • Grass response: the mask decides only where fronds root and how far they spill (§2.6); it never cuts a blade.
  • Ground texture: a soft trough −0.4 mm·m_s (m_s = the mask blurred at σ 0.8 mm, so no step outlines the patch) in H₀, plus the shared dirt texture dirtlab.dirt_relief (roughness, clods, grit, pebbles) over all exposed ground: bare patches and between blades.

2.5 Strands and spines (make_strand_roots, plan_fronds)

  • Strands: Poisson seeds at 2.65 mm over the tile + 8 mm margin. Each strand is U(15, 30) mm long and starts U(0.2, 0.6) of its length upstream of its seed (traced backward), then is traced forward.
  • Roots every 0.5·L·U(0.8, 1.2) along the strand, jittered ±0.3 mm laterally, with L = U(3.5, 6.5) mm; only roots inside the tile are kept.
  • Per root: swath owner and (s_b, l_b); bank = within 4 mm of soil and not in it. Heading offset: strays (5%, or 12% for bank roots) get ±U(20°, 35°), others U(±4°); bank roots add U(±15°) wander.
  • Spines: one batched RK2 trace per root along the final flow, at 0.25 mm unit-speed steps, rotated by the root's offset, to 6.5·1.1 + 0.5 mm. Neighbouring strands 2.65 mm apart against blades 0.9–1.8 mm wide leave dirt showing between ribbons (≈ 9% of the grass area).

2.6 Frond draw and shortening (draw_frond)

  • Skips: roots under an obstacle (meadowlab's rocks, shorelab's water); roots in soil become sprigs with 10% probability (L U(1.8, 3.0), half-width 0.5, crest 0.6 mm), otherwise are dropped.
  • Dimensions: L = L_strand·(0.8 + 0.3·s_b) (2.8–7.2 mm, longer toward the swath crest); nominal half-width U(0.8, 1.25) (→ width in lines); crest height h_f U(1.5, 2.0) mm. Bank fronds: h_f × U(0.65, 1.15).
  • Per-frond endings: rim tip inset 0.2 + 1.3·U² mm; soil spill 0.8 + 2.4·U² mm, or 4 mm for wisps (4%).
  • Shortening (never slicing): the spine stops at the first sample inside the rim inset, deeper than the spill into soil (EDT), or deeper than tuck_mm into an obstacle; L becomes that arc length. Fronds shorter than 1.5 mm are dropped. Shortened fronds are lower: h_f × clip(L/L₀, 0.3, 1)^0.8 (REQ-EDGE-5).

2.7 Blade strokes (blade_layers)

A blade of crest height h has N = round(h / 0.2) layers. Layer k is a stroke along the traced spine (the frond's streamline), a whole number of lines wide, built as nested round-capped buffers of the spine segment:

  • Cross-section (line counts per layer): n_k = round(n_max·sin(π(k + ½)/N)^0.6), n_max = clip(round(2·half-width / 0.45), 2, 4); the bottom layer ≥ 2 lines (the keel), the top layer exactly 1 line (the sharpest ridge FDM can make), and n_k ≤ n_{k−1} + 1 (growth ≤ ½ line a side per layer ≈ 45°). Example, 10 layers, 4-line body: 2 3 4 4 4 4 4 3 2 1.
  • Profile (where a layer starts): u_back = L·(1 − (1 − (k + ½)/N)^{1/2}), the root-side end of the crest 1 − (1 − u/L)²: a convex arch rising from the ground at the root and levelling out at the tip.
  • Tip (where a layer ends): u_front = L − (N − 1 − k)·0.2 mm. Only the top layer reaches the tip; each layer below stops 0.2 mm shorter, so the bow is raked and overhangs open air to the last layer, with the keel underneath.
  • Points: within a layer the stroke loses one line every 0.45 mm toward each end, so both ends close to a single line's round end: in plan, every horizontal section ends in a point.
  • Height map: each layer's footprint raises the grass-top map T to H₀ + (k + 1)·0.2 on the 0.12 mm grid (used to seat rocks, for the DB band check and the gap statistic).

The stroke stack is exactly what an FDM slicer would draw: opening each layer by just under half a line keeps 99.9% of its area (blades_demo.py --strokes writes it unsmoothed).

2.8 Smoothing and seating (blade_mesh, build_blade, ground_solid)

  • Loft: each layer outline is closed by 0.3 mm (fills the notches where a stroke steps down a line) and becomes a 2-D signed distance field on a 0.05 mm grid. In height above ground h, the 3-D field blends linearly between layer centres, so the surface runs straight from one outline to the next: the layer steps vanish but the plan shape (outline, keel, point) is kept. Above the top centre the field closes ½ line over ½ layer (a rounded ridge); below the bottom centre it continues 0.3 mm into the ground (EMBED_MM).
  • Blur σ 0.05 mm in plan and 0.15 mm in h rounds the loft's creases at each layer centre. (A vertical blur alone could not remove the steps: a layer's back end moves ≈ 0.5 mm along the blade per 0.2 mm up. Catmull-Rom interpolation between layers overshot the whole-line jumps and made the blade lumpy.)
  • Mesh: marching cubes; each vertex is lifted by the bilinear H₀ under it, so blades follow the hills; quadric decimation to 1,500 faces (≤ ≈ 20 µm off the field, below resin XY resolution; 800 faces read faceted in a viewer).
  • Obstacles: with side_low < 1 (meadowlab), a blade's height is scaled by the side factor at its tip, where it presses against a rock.
  • Ground: H₀ + dirt relief as a heightfield solid from z = 0 (make_heightmap_solid); blades are meshed on worker threads and batch-unioned with it. Internal voids (sealed under overlapping blades) are separate negative-volume bodies and are dropped with the crumbs, i.e. filled.
  • Base: ∪ exact 1 mm slab box ∪ DB base (peg height from the tallest point), simplify(0.005 mm), keep the largest body, stl_safe, export.

build_grass(…, blocker=None, tuck_mm=0.5, side_low=1.0, side_mm=1.0) is the hook meadowlab and shorelab use for rock footprints (and water); with blocker=None it is grasslab's own behaviour. With side_low < 1, the side factor side_low + (1 − side_low)·smoothstep(d_out/side_mm) at distance d_out outside the blocker, falling to 0 over SIDE_INNER_MM (0.8 mm) inside it, scales each blade's height at its tip, so grass laps against an obstacle's foot instead of swallowing it. It returns Grass with solid (dirt ground ∪ blades, a Manifold), T (grass-top map) and H0 (seating ground).

4. Known quirks

  • WISP_FRAC is drawn for every frond; it only matters where a spine reaches soil.
  • Swaths no longer carry height (no turf mass), only the strand length scaling through s_b; their envelope code is unused but kept for the RNG stream and as the hook for a future turf base layer.
  • Meshing ≈ 80 s of the ≈ 100 s build (3,300 blades, ≈ 24 ms each on 4 threads); a 2 × 2 STL is ≈ 85 MB at 1.7M faces.

5. Superseded designs (lessons kept)

  • Height-field fronds with lips, ledges and a voxel backstop (rev 1–4, v1–v2g, retired 2026-10-29): each frond was a skirt (carrying the swath envelope) plus a domed blade soft-maxed into a mass map T, with column intervals for 45° tip lips and side ledges, a field F = max(T − z, intervals), an FDM voxel backstop (support filter, hole fill, sealed pocket fill, slicer-island removal) and marching cubes at 0.12 mm. Lessons:
    • A height field plus carved intervals never gave a visible undercut: neighbouring skirts walled the air under every tip, the walls sealed it, and the pocket fill then filled it (cross-sections showed it). Undercuts must be part of the blade's own construction.
    • A shape defined as a "theoretical ideal" (domes, knife edges, needle points) is not what prints: everything narrower than a line was dropped by the slicer, or mangled by "detect thin walls". Define the blade in layers and lines instead.
    • The turf mass (skirts) existed only to hide the ground between blades; on a lone blade it read as a stair-stepped pad, and between blades it buried the undercuts. Removed; blades stand on dirt.
    • Tried on the way and dropped: a 45° cone from the blade core (blunt front face; round sections at low heights), a hull of nested pointed sections (vertical keel post until the sections grew from the keel), a J-curve (concave) rise.
  • Free-standing envelope layer (rev 2, v1–v1e): T was seeded with the swath envelope, eased to 0.5× at the rim and cut at soil. Result: rim bevel, carved holes, border steps ("clay", F19). Lesson: no grass height outside a blade.
  • Swath-axis fronds with fan (l_b·12° fan, ±4° jitter, κ ± 0.03/mm): superseded by streamline fronds; the flow, not the cell, carries direction.
  • Seam soil (v1e–v1k): lanes along swath seams (triple-junction chains, width from noise at the nearest seam pixel, slits < 2.2 mm closed). Better than puddles, but unrelated to the flow; replaced by lenses the flow wraps.
  • Curvature field and sickle spines (v1j–v1k): κ = 0.24·tanh(1.6·n) /mm at σ 5.5 mm, bend concentrated toward the tip. Coherent but still per frond: "commas" (F24) beside the lodged-grass photo.
  • Per-frond edge steering (v1i–v1j): steering each frond at its root. Superseded by steering the field; per-point side flips had created a starburst.
  • Global soft frond ends (v1h-a/b): fish scales everywhere.
  • Soil roughness and faceted pebbles: retired for the shared dirt texture (2026-10-29).
  • v0: random lens blades in a binary voxel volume; see REQUIREMENTS §7.2.

Export guarantees (2026-10-27)

  • Exact slab box: the marching-cubes solid is unioned with an exact box ([0, W] × [0, H] × [0, 1 mm], inside the ground, which sits at the 3.4 mm edge level or higher) before the DB base. The base must meet a clean planar face. Meeting the MC bottom directly left 4 sliver edges at the peg-groove apexes under the tile seams, and they collapsed on STL reload.

  • stl_safe, run before every export:

    1. dharmatiles.stone.solidify.separate_pinches nudges coincident but unconnected vertices apart.
    2. The mesh is merged exactly as a slicer does (float32 positions, merge by position).
    3. Zero-volume components (back-to-back union "fins") are dropped.
    4. It raises unless the result is one watertight body.

    Verified on seeds 1–4. Before this, grass seed 3 would have exported a non-watertight file without any warning.

Exposed ground = dirt (2026-10-29; written for the v2 height-field grass)

Shawn: dirt should be the texture wherever ground is exposed, across all labs, and mud and other dirt textures are eliminated. This lab now uses dirtlab.dirt_relief(seed, W, H): dirtlab's soil roughness, clods, grit and pebbles, without its hills or big stones, as a relief offset sampled onto this lab's grid.

  • Surface: fronds still seat on plain H0 (the old soft soil dip is kept), so the grass look is unchanged. T starts at, and is floored to, H0 + dirt, so dirt shows on bare patches and in the gaps between fronds.
  • The old soil roughness and pebbles are retired. soil_relief is still called, and its output discarded, only to keep the RNG stream and so the grass layout identical.
