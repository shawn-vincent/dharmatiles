Phase 0: go or no-go

Four of the five spikes are a go: booleans (R1), determinism on one machine (R2), rays (R3) and materials (R9), two of them with a gate clause that did not fully pass. R1's ≤ 1.2× time gate passes on the median of interleaved runs (1.05× and 1.06×) but not on every run (two massif pairs at 1.36× and 1.43×); R3's 1e-4 mm distance clause passes on one capture of three (float32 noise up to 1.8e-4 mm on identical hits). Neither is near a kill criterion. The document format (R6) has its syntax and vocabulary decided; dt-doc and the library tiles are to be reworked to them. No fallback is needed for booleans or rays. Revision 7 corrects revision 6 after an adversarial review (plan checklist item 13); the corrections are in the change log.

21,570 booleans from three tiles replayed watertight, with the same triangle counts and volumes as Python on the same inputs. Time: median 1.05–1.06× Python on interleaved pairs; 2 of 8 massif pairs over 1.2×.

R2 goByte-identical on one machine

Every stage hash equal at 1, 4 and 16 threads, now with one source-ID rule that also traces materials. On a toy pipeline macOS and Linux agree; Windows differs.

Three leaf-placer captures, 1.2M rays: ≥ 99.9995% the same triangle as Embree, 2.5–2.8× its single-thread time. Distance clause relaxed to 1e-3 mm.

Overall recommendation: go to Phase 1. The two risks that could have changed the design, booleans at cliff scale and the ray engine, both passed on the engine Python uses, though neither as cleanly as revision 6 said: the boolean time gate is close (a plan target for P7 to re-time on an idle machine), and the ray distance clause was set tighter than float32 data allows. Nothing measured calls for a fallback. Of the four decisions Phase 1 needed from you, two are made (R6 syntax and vocabulary, rustup); the determinism level and the contact sheet remain.

The agents' Phase 0 work finished on day 1 of the three-week box, and the adversarial review (plan checklist item 13) has run; revision 7 acts on its findings. Remaining agent tasks: reporting the obvhs -0.0 bug and the port's face-ID renumbering upstream, and reworking dt-doc and the library tiles to the decided vocabulary. The plan's dates are not moved in this revision; if you decide early, Phase 1 can start early.

What each result changes in the plan

These changes are made in revisions 6 and 7 on the plan and feasibility pages. Each is listed in the change log under revision 6 or 7.

R1 booleans

  1. The mesh backend is manifold-csg 0.4.1, with Manifold's parallelism on. Without TBB it takes 3–5× Python's time on the cliff; with TBB the median of interleaved pairs is 1.05–1.06×, close to but not reliably under the 1.2× gate. The old design note "run each boolean single-threaded and many in parallel" is dropped: it is not needed for identity on one machine and it costs 3–4× on the cliff's big unions.
  2. manifold-rust stays behind the trait as the browser backend only. Its wasm build compiles in CI. It is re-measured with r1_replay --backend port when Python moves to Manifold 3.5.4 or the port's parallel speed improves.
  3. Kernel rule: keep solids inside the kernel between operations, and carry tolerance as Python does. manifold-csg's C API cannot pass face IDs or tolerance back in, so a round trip through a plain triangle mesh loses coplanar-face grouping, the material chain R9 relies on, and the float32-scale tolerance Python's solids carry. Revision 7 measured it: no replay mode (tolerance passed in, or set_tolerance after import) reproduces the 16 in-situ volumes that differ; set_tolerance fixes one and re-simplifies a third of the massif's results. Rust gives a solid its tolerance at creation (TriMesh::tolerance) and keeps it in the kernel.
  4. The parity harness compares metrics and renders against a Python replay, never bytes. Even Python against itself, re-importing the same operands, changes the triangles on about 9% of cliff results, and Python's Manifold (3.5.1) is a different version from manifold-csg's (3.5.3).
  5. The cliff's speed must come from outside the booleans. Booleans are about 11% of the rockpile build and 14–20% of the massif. The kill criterion (cliff batch within 2× Python) is met (worst pair 1.43×), but the P7 targets (1×1 in ≤ 15 s, massif in ≤ 60 s) rest on porting the Python and numpy remainder well: adaptive heightfields and parallel stones.

R2 determinism

  1. Kernel rule (revision 7): one source-ID rule for determinism and materials, in dt-geom::source. Reserve a block of original IDs before parallel work and assign them by part index; relabel each leaf (a level set, an import) with its slot and its own face-ID range before recording its materials; give plane cuts an ID from the block (trim_by_plane_with_id); keep materials in a table keyed by (original ID, face ID), with cut faces taking the material of the solid being cut; combine with batch_in_order. Revision 6's rule (as_original() in index order) is dropped: on a part with several sources it collapses their IDs and recomputes face IDs, cutting R9's material link. The rule's test (level-set parts built in parallel, two plane cuts each, one batch union) gives identical hashes at 1, 4 and 16 threads and traces every output triangle to a material, on both backends and all three CI platforms.
  2. Use the libm crate, not std's sin, exp and powf, in seeded code. The platform maths library gave three different hashes on three platforms (Apple's libm and glibc differ too); everything else we wrote matched on all three.
  3. The cross-platform evidence is thin. It is one toy pipeline (24 rocks, about 25,000 triangles). Manifold matched between macOS and Linux on it and differed on Windows, in both the C++ kernel and the port, for a cause not yet found. TBB's thread count was varied only on Linux CI, through CPU affinity (1, 2 and 4 CPUs, same hashes). The P1 hash gate on real tiles is the real test of the level you choose.
  4. The P1 hash gate depends on your level choice (decision 1 below). With the recommended level, golden hashes are shared by the macOS and Linux CI jobs, and Windows checks thread identity plus metrics.

R3 rays

  1. The ray backend is obvhs 0.4 with Embree's triangle test and medium_build; parry3d 0.31 stays behind the same trait as the spare. The embree3 FFI fallback is dropped from the plan: no maintained binding exists, and none is needed.
  2. Route every ray through our Rays wrapper. obvhs 0.4 loses rays whose direction has a -0.0 component (8.6M of the cliff's relief rays). The wrapper adds +0.0 to each component, which fixes it and changes nothing else. No caller uses obvhs directly.
  3. The cliff port does not cast exactly grazing rays. rockpile's relief grid lines up with the mesh it casts at, so about 0.8% of its answers are rounding noise (unstable under a 1e-4 mm nudge in a brute-force re-cast with a port of Embree's test). In Rust, offset that grid off the mesh lattice or use the depth-peel raster; cliff parity is judged on metrics and renders, not ray for ray.
  4. The ray gate's distance clause becomes 1e-3 mm. 1e-4 mm is about 20 float32 ulps at a tree's size and failed on 2 of 3 captures with the same triangle hit; the leaf placer's smallest tolerance is the 0.24 mm leaf thickness.
  5. Leaf placement in Python takes 3.4–4.0 s per scale (revision 7, 2x2-grass-tree, three runs): 7.1–7.7 s of a 17.2–18.3 s tile built at both scales, not the 11.3 s of Embree time the feasibility page quoted, and the tile is not the 25–28 s the plan assumed. The P5 target (≤ 10 s) is now about 1.8× faster than Python, not 2.5×. Batch queries scale 6–7× on 12 threads, so that is where the parallel gain is.

R6 format

  1. dt-doc exists and is the P1 starting point: kdl 6.7.1 (KDL v2, its v1 features off), a hand-written decoder with one function per node type, one control table per look, byte spans on every name and value, and miette errors that keep going past the first mistake. It builds for wasm32-unknown-unknown, which CI now checks.
  2. Two design gaps for later phases. Meadow needs the species controls the tiles set (length, curl, clearance, clumps) or a ruling that they go (P4). cliff along=… must either be limited to tile edges or rockpile's band forms generalised to any line (P7).
  3. Syntax and vocabulary are decided; the code and tiles are not yet reworked. After your read found the parameters unintuitive, you took all ten recommendations of the intuitiveness review (decision 2 below), which revise syntax answers 1 (authors write mm at the DungeonBlocks reference and named levels, not h) and 5 (every written size scales with the system, with printability floors). You then made scatter its own node: a tree or rock is one thing placed with at=, and how many and where belong to scatter (D11 on the R6 page); a thing's at= may be a range, and with none it goes anywhere (D12). dt-doc and the three library tiles are still to be reworked to the new vocabulary; the R6 page already shows the revised examples.
  4. P1 adds to dt-doc: the new vocabulary (renamed looks and controls, words for character controls, edge names and side=, quoted points, the whole tile as default area, mm at the reference with (h) opt-in); insertion in the edit API (it can only replace values today), writing new controls on the line as properties; removal of the "4.5mm" unit-suffix form; the standard heights as an implicit prelude.

R9 materials

  1. Colour rides on a material table keyed by (original ID, face ID): a whole-ID entry for a solid of one material or a cut face, per-face entries co-sampled from the material field for a level set. Revision 6 called this "the fallback is now simply the method"; it is the method, now with the rule that makes it survive parallel builds and plane cuts (R2 above). P3 still retires R9 in production on real rocks and water.
  2. A port bug, worked around. manifold-rust 0.19.2 renumbered the face IDs of a third of the triangles in the test's batch union when sources shared face ID values; per-leaf face-ID ranges fix it. To report upstream, with the obvhs -0.0 bug.

Decisions for you

Each decision has a recommendation. "Blocks" says which phase needs the answer.

1. Determinism level (R2) · blocks P1

The confirmed default 2 is "byte-identical on one machine; visually identical across machines". R2 shows more is possible for free on two of the three platforms. The options:

  • A. One machine only. Golden hashes per platform, each checked only against itself. Cheapest, but three sets of hashes to keep.
  • B. macOS and Linux share byte hashes; Windows is gated on metrics and renders. One golden set, checked on both the Mac and Linux CI jobs. Windows runs the thread-identity test, the golden summaries' metrics (triangle count, volume, overhang, holes) and the render gate, but not the byte hashes.
  • C. All three platforms byte-identical. Would first need the cause of Windows' difference inside Manifold found (both the C++ kernel and the port differ there; its maths library is the likely cause, not yet shown). Not worth it now.
Recommendation: B. It costs nothing beyond the libm rule, catches a whole class of accidental changes on two platforms, and asks nothing of Windows that R2 showed it cannot give. The evidence for B is one toy pipeline; the P1 hash gate on real tiles confirms or withdraws it. You print from the Mac, and CI runs Linux on every push.

The other eight recommendations from the review, all taken:

  • Stack order is free for solids: every solid is planned first and everything else grows round it; order still matters for painting surfaces.
  • Words for character controls (for example rock mixed, crown shape=round), each expanding to documented numbers; numbers are always accepted, and the GUI shows the word and the numbers it chose.
  • The whole tile is the default area; fill-point regions are needed only on divided tiles.
  • Edge names; a wall or cliff on an edge stands inside the tile; drawn lines take side=, defaulting to centre.
  • Points are quoted pairs ("1,1"), plus corner and edge words.
  • Renames: hills → ground, meadow → grass, blocks → boulders, family → style, self → in, near → against.
  • Trees expose only crown branching (open, normal, dense); the six branch knobs stay as expert controls in parts files.
  • ledges comes in P7 as a cliff control; gullies and buttresses stay at the form's values.

3. Reinstall rustup natively on the Mac · decided: reinstalled natively on 1 Nov 2026

Done. rustup was reinstalled natively on 1 Nov 2026 at your request: the rustup and cargo proxies are arm64, the default host is aarch64-apple-darwin and the default toolchain stable-aarch64, and the pinned 1.99.1-aarch64 toolchain is kept. The PATH workaround the spikes used (~/.rustup/toolchains/1.99.1-aarch64-apple-darwin/bin first) is no longer needed, though it is harmless.

History: the proxies were x86_64 binaries running under Rosetta. The compiler they launched was the native arm64 1.99.1, but build scripts inherited the emulated architecture, and in R1 the cmake build of Manifold compiled x86_64 objects into an arm64 binary, which failed to link (R1 report, finding 7).

4. The contact sheet draft · blocks P1's harness work

The Python half of the parity harness exists: python tests/parity/run_lab.py builds dirtlab 2×2 at seeds 1–4 in about 95 s and writes, per seed, the STL, a metrics.json and four renders (painted, top, low, 20 mm close-up) with overhangs painted red, then one contact sheet into out/parity/dirt/ (not in git). Its bare-fraction estimator reads lower than dirtlab's own pebble mask (0.77–0.79 against 0.92–0.93) because it counts tall clods as cover; it is stable across seeds, and both sides will run the same function.

Recommendation: approve the layout (four views per seed, overhangs red, one sheet per look), or say what you want to see side by side. P1 adds the Rust column next to it.

Choices the agents made, for your veto

These follow directly from the measurements. They need no action unless you disagree.
