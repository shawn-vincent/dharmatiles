Phase 0 · R6 follow-up · adversarial read · author vocabulary

Could a gamer write these tiles?

I read the three R6 tiles cold, as a tech-savvy tabletop player who has printed terrain and poked at Blender, before reading any explanation. The shape of the language works: a short file, a stack of named looks, reusable parts. The words do not. Most of them come from inside the generators, and the numbers come in five different units that the page never shows.

Top findings.

  1. Heights are written in h-decimals nobody can compute. foot=0.121 and height 1.057 stand for 4 mm and 35 mm. The tool’s own error message has to do the division for the author. Write mm and named levels; keep h inside the engine.
  2. “height” means four different things in three tiles: a named level, a size in mm, h above the foot, and (as top) h above the tile base.
  3. Stack order secretly decides what avoids what, and not consistently: grass must come after the tree, yet dirt listed before the cliff banks against it.
  4. The vocabulary is the generator’s, not the modeller’s: self, tips, attractors, mass, form=run, bay, reveal, roundover, "1..6^2".
  5. The sizes that decide whether a tile works on the table are invisible: how tall the tree is, how far the cliff reaches into the square, how bumpy the ground is.

Trying the obvious next tiles

With only the three files as examples, I tried to write three new tiles.

  • A straight wall along the south edge. Doable by copying: line s { through 0 0 1 0 }. But I had to work out that walking west to east puts the wall on the left, which is north, which is inside. I got it backwards the first time.
  • A 1×2 grass tile with a rock cluster and a path. Not doable. There is no rocks example, no way shown to give a line a width, and no example of a region that is “everything except the path”. I can’t place the cluster at a spot either: the only placement shown is per-square.
  • A taller cliff with a ledge. I can change height 1.057 to … something. 50 mm is 1.511 h. There is no visible way to ask for a ledge, though the generator builds tiers inside the run form.

Problems, ranked

  1. highHeights in h decimals, and five unit systems.

    Squares (positions, wall thickness), h (levels, cliff and tree heights), mm (details), per-square (densities, sometimes per square of run), and shares that are sometimes of the whole ("38%") and sometimes of a stone’s own size (sink). None is written. 0.121, 1.057 and thickness 0.2 are numbers the author had to compute from mm they already knew.

    Fix: one rule. Positions are in squares; every size is mm; levels have names. h stays as the engine’s vertical scale and as an opt-in (h) annotation. See Heights.

  2. high“height” has four meanings.

    dirt height=foot (where a surface sits), bank height="1.5..3.5" (how tall a thing is, mm), cliff height 1.057 (h above its foot), wall top wall (h above the datum, with height also accepted and meaning “above what it stands on”).

    Fix: two words. level is where a surface sits (a named level, or mm above the tile base, or "ground - 1"). height is how tall a thing stands above whatever it stands on, always mm, or full/low for walls.

  3. highStack order silently controls obstacle avoidance.

    The grass tile says the blades must come after the tree; the wall tile says the same for grass. But the cliff tile lists dirt before the cliff and the dirt still banks against it, because builds see every plan. An author who orders things “wrong” gets grass growing through a trunk, with no error.

    Fix: make it true everywhere: every solid thing (rocks, trees, walls, cliffs, boulders) is planned before anything grows, and everything grows round every solid regardless of order. The stack order then only means what an author already expects: which surface paints over which. If a case needs the opposite, it gets an explicit control (grass … over=rocks), not a position.

  4. highGenerator words instead of modelling words.

    self, tips, attractors, mass, form=run, shatter zones, family, course/bay/reveal/roundover, clearance, sink, kinds, "1..6^2". The schema still carries eagerness, target, balance, exponent, min-radius, swing, meander.

    Fix: the vocabulary below. Hobby words where they exist (tufts, clump foliage, coping, battlements, scree, boulders), result words otherwise (how tall, how wide, how deep the gaps look). Algorithm knobs move inside the look or become derived.

  5. highThe sizes that matter on the table are invisible.

    The grass-tree tile never says how big its tree is (40 mm tall, 30 mm wide). The cliff never says how far it reaches into the square (8–20 mm, inside form=run). The ground never says how bumpy it is. These decide whether a miniature can stand on the tile.

    Fix: every look leads with its size controls, and library tiles write them out. Add dharmatiles explain tile.kdl, which prints the tile with every default filled in as a comment, and have the GUI show the same.

  6. mediumWhole-tile regions are boilerplate, and fill points are magic numbers.

    Two of three tiles declare region meadow { fill … } just to mean “the whole tile”, and every entry repeats self=meadow. fill 0.5 0.5 reads as the tile centre but is the centre of the first square; fill 0.5 0.35 is a leftover from Python.

    Fix: the default area is the whole tile. Regions (Shawn’s lines-plus-fill-points model, unchanged) appear only when lines divide the tile, and are named with in=. Points are written as one quoted pair, "0.5,0", so they can’t be mis-paired.

  7. mediumThe left-of-the-walk rule.

    Walls and cliffs stand on the left of their line as you walk it. Correct by construction, but an author has to simulate it, and reversing the points puts the wall off the tile.

    Fix: built-in edge lines north, south, east, west, and paths of edges such as along="north west". On a tile edge a wall or cliff always stands inside, outer face flush. A drawn line takes an explicit side=left|right|centre, default centre.

  8. mediumThe same thing has different names; different things share one.

    Plain ground is hills in one tile and dirt in another. Grass is lawn, long-grass and meadow, and meadow is also the region. stones is a size group (wall), a mix (cliff) and a scatter (scree). “Where” is said with self= (region), along= (line) and near= (tag).

    Fix: one noun per thing (ground, dirt, grass, rocks, tree, wall, cliff, scree) and four place words with one meaning each: in= a region, along= a line or edge, at= a point, against= a kind of thing.

  9. mediumCounts and mixes are indirect.

    per-square=0.25 to mean one tree. Scree per-square is per square of cliff foot, not of tile. Mix shares look like they must sum to 100%.

    Fix: count for a few things, per-square for many, always per square of the area the entry covers (for things along a line, per square of that line’s length, said once in the docs). Mixes are relative weights, normalised, written as plain numbers; most authors use a named mix (rock mixed) instead.

  10. mediumThe skew syntax reads as arithmetic.

    "1..6^2" looks like 1 to 36. It means “1 to 6, mostly small”.

    Fix: size="1..6" mostly=small (small, even, large). Keep ^k as an expert form that the GUI never writes.

  11. lowBooleans and debug switches in tile files.

    #true is correct KDL v2 but reads as a typo to newcomers, and /- debug attractors=#true is a developer tool.

    Fix: prefer choices that name the result: top dirt / top bare, leaves on / clumps-only / bare. Move debug to the command line: dharmatiles build --show attractors. KDL v2 stays.

  12. lowuse hides what it brings in.

    Fix: name the imports: use "parts/grass" lawn long-grass. Unnamed use could still bring in everything.

  13. lowSmall inconsistencies.

    cols=2 rows=2 where file names and players say “2×2” (size="2x2", columns first, both forms accepted). about in one tile, comments in two. A second seed on the wall without a word on why (it pins that wall’s stones while the tile seed varies everything else; worth one line in the docs).

Should an author ever see h?

Shawn’s reasons for h, from the design conversation: one vertical scale (the tall wall is 1.0, and a single mm figure says what 1.0 means), standard heights as fractions of it, and variability measured on the same scale (“normal variability would be like 0.2”). Those are engine properties. None of them needs the author to type fractions of a wall.

Recommendation: authors write millimetres, measured at the DungeonBlocks reference (35 mm square, 33.1 mm wall top), and named levels. The engine keeps h: named levels are defined once in h in the system file, and when a tile is built at another scale every size the author wrote scales with it, like a photocopy. The rule for the author is one sentence: write sizes for a 35 mm square; other systems scale them for you. Printability floors (blade and leaf thickness, minimum branch radius, gaps) are hidden and never scale below what prints.

This keeps both settled decisions (“wall thickness and tree size scale with the square” becomes “all visible sizes scale”) and drops one (“authors write h”). It is a strong enough reason to reopen it: the R6 error message already converts mm to h for the author (“35 mm is about 1.057”), which is the tool admitting the author thinks in mm. (h)1.2 stays legal for anyone who wants wall-relative numbers.

Escape hatches stay small: level="ground - 1" or level="wall-top / 2" (the R6 expression parser already does this), and height from=10 to=40 for a ramp.

Proposed vocabulary, per look

Each look gets a handful of author controls named for what you see. Sizes are mm at the reference scale, positions are squares, angles are degrees, densities are per square. Words in a choice expand to the generator’s numbers; a number is always accepted too. Reference vocabulary: hobby terrain (static grass, tufts, clump foliage, scree, boulders), dry-stone walling (courses, coping, cope stones on edge), Blender’s Distribute Points and Noise nodes (density, seed, scale, roughness), and game terrain editors (brush size, falloff, height).

The three tiles, rewritten

All proposed files parse as KDL v2 with the same kdl 6.7.1 crate dt-doc uses and print back byte for byte. They use the proposed vocabulary, so dt-doc’s current schema would reject them.

2x2-grass-tree

  • No region and no self=: the whole tile is the default.
  • The tree’s size is on the page, in mm. count=1 instead of per-square=0.25.
  • tips=32 becomes crown clumps=32; foliage=#true is the default and goes; the debug node moves to the command line.
  • The order comment goes: grass avoids the trunk wherever it is listed.

1x1-wall-fieldstone-corner

  • along="north west" replaces a six-number line and the left-of-walk rule.
  • height full and thickness 7 instead of top wall and 0.2 squares.
  • Five masonry words become three groups a modeller can picture: stone sizes, joints, edges.

2x1-cliff-run

  • levels foot=4 and height=35 instead of 0.121 and 1.057.
  • form=run is implied by one edge. mass 1.35 becomes stone-size massive; the five-way share becomes rock mixed.
  • Grit moves into the dirt as pebbles, where it lies. Scree kinds follow the cliff’s rock.
  • "1..6^2" becomes size="1..6" mostly=small.

parts/grass

The look inside is grass, so it no longer shares a name with regions called meadow. clearance is hidden.

A new tile, from scratch

The tile the current examples couldn’t produce: a 1×2 grass strip with a winding dirt path and a boulder cluster. Fourteen lines of content, and every number is something an author would measure with a ruler or read off the grid.

What it needs that R6 doesn’t have: a width and wobble on lines (Shawn’s boundary with a width, and his controllable organic randomness), bounded-by in a region, the rocks look with at and spread, and the level expression. All are in the design conversation or the plan; none needs a new concept.

The other two next tiles

ledges needs P7 to expose rockpile’s tier height (TIER_Z) and full-height share (FULL_P) as a control; the tiers already exist inside the run form.

Open questions for Shawn

  1. Do authors write h at all? Recommendation: no. mm at the DungeonBlocks reference plus named levels, with h kept as the engine scale and as an opt-in (h). This reverses “authors write h” but keeps “one vertical scale, levels as fractions of the wall” inside the engine.
  2. Does every size the author writes scale with the system? Recommendation: yes, like a photocopy, including stones, leaves and blades, with hidden printability floors. Today only thickness and tree size scale and stones don’t, so the stones-per-wall-thickness ratio changes on OpenLOCK and nobody can see why.
  3. Is stack order free for solids? Recommendation: yes. Plan every solid first, grow everything round them, keep order for painting surfaces. R6’s cliff tile already depends on this.
  4. Words or numbers for character controls (rock mixed, stone-size massive, flow wild, crown shape=round)? Recommendation: words first, each expanding to documented numbers; numbers always accepted; the GUI shows the word and the numbers it chose.
  5. Whole tile as the default area? Recommendation: yes. Your fill-point region model stays exactly as designed for divided tiles; it just stops being required for undivided ones.
  6. Edge names and “walls on an edge stand inside”? Recommendation: yes. Drawn lines keep the left-of-walk rule but must say side=, defaulting to centre.
  7. Point syntax. through 1 1 0 1 0 0 or through "1,1" "0,1" "0,0"? Recommendation: the quoted pair, plus corner and edge words.
  8. Look renames: hills → ground, meadow → grass, blocks → boulders, family → style, self → in, near → against. Recommendation: take them all; the GUI and MCP inherit the names, so this is the cheapest moment.
  9. How much of the tree’s branching to expose? Recommendation: crown branching=open|normal|dense only. The six branch knobs stay reachable as expert controls in parts files, and out of tiles and the GUI’s first view.
  10. Cliff ledges, gullies, buttresses as controls? Recommendation: expose ledges in P7 (a ledge is somewhere to stand a mini, which is what a cliff tile is for on the table); leave gullies and buttresses at the form’s values until a tile needs them.
