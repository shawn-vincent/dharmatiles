Few mechanisms, many outcomes

The library and the seven labs contain about seventy separately written mechanisms. In the engine they reduce to five kinds of value, ten operations and three contracts. Each approved look becomes one built-in that combines a handful of these. Tile documents name the built-ins; they don't call the operations directly.

Revised. The first draft claimed seven verbs (scatter, trace, build, relieve, seat, fill, pack) and that every approved surface was a short composition of them "with no special mechanism". Walking each lab through them disproved that. relieve merged face relief (a depth raster subtracted from the stone) with grain (refine and displace). pack merged the cliff's gravity stack with wall courses, which have no gravity at all. Stroke blades are a stack of 2D layers, not a sweep. The set below is the corrected one. See the change log.

Which operations each look uses

Each approved look uses between one and ten primitives. The composition happens inside the look's built-in, in Rust, together with the look's own tuned constants (the meadow has fifteen rim-steering constants; the cliff has dozens of guard and seat rules). That is why the first draft's claim that "no row needs a special mechanism" was wrong: the primitives are shared, but each look is still its own solver.

Mechanisms that looked the same but are not

Each card is a merge the first draft made on paper that the code does not support, and what replaced it.

Stroke blades are layers, not a sweep

Each 0.2 mm layer is its own 2D stroke over a different span of the spine, a whole number of 0.45 mm lines wide. The layers are lofted through 2D distance fields, then sheared onto the hills (grasslab.py:812–911). A sweep moves one profile along a curve. Now: loft with a layer-stack mode, plus drape.

Gravity stack and wall courses

The cliff stack picks the lowest unfilled cell, grows a footprint, fits a bed and may merge crumbs into earlier stones (rockpile.py:554–782). Wall courses are a deterministic split of a known rectangle with no gravity (masonry.py:286–365). Fieldstone draws cracks first. Now: partition with three methods.

Face relief and grain

Cliff face relief ray-casts each stone into a depth grid, clamps it, lifts it and subtracts it, one-sided and crisp (rockpile.py:1255–1332). Grain refines the mesh and pushes vertices along normals (rocklab.py:283). Dirt relief is plain grid arithmetic before lift. Now: relief, grain, and field operations.

Seat has several bed rules

Meadow rocks use a 35th-percentile bed and a plinth. Cliff stones get an underside of cones at the overhang limit. Scree sits at the lowest ground minus a sink. rocklab uses a fixed sink. Now: one seat with a named bed rule.

Swell is a solver, not relief

Geodesic distance through water, a per-kind band table, coherence growth, a wake that couples rock to land, enclosure, corner and far sources, then global normalisation (waterlab.py:719–944). Only the last step displaces a surface. Now: a native water built-in that uses field operations and fill.

Space colonisation returns a graph

Attractor-ownership growth with a whole-graph radius pass afterwards (skeleton.py:529). The tubes need C1 tangents at forks and bark lines threaded across edges. Now: trace returns an attributed graph and loft can follow a graph.
