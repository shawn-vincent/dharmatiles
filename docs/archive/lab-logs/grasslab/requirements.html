1. What this is

A standalone experiment, kept apart from the dharmatiles tile pipeline, that writes one STL: a standard DungeonBlocks tile of gently hilly ground covered in sculpted grass, with a few bare-earth clearings.

Build the illusion, not botany. At tabletop distance grass reads because of its mid-scale organisation: combed ribbons that flow, meander and part around obstacles, made of blades with lit crests and dark undercut tips. Fine detail only decorates that structure.

1.1 Visual scales

Scale Size What it does
Flow / ribbon strands 15–30 mm long, 2–3 mm apart; meanders ≈ 15 mm wavelength Combed ribbons of blades following one smooth direction field; the "flowing grass" read.
Swath cells ≈ 11 mm apart A height wave only: low upstream and at the sides, cresting downstream, so ribbons rise and lap over each other.
Blade 2.8–7.2 mm long, 0.9–1.8 mm wide, 1.5–2.0 mm tall Separate pointed blades standing on the dirt, shingled along each strand; each tip overhangs open air.
Gap the dirt between blades, the air under each tip Dark ground and shadows, where the base coat survives a drybrush.
Leaf point < 1 mm Paint and lighting, not geometry.

1.2 Geometry is defined in the printer's units

(Shawn, 2026-10-29: "make it more honest about the shape, so that we can refine THAT rather than a theoretical ideal.") A blade is a stack of 0.2 mm layers, each a stroke a whole number of 0.45 mm lines wide; what is modelled is what an FDM slicer draws. For resin the same shape is smoothed (the layer steps melted); its outline, keel and points are kept. Undercuts are part of every blade: its upper layers reach further toward the tip than the keel below.

1.3 Scope

In scope: a standard DB tile (35 mm squares, the existing socket-peg base, the official grass height band); independent hills; the grass; bare-earth clearings; printability by construction plus an audit. Out of scope: OpenLOCK, colour, seamless tiling, .tile.py integration (rocks live in rocklab/, the combination in meadowlab/). The project lives in grasslab/, imports dharmatiles primitives freely, never modifies src/dharmatiles/, and writes to grasslab/out/.

2. Requirements

Each requirement is checkable from a viewer or render ([R]) or from a script or statistic ([S]).

2.1 Output and mesh

  • REQ-OUT-1 [S] A run writes one binary STL, grasslab/out/<cols>x<rows>-sculpted-grass[-flat]-s<seed>.stl.
  • REQ-OUT-2 [S] The mesh is one closed manifold body: watertight, consistent normals, no self-intersections, no internal voids (voids sealed under overlapping blades are filled). Crumbs are dropped and reported.
  • REQ-OUT-3 [S] Standard DB socket-peg base (dharmatiles.bases.dungeonblocks). The mean grass-top height lies in the official DB grass band, 15.3–16.8 mm above the piece bottom (4.4–5.9 mm above the terrain datum; commit 6eb0a55).
  • REQ-OUT-4 [S] Footprint cols × rows DB squares, default 2 × 2.
  • REQ-OUT-5 [S] Deterministic: the same seed and parameters give a byte-identical STL.
  • REQ-OUT-6 [S] Resin resolution: blade surfaces within ≈ 20 µm of their smooth definition. The 2 × 2 STL stays under 100 MB and the build under 2 minutes.

2.2 Blade construction (print-native, then smoothed)

  • REQ-B-1 [S] Whole layers and lines. Before smoothing, every blade is a stack of LAYER_MM (0.2) layers, each a stroke whose width is a whole number of LINE_W_MM (0.45) lines; nothing narrower than one line. Opening each layer by just under half a line keeps ≥ 99% of it.
  • REQ-B-2 [R] Cross-section. A keel at the bottom (≥ 2 lines), widest in the middle, a single-line ridge on top. Width grows ≤ 1 line per layer going up (≈ 45°).
  • REQ-B-3 [R] Profile. A convex arch: the blade rises from the ground at its root and levels out toward the tip; no straight ramp, no pad.
  • REQ-B-4 [R] Raked bow. Only the top layer reaches the tip; each layer below ends RAKE_MM (0.2) shorter, so the tip overhangs open air to the last layer.
  • REQ-B-5 [R] Points. Every horizontal section of a blade ends in a point at both ends (strokes step down a line at a time to one line's round end). No blunt front face.
  • REQ-B-6 [R] Smooth. The STL blade is the stroke stack smoothed: no layer steps or ribs, outline, keel and points kept.
  • REQ-B-7 [S] Every blade is fused to the ground (embedded 0.3 mm); no islands, no hinges. Grass stays ≤ 3.5 mm above the local ground.
  • REQ-B-8 [S] An audit (overhang area, crumbs) runs on every output; blades_demo.py writes single blades, smoothed or as the raw stroke stack, for inspection.

2.3 Hills

  • REQ-HILL-1 The ground H₀ is generated independently of the grass and reads as rolling ground with the grass removed.
  • REQ-HILL-2 [R] Broad and irregular: landforms 15–40 mm across, no grid, no repeated bumps, no high-frequency noise.
  • REQ-HILL-3 [S] Rises only. (Shawn.) The perimeter level is the lowest ground on the tile: hills rise from flat plains at edge height, about 40% of the interior stays plain, nothing dips below the edge.
  • REQ-HILL-4 [S] At least 60% of the area has slope ≤ 12°; no slope exceeds 34°.
  • REQ-HILL-5 [S] The ground is feathered to a constant edge height.
  • REQ-HILL-6 The grass seats on H₀ and follows its curvature.

2.4 Flow (the defining feature)

  • REQ-FL-1 [R] Fronds follow the flow. Every frond's spine is a streamline of one direction field, with no per-frond bend. Neighbours stay parallel along their length; curvature belongs to the flow.
  • REQ-FL-2 [R] Meandering, varied flow. Gentle S-curves (wavelength about 15 mm, ±10–30°) that are coherent across the flow (neighbouring streamlines wave in phase, like combed hair), with phase and amplitude drifting so waves are never regular. Several flow regions, not one sweep: a 2 × 2 tile shows at least three dominant directions.
  • REQ-FL-3 [R] Ribbons. Fronds gather into strands about 2–3 mm apart, each 15–30 mm long, made of fronds shingled along one streamline. Dark seams run between strands. Strands cross only at shallow angles; a few strays (about 5%) cross more steeply.
  • REQ-FL-4 [R] Flow wraps around bare soil. The field bends tangentially around each clearing: grass parts at the upstream end and rejoins downstream, like water around a stone.
  • REQ-FL-5 [R] No crowns or vortices. The field is singularity-free except the deliberate partings and rejoins at clearings: no starbursts or spirals at any scale.
  • REQ-FL-6 [R] Banks read as piles of blades. (Shawn: "a big pile of grass leans up right against [a clearing]… undercuts at the bottom of the grass blade so there's clear ridges down the side.")
    • Where grass faces a clearing, the last blades stand separately on the dirt, each with its own keel, raked bow and undercut tip (REQ-B-*), so the bank is a pile of blades with shadows between them, never a smooth wall (F25).
    • Bank grass varies: fronds wander in heading, height and spill, some cross at an angle, and how strongly the flow hugs each clearing varies, so the bank line meanders.

2.5 Swaths

  • REQ-SW-1 [S] Total coverage. Every point belongs to exactly one swath cell. Swaths group strands and lengthen fronds toward each cell's downstream end; they no longer carry a height wave (there is no turf mass to carry it).

2.6 Blades in the field

  • REQ-FR-1 [R] Blades are elongated along the flow, 2.8–7.2 mm, longer toward each swath's downstream end.
  • REQ-FR-2 [R] Shingling. Each tip lies over or beside the next blade's base downstream; overlap always reads in that direction.
  • REQ-FR-3 [R] Blades on dirt. (Shawn, 2026-10-29: "no turf mass.") Every bit of grass is a blade; the dirt shows between them. No pad, skirt or filler under or between blades.
  • REQ-FR-5 [R] Variation without repetition. Length, width (in lines), height and small heading offsets vary; no two neighbours are identical copies (F7).

2.7 Edges: grass ends as grass

From Shawn's review of v1e: "the grass is just being uniformly chopped down like it was all made of clay."

  • REQ-EDGE-1 The grass is made of blades. No grass height exists except as part of some blade: no free-standing envelope or mass layer, no mask or scale applied to the grass as a whole.
  • REQ-EDGE-2 [R] Soil banks are the outlines of the last fronds (tips and sides at varied heights, dark gaps between, lapping slightly onto the soil), never carved walls or a hole in a volume (F19).
  • REQ-EDGE-3 [R] Tile rim. Fronds are shortened (never sliced) so tips stop just inside the wall; fronds rooted at the rim keep their rounded bases; no bevel or height ramp toward the edge.
  • REQ-EDGE-4 [R] Swath borders inside the grass are overlapping frond lobes, never steps.
  • REQ-EDGE-5 [R] Fronds cut short are also lower, so a fringe steps down instead of ending in tall stubs.
  • REQ-EDGE-7 [R] Grass flows to the rim, not away from it. Near the edge, the flow points at or runs alongside the wall, never away (F21). The steering fades in over 9–15 mm and blends into the interior with no seam (F26); flow already heading outward leans further out near the rim; long stretches of rim turn the same way (one side per stretch, corners continuing the adjacent edge), so the flow does not part along the wall; where two stretches meet, the flow comes square to the wall and ends there: a short parting, never a long feathered V or a starburst; at the very rim nothing heads inward past parallel.
  • REQ-EDGE-8 [R] Irregular rim endings. Tip insets and steering strength vary along the rim; never a ruled line of tips (F23).

2.8 Bare soil

  • REQ-SOIL-1 [R] Lens clearings. Bare patches are eye-shaped openings elongated along the local flow, with slightly irregular edges, preferring low ground; never puddles or amoebas (F16).
  • REQ-SOIL-2 [S] A few. About 1.3–1.8 clearings per square, at least 13 mm apart and 7 mm from the rim, covering about 10% of the tile. Grass stays dominant.
  • REQ-SOIL-3 [R] Banks, not ramps. The soil mask decides only where fronds may root; it never cuts the grass. Each frond laps 0.8–3.2 mm into the soil (skewed short); about 4% are wisps reaching 4 mm.
  • REQ-SOIL-4 [R] Dirt everywhere exposed. Bare patches and the ground between blades carry the shared dirt texture (dirtlab.dirt_relief). Patches sit about 0.4 mm below the surrounding ground with no outlining step. A few sprigs grow in the soil.

2.9 Surface quality

  • REQ-GR-1 [R] Dark gaps between neighbouring blades and air under every tip.
  • REQ-GR-2 [R] No voxel terracing, layer steps or ribs on any blade (REQ-B-6).

2.10 Parameters and diagnostics

  • REQ-PAR-1 Knobs are named, bounded module constants grouped by stage, with a seed.
  • REQ-DIAG-1 [S] Each run prints mesh, hills, soil, grass-height, DB band, overhang audit and timing statistics.
  • REQ-DIAG-2 Before any STL is handed over, Claude checks it with quick monochrome renders (--check), as an internal filter only. Shawn's eye in a viewer is the judge.

7. Lessons from previous attempts

7.1 dharmatiles grass, Eras 0–8

Recorded in docs/meta/history/2026-07-03-grass-greenfield-requirements.md and docs/design/grass-mound-thatch.html. Recurring needs: proud-of-lower layering (reinvented five times), coherent but varied direction, designed (not emergent) depth. Recurring failures: height blowup, Z-jitter, bare borders, a flat mat, indecision churn. Here each need is a by-construction guarantee: shingling (REQ-FR-2), a singularity-free field (REQ-FL-5), total coverage (REQ-SW-1), the height budget on the inputs (REQ-FDM-7).

7.2 grasslab v0: why it failed (crumpled foil, F9)

v0 built ~1,800 small lens blades in random order inside a binary voxel volume. It was printable and watertight, and read as crumpled foil.

  • Built bottom-up: no mid-scale structure was required, so none appeared. → Specify top-down, by visual scale.
  • Undercuts appeared incidentally on every blade side, and the 45° filter chamfered them into grit. → Design undercuts; only at lips.
  • Random stacking order gave Z-jitter; binary voxels plus blur gave terracing; clipped blades left a ragged rim.
  • Process: the 2 × 2 tile was built before S1 was looked at, and the STL was handed over unseen. → S1 first; self-check renders always.

7.3 Retired requirements and rejected approaches (keep the lessons)

Retired / rejected Why Replaced by
Swaths as the defining feature, fronds fanned ±12° off each swath axis (rev 2) Read as clumps once compared with lodged grass; the flow, not the cell, should carry direction Streamline fronds in strands (REQ-FL-1/3); swaths kept as a height wave only
Coherent sickle curvature field (v1j–v1k, REQ-FR-7) Each frond bent on its own: "clumps of commas" (F24) Curvature belongs to the flow's in-phase meanders (REQ-FL-2)
Per-frond rim steering (v1i–v1j) Per-point side flips made a starburst (+1 source) Steering applied to the field as a smoothed scalar rotation (REQ-EDGE-7)
Free-standing swath envelope with rim height ease and soil cut (v1–v1e) Grass "chopped like clay": rim bevel, holes, border steps (F19) Grass made of fronds; boundaries are frond outlines (REQ-EDGE-1…5)
Soft ends on every frond (v1h-a/b) Whole tile turned into fish scales (F7) Soft ends for soil-stopped bank fronds only (REQ-EDGE-6)
Noise-warped ellipse puddles with ramp and trench rim (v1c/d) "Terrible": puddles, bubbles (F16/F17) Seam lanes (v1e), then lens clearings (v2a)

  • REQ-FDM-1…8 (rev 3) → REQ-B-1…8 (rev 4). The voxel FDM rules (45° downward faces, wedges not slots, ≥ 0.6 mm free features, a voxel backstop) described a height field with carved lips. They never gave a visible undercut, and the slicer still dropped everything narrower than a line. Replaced by defining blades in layers and lines.
