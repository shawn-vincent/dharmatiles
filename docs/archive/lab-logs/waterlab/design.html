1. Scene construction (shared by every option)

  • Grid: 0.1 mm cells over 70 × 70 mm. The final surface is z(x, y) = max(land, water) on that grid.
  • Land: the shoreline position along the left side is x_s(y) = 17 mm + 4 mm·n(y), with n smooth noise. For signed distance s from the shoreline (positive into land): land = (WL − 1.0) + (LAND_TOP − WL + 1.0)·smoothstep(s / BANK_W), with BANK_W = 7 mm, plus a little broad noise. So the bank enters the water as a slope and the shoreline emerges where land crosses the water surface.
  • Rock: rocklab.make_rock, one stone about 15 mm across, seated with its base 1.4 mm below the water level. Its footprint at the water level (Manifold.slice) is rasterised as an obstacle.
  • Obstacles: O = land above the water level, or the rock footprint.
  • Distance: d = EDT from O (mm), lightly smoothed (σ 1 mm) so iso-lines don't trace every facet or bump. The nearest obstacle point comes from the EDT indices and tells whether a cell is nearer the rock or the shore.
  • Rim: relief × smoothstep(dist_to_rim / 4 mm), so the water meets the tile edge at the calm level (REQ-W-9).
  • Solid: dharmatiles.core.mesh.make_heightmap_solid (top = z, bottom = 0) ∪ rock ∪ DB base, with stl_safe-style reload verification.

2. Water generators

All return relief r(x, y) in mm, added to WL in water cells.

2.1 legacy (dharmatiles port)

  • Noise: white noise smoothed at σ 3 mm, normalised to RMS A_n.
  • Ripples: N ≈ obstacle perimeter / 6 mm segments.
    • Each is a sine of wavelength 3 mm on the smoothed distance field, within one wavelength band after a random offset of 0–20 mm, with amplitude falling linearly with the offset.
    • Each has a flat-top length of 10–20 mm and a Hann-faded tail of 15 mm, measured along the shore via the smoothed nearest-source coordinates.
  • This mirrors make_water_displacement + make_water_ripple_displacement in spirit and parameters. Amplitudes are reduced to fit REQ-W-3: noise RMS 0.25 mm, ripples 0.5 mm.

2.2 lapping (contour wavelets + quiet swell)

  • Phase: the distance d is first warped by d_w = d + 0.7 mm·n₁ (smooth noise at σ 3 mm), so crest lines wobble rather than trace perfect offsets (W2). Spacing widens outward: λ(d) = λ₀·(1 + g·d), so φ = ln(1 + g·d_w)/(g·λ₀).
  • Crest profile: c(φ) = ((1 + cos 2πφ)/2)^p with p ≈ 1.8. That gives narrower rounded crests and broad smooth troughs (REQ-W-8), not a symmetric sine (W3/W5).
  • Breaks: the gate is computed per crest index k = round(φ). Crests fade in and out along their length via smooth noise sampled at (nearest obstacle point + k·offset), so each crest breaks in a different place (no targets, W2). The gate is smoothstepped to give clear gaps.
  • Envelope: A(d) = A₀·exp(−d/decay), with rock A₀ and decay larger than the shore's. The two are blended by nearest-obstacle type. Because it is one distance field to the union of obstacles, rock and shore crests merge smoothly where they meet (REQ-W-7).
  • Contact lip: a raised lip of 0.25 mm at d ≈ 0.6 mm (the meniscus), where the water climbs the obstacle.
  • Open water: a quiet swell, isotropic band-limited noise (σ 5 mm, RMS 0.15 mm), × (1 − 0.7·near), where near is the normalised wavelet envelope.

2.3 crescents (lapping + open-water crescents)

Lapping near obstacles, plus sparse finite crescent crests in open water:

  • Placement: Poisson-disc centres 9 mm apart, at least 6 mm from obstacles.
  • Shape: each crescent is an arc of radius 4–9 mm and arc length 5–11 mm at a random orientation, with a rounded cross-section (half width 0.7 mm) and tapered ends.
  • Height: 0.25–0.45 mm, faded near obstacles so they don't collide with the lapping crests.
  • Combination: a soft maximum of the crescents, so overlaps blend instead of spiking.

2.4 strokes (wind-combed sculpted strokes)

  • Crest-direction field θ: an unoriented field, blended in doubled-angle space.
    • Near obstacles it is tangent to the iso-distance lines, with weight exp(−d/7 mm), so strokes wrap the rock and run along the shore.
    • In open water it is a smooth meandering field (σ 12 mm, ±70°): locally coherent, not globally aligned.
  • Strokes: Poisson seeds 4.6 mm apart. Each traces a centreline along θ in both directions.
    • Length is 9–18 mm near obstacles and 7–14 mm far away.
    • The cross-section is rounded, (1 − (r/hw)²)^1.3, with half-width 1.0–1.1 mm and tapered ends.
    • Height is 0.5–0.85 mm near obstacles and 0.15–0.35 mm far away.
  • Merging: overlaps use a smooth union, applied only where two strokes overlap. A blanket softmax lifted every patch by k·ln 2 and drew visible box edges (v2 bug).
  • Contact lip plus a weak swell.

2.5 hybrid (lapping contact crests + strokes)

Lapping owns d < 6 mm, and strokes fade in beyond it.

5. Round 2 (2026-10-28): combed / lapped / rings + shared tide

Shawn's feedback on v3: bigger relief is good. Strokes and hybrid both have good features. Two changes requested:

  • Stroke ends: these should fade gradually and organically, so they look like ripples, not worms.
  • A larger-scale motion under the ripples: like dharmatiles' shared sinusoidal edge profile.

The options were renamed, and the earlier ones were retired from the sheet: strokes → combed, hybrid → lapped, lapping → rings. crescents was dropped.

  • Tide (shared by every option, including legacy):
    • Interior band-limited noise at σ 9 mm, RMS 0.35 mm.
    • Within 9 mm of the rim, it is forced to dharmatiles' shared edge profile: 0.6·sin(2π(t − (17.5 − λ/4))/λ) with λ = 17.5 mm, the same phase convention as _force_tile_edge_water_profile, and inverse-distance weighted at corners. Any two water tiles therefore meet with the same dip-and-rise.
    • Ripples still fade to 0 within 4 mm of the rim, so the rim is exactly the profile.
  • Direction field: an oriented regional field. The component normal to obstacles is removed with weight exp(−d/7 mm), so it runs tangent to the rock and the shore. This replaces the doubled-angle near/far blend, which radiated out of the rock in v3 "hybrid".
  • Ripple strokes:
    • Gaussian crest exp(−2.2 q²), with no hard edge.
    • Shallow troughs either side (−0.28 h, centred at q ≈ 1.55).
    • Height bell sin(πs)^1.3 along the length; width tapers 35% → 100% → 35%.
    • Height varies ±20% along the stroke.
    • Poisson spacing 5.6 mm. Lengths are 10–20 mm near obstacles and 7–15 mm far away; heights are 0.75–1.15 mm near and 0.3–0.6 mm far.
  • lapped: rings own d < 8 mm, with break bias 0.45; strokes beyond.
  • rings: lapping with 1.2 mm wobble, amplitude 1.0 mm (rock) and 0.9 mm (shore).
  • Per-option RNG is keyed by option name (crc32), so the sheet order doesn't change the results.
  • Contact sheet: waterlab/contact_sheet.py writes out/YYYY-MM-DD-HHMMSS-contact-sheet-s<seed>.png. Rows are ranked best → worst, legacy is last, and every image carries a caption.

6. Round 3 (2026-10-28): physics-first ("real water, exaggerated")

Shawn's feedback on round 2:

  • rings and legacy are still liked.
  • The strokes are too short and too uniform. The longest should be a significant share of the tile, and ripples near obstacles should be a different size from ripples further out.
  • Scales are unrelated. Big swells and short uniform ripples, with nothing between.

Diagnosis: real water is a continuous spectrum. Height scales with wavelength, and obstacles don't stamp rings. Incoming waves shoal (shorter and taller in the shallows), reflect and diffract, so the near-obstacle size difference and the related scales emerge from one process. Fantasy water is that physics, exaggerated: sharper crests, louder near obstacles.

  • _wave_sim: a 2D damped linear wave equation on a 0.25 mm grid, 1400 steps.
    • Speed c = 0.45 cells/step × √(depth / 1 mm), clamped at √0.35. Clamping lower made grid-scale spikes at the bank.
    • Rock and land are fixed (reflecting) boundaries.
    • Waves are injected in a 5 mm band at the three water edges as a sum of 6 wavelengths (2.5–14 mm, log-uniform) × 3 sub-directions. Amplitude ∝ λ^0.7.
    • Directions are uniform (sim) or within ±30° of one wind (sim-wind).
    • The snapshot is zoomed to 0.1 mm and normalised.
    • v1 failure: forcing by in-place oscillating noise patterns, which pulsed blobs instead of propagating waves.
  • _sharpen: u + k(u² − 1)/2 on tanh-tamed unit-RMS u, giving sharp crests and broad troughs.
  • sim / sim-wind: sharpened × 0.32 mm RMS × (1 + 0.9·e^(−d/6)), plus the contact lip.
  • sim-rings: the sim chop × 0.75, calmed up to 85% within 7 mm of obstacles, plus the rings field × 1.25 (break bias 0.25).
  • spectrum: 70 plane waves (2.5–22 mm, a ∝ λ^0.65, ±75° about a wind), a Stokes second harmonic, and phase compression near obstacles. This one is the weakest: lumpy, with an odd calm zone round the rock.
  • The tide (round 2) still underlies every option.
  • combed and lapped stay in the code but are retired from the sheet.

7. Round 4 (2026-10-28): emitted (waves come from sources)

Shawn: fewer crossing directions. Waves should come from the obstacles, with land a strong source and rocks weak. With neither, pick a couple of tile edges and send waves perpendicular to the edge. All-directions chop "ends up with a whole bunch of spiky shit".

  • Sources, each with (strength, decay, λ₀, growth):

    Source Strength Decay λ₀ Growth
    land 1.0 34 mm 3.8 mm 0.022/mm
    rock 0.55 10 mm 3.0 mm 0.05
    2 random non-land tile edges 0.28 50 mm 5 mm —

  • Distances: geodesic (Dijkstra through water, 8-connected, on a 0.25 mm grid), so land waves wrap round the rock instead of passing through it.

    • Distances are smoothed within water only (normalised convolution). Plain blurring leaked the 1e3 "unreachable" values out of land and rock and erased every wave (v4c bug).
    • Edge sources use plain distance from the edge, so waves travel perpendicular to it.

  • Train per source: a wobbled log-phase (spacing widens outward) and sharpened crests ((1 + cos)/2)^1.7. Long, rare breaks (bias 0.55, σ 7 mm) apply to land and rock only; gating edge waves made dashes. A same-source swell at 0.45× wavenumber and 0.55× amplitude rides underneath, so the two scales are related.

  • Combination (emitted): a partition of unity with weights w⁸, so the dominant source rules its territory and only a few directions cross, at hand-overs. Amplitude × (0.35 + 0.65√w_max): far waves are quieter but still readable.

  • emitted-sum: trains weighted by w and summed, so more crossing.

  • Result (seed 1): shore-parallel crests sweep out from the bank, wrap the rock and its contact rings, with crossing edge waves at the far corner. Risks to watch:

    • wood-grain or directional rows where land dominates;
    • small dots and dashes near the shore at break gates.

8. Round 5 (2026-10-28): emitted-chaos (coherence decays with distance)

Shawn: emitted is liked but still too regular. A source's influence should get less clear the further you go. He suggested:

  • physical emitters strong (land, rock);

  • the open-water corners as very low-intensity emitters;

  • a pure water tile gets all four corners, so it becomes chaotic and non-directional, and so does any water far from the physical emitters.

  • Emitters:

    • land: 1.0 strength, 34 mm decay;
    • rock: 0.55, 10 mm;
    • each open-water corner, a weak radial emitter: 0.3 strength, 70 mm decay, λ 4 mm. A corner counts as open water if the water fraction around it is above 0.5.
    • The edge fallback of emitted isn't used here.

  • Coherence loss: the phase wobble grows with distance from the source, 1 mm + 0.06·d. Corners grow at half that rate. Crests are crisp near a source and wander and break up far away. Physical crests rarely break (bias 0.9); the wobble does that work.

  • Order versus chaos: order = the max physical weight, and c = smoothstep over 0.12…0.45.

    • Dominant: w⁸ partition of unity; the nearest source rules.
    • Chaos: Σ w·train / √Σ w², an amplitude-preserving interference sum.
    • z = (c·dominant + (1 − c)·chaos) × (0.7 + 0.3c).

  • Open scene (--open): no land and no rock, so only the four corner emitters. This is the all-water tile case.

9. Round 6 (2026-10-28): spectral-chaos (a wavelength band per emitter)

Shawn: emitted-chaos is "the best I've seen so far". Next: each emitter should emit a range of frequencies, not one, and the range should depend on the type of object.

  • _band_train: each emitter sums n crest trains at log-spaced, jittered wavelengths across its band.

    • Amplitude ∝ λ^1.0, so longer waves are taller.
    • Each component has its own phase and its own coherence wobble. Shorter waves decohere faster: (λ/λ_hi)^−0.3.
    • The sum is tanh-tamed and Stokes-sharpened (0.85), giving crisp crests over broad troughs. The interference forms wave groups.

  • Bands (λ_lo–λ_hi mm, spacing growth, components):

    Emitter Band Growth Components Character
    land 3.2–11 0.015 4 long and medium waves
    rock 2.2–4.8 0.04 3 short choppy wavelets
    corner 4–12 0 3 weak long swell

  • Otherwise the same as emitted-chaos (§8): order-to-chaos hand-over, corner emitters, the open scene. Amplitude is 0.42 mm per unit-RMS train.

  • v6a used land 2.6–9, rock 1.8–4.2 and λ^0.8. It was too busy and fine; shore lines read like wood grain.

10. Round 7 (2026-10-28): swell-chaos and the painted preview

Prompted by Shawn sharing ChatGPT's acrylic painting of spectral-chaos. What the painting showed: deeper relief, a few broad rolling swells off the shore with smaller waves riding on them, and finer, busier chop behind the rock.

  • swell-chaos (_swell_train):
    • Long components stay round (sharpen 0.15). Components shorter than the band's geometric midpoint are crest-sharpened (0.9). Amplitude ∝ λ^1.4, so the swells dominate.
    • Bands: land 5–16 mm, rock 2.0–4.5 mm (≥ 2 mm, so it prints), corners 5–14 mm.
    • Relief: ≈ 2× spectral-chaos. Relief range is −0.74…+2.2 mm.
    • Rock: weight × 1.15, plus a downstream wake.
      • The weight gains ×(1 + 0.9·down·e^(−d/2.5·decay)).
      • down = ∇d_land · ∇d_rock: the side of the rock facing away from the shore, the direction land waves travel.
    • Soft trough floor: a softplus at −1.1 mm.
  • Geometry fixes found on the way:
    • Terraces: deep troughs were clipped flat by max(land, water) against the underwater bed (WL − 1 mm). The bed is now WL − 2.2 mm (BED_DEPTH_MM, all options), and the troughs are softly floored.
    • Faceted swells: the adaptive Laplacian mesher dropped vertices on broad smooth swells, so they rendered faceted even though the heightmap was smooth (checked by hillshading the raw grid). The top is now a uniform 0.2 mm mesh (MESH_SUBSAMPLE = 2), which is also smaller than the adaptive mesh was.
  • Painted preview: the first column on the sheet.
    • Water is coloured by drybrush affordance: t = 0.65·(z − blur₂ₘₘ)/0.5 + 0.35·(z − blur₆ₘₘ)/0.9, mapped onto navy → cobalt → turquoise → pale crest → near-white tip.
    • Land is tan, rock grey, and tile walls (|n_z| < 0.35) dark.
    • Faces are quantised to the palette and rendered through dharmatiles.render (direct pyrender vertex colours came out blank).
    • The renderer boosts saturation, so treat colours as indicative.

11. Scenarios (2026-10-28)

Shawn: the seed sweep is good. He wanted to see how swell-chaos handles other situations. SCENARIOS in waterlab.py lists, for each one, the land sides and the obstacles (rock / cube / pyramid):

  • bank-rock
  • open
  • open-rock
  • open-2rocks
  • shore-shapes
  • land-2
  • land-3
  • land-4

The land is the union of per-side banks, with a 14 mm inset when it is on more than one side. Select one with --scene; render all with contact_sheet.py --sheet scenarios. bank-rock output is byte-for-byte unchanged.

Observations (seed 1):

  • Open sea, one and two rocks: good. Each rock organises its own rings, and they merge into the corner chaos between rocks.

  • Cube and pyramid: the rings take on the obstacle's shape (squarish around the cube), which is plausible.

  • land-3 / land-4 are the weak point. Land waves dominate a shrinking pool, so iso-distance crests nest into concentric U and square contours: topographic loops (W2 targets). The rock barely registers. Square land corners come through as mitred crest corners.

    Likely fixes:

    • cap land influence by pool size (decay ∝ pool width);
    • add more wobble, or rounder distance, at concave corners;
    • give enclosed water its own weak interior emitters, so the centre of a pond goes to chaos.

11a. Enclosed-water fix (2026-10-28)

Shawn: the cube and pyramid shape-following is "perfect", but square land surrounds "look a bit odd".

  • Rounded land offsets (_round_offsets, land only).
    • The land distance field is blended toward progressively blurrier copies, σ ≈ 0.45 · d, at σ levels 2/4/8/16 mm.
    • Crests hug a straight or square shore closely, then relax into rounded curves with distance, as real waves diffract.
    • Obstacles keep sharp offsets; their shape-following was liked.
  • Enclosure-scaled land reach.
    • f = clip((land share of the tile perimeter − 0.3) / 0.7, 0, 1), and the land decay is multiplied by (1 − 0.75 f).
    • One bank (about 25%) is untouched, so bank-rock keeps the loved look. A bay or pond shortens the land's reach, so the middle of enclosed water goes to the rock and to chaos instead of nested U or square contours.
    • Tried first and rejected: scaling by the pool radius (99th-percentile distance). It also bit bank-rock and shore-shapes, because rocks shrink the "pool".
  • Far emitters: when fewer than 2 tile corners are open water, 3 weak emitters about 220 mm outside the tile, at random bearings, send in near-plane waves. They supply the open-sea chaos that enclosed water has no corners for.
  • Result: land-3 and land-4 show rounded shore waves along the banks, clear rock rings in the middle, and calm crossing water between. The other scenarios are visually unchanged.

Exposed ground = dirt (2026-10-29)

Shawn: dirt should be the texture wherever ground is exposed, across all labs, and mud and other dirt textures are eliminated. This lab now uses dirtlab.dirt_relief(seed, W, H): dirtlab's soil roughness, clods, grit and pebbles, without its hills or big stones, as a relief offset sampled onto this lab's grid.

  • Exposed land (above WL, fading in over 0.1–0.6 mm) gets dirt in the final surface only. sc.land stays plain, so distances and waves are unchanged.
