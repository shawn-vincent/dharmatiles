*v0, 2026-10-28. Exploratory: Shawn hasn't found a water he likes yet, so this lab compares several generators on one fixed scene. Inputs:

  • dharmatiles' current water (src/dharmatiles/layers/water.py, docs/design/water-surface-model.md), the closest to liked so far;
  • the ChatGPT design-space handoff (2026-10-28), whose negative design space is summarised in §4.*

1. Scene (fixed across all options)

  • Tile: a 2 × 2 DungeonBlocks tile (70 × 70 mm) on the standard socket base.
  • Land: a rise along one side, with an irregular shoreline and a sloped bank into the water. The land is plain ground; it is context, not the subject.
  • Rock: one faceted rock (from rocklab) standing in the water.
  • Water: everything else. The water surface is the subject.

2. Requirements

  • REQ-W-1 [S] One STL per option. Each is one watertight body that survives the STL reload check (float32, merge by position), with the DB base. Deterministic per seed.
  • REQ-W-2 [S] Single-material sculpture. The water is sculpted relief on the solid top surface, a single-valued height field with no overhangs, printable supportless with a 0.4 mm nozzle.
    • Crests are at least about 1 mm wide.
    • No needles and no sub-0.3 mm detail.
  • REQ-W-3 [S] Heights. The calm water level is about 2.6 mm above the terrain datum. Relief stays within about −0.8…+1.2 mm of it. The land rises to about 6 mm (the DB grass band).
  • REQ-W-4 [R] Reads as fantasy water before paint. It is exaggerated and sculptural, but recognisably water. A monochrome render must already read as water.
  • REQ-W-5 [R] Obstacles organise the water. The rock and the shore generate structure: wavelets that hug the contact, fade outward and break irregularly. The strongest relief is near obstacles.
  • REQ-W-6 [R] Open water is quieter, but still sculptural. There is enough relief to drybrush. It is statistically direction-neutral: no global orientation, and acceptable after rotating 90°.
  • REQ-W-7 [R] One vocabulary. Open water, rock contact and shore contact are one continuous surface. There are no pasted-on decals and no seams between treatments, and where the rock and shore fields meet, they merge without spikes.
  • REQ-W-8 [R] Drybrush affordance. Broad, rounded crests with smoother, wider troughs: brush-catching forms, not engraved lines.
  • REQ-W-9 [S] Rim. The water meets the tile edge at a consistent calm level, so adjacent water tiles butt cleanly.

3. Options to compare (v0)

Each option is one water generator applied to the identical scene:

Option Idea Source
legacy Current dharmatiles water: band-limited noise plus scattered-segment sine ripples on a smoothed distance-from-obstacle field the closest-to-liked baseline
lapping A few broad, rounded, broken crests following the distance from rock ∪ shore, with spacing that widens and height that decays outward, over quiet, low, broad swell ChatGPT's best untested idea plus the dharmatiles ripple lineage
crescents The lapping crests near obstacles, plus sparse finite crescent crests scattered at random orientations in open water the ChatGPT B/D families

More options are added as iteration suggests.

4. Failure modes (negative design space, inherited)

# Failure Looks like
W1 Cells / bowls a honeycomb or Voronoi network of rounded depressions with bright rims
W2 Targets perfect concentric rings; raindrop rings in open water
W3 Wave rows parallel directional swells or corduroy (seen in dharmatiles' wave-train model)
W4 Swirls spiral or whirlpool ornament
W5 Busy lines many thin ridges everywhere; reads as stormy foam
W6 Lumpy porridge isotropic noise as the main generator, with no structure
W7 Pasted decal the ripple zone visibly bounded and stuck onto a different background
W8 Clones options that are only scale variants of each other

5. Process

Exploratory, with Shawn's eye as the judge:

  1. Claude generates the options and self-checks them with monochrome renders, rejecting obvious failures.
  2. Shawn looks at a shortlist.
  3. Together we choose a direction, then iterate.
