• One scatter for every thing: rocks, flowers and trees were each scattered by a scatter of their own, with their own spacing, seating and order, and a mix by a fourth that placed the three kinds in three fixed passes. There is now one scatter that places any thing: each thing says how big it can be (its extent), how it meets the ground (bedded, dry, upright) and what a scatter of it uses where the document writes nothing, and the scatter places the things with the largest extent first. A tile that scatters rocks, flowers or trees alone, or a mix that writes its gap and its count, builds exactly as before. A mix that writes no gap now takes the first child’s own (rocks and flowers: 1.5dmm, not 1dmm), and one that writes neither count nor perSquare takes the first child’s own density.
