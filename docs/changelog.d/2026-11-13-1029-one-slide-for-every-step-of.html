• One slide for every step of the trail, folders and documents too: opening a map’s piece slid, a folder’s subfolder slid, and opening a tile or a map from its folder slid, but each by its own code (the shell’s, the folder list’s ghost, a CSS transition on the strip holding the two sidebar pages) with its own timing, so the step from a map to a piece of it did not look like the step from a folder to a tile. Now one function (js/slide.js) takes a level off and the next one on, and every step is a call to it: a tile’s levels and a map’s (the shell), the folder levels, the Add tiles pane’s own folders (inside its box), and the step between a folder and a document, which is one level deeper in the trail (trailDepth in nav.js: the folder’s depth, one for the document, then its own level), so opening a tile or a map slides in from the right and Up from it, or a crumb to a folder, slides back from the left. One rule gives the direction (slideDir), one place the timing (200 ms), the easing, the reduced-motion swap, the scroll kept per level and the wait before the keyboard follows (it follows a step only when it was in the panel, into the row it came from); the browser’s ghost-slide and the pages’ transition are deleted, and a test fails on any other code that animates a level.
