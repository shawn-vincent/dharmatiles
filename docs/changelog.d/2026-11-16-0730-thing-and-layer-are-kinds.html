• Thing and layer are kinds: they sit beside length, angle and plain number in the language’s one kind system, and one rule says whether a value fits where another is wanted: a thing fits where a layer is wanted (placing a thing makes it a layer), a layer never fits where a thing is. A param can now hold a layer (param ground dirt, bank ground=water at=pool), and say what it holds with kind= (kind=thing, kind=layer, kind=length); a param with no default and nothing deciding its kind is dt::kind. Every kind mistake is worded once (‘dirt’ is a layer, and ‘stand piece’ wants a thing), a part whose one entry is placed is a layer, not a thing, and the nested quotes in a place word’s message are gone. The editor’s completion offers a thing slot the things, a layer slot the layers and things, and a number slot nothing.
