• A param can hold a thing, and an element is a thing or a layer: every element is now one of two kinds, a thing (a rock, stone, boulder, pebble, flower, tree, or a definition whose stack is one of them) that makes one solid from its controls and a seed, or a layer that also reads the scene so far; the schema says which, once, and each element page says it under Kind. A definition’s param can now hold a thing: param leaf pebble, used as an entry or a scatter child (leaf at=…, scatter count=3 { leaf }), and the caller passes another by name, shrub leaf=boulder, an element or a definition of one thing. Properties written where the thing is used apply over its own, and one it does not take is refused at the line. A layer, a number or an unknown name where a thing belongs is refused at the line with what to write (dt::type), and a layer written as a scatter’s child says it is a layer. Each thing is seeded by its call path like every entry, so two definitions sharing one thing draw it differently. No tile changes. The guide has a new page, Things and layers, and the editor completes a thing param’s value with the things it may be.
