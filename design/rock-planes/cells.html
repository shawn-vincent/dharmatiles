Rocks from cells

Option 3 for a concave shape, drawn as a slice through it. The planes don't make the rock by all cutting one block. They divide all of space into cells, and the rock is the group of cells that lie mostly inside the shape. A group of convex cells can have notches, so the rock can be concave. In 3D every line here is a plane and every cell is a convex block; the steps are the same.

plane on a face plane at a corner fracture plane

In 3D

Each line here is a plane and each cell is a convex block, cut from a box around the shape. The program already has the pieces: a block cut by planes is how Rock Planes builds today, and joining solids is a Manifold union. Only cells near the shape matter, so you split only those and leave far-off space alone.

What it costs

  • Thin slivers: two nearly parallel planes leave a thin cell that may or may not be kept. They need a rule, such as merging a sliver into its neighbour.
  • The cell count grows fast with the number of planes, so fewer planes go much further than in Rock Planes.
  • Touching the shape and the bevel shares become rules about which cells are kept, not about how deep each plane sits.
  • An inside corner faces downward on one side, so the overhang gate needs looking at there.
