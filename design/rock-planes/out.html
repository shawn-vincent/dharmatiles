Rock Planes

A rock as planes all around a shape. Each plane is put on the shape (on a face, along an edge or at a corner), turned a little at random, sunk a little and cut. That is the whole algorithm.

How a rock is built

  1. The shape is the space the rock should fill (sizes in mm, at the 35 mm square drawn on the plate). Its bottom is cut at the Foot height and the rock is set down so that cut sits on the plate. Nothing is anchored below the foot.
  2. Where the planes go. Every corner of the shape gets one. Then points are thrown along the edges and over the faces (about On edges of them on edges), each kept a spacing away from those already taken, until there are Planes in all. A sphere or an oval has no edges or corners, only surface; a cylinder has its top rim as an edge.
  3. Each plane starts as the shape's own tangent there: on a face, the face's direction; on an edge, halfway between its two faces; at a corner, the average of the faces that meet there. On a flat face it is the tangent of the face domed a little instead: it leans away from the face's middle, by up to Dome at the rim, and drops by the dome's sag. (Tangents of a curved surface all show; tangents of a flat one are all the same plane, and the deepest would cover the middle.) It is turned at random by about Spread, about the point where it was put, then sunk by a drawn amount between half and all of Sink, Edge sink or Corner sink. Edges and corners sink deeper, or the face planes beside them would cut past them. A plane turned about its point can dip into the shape far from it, so a turn that would cut deeper than its own sink plus Lean limit anywhere (below the dome, on a face) is drawn again, up to twelve times, and the shallowest is kept. No plane may point more than 50° below level, so no face overhangs past the 55° print limit.
  4. The rock is everything inside all the planes and above the foot: one closed convex solid, cut in one pass. A face with several planes on it breaks into facets; an edge with planes along it becomes a chamfer that changes width and angle as it goes; a corner plane makes a corner facet.

Corners, edge points and face points draw from separate seeded streams, so moving On edges or Planes keeps the shape of what was there where it can. Overhang paints orange past 55° and red past 65°, as in the program. A magenta face means the planes left a gap (a fault).
