Cliffs, Reconsidered

If Cliffs from Spines were written again today, with everything its 222 commits taught: what the engine does, where it fights itself, the options at each layer, and what I would build. Read from the engine itself (2,028 lines), its history, the plan and speed pages, and seven earlier design pages. The look stays within reach of today’s; nothing here needs pixel identity.

The short answer

Verdict: one good idea, said five ways

The engine’s good idea is that a stone is a convex cell: a list of planes. Everything solid about it comes from there: support by construction, no red on an unflaked wall, a Granite wall’s stones in 18 ms. Everything that hurts comes from the places that leave the idea: holes and repairs, a second and a half for the surface, five flaking designs in two days, cracks that cannot cross a scar.

Three changes, in order of value:

  1. Make the surface; do not cut it. A flake scar is a dent half a millimetre deep in a face. Today it is a 3D solid subtracted from a 3D solid, and the pass that recovers the surface is 89% of the build. As a height over its face a scar is seven planes, and the flaked face is a flat map of polygons, each with one plane. A crack is then a V sunk along a line into whatever the map holds, so it runs across scars by construction.
  2. Know; do not ask. No stage ever makes the wall’s outside. Each one finds it again by asking “is this point in another stone?”, up to 6.3 million times a wall, each with its own tolerance and its own misses. Work out once, exactly, which part of each face is covered, and let bevels, scars, cracks and the overhang paint read it.
  3. One cell, one cut, one break. The engine has nine plane clippers, three ways to write a plane and three partition makers (spines, the crack network, facet sectors). One convex cell type, in two and three dimensions, with one cut, carries all of it. “Break a cell into cells” then becomes a thing the design can name, repeat at a smaller size and turn to any angle, which is where the reference pictures the engine cannot reach today come from.

What stays: spines and their cells, the fuzzy envelope, the five styles as settings, overlap as support, the cone over what holds a stone, every stone shaped by planes only, one seed stream per job.

First step: a two-day spike of the surface map on today’s stones, judged by eye beside today’s walls. It carries most of the gain and most of the risk. The measured fallback is the program’s own kernel, Manifold, which does today’s job with a third of the triangles and no code of ours, at about today’s speed.

Form here is the silhouette and its masses; structure is the stones and their joints; surface is cracks, chips and scars. The engine’s form is one box with fuzzed faces, which costs nothing because it says almost nothing.

Findings

Surface marks are made as volumes

Each scar is a cone subtracted from a stone, each crack step a wedge, and the surface pass recovers the outside of what is left. The cost belongs to that way of stating the problem, not to a slow mesher: the program’s own kernel pays the same.

Evidence. The page’s mesher spends 0.31 to 0.67 ms a scar; Manifold on the same input spends 0.29 to 1.3 ms. The page emits 71 to 143 triangles a scar where the exact surface needs 24 to 45. A scar is 0.5 to 1 mm deep and tilts 7 to 12° off its face.

The engine asks instead of knowing

The outside of the wall is never made, so every stage that needs it samples it. The carve asks whether an edge is seen, holding asks what lies 0.1 mm under a face, a scar walks out from its strike point asking how much room it has, and the overhang paint tests columns on a 0.1 mm grid. Each asker has its own tolerance, so each has its own misses.

Evidence. 0.3 to 6.3 million point tests a wall in the surface pass alone. The paint takes 19 to 617 ms, as long as making the stones. The misses are this week’s bug list: the bare line beside a seam, the roof over a scar’s mouth, two floors flickering in one place (the 0.01 mm fix), “five points missed the seen strip”. They are one class: several paths to one fact.

Cutting a face into convex pieces is what triples the triangles

The surface pass cuts each face into convex pieces and fans each piece alone. Neighbouring pieces then disagree about the corners on their shared edges, which is what the weld, the search for corners on edges, the fins and the gap fans exist to mend.

Evidence. On Granite seed 1 (before today’s change) the pass made 173,747 triangles and then found 167,032 unmatched edges to repair. I built the map I recommend below the naive way, from convex pieces, and it fragmented just as badly: each mark leaves 5 to 6 flat faces, held as 8 to 12 pieces each. So the bloat is a property of convex pieces, in two dimensions as in three. A face must stay one polygon.

One idea, nine clippers

“Cut a convex thing by a plane” is written nine times: clip, clipS, cutSolid, clipPoly, clipConvex, cut3, less, clipBot, cutV, beside 2D helpers for tops and bottoms drawn in plan. A plane is written three ways, a stone three ways. Each copy has its own notion of “on the plane”.

Evidence. 171 tolerance literals of 14 magnitudes, from 1e-16 to 1e-3, in the engine. A plane is a six-entry array whose last entry is a spine or one of seven magic negatives; a face’s kind is packed into four bits of a tag. The carve takes its arguments and returns its report through 14 module-level variables.

Stages are coupled through time

Each stage hands the next finished geometry, so each later stage must re-derive what the earlier one knew, and each earlier stage must leave room for the later one. A lower stone keeps flat patches for stones not yet made. A crack leaves whole what holds a stone. Scars had to keep off cracks, and now that they do not, a deeper scar erases the crack. A crack’s V pieces assume a convex face, so they cannot follow a flaked one: a dozen constructions failed at that on 28 Nov.

Evidence. Flaking was designed five times in 46 hours. 27 Nov 08:17 paraboloid scars, lowest wins. 09:36 a field per plane, cut along ridges. 13:06 one formula, each face on a lattice (holes at joints and corners, 46 to 70 s). 28 Nov 00:26 one closed mesh, points moved (closed; 230 to 760 ms). 05:50 convex cutters and an exact pass (the look that was kept; 0.7 to 1.8 s, repairs, no cracks across scars).

Placement is by trial and refusal

A scar is tried, tested against the rules and usually refused. The rules are the design: Flaking has about twenty constants and takes some 700 words to state. A bevel reports one of 14 reasons it was or was not made.

Evidence. 70 to 89% of scar attempts are refused (Fieldstone: 21,609 tried, 2,311 made). With the map, the bare patches are cells that still carry the face’s own plane, so a pit goes where one is, with no grid of samples: the sketch covers a face to under 1% bare that way.

Three partitions that do not know they are one

Spines make cells that become stones. The crack network makes cells whose boundaries become cracks. Faceting makes sectors of a face. Each has its own code and its own words: Direction, Reach, Width, Thickness, Order and Wander for one; Network, Spacing and Planes, Columns or Blocks for another. One meaning, several words.

Evidence. Cliff Cleaving (18 Nov) already named the single idea, a volume cut by families of planes at several sizes, and the two best walls of that week (O v14, N v13) came from cutting the same families again inside each cell at 0.6 and 0.4 of the spacing. Spines kept one size.

Form cannot be said

The envelope is one box. Direction has three values. So a dip slope (reference N), a 60° set (D), spires leaning to a point (P), masses stepping back, a lintel or a buttress cannot be asked for, whatever the settings. The page lists most of these under “Not here yet”.

Evidence. The same study says a wall reads from 1 to 5 masses and “a few dozen pieces, not hundreds”, and that variety comes “from where pieces start and stop, not from their angles”. Fieldstone has 443 stones and about 2,300 scars.

Today’s cracks break the project’s own crack rules

A free crack end tapers out in the middle of a face. A crack is 0.2 to 0.6 mm wide and 0.14 to 0.42 mm deep. The earlier study asks for cracks that run edge to edge or end on another crack, at least 0.4 mm wide and deep, often with one side standing proud.

Evidence. Rock at Every Scale: “never in the middle of a face”; short scratches “read as gouges”; nothing under 0.4 mm prints. A crack that is the opened boundary between two cells meets all three by construction.

The prototype became the product, in a second language

The page grew its own geometry kernel, its own exact mesher and its own overhang measure. The Rust program already has all three (Manifold, the audit gate), and every line of the page must be written again there. The by-construction rule calls a second path the defect.

Evidence. 3,808 lines, 417 KB. The engine is 2,028 lines; wall alone is 514 lines and 59 KB, one closure over a “current stone” held in shared registers (initBox, clip, snap, restore). One function was split out only because “wall is past the size V8 will optimize”.

The design I would build

Three kinds and a short list of verbs. Everything the engine does today is one of them.

Cell
A convex region written as its half-spaces: lines in a face, planes in the wall. One verb, cut. From it: meet (join two lists), section (a 3D cell cut by a plane is a 2D cell), grow and shrink (move the planes), the cone over a cell.
Break
A cell turned into cells. Spines make one, a family of planes makes one, strike points on a face make one.
Map
One face’s surface: polygons that share their edges, each with one plane, a height over the face. Three verbs. Cover marks what another stone hides. Lower takes a bowl and keeps the deeper of the surface and the bowl. Groove sinks a V along a line into whatever surface is there. No verbs, and the map is the flat face.

A sketch of the map, running

One 30 by 18 mm face from above, lit from the top left. The scars are today’s: a point under the edge, five planes rising 7 to 12° across the face, three generations. This sketch is 90 lines and holds each flat face as convex pieces, so it fragments, as finding 3 says; the real map keeps a face whole. It has no index either, so its time grows with the square of the marks.

What the sketch shows. The surface is a partition: the pieces’ areas sum to the face exactly. A bowl is never refused for lack of room, since it is clipped by what is there. Bare patches are read off the map. And the crack crosses every scar at its own depth and width, because in each piece the strip either side of the line takes that piece’s own plane, lowered at the line. That is the thing a dozen 3D constructions could not do.

Where two faces meet

This is the one place the map is a new definition rather than the old surface made another way. Both faces’ maps end on the same points along their shared edge, and each such point sits as deep under each face as that face’s map says there (two planes, one point; three at a corner). An edge strike lowers both faces from one point on the edge: the long scar on one, the notch on the other. Across the edge the result is close to today’s cone, not identical. The spike has to show it reads as well.

What it promises, by construction

  • Closed. A map’s polygons share their edges; two faces share their edge points. Nothing is cut, welded or mended, so there is no step at which a seam can open.
  • Printable. One check, in one place: when a plane is given to a polygon, it may not face down past 55°; if it would, the mark is laid flatter. Support is decided once, in Hold.
  • Zero is identity. A stage that lowers nothing leaves the map as it was, polygon for polygon.
  • Order is what is written. Flaking then cracks means the cracks cut the flaked surface. The other order means scars cross cracks.
  • One answer to “what is outside”. Bevels, scars, cracks and paint read the same covered polygons.

What it opens

These are not needed for the rewrite, and each needs its words agreed first. A bowl with a flat floor over one cell of a coarser break is a step: a block set back, “one side standing proud”. A groove along every boundary of that break is a crack that runs edge to edge or ends on another crack. The same break applied again at 0.6 of its spacing is the nested size the best earlier walls had. A break made by plane families takes a dip and a facing, which reaches the dip slope and the leaning spires. An envelope that is itself a few cells gives masses.

Language check

  • Words: in the page, steps 0 and 1 add, rename or remove no control. Step 2 is different: it replaces the Rust cliff element, which has words of its own, so it needs a reviewed table from the page’s words to the registry’s before it is built. Inside the code: cell, cut, break, map, cover, lower, groove, hold. “Break” is new and would need a registry row if it ever reaches the tile language; “cleave” is the earlier word for the plane-family maker and was ruled a kind by Orin on 18 Nov.
  • Kinds: three (cell, break, map) where today there are about a dozen (piece, mass, body, joiner, guard, top, rest patch, cutter, trace, chain, step, scar, pit). A stone is a cell with a map on each face.
  • Kernel: a cell has two verbs, cut (by a half-space) and corners. Section, grow, hull, the cone, overlap and emit derive from those two. (An earlier version of this page said cut alone; Orin corrected it.)
  • Second paths removed: nine clippers to one; support decided once and painted from it; what is outside decided once; the page’s kernel, mesher and measure against the program’s.
  • One meaning, several words (open, needs Shawn): Direction, Reach, Width, Thickness for stones against Network and Spacing for cracks. Both describe a break.
  • Holes closed: a crack on a scar; a scar at a seam; a stage at zero.
  • Review: Orin reviewed this design on 28 Nov; the rulings are below. Step 4 adds words and goes back before it is built.

Orin’s review

Verdict: accreting. The core is sound (a stone as a convex cell of planes; a face’s surface as a height that marks only lower), but the page claimed more unity than it has. I accept all of these; none changes the first step, and several make it simpler.

  1. The cell already exists. dt_geom::Convex has cut and corners; it needs a 2D sibling, not a new type. dt_kit::Support already means what Hold means, and cliff/relief.rs is already a height over a face. The rewrite extends these.
  2. Break claimed too much. It should be called cleave (ruled a kind on 18 Nov) and mean only an exact partition of a cell: by planes, seeds or spines. Faceting is a cut, and strike points do not make a partition. Joint is a grow and Fuse makes a body; both act after it.
  3. The map has one verb, lower. A crack is a mark measured from the surface it lands on; a scar is a mark measured from the face. Groove is not a second verb, and cover is not a verb at all.
  4. One word for what no other stone hides: seen. Today seen, open, outside and free face all name it, and cover already means something else in the registry.
  5. The edge should be the general case. A stone is its cell less its marks’ cells, and a mark measured from a face lowers every face map its cell reaches. Stated that way the result is today’s cone exactly, and the edge rule above is only a stopgap. A scar that reaches a neighbour chips it by the same sentence, so the “within a scar’s depth of open” rule goes; only faces that coincide need an owner.
  6. Support is a value, a list of contact cells, passed to the shaping and marking stages, and the page must say which shapes the sections are taken from (the carve reads “seen” before the stones are finished).
  7. The 55° check belongs to the mark, made before it lowers anything. Done polygon by polygon, a flattened wall stops meeting its neighbour.
  8. A step is a cleave with a setback, not a flat-floored bowl: a riser square to the face is no height over that face.
  9. Before the Rust element: a word table from the page’s controls to the registry’s (Reach, Width, Thickness and Spacing to spacing; Span to carry; Wander to bend; Direction to dip and facing), and the map and support placed behind dt-kit.

Orin’s kernel: four kinds (plane, cell, cleave, mark) and five verbs (cut, corners, inside, carry, lower). A map is a face and its marks; a stone is its cells less its marks; everything else derives. Approved as they stand: A5, B3, D2, F2, the intent of C3, and the spike first with Manifold as the fallback.

What I would not change

  • Spines. A stone as the cell of its spine is a good, small model, and five styles fall out of its settings.
  • Planes only, for the stone. Bevel, Round, Faceting and Weathering as cuts keep the stone convex and its printability a one-line check.
  • The cone over what holds a stone, the flare that is also the bottom bevel, and overlap as support. The idea is right; only its statement is long.
  • The fuzzy envelope drawn from smooth fields, ranges for every drawn setting, and one seed stream per job.
  • Shells, not a union, in the page; and a stone as a thing with a page.
  • The scar itself: the cone with its point under the edge, the long shallow side, the notch behind, the growth from rims. The map lowers by the same bowls.

Risks

  • The look at stone edges. The rule above is new there, and edges are where scars are struck. This is why the spike comes first and is judged by eye.
  • Keeping faces whole. The sketch shows the easy version fragments. The real map needs polygon difference and a shared edge structure: more code than the sketch, and the part most likely to grow. Its floor is known: Manifold’s counts.
  • Floats. A map is cut by lines computed in floats. Corners are made once and shared, so nothing is welded, but a cut that passes within a hair of a corner must be told to use it. That is one tolerance, in one function.
  • Support on volumes will not match today’s flares wall for wall. It should be held to the seven-wall check and Shawn’s cameras before the old chain goes.
  • The loop in Rust. A look round that was a reload becomes a build. Keep the page for look spikes; keep the element for everything settled.
  • Nested sizes have read as rubble, shingles or crystal when the offsets or tilts were large (Weathered Stone, Wall O). They are an opening, not part of the rewrite.

Plan

  1. Spike the map on a copy of the page, in place of the surface pass: cover, lower, groove, emit, and the edge rule, with faces kept whole. Two days. It passes if all seven walls are closed by index, red is no worse, a wall’s surface is under 150 ms, triangles are within a quarter of Manifold’s counts, and the renders at Shawn’s cameras read at least as well as today’s. If the edges do not read, fall back to A2 in Rust and keep the map for cracks only.
  2. Cover replaces the asks: the bevel’s seen test, the scar’s room and held tests, and the paint all read the sections. Each wall’s red and seen-edge counts are compared with today’s.
  3. One Cell through spines, holding and the carve, written in Rust as the cliff element (dt-geom for the cell and the map, dt-elements for the stages). The page stays as the oracle: same seed, same stones, compared by volume and by render.
  4. Hold on volumes; the paint reads it; the program’s audit is the independent check. The page becomes the element’s explorer.
  5. Break as a kind (needs words, Shawn and Orin first): plane families with dip and facing, a second size, cracks as opened boundaries, an envelope of masses.

Each step lands on the seven-wall check and a contact sheet, and can be the last one taken.
