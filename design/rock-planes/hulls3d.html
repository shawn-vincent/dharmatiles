Rocks from Hulls 3D

The 2D page's rule in three dimensions. A shape is written as alternating convex hulls: a hull, minus hollows, plus bumps inside the hollows. Each rock hull gets Rock Planes; each hollow gets planes that bow into the rock; the rims where a hollow opens get their own chamfers.

How a rock is built

  1. The shape is written as a tree of convex hulls: the first is rock, its children are hollows cut from it, their children are bumps of rock inside a hollow, and so on. The L is its hull less a wedge; the ring an octagon less a heptagon; the notch with a peak a block less a notch plus a peak standing in it. A face that lies on its parent's face is a mouth (a hollow's opening, or where a bump stands on a wall): it is not the shape's surface and gets no planes.
  2. Rock hulls are built by Rock Planes as they are, bounded by the hull's own faces: touch points, crowns, face planes, edge and corner chamfers. Only where the hull's face is real surface does it get a touch point, a spot or a chamfer, so nothing is put across a hollow's mouth. Each hull takes its share of Planes by its real surface area, at the whole rock's spacing, and its own seed keyed to its place in the tree.
  3. Hollows are cut with a pocket. Each real face of the hollow gets about one plane for each plane spacing squared of its area (one to six), each leaning into the rock a different way by half to all of Sink plus Tilt across the face, then pushed out until it holds the whole hollow. So the pocket always holds the hollow and touches it at an edge or a corner, and its walls bow into the rock as facets. A mouth lets the pocket out a little past it; a real face no further than its planes reach.
  4. Rims. Where a hollow's wall meets a rock face at an outward edge (the L's two tips, the top of the ring's hole, the arch's edges), neither hull owns the edge. Each such edge is chamfered by Chamfered of their length, drawn: the one chamfer rule (a turn of up to half of Tilt, capped by the angle between the faces), cutting half to all of Sink into what is there.
  5. The rock is the first hull's rock, less each hollow's pocket, plus each bump's rock, in order, kept as convex pieces; the rims are cut from the pieces last. What shows is each piece's faces less where another piece covers them.

Every job draws from its own seeded stream keyed by the hull's place in the tree, the face or the rim, so one control never reshuffles another. Overhang paints orange past 55° and red past 65°; with no ground case the underside, and the arch's roof, are overhang for now.
