Chapter 3

Anatomy of one stone

Close up, every major stone in the four sheets is the same thing: a box whose edges have all been planed off. Nothing is carved in and nothing sticks out.

Reading a stone

Estimated From the smooth sheet at three times its size. The numbered parts are on every stone.

  1. Top face. One flat plane. Its outline is a rectangle with its corners cut: five to eight sides, never four, never round.
  2. Top bevel. A band all round the top, 8 to 15 percent of the stone's width, flat or faintly rounded. It catches the light: on the sheets it is the brightest strip of every stone.
  3. Main faces. Two show: one toward the figure, one toward the cut end. Flat and upright, or closing a few degrees toward the top.
  4. Upright chamfer. The edge between two main faces is a third, narrow face, 5 to 25 percent of the width. On M's columns it is so wide that the top is nearly a regular octagon.
  5. Joint. Where two stones meet, their bevels make a V. It widens toward the top, because both stones close a little as they rise.
  6. Kink. An edge is not ruled: over its length it steps aside once or twice by 2 to 5 percent, with a soft notch. This is the largest feature left out of the model below.

The edges between all of these are soft, a rounding of 2 to 3 percent of the width. There is no sharp arris anywhere, and no concave face.

Built in seven moves

Proposed A box, and six things done to it. Each move is one or two numbers. The stone is a rounded box, not a chamfered one: the reference edges read as soft bevels at a glance and as true rounds up close. Where two stones have fused along a stretch of their joint, a flat filler piece is added between them (chapter 8); it is a plain mesh laid over the stones, and the stones themselves stay exactly the rounded boxes described here. Inside corners are never rounded: they are right angles or crisp creases.

True shape

Proposed The same stone from the front, from the cut end and from above, with nothing foreshortened. The stone is described by its box (length, depth, height) and by shares of its shortest side for everything planed off it, so one description fits a toe and a pillar.

The top view is the one the references never give and the one that most fixes the look: an eight-sided top, four long edges and four short ones, with the short ones unequal.
