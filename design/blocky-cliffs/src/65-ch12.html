Chapter 13

Sensitivity and the style's edges

One formation, one number changed at a time. Pictures left at full strength are inside the style as the four sheets show it; dimmed ones have left it, and the caption says for where.

Proposed The base is three stones on the ground with a few laid on and two in front (seed 13); a gate, a leaning set, a ramp or a stair where the number only matters there. Everything not named is held. The in-style ranges are judgements against the 52 references, not measurements.

Which numbers the style is sensitive to

Proposed Ranked by how little change it takes to leave the style.

  1. Each stone's own lean or turn. A few degrees drawn per stone is enough (chapter 11). The tightest bound of all.
  2. Bevel. In the style over a factor of two (0.10 to 0.22); outside it within a step either way.
  3. Open joints. 0.06 fh of slack and the mass falls apart into posts.
  4. Level tops. One run sharing a top is in the style (a small step with a shared dip); every top level reads as masonry.
  5. Stone count. Wide: 4 to 11. Two is a monolith and twenty is a wall.
  6. Height, fall, base, front, peak. Widest: the sheets themselves span most of the range. A shows that half height stays in the family, H and T12 that a wall with almost no fall does, F and L that a tall stone at the right front does.

The order is the grammar's order turned over: the numbers decided last and lowest (chapter 11's scales 4 and 5) are the ones with no room, and the numbers decided first have the most.
