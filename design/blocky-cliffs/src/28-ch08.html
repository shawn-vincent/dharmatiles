Chapter 8

Repeated arrangements

Between one stone and the whole cliff there are a handful of small groupings that come back again and again. A formation is one foot, one body, and sometimes a head.

Estimated Cells listed by eye. Proposed The grey pictures are generated, one per arrangement.

How they combine

Measured Counted from the tracing labels and the chapter 1 classes. The foot is nearly universal; the head is optional in every class; clefts and overhangs are rare and never both missing from a gate.

Measured In of 52 formations the first stone traced (the one at the right front) tops out below half the formation's height.

Estimated Arrangements nest only one way: a body may be two bodies side by side (T18: a pillar wall and a stair; D: a wall with slabs on it), but a foot never has a foot and a head never has a head.

Chapter 9

Hierarchy

Is a formation one mass cut into stones, or stones piled into a mass? Neither. The evidence fits a shallow, fixed hierarchy of three levels, and rules out anything deeper.

Three explanations, tested

Each model predicts something the traces can check.

Cut from one block. Joints would run through, and the two sides of a joint would share a top. Measured 9 percent of neighbours are level.

Piled up. Stones would overlap, leave holes, and any stone could be anywhere. Measured The outline is 74 percent full with joints closed, and the lower stone is in front 81 percent of the time.

Rows of columns under one surface. The plate's back half is divided once into rows, each row once into stones; each stone rises from the ground to its own height near a shared envelope; a tall one may be cut across by a level joint. This predicts tight packing, unlevel tops, low stones in front, and a skyline that climbs. All four are measured.

The three levels

Proposed Formation: one envelope, one grain, one bevel. Row: 1 to 4 bands from front to back, each a band of height. Stone: 1 to 4 per row, each a column, cut at most twice.

Pull the rows apart to see them. Each row is drawn in its own grey. In plan the rows are bands and the stones tile them with no gap: the division is done on the ground, and height is added after.

Measured There is no fourth level. If stones were themselves groups of smaller stones, sizes would fall into two clusters. The shares of chapter 5 have one hump, and the smallest traced stone is still 2 percent of its formation.

Estimated The classes of chapter 1 are this one structure with different counts: pillars are few rows and many stones per row; courses are many rows of one long stone each; blocks add level cuts; leaners add a common lean; gates take one column out of the back row and bridge it.
