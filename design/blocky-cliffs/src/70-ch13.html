Chapter 14

A generator

The grammar of chapter 12, running. It is about 125 lines for the formation and 110 for the stone: one rule for laying stones on, three families of numbers. Fifty formations build and draw in about a third of a second. It is a sketch to test the rules against the sheets, not production geometry.

Does it measure like the sheets?

Measured 200 formations were drawn with every number taken from the proposed ranges, the three families in the counted proportion (25 : 17 : 10). Each was then measured the way the traces were, from the references' camera: stone sizes, and the relations between touching stones.

The table gives every measure for both, and the generator's from two angles: 45 degrees off the face, where the references' A–P plates stand, and 25, which is nearer how the sheet of sixteen is seen and how several A–P cliffs sit turned on their plates. Where the generator misses, the miss is left showing:

  • its ground-standing stones are lower and broader than the sheets' (median face height 0.74 against 0.95; the like-for-like row, of what shows, is 1.16 against 1.67): the widths are held at 0.42 or more, so a tall stone is a tall wide one;
  • its resting stones are thinner (0.34 against 0.52) and fewer bear on two (8% against 18%), though as many rest (34%) and the stacks are nearer: 9 / 49 / 39 / 4 per cent against 13 / 35 / 38 / 13;
  • its widths are more alike (1.6 against 2.5 for widest to narrowest);
  • the lower of two neighbours is in front in 65% of pairs against 83%, and the far end is 0.80 of the highest point as seen against 0.62;
  • on the support check, of 820 laid stones in 400 draws none fails and none stands out past its support by more than 0.25 of its length; without the check 67 of 851 failed it and 28 overhung by more than half. All 23 gates in 400 draws keep their opening under a lintel that lies on both piers.

The numbers do not say whether a draw looks right. The pictures show the limits: chamfers that are all flat where the sheets' edges wander, no kinks on edges, stiff dipping beds, and no niche or shelter.

formation(numbers, seed):                         every stone is first a plain box with a place
  1 ground      cols stones side by side along the back, widths unequal (spread), none under 0.42
       height   base x envelope x 0.92-1.03, where base runs to 1 by a cubed draw per stone, so a
                few stand alone; a neighbour within step is moved by step
       stand    forward of the wall by up to 0.38, the lower of two the more
       lean     the first stone is an end stone (upright near side); the rest lean 15-24 deg
       opening  two piers, and a middle stone far back and 0.32-0.42 lower
  2 front       front stones (0 to 3): a buttress 0.55-0.8 of the wall behind it, hard against
                it; a toe 0.3-0.55 high in front of it or beside it; a second block beside it
  3 pile        until pile stones are tried, no top has room (0.22 fh) or eleven stones stand:
       joint    with chance bond, two neighbouring tops that differ by under 0.3 and have free
                length: raise the lower to the higher and lay one stone across them (0.42-0.9 long)
       top      else on the top that scores most: envelope there - (1 - stack) x its height;
                in the largest free length of it, at the cut end side 6 times in 10, set back
                by setback x (0.3-1.7) and shorter by up to 2 x setback
       height   the room if it is under 0.5 fh, else a third to a half of it, and all of it if
                less than 0.22 would be left
       stands   the support is the tops under its footprint within 0.03 fh below it; it passes if
                the middle half of its footprint (a quarter of its length and depth either side
                of its centre) is over the support, or it has support under the outer eighth of
                both ends of its long side (a bridge: required over any gap); else raise a short
                top under it to meet it (up to 0.25 fh, if nothing is on it), else slide it the
                smallest way that passes, then shorten and narrow it (to 0.55), else do not lay it
       gate     first lay the lintel across both piers; if it cannot be laid, shut the opening
  4 shape       each stone finds which of its 12 box edges are exposed (a point along it which no other stone and
                not the ground touches it at any point: 7 samples), then
       exposed edge: a shallow chamfer (below); one edge in two a broad one; a covered edge
                     (against a neighbour, under a carried stone, on the ground): a hair, so
                     joints stay crisp creases
       front stones: a top sloping along the cliff or into the wall, a battered front
       others:       battered only on a side that stands clear of its neighbour; a top sloped
                     (the dip, if any) unless it carries a stone; some many-sided
  5 count       laying stops at eleven stones

stone(box, numbers):
  the eight corners of the box, each moved by batter, top tilt, lean and shear
  cut by planes (each keeps the part of the stone on one side):
    each top edge:    a shallow bevel: e deep into the top (0.03-0.15 fh, mostly small, on the
                      front and cut-end edge, 0.03-0.07 on the far side; 0 only if covered),
                      dropping min(drop, 0.58 e) down the side, drop 0.015-0.04 fh per stone
    each bottom edge: if exposed, a bigger one (measured on K and S9): 0.05-0.16 deep into the
                      underside, rising 0.04-0.1 up the face (up to 45 degrees)
    each upright edge: if exposed, broad (measured on S9): 0.04-0.14 wide
    top corners:      0 to 3 angled planes across them, 0.04-0.12 fh in, dropping as a top chamfer does
    every covered edge: a hair, 0.01 fh (a bottom edge on the ground or a stone: crisp)
  cut each of the six faces by each plane and add the face a plane leaves; turn
fuse(stones):
  for each contact (a bed, or an upright joint between neighbours) draw chapter 8's rule,
    but never across an exposed edge (a bed: the upper's bottom front edge and the lower's
    top front edge covered; upright: the shorter stone's edge along the joint covered):
    whether it fuses (the chance only; its place and ends are not drawn)
  grow, when the stones are built, those on a fused joint toward each other:
    upright joint: each stone sideways by its widest upright chamfer + 0.01
    bed: the upper stone downward by the lower's front top chamfer + 0.01
  the stones then simply overlap; no material is added, none is cut, no boolean is needed
bear(stones):  (lowest stone first)
  for a stone off the ground, find the samples of its underside with no stone top within
    0.01 fh below; for each, the drop to the stone mass below (or open air)
  d = the drop that 3 in 4 of those samples are within, + 0.015 (rests 0.015 into the stone)
  if d <= 0.25 fh: build the stone taller by d (top stays, bottom moves down); else leave it

Proposed There are three families, which are regions of the numbers and not different code. Columns (25 in 52): base 0.4 to 0.95; with a small step and a shared dip a plateau wall, with an opening a gate. Tiers (17): base 0.3 to 0.7, the lower the base the deeper and the more set back: courses at one end, blocks at the other. Slabs (10): a lean (fins) or a dip (beds). The generator page's picker offers these three. The seven kinds of the sheets are what a draw reads as, and lookOf names them.
