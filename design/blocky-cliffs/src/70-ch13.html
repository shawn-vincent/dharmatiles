Chapter 14

A generator

The grammar of chapter 12, running. It is about 250 lines: one function for a stone, one for a formation. Fifty formations build and draw in about a third of a second. It is a sketch to test the rules against the sheets, not production geometry.

Does it measure like the sheets?

Measured 200 formations were drawn with every number taken from the proposed ranges and classes in the counted proportion. Each was then measured the way the traces were, from the references' camera: stone sizes, and the relations between touching stones.

The table gives every measure for both, and the generator's from two angles: 45 degrees off the face, where the references' A–P plates stand, and 25, which is nearer how the sheet of sixteen is seen and how several A–P cliffs sit turned on their plates. Where the generator misses, the miss is left showing:

  • its ground-standing stones are still lower and broader than the sheets', though less so once hidden parts are left out, as a traced outline leaves them out: the two rows marked "of what shows" are the like-for-like ones;
  • fewer of its stones rest on others or bear on two, and more formations have nothing stacked;
  • its widths are more alike;
  • block formations put their highest point left of the middle more often than the sheets do.

The numbers do not say whether a draw looks right. The pictures show the limits: flat chamfers where the sheets are softly rounded, no kinks on edges, stiff dipping beds, and no niche or shelter.

References: Measured from the traces. Generator: Proposed rules, measured on the true solids as the camera would see them. "Foot nearer" compares the lowest point of each outline in the picture; on the traces a hidden foot ends where the stone in front hides it.

The whole of it

formation(numbers, seed):                         every stone is first a plain box with a place
  1 back mass   by class, along the back edge:
       pillars   two to four columns, none under 0.42 wide or deeper than 1.3 times its
                 width, under the envelope; a plateau run on one
                 sloping plane; a cleft; steps of stepMin elsewhere; some stand proud
       blocks    two or three columns of big blocks; where two neighbours' first blocks top
                 out level the next block lies across the joint, a third of upper blocks
                 standing forward (one draw in five: level tiers)
       courses   tiers of the whole footprint, each lying on the one below, nearly as long,
                 set back toward the back corner. A tier is two or three stones of unequal
                 length; their treads stand 0.05 to 0.15 apart, the taller usually nearer
                 the cut end and a little forward; the tier above may lie across the joint
       slabs     three or four leaning slabs, tight, graded up to the cut end; the first is
                 cut by the cut plane, its near side upright; it may carry a block
       beds      one thick dipping bed, one or two beds tight on it, each broken across in two
       gate      two piers 0.64 to 0.75 of the height, a deep recess between them, a lintel
                 course of two blocks over the opening and both piers, sometimes a stone on it
  2 front       three ranks: sometimes a main pillar applied over a joint near mid length;
                a buttress 0.55 to 0.8 of the wall behind it, hard against it; a toe a third
                to a half of the buttress; sometimes a second block, or a stone tucked in
  3 pile        0 to 2 stones on the tops, as the top of the wall: first a block straddling a
                joint (the two tops under it are brought level), else an upper block 0.5 to
                0.75 wide, two side by side where the support is wide; part of its height is taken off what carries it. A tall pillar may
                instead be split by a level joint. None on a spire or a narrow stone.
     height     if the pile has carried the whole more than a tenth past the envelope,
                everything is pressed down in proportion
  4 shape       each stone finds which of its top edges are exposed, then
       exposed edge: a wide bevel; one edge in four a chamfer of up to 0.25 fh, on the
                     front or (a shoulder cut) on a side; under a carried stone and
                     against a neighbour: nearly sharp, so joints stay creases
       front stones: a top sloping along the cliff or into the wall, a battered front
       toes:         flat, with one broad chamfer
       tall stones:  battered only on a side that stands clear of its neighbour;
                     one in ten a spire; some many-sided or with a prow
       piled stones: tight on what carries them
  5 count       if more than eleven stones came out, drop a piled stone or a column and draw again

stone(box, numbers):
  a signed distance field of a true rounded box: radius 0.07 fh on top and upright edges,
  0.04 under a resting stone, 0.02 at a ground stone's foot (outer edges only);
  broad faces swell 3.5 percent; shoulder and corner cuts are planes applied softly
  move each face in at the top (batter), scale, tilt the top, shear, turn (the field is
  evaluated through the inverse of these)

solid(stones):
  plain union of the stones' fields: no smooth union, no inside fillet
  each contact (a bed, or an upright joint between neighbours) gets a bridge that fills
    the slot between the two stones, flush with their faces, then a V wedge cut along
    its free edge: depth 0.05 (bed) or 0.06 (upright) fh, flanks 45 degrees
  the wedge's depth along the joint: full, or 0 where the joint is fused (chapter 8's
    chance, share, place and ending), ramping to 0 over 0.15-0.5 fh or ending in a notch;
    parts of one stone are fused whole
  polygonise: surface nets on a grid of 0.035 fh (0.025 for the live view), an octree
    search so only a narrow band round the surface is evaluated, each vertex stepped
    onto the surface

Measured What it costs, in a browser: a sheet of 24 formations built and drawn takes about 0.55 to 0.65 s as one solid each (about 0.2 s as separate rounded boxes); one formation takes 20 to 40 ms at 0.035 fh and 35 to 55 ms at 0.025 fh, so a new seed in the generator is instant.
