A generator

The grammar of chapter 11, running. It is about 150 lines: one function for a stone, one for a formation. It is a sketch to test the rules against the sheets, not production geometry.

Does it measure like the sheets?

Measured 200 formations were drawn with every number taken from the proposed ranges and classes in the counted proportion, then each stone was measured the way the traces were: its width and face height as seen from the references' camera, in figure heights.

Medians and usual ranges land on the references for width, face height, their ratio, and the formation's height. Two things do not match and are left showing: generated formations are narrower as seen than the sheet of twenty (whose plates are wider), and the generator's stone count has a longer upper tail.

This checks sizes only. It does not check that the result looks right; the pictures do that, and they show the limits: no kinks on edges, flat chamfers where the sheets are softly rounded, and beds that dip are stiffer than N's.

The whole of it

formation(numbers, seed):
  1 rows     split the depth into rows, front rows thinner and shorter       (depth, rows, frontLen)
  2 columns  cut each row along its length into stones                       (nA, sizeVar)
             shift the joints against the row behind                         (stagger)
  3 heights  h = height × lerp(front, 1, row) × (1 − fall × distance from peak) × (1 ± 9%)
             lower any stone that stands in front of a lower one             (rule: low in front)
             lower any stone within stepMin of its neighbour                 (rule: never level)
  4 gate     optional: set one inner back column back and down; two caps over it
  5 cuts     where the class has them, cut a column level every cutEvery     (cutEvery)
  6 caps     optional: the top of the tallest column becomes a wider stone   (cap, over)
  7 shape    each stone = stone(box, bevel, batter, top tilt, corner cut, 2% unsettle)
  8 lean     shear everything about the plate by the common lean             (lean)

stone(box, numbers):
  24 to 32 points: each corner of the box replaced by the points its chamfers leave
  scale the top (batter), tilt the top, shear (lean), turn (yaw)
  return the convex hull
