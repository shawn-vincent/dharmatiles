Chapter 13

A generator

The grammar of chapter 11, running. It is about 200 lines: one function for a stone, one for a formation. It is a sketch to test the rules against the sheets, not production geometry.

Proposed Choose a class, then change any number. Drag to turn the view; scrolling scrolls the page; pinch to zoom. The post is one figure high.

Does it measure like the sheets?

Measured 200 formations were drawn with every number taken from the proposed ranges and classes in the counted proportion. Each was then measured the way the traces were, from the references' camera: stone sizes, and the relations between touching stones.

Sizes land close. The relations are the harder test, and the table gives each one for both. Where the generator misses, the miss is left showing:

  • its lower stone is in front less often than the sheets', because stones in one row share a depth and the test then turns on noise;
  • its widths are more alike than the sheets';
  • its far end reads higher against the peak than the sheets' does;
  • it puts a cap or an oversailing block in half as many formations.

The numbers do not say whether a draw looks right. The pictures show the limits: flat chamfers where the sheets are softly rounded, no kinks on edges, stiff dipping beds, and no niche or shelter.

References: Measured from the traces. Generator: Proposed rules, measured on the true solids as the camera would see them. "Foot nearer" compares the lowest point of each outline in the picture; on the traces a hidden foot ends where the stone in front hides it.

The whole of it

formation(numbers, seed):
  1 rows      split the depth into rows; front rows thinner, shorter, lower    (depth, rows, frontLen, topLen)
  2 columns   cut each row into stones of unequal width, none under 0.3 fh,
              joints kept off the joints of the row behind                    (nA, sizeVar, stagger)
  3 heights   h = height × lerp(front, 1, row) × (1 − fall × distance from peak) × (1 ± 8%)
  4 plateau   optional: a run of 2–3 back-row neighbours shares one top plane (plateau)
    cleft     optional: the two tallest neighbours stand level, joint open    (cleft)
  5 rules     neighbours not on a plateau at least stepMin apart
              a stone lower than whatever stands behind it
  6 lean      optional: back-row joints lean, most at the far end, least by the
              cut end, where an upright stone closes; feet and cut face stay   (lean, closer)
  7 gate      optional: an inner back column becomes a set-back wall; a cap from each unequal pier
  8 stones    a column is one stone, or
              tiers: a level first tier, shorter tiers on it with their own joints   (tiers)
              beds:  one thick dipping bed and one or two thinner on it        (dip)
              oversail: the far-end column split; its upper block over a set-back wall (oversail)
  9 rest      optional: a smaller stone set back on a top, over one joint if the two tops agree (rest)
 10 count     if more than eleven stones came out, draw again with fewer along

stone(box, numbers):
  24 to 32 points: each corner of the box replaced by the points its chamfers leave
  scale the top (batter, or a leaning joint on each side), tilt the top, shear, turn
  return the convex hull
