Chapter 11

The grammar

Everything above, folded into one small set: one stone, three levels, two fields that every stone reads, and four rules between neighbours.

Vocabulary and productions

Proposed Read each line as "is made of". Square brackets give how many; a slash is a level joint.

A formation is fully described by about fourteen numbers and a seed. Nine of them are in the envelope and the counts; the stone's own shape takes five, shared by all stones.

Eight rules, each from what was seen to what it does

The classes are settings, not types

Proposed Each class of chapter 1 is this grammar with a few counts changed. Nothing else differs: the same stone, the same envelope, the same rules.

Two readings of the classes fit the sheets, and they differ in what else they allow.

Five types. Pick a type, then draw inside it. Safe: every result looks like a reference. But it cannot make T18 (a pillar wall with a stair in front) or D (blocks with slabs on top) except as new types.

One family, four dials (rows, stones along, level cuts, lean). Mixed cases come free. The risk is the corners of the dial space no reference occupies: a stair of leaning treads, four rows of five pillars. The grain chart in chapter 1 shows the references do fill the space between the classes thinly, which favours dials; the counts (22 pillars to 7 courses) say the dials are not drawn evenly.

What stays undecided

Reconstructed One camera angle leaves three things open. The grammar takes the simpler reading of each and says so.

  • Does a tread run under the next step? A stair slab may be a thin stone lying on the one below, or a tall column whose top shows. Both look the same from the front. Columns are used: they need no stacking order.
  • What is behind? No sheet shows the back or the far end. They are taken as flat and flush with the plate, like the cut end, because a cliff tile would meet a neighbour or the table edge there.
  • How deep is a stone? Depth shows only at tops. Where tops show it is 0.7 to 1.1 of the length for pillars and blocks (M, H, O). For slabs it is the tread, a third of the length or less.
