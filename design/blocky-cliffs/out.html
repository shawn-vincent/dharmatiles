Blocky Cliff Atlas

A geometric study of four reference sheets of stylised cliffs built from large, smooth, block-like stones: what the stones are, how they sit against each other, what varies and what never does, and the smallest set of rules that would make more of them.

    Chapter 1

    The four sheets

    Fifty-two formations are the whole of the evidence. Every one was cropped, named, and its major stones outlined by hand: 350 stones in all. Every measured number on this page comes from those outlines.

    Look at any formation

    Pick a sheet, then a cell. Blue lines are the stones as traced; green patches are the top faces that show. Point at a stone to read what was measured from its outline. Lengths are in figure heights as seen (fh): the stone's size in the picture divided by the figure's foot-to-head height in the same cell.

    Measured Outlines are good to about 2 percent of a cell's width (roughly 0.05 fh). Block bottoms run 1 to 2 percent low. Where a stone runs behind the figure or another stone its outline is closed by a guess, and where a groove is faint a wide stone may really be two: the tracing notes list 25 such places. Counts lean toward fewer, wider stones.

    What the scale really is

    The figure's round base gives a ruler that lies flat on the plate. Its top rim is a circle, so the ellipse it makes in the picture tells how far above the horizon the camera sits (15 to 21 degrees), and with that the plate's two visible edges can be laid flat and measured in base diameters.

    Measured The plate is 2.4 to 2.8 base diameters on a side in the A–P sheets, about 2.3 by 3.0 in the sheet of twenty (a rectangle), and 1.8 to 2.1 in the sheet of sixteen.

    Estimated A 28 to 32 mm figure stands on a 25 mm base. On that reading the plates are 45 to 70 mm across, not one 35 mm square: a 25 mm base would cover seven tenths of a 35 mm square's side, and here it covers four tenths. This much is sure. Which size the plates are is not: the three sheets disagree with each other by a third, so the plate was never drawn to a fixed scale against the figure.

    So the page measures in figure heights. The figure is drawn the same in every cell, it stands upright like the cliff faces, and it is the scale a player reads the cliff against.

    Five kinds of formation

    Estimated Sorted by eye into five classes by what the big joints do. The classes are names for regions of one family, not separate designs: chapter 9 shows what they share, and the scatter below shows they run into each other.

    Where the sources disagree

    The smooth A–P sheet and the textured one show the same sixteen compositions, but the textured sheet breaks faces the smooth one leaves whole. The smooth sheet is taken as the form; the textured one only confirms the masses.

    The older sculpting guides in the same folder describe a different rock. Where they differ from the four sheets, the sheets win:
