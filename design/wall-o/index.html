How it is made

Mass

Three big boxes

The wall starts as three boxes side by side. Each has its own height, so the skyline steps down to the right, and its own front depth.

Courses

Level beds

Each mass is cut by horizontal beds, 8 to 13 mm apart. The courses of neighbouring masses do not line up.

Joints

Vertical joints, some running through

Joints split each course into blocks. A joint either repeats the x of the one below, so it runs through two or more courses, or keeps 3 mm clear of it.

Depth

Push each block back by its own amount

Each face moves back 0 to 5 mm. One block in the middle course of the left column goes back 6 to 8 mm and the block above it comes forward 2 to 3 mm, so a recess sits under an overhang. A few small blocks of the same rock are laid at the foot. Skyline blocks also differ in height. A shadow map from the key light turns every recess and joint dark without any extra geometry.

Cleave

Break faces along planes

Each block is cut by one or two planes tilted a few degrees off the face, so the face becomes two flat facets that meet at a sharp crack. Tops tilt slightly; sides crack the same way.

Chips

Bevels, then corner chips

A chamfer plane along the top-front edge of most blocks, and often a second along a side edge. Corner chips come with the skin below: a plane at a corner along its averaged normal, tilted up to 25 degrees, only ever removing material.

Skin

Cleave the outer skin into solid pieces, set each back

The outer 3 to 4 mm of each block, on its front and top, is cut by two or three straight cracks (verticals and horizontals within about 15 degrees, a few diagonals; a split is refused if a piece would be narrower than 0.4 of its parent). Each piece is a closed convex solid, set back 0.2 to 0.8 mm and turned 1 to 4 degrees, with a 0.1 mm seam to its neighbours. The core behind stays whole and darker, so the block is one mass and no crack can show through it. Each piece then loses up to three corners to planar chips. This is the same cleave the blocks came from, applied again inside them, at a smaller size.

What is still off

Round 6 (v14): about a dozen fat blocks, the outer skin almost invisible (none on most faces, at most one split, set back 0.1 to 0.3 dmm), every exposed edge chamfered 1 to 2 dmm, each right-hand course a little proud of or behind the one below, no back slabs, and the surface is flat faces scored with thin V cracks (0.5 dmm half-width, 0.4 deep, near-vertical, one branch now and then). No relief facets. Still off: the cracks are short and scratchy where the panel has long branching ones that cross a face and meet the edge; their sides are stair-stepped (they come from a 0.2 dmm grid, not a strip along the crack); no small pebbles on the tops; the wall is lower than the panel's; no deep recess shadow line between courses.
