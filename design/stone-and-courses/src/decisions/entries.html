Joints and seals

Joints made by insetting blocks

15 to 16 Nov 2026

Tried
Shrink every stone to leave a gap for the joint.
Chosen
Stones meet at their planes; the rock’s wear opens the groove.
Why
Insetting moves a stone off the plane it has to meet.

Mirrored sites to make faces

15 to 16 Nov 2026

Tried
Mirror the sites across the footprint so cells reach the face.
Chosen
Clip the cells to the footprint, then cut faces (step 3).
Why
Mirrored sites carve holes at re-entrant corners and make faces recede from the footprint.

Exact shared planes between neighbours

15 to 16 Nov 2026

Tried
Neighbours share a plane exactly.
Chosen
A 0.15 mm plan overlap at joints.
Why
Coincident faces break the union.

A seal under every stone

16 Nov 2026

Tried
A bed sink and an overlap along the row planes.
Chosen
No seal: stones meet exactly at their planes.
Why
The seal kept the gate green but added a slab at every row plane. The cost is thin overhang slivers where two outlines differ by a hair; they are listed under “Where the model breaks”.

Roofs over joints

16 Nov 2026

Tried
A lean-to wedge roofing the top of every V between stones.
Chosen
The joint is only the groove the rock’s wear leaves.
Why
The roofs looked wrong and were removed at Shawn’s request.

The rock’s mesh

The sampled mesh straight into the union

17 Nov 2026

Tried
Hand the rock’s surface-net mesh to the union as it came.
Chosen
The mesher takes the two outside corners of an alternating grid face in, so every mesh is a closed manifold.
Why
Surface nets leave an edge shared by four faces where a grid face alternates inside and outside, and the union refuses it.

“The union is the only expensive step”

17 Nov 2026

Tried
Describing the cost that way.
Chosen
The union is the largest step, not the only one.
Why
The levels together cost about a quarter of it. Current numbers are on the page, from the sweep.

Rules for what the rock may do on a wall

Rounding a held bed edge

17 Nov 2026

Tried
Wear rounds every edge, the held bed included.
Chosen
A held face that leans down is not rounded.
Why
A rounded held bed hangs an arc past 55 degrees at every joint (0.2 to 1 % of the downward area).

An outline cut into a held top or bed

17 Nov 2026

Tried
Cuts anywhere on the stone.
Chosen
A cut may not touch a held face that faces up or down, nor make a pocket no air reaches. The cuts stage has preserve top, sides and bottom.
Why
A cut into a held top left the stone above a flat roof over a notch (17 % red on preset L).

Chips and chunks on a wall stone

17 Nov 2026

Tried
Chips and chunks in the wall’s default rock.
Chosen
Both stay in the panel and are off in every wall.
Why
A chip is a plane and an ellipsoid, and the ellipsoid’s underside is a roof; a chunk set back under the one above is a ledge. Both fail the gate (3 to 50 % red).

Grain and pockets on a chamfer

17 Nov 2026

Tried
Grain and pockets everywhere.
Chosen
They fade out on a face that leans down past 37 degrees.
Why
A face the facing rule leaves at 48 degrees was roughened past 55.

A chamfer along a lip edge

17 Nov 2026

Tried
Size the chamfer from the lip’s own edge.
Chosen
Chamfer from each supporting cell’s top outline (step 9).
Why
It was sized from the wrong support.

The rock cut and chamfered per wall preset

17 Nov 2026

Tried
The same rock on every wall.
Chosen
The default (cuts, a little grain, wear) on the plain walls; a spire, a lip or a tilted row loses its cuts.
Why
A cut on a tapering or overhanging cell leaves the stone above a roof over a notch.

One cell, one stone

Splitting a stone to its supports

18 Nov 2026

Tried
Cut each cell into pieces, one for every stone it rests on.
Chosen
A cell is one Voronoi cell. Only a lip row still splits. What a stone bears on is the rock’s business: its bed carries the convex hull of the cut tops below it (a shrink wrap), and a gap inside the hull is a bridge.
Why
Pieces made extra joints and slivers. Bridges are painted blue in the Overhang view and measured apart from the gate; the gate itself is unchanged.

One body again

18 Nov 2026

Chosen
Every wall is one body. Each stone’s top is set when it is built: its row top plus the crest planes. The crest is graded and uses its whole band. The magenta target draws each course’s front line.
Why
Loose bodies were the failure that most often hid under a passing count.

Rock lengths scale with the system

18 Nov 2026

Tried
The wall’s lengths scaled under OpenLOCK (0.7257); the rock’s millimetres did not.
Chosen
Every length of every rock stage scales with the system, and a density per centimetre grows by the same factor. The 57 OpenLOCK builds changed; the 57 DungeonBlocks builds did not.
Why
Under 25.4 mm the stones kept full-size strata, pockets and chips. Gate passes under OpenLOCK moved from 6 to 9 (as the dot judges) and from 33 to 36 (bridges apart).

The editor

Four panels, two pairs

18 Nov 2026

Chosen
Region, Courses, Rock region, Rock: the region and what fills it at the scale of the wall, then at the scale of one stone. Preset, Scale and seed are Setup; the view is the viewer. Four kinds of control: number, range, choice, group.
Why
Every control has one place. A review by Orin ruled on the titles: Region (not Area) because region is the tile language’s word; Courses (not Wall) because wall is an element; Setup (not Build) because build is a verb of the program.

The Failure list

18 Nov 2026

Tried
A second list of four walls turned until the gate refused them.
Chosen
Two of them are presets marked “refuses”; the sweep asserts the gate refuses them. The other two were the walls M and O at their default seed.
Why
A failure is a preset that fails the gate. A heading that says every marked wall fails would have been false: the sweep shows some real walls fail in most builds.

The crest has no on/off

18 Nov 2026

Chosen
The crest is always on; the flat weighting is its plainest form. Its hidden switch and Solo are removed.
Why
There is no wall without a top line, so there is no identity to switch to.

The union lab

18 Nov 2026

Chosen
The union watch and the four-route timing moved from among the controls to a folded Measure tool under the panels.
Why
They read the build and change nothing, so they are not controls; nothing was removed.

Every step as current state

18 Nov 2026

Chosen
Each step is now a one-line rule, a picture drawn by the build or a reference photograph, a short how, a short why, and the controls it reads with their panel names. The text carries no number of its own: constants come from the engine, measurements from the sweep, the Rust baseline and the photograph method from one small hand-kept file with its sources.
Why
Several steps read as the history of how the rule was found. That history is kept below as the old prose.

Words

gap, bevel, facet, tilt and chip

17 to 18 Nov 2026

Chosen
Removed from the wall and its presets. A stone’s weathering is the rock’s six stages, one word each, as on the Weathered Stone page. The 0.6 mm margin from the tile edge, which gap also set, is a constant of the tile; the joint’s lower mouth is another.
Why
The wall had a second way to bevel, facet, tilt and chip a stone. Nothing read the five preset keys.

rise, span, dip and wave; wander

17 Nov 2026

Chosen
Four new words, one meaning each: rise (the chance a stone carries on into the next row), span (the chance neighbours of one height join), dip (the tilt of the row planes) and wave (their waviness). height, bond, setback and regularity are reused. wander (a random height for each stone in its row) is redundant with variation in the planes and can be retired.
Status
These words wait for an Orin review before they enter the registry.

The rock’s region: an area and a top, or a box

17 Nov 2026

Chosen
The box (width, breadth, height) is an area that is a rectangle, a seat and a top at the height; the code makes it that way, so there is one path. The author can still write either.
Status
Flagged for Shawn’s ruling that both stay for now.
