Design check for the four newest words

QuestionAnswer
WordsReused: height (a row’s minimum and maximum), bond, setback, regularity. New, one meaning each: rise (chance a stone carries on into the next row), span (chance neighbours of one height join), dip (tilt of the row planes), wave (waviness of the row planes). The wall’s dip leans the row planes; the rock’s strata dip is not a wall word (the courses are the strata, each stone takes its bed’s lean). These four wait for an Orin review before they enter the registry.
Redundantwander (a random height for each stone in its row): a stone’s top is a plane, and variation belongs to the planes; it can be retired. Not redundant: bond (joints between rows) and regularity (cell shape); rise is not bond at 0, because a carried-up cell keeps a joint and a risen stone does not. The scatter’s clump is a pattern of where things fall, a different thing.
KindsA row plane is data of the build, not a kind a document can name, pass or override. Naming planes (a list of heights and tilts) would make it one; left open.
One meaning, two formsThe rock’s region is an area and a top (the words of at= and top=) or a box, width, breadth, height: the box is an area that is a rectangle, a seat and a top at the height, and the code makes it that way (one path), but the author can still write either. Flagged for Shawn’s ruling that both stay for now.
Removedgap, bevel, facet, tilt and chip: the stone’s weathering is the rock’s six stages (one word each, as on the Weathered Stone page). The wall keeps no second way to bevel, facet, tilt or chip a stone. The 0.6 mm margin from the tile edge, which gap also set, is a constant of the tile (EDGE_MARGIN), and the joint’s lower mouth is the constant MOUTH.
KernelEverything reduces to what exists: a partition per row, a roll per stone from the same seeded stream, a prism cut by two planes.
Second pathsA column of stacked cells (bond near 0) and a risen stone differ only by the groove the rock’s wear opens; wander and plane variation both vary heights.
HolesRise and span on sloped planes; a lip stone that rises.
