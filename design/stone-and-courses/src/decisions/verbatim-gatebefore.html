a-front

The gate, before and after

It fails. Today 50 of 114 builds pass (the same 50 as before this step). With the front band at depth 2 dmm only 2 of 114 pass, with or without the stay-behind rule; the median build overshoots the overhang budget (past 65 degrees: 0.23% of the downward area against 0.1%; 55 to 65 degrees: 0.35%) and 6 builds split into more than one body. The cause is not the cut itself: with the rock switched off the banded wall is at the edge of the budget (preset L: 0.10%), and the wall’s rule for what lies under a bed (step 9: the lower mouth measured from the top of the cell below, with its facets and chips) knows only the old straight front. A stone’s bed now meets the rounded top edge of the stone under it along new edges, and the facet this step adds along them (with no measurement of the stone below) is not enough. Proposed rule: read the lower stone’s actual banded top outline in step 9 (its tsList entry is the cell’s today) so the mouth is sized as it is for a straight edge. Not built; it needs the gate to confirm it.

a-outline

The gate, before and after

It fails, more than the front band. With the outline at strength 0.5, 0 of 114 builds pass (today 50), the median overshoots at 0.53% past 65 degrees and 0.80% from 55 to 65, and 20 builds split into more than one body. The same cause as the front band, at more edges (the support clip gives a stone on a stone a free edge wherever the stone below is smaller), and more parts to a stone (a median of six against one). The table above is the sweep for both stages. Proposed rule: the same lower-mouth rule from the banded outline of the stone below, and parts that are nearly convex are kept as one part.
