The gate passes 50 of 114 builds across 19 presets, both systems and 3 seeds; the nine shipped presets pass 30 of 54. Flat rows are close; tilted and waved rows fail.

  • No seal, by Shawn’s choice. Stones meet exactly at their planes. Where two stones’ outlines differ by a hair (a chamfer or the rock’s wear on either), the union leaves thin down-facing slivers that count as overhang past 65 degrees: 64 builds fail on overhang and 0 on loose bodies; watertight never fails. Passes by preset (DungeonBlocks and OpenLOCK): L wall 1 and 0, B ragged wall 1 and 0, E terrace 3 and 0, M columns 2 and 1, G leaning 3 and 0, O chunky 3 and 3, F sheer wall 3 and 1, A low massif 3 and 1, N masonry wall 3 and 2, D cap course 1 and 0, K deep lip 1 and 0, Lf cantilevers 0 and 0, J corner 3 and 0, J corner, 2x2 2 and 2, C spire 1 and 0, P spire 0 and 0, T tall beside short 2 and 3, R fieldstone, tilted rows 0 and 0, S near slab 3 and 2.
  • A groove has a roof. Wear opens a groove along every joint, and the stone above the groove is a flat ceiling over it. The rock leaves a held bed sharp (the chamfer of step 9 is the roof), but a chip is a cut with its own roof: chips and chunks are off in every preset because they fail the gate (on preset L, chips 8 per cm: 1.6 % red; chunks 0.5: 2.2 % red and two bodies).
  • Cells the rock cannot save. The spires, the lips, the tilted rows and the courses of D, J and R fail the gate with every stage at 0: their cells already hang (the chamfers of step 9 were written to read the facets of the old step 8). The rock is held to no cuts on them.
  • Tilted and waved rows. Every piece of every stone has its bed on a different facet, so the slivers are everywhere: dip 3 or wave 1 and up pass in no build I measured. Row planes draw correctly and blocks follow them.
  • Tall stones that touch only along an edge are loose bodies; they fail whenever the roll makes one.
  • A lip stone never rises, so a tall lip is not reachable. A stone with a hole, or a ring, is not reachable: cells are convex.
  • Planes cannot be named. They are drawn at random; a document cannot give a wall a known rake or a step in its rows.
  • Cost. Many small stones are expensive: R takes 1659 ms for a 1×1 against 822 for L.
  • Photos not yet reached. No preset reproduces the fine surface of the references (cracks, lichen, wear); the rock makes broken faces, rounded edges and grain; the cracks, lichen and staining of the photographs are not reached.
