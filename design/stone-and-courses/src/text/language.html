Design check

  • Words. The registry’s own words are reused (top, width, height, bond, seed). The registry’s depth means the gap between stones, so the cliff’s depth is region depth and the face band’s is face depth. The wall’s dip leans the row planes; the rock’s strata dip is not a wall word, because each stone takes its bed’s lean. A stone’s weathering is the rock’s six stages, one word each; the wall has no second way to bevel, facet, tilt or chip a stone.
  • Kinds. A control is a number, a range, a choice or a group (a stage, a weighting). A preset is a state of those controls, not a kind. A row plane is data of the build, not a kind a document can name, pass or override.
  • Kernel. Everything reduces to a partition per row, a roll per stone from one seeded stream, a prism cut by two planes, the rock, and one graded rule that draws the crest, a course’s front and a stone’s outline.
  • Second paths, left visible. Two rules share a range: the skew of width and height (a power law) and the weighting of the crest, front and outline. A column of stacked cells (bond near 0) and a risen stone differ only by the groove the rock’s wear opens. Wander and plane variation both vary heights.
  • Holes. Rise and span on sloped planes; a lip stone that rises; naming the row planes.
