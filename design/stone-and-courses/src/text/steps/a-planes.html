Step 2 (L1) The row planes

It draws every row boundary for the whole wall before a single stone exists.

Goal

Decide where every row of stones starts and stops before a single stone exists, so that stones of any width and height can follow the same lines.

How

First the rows. Row heights are drawn one at a time between the two ends of height (the thinnest and the thickest a row may be) until they reach the highest crest height (the larger end of top), and are scaled together to fit it exactly, so there are courses up to the top of the arch; where the crest is lower, the stones of the upper courses are built inside the envelope and left out if too short (step 6). The rows are then stretched a little so they add up exactly.

Then the planes. Each boundary between two rows is a plane through the whole wall. It starts level at the sum of the rows below it. dip tilts it by up to that many degrees in a random direction. wave adds two random sinusoids of random direction and a wavelength between 30 and 90 mm, plus a third as much value noise. The ground plane stays flat.

No two planes may come closer than 0.6 of the row's height anywhere in the wall. If they would, the upper plane's tilt and waves are scaled down until they don't. A plane is not a smooth surface: it is laid on a grid of flat 12 mm triangles, so every stone that stands on one triangle shares exactly the same plane.

Last, the sites: the centres of the stones. They sit on a grid with spacing set by the middle of width. regularity decides how far they are shaken from the grid (0.45 of the spacing at 0, none at 1) and how far every other row is offset (half a stone at 1). From one row to the next, bond is the chance a site is moved instead of carried straight up; shift is how far it moves.

Takes in
height, width, regularity, bond, shift, dip, wave, the crest
Hands on
the row planes; the sites of every row
Deep numbers: the constants
  • row height drawn with the weighted range height (third number: bias), never under 4 mm; the last row is dropped if that brings the sum nearer to the crest mean; then all rows are scaled by mean crest / sum;
  • plane facets are 12 mm triangles on a grid anchored at (-90, -90) mm;
  • a plane's tilt magnitude is a uniform draw between 0 and tan(dip); the two waves have amplitude 0.7 and 0.4 of wave times a draw of 0.5 to 1.5;
  • carried: probability (1 - bond) x (0.4 + 1.2 x a draw), at most 1; bond runs from its first number at the foot to its second at the crest;
  • a moved site stays inside the footprint and at least half a spacing from another.

Printing

Beds and tops that follow a tilted plane are overhangs of up to the tilt. The gate says how far this can go: tilts and waves run into trouble when stones no longer meet exactly (see where the model breaks).
