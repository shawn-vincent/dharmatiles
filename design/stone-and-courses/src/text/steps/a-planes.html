Step 2 (L1) The row planes

Every row boundary is drawn for the whole wall before a single stone exists.

Row heights are drawn between the two ends of height (with height skew) until they reach the highest crest, then scaled to fit it exactly. Each boundary between two rows is a plane through the whole wall: dip tilts it, wave adds two waves and a little noise, and no two planes may come closer than a share of a row. A plane is laid on a grid of flat triangles, so every stone that stands on one triangle shares exactly that plane. The stone sites sit on a grid set by the middle of width; regularity decides how far they are shaken from it; from one row to the next, bond is the chance a site moves and shift is how far.

Why. A stone that follows a plane has a flat bed exactly on the top below it, which is what keeps stacked stones from hanging in air.
