Step 2 (L1) The row planes

Every row boundary is drawn for the whole wall before a single stone exists.

Row heights are drawn between the two ends of height (with height skew) until they reach the highest crest, then scaled to fit it exactly. Each boundary between two rows is a plane through the whole wall: lean (side to side, the slope along the wall) and tilt (front to back, the slope into it) turn it, each drawn from its own range in degrees across the planes up the wall by one weighting, so the rows run from a low angle to a high one (a range may be negative); wave adds two waves and a little noise on top. The ground plane stays flat. No two planes may come closer than 0.6 of a row: the ranges are held to the angles that keep that true, before anything is drawn, and the card says when a range was limited and to what. A plane is laid on a grid of flat triangles, so every stone that stands on one triangle shares exactly that plane. The stone sites sit on a grid set by the middle of width; regularity decides how far they are shaken from it; from one row to the next, bond is the chance a site moves and shift is how far.

Why. A stone that follows a plane has a flat bed exactly on the top below it, which is what keeps stacked stones from hanging in air.
