Step 5 (L2) Span: neighbours join

Neighbours that stand between the same two planes roll to become one stone.

After the stones have risen, two neighbours may join if they start on the same row and end on the same row. Each such pair rolls against span. A pair that joins is one stone: the edge between them is no joint and no free face, and the rock is built once for the whole group, from the union of their cells, with a crack where they met that no cut or wear takes. The group shares one top (the highest of its cells); each cell keeps the bed it stands on. At 0 every pair stays apart; at 1 every pair that can join does, and a course between two lips can be one long stone.

Why. A wide stone is one rock, as a tall stone (rise) is: there is no groove across it and wear, grain and cuts run over the whole of it. It is the same by construction in both directions; the gate judges the result.
