Step 5 (L2) Span: neighbours join

Neighbours that stand between the same two planes roll to become one stone.

After the stones have risen, two neighbours may join if they start on the same row and end on the same row. Each such pair rolls against span. A pair that joins loses the joint between them: the edge is still held, but it gets no groove. The two cells stay two convex solids that overlap by {{OVL}} mm. At 0 every pair stays apart; at 1 every pair that can join does, down to a near slab.

Why. Fewer joints mean fewer grooves; a wide stone is still made of convex pieces, so the union joins them and the gate judges the result.
