Step 4 (L2) Rise: a stone carries on

A stone rolls, again and again, to carry on up through the next row.

Goal

Let some stones be tall: one stone that stands through several rows, beside stones of one row.

How

Every stone starts in one row. It then rolls against rise: if the roll succeeds, the stone carries on into the next row, and rolls again. It stops at the first failed roll or at the top of the wall. At 0 every stone is one row; near 1 stones run all the way up.

A stone that rises keeps its footprint in the rows above (step 3 cuts the other cells round it) and its top is the top plane of the last row it reaches. A stone on a lip row never rises.

Takes in
the cells of a row, rise
Hands on
each stone's last row; a list of footprints that the rows above must leave alone
Deep numbers: what rise does to the count

Each stone rolls once per row it reaches, so with rise r the chance a stone spans k rows is rk-1(1-r). At 0.6 about 4 stones in 10 carry on at least once, and 1 in 6 reaches three rows. Preset L has 171 solids and a 153 ms union at rise 0; preset T (rise 0.6, span 0.3) has 115 and 80 ms.

Printing

A tall stone is one convex piece, so it is never a bridge. Beside it, the stones of the rows above rest on the stones below as before.
