Step 3a (L2) The front band

The front of a course is a band between today's front and one depth behind it, drawn course by course by the graded rule.

Goal

Give the front of the cliff a depth: instead of one straight line every course is cut to, the front of a course is an area band between today’s front (the inner line) and a second line depth behind it (the outer line), and each course draws its own edge across the band from its own keyed seed, so courses differ. Depth 0 is today’s straight front, exactly.

How

One rule draws every outline from a weighting (this step, along a line, and step 7a, round a circle). Make a smooth wobble along the outline, with a bumps control for how many wobbles. Rank it: sort the points by wobble value and give point i of N the rank (i + 0.5) / N. Map each rank through the weighting’s quantile to get t, a place in the gap from 0 to 1. A point’s distance is inner + gap × t. The weighting says how far and bumps says how many, so every edge has exactly the weighting’s mix of distances; only where they fall changes. It is the rule a dirt top already uses (Band::rolled, a ranked smooth field, with bumpSize as the how-many control), bent along a line.

Here the inner line is the course’s front as steps 1 to 3 made it (the footprint’s front edge moved by the course’s setback), the gap is depth, and the wobble runs along the front with bumps per square bumps for every 35 mm. The points are the nodes of one line shared by every course (about six to a bump); the edge between them is straight. The seed is the wall’s, the step and the course, so a course’s edge never depends on another course’s.

Where it is applied. The cells of step 3 stay the convex cells they are; each stone is cut to its course’s edge when its cell is handed to the rock (step 8). The cut leaves a front that is a polyline, so the stone’s area may not be convex: it is split into convex parts, which the rock already takes, the edge two parts share being a crack inside the stone (never a joint with a neighbour). A joint is never moved: it is trimmed where the front now meets it, on its own line.

Overhang

A course may reach out beyond the course below it. Where it does, its bed has nothing under it, and the gate sees a flat roof. The rule stay behind the course below (on by default) says a course never stands in front of the course under it where the unbanded wall does not either: node by node the edge is the larger of the course’s own line and the one below it, and because every course’s line has the same nodes, each edge is above the one under it everywhere, not only at the nodes. A lip course, which stands out on purpose, is left alone. The cost is honest: the running maximum means the upper courses stand further back, so over a tall wall the front trends toward the outer line and the variation shrinks. With the rule off each course draws its own line and overhangs where it is further out than the one below.

Each stone’s bed edge also needs the 48 degree mouth the wall cuts along a free edge (step 9’s rule), so a bed never starts in front of the rounded top of the stone under it; a stone with a new free edge gets that facet along it.

Reading the weightings

What each weighting should look like, against what the page draws (plan of course 0, the band shaded, depth 3 dmm, 6 bumps per square). Left: the course’s stones cut to the band. Right: where the points fall in the gap, from the inner line to the outer.

low “mostly small, with maybe a few big lurches out”
high “mostly large, with a few lurches in”
ends “in, out, in, out, not much in the middle”
bell “mostly in the middle of the range, not a lot of extremes”

How it reads. All four rows read as Shawn describes them. Low: the edge lies on the inner line for most of the front, with a few narrow lurches back toward the outer line. High: mostly on the outer line, with a few dips in toward the inner. Ends: the edge is on one line or the other and crosses the middle quickly. Bell: a soft wobble about the middle with few extremes. The histograms are the weighting’s own mix, exactly, because the values are ranked. What is rough: at 6 bumps on a 35 mm square a lurch is only 1.5 to 3 mm wide, so low and ends look spiky from the front; fewer bumps make broader steps.
