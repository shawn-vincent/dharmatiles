Step 9 (L2 to L4) Fitting to what is below

Where a stone stands over a cut-back edge, it is chamfered so nothing hangs in air.

Goal

Make sure no stone hangs in air: where a stone stands over a stone that has been cut back, give it a chamfer to match.

Stacking on the cut tops

The rock cuts a stone’s top like any other free face, so the top a stone offers the next course is less than its cell. A stone is built after the stones it rests on, and it bears on the shrink wrap of what they leave: the convex hull of the upward faces of their built meshes, intersected with the stone’s own cell. Holes and gaps between the supports inside the hull are bridged, and the stone is one piece, not one piece for each stone under it. A hull is always convex, so the outline the bed must lie within (bear, step 8) is one convex polygon and nothing is thrown away. Wherever the cell reaches past the hull, the bottom-cut kind of cut, at an angle drawn the same way and never more than 30 degrees below horizontal, takes the bed in. A stone with nothing under it, or with a hull under 4 mm² or 15% of its cell (half of a cell under 8 mm²), is not built. A bridge’s underside is flat and faces down, so the gate counts it as overhang; the editor paints it blue and says so in the legend, and the gate popup lists it. Rise changes nothing here: with every top free the cuts reach the same faces whether stones carry on into the next row or not.

How

This is not a step of its own: it runs inside steps 3 to 8.

  • Beds settle. A stone's bed is the highest top of the stones under it, and all of them are raised to match, until none moves.
  • The mouth. At each free edge the stone looks at the stones under it. If their top stops short of its edge, the edge is chamfered at 48 degrees from vertical (53 if the stone below stops more than 0.6 mm short). If it stops more than 0.6 mm short and the bed would stay at least 1 mm², the edge is instead cut back vertically.
  • The top outline (R9). Wherever the top outline of a stone below has no other stone's top beyond it, the bed is chamfered from that outline at 51 degrees from vertical, so a corner the rock takes from the top edge of the stone below never leaves a flat ceiling.
  • Lips (R8). A lip stone is cut into pieces over its supports, each chamfered from its support's outline the same way.
  • Tilted planes. On a tilted bed the chamfer planes are tilted with it, and eased by the bed's slope so they stay within the overhang band.

If none of these can make the edge safe (an overhang over 1.2 mm that cannot be cut back), nothing is done, and the gate reports it.

Examples

Deep numbers: the chamfers
  • mouth chamfer: 48 degrees from vertical (53 if the cell below stops more than 0.6 mm behind the edge); cut back vertically instead if the bed stays at least 1 mm²;
  • top outline and lip chamfers: 51 degrees from vertical; reduced on a sloped bed by 1.1 degrees for each degree of slope, so they stay under 55 degrees;
  • no chamfer is made if the bed would drop under 0.5 mm².

Printing

These rules are why a wall of stacked stones passes the gate at all. A flat bed over air is the most common failure when a rule is switched off (the walls marked "refuses" in the Preset list show it).
