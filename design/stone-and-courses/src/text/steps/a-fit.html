Step 9 (L2 to L4) Fitting to what is below

Where a stone stands over a stone that has been cut back, its bed is chamfered so that nothing hangs in air.

This is not a step of its own: it runs inside steps 3 to 8. Beds settle: a stone’s bed is the highest top of the stones under it, and they are raised to match until none moves. At each free edge the stone looks at the stones under it. If their top stops short of its edge, the edge is chamfered, steeper than the overhang limit, so the flat underside never starts in front of where the stone below stops. Where the stone below stops further back than the chamfer can reach and the bed would stay large enough, the edge is cut back vertically instead. Where a stone below has no other top beyond its outline, the bed is chamfered from that outline, and a lip stone is chamfered from each supporting stone’s outline. On a tilted bed the chamfers tilt with it. The rock does the same at the edges of a part it adds (the joint’s lower mouth, {{MOUTH}} mm deep).

Why. These rules are why a wall of stacked stones passes the gate at all. When a chamfer cannot make an edge safe nothing is done and the gate reports it; the walls marked “refuses” in the Preset list show what a switched-off rule costs.
