Step 7 (L3) Joints

It decides which faces of a stone are joints and which neighbours are one stone; it cuts nothing.

Goal

Decide, for every face of every stone, whether it is a joint shared with a neighbour or open to the air. Nothing is cut here.

How

Step 3 already marked every edge of every cell: a joint edge is shared with a neighbour, a free edge is open to the air, and an edge between two cells that step 5 merged is joined, not a joint at all. This step only reads those marks and hands them on. The neighbours touch behind the face: each cell overlaps the next by 0.15 mm in plan, so the union has no coincident faces.

The V you see in a finished wall is not carved here. It is the groove the rock’s wear opens in step 8 where a stone’s surface meets a joint face: a joint face is held by the neighbour, so the rock never cuts it and wear rounds only its edge with the surface. That keeps this step simple: change which faces are joints and the weathering follows.

Takes in
the cells with their edge tags (step 3), the merges (step 5)
Hands on
the same cells, with every edge classed as joint, free or joined

Printing

Nothing is cut, so nothing here can fail. The overlap of 0.15 mm keeps neighbouring stones one body; where they overlap they are not a joint at all.
