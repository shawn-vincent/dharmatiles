Step 7 (L3) Joints

It decides which faces of a stone are joints and which neighbours are one stone; it cuts nothing.

Step 3 marked every edge of every cell: a joint is shared with a neighbour, a free edge is open to the air, and an edge between cells that step 5 merged is joined. This step reads those marks and hands them on. The neighbours touch behind the face, each overlapping the next by {{OVL}} mm in plan. The V in a finished wall is not carved here: it is the groove the rock’s wear opens in step 8 where a surface meets a joint face. A joint face is held by the neighbour, so the rock never cuts it and wear rounds only its edge.

Why. Change which faces are joints and the weathering follows; nothing is written twice.
