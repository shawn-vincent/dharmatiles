Step 1 (L0) The footprint and the crest

It fixes where the wall may stand and how high it may rise.

Goal

Fix the box the wall must fill: where it stands, and how high it rises. Everything later is measured against it.

How

The tile is a square of 35 mm (DungeonBlocks) or 25.4 mm (OpenLOCK, 0.7257 of it). A wall of Run 2x1 is two squares long. The wall keeps 0.6 mm (EDGE_MARGIN, a constant of the tile) inside the tile edge so two tiles side by side never touch.

The footprint is the rectangle that is left: from that margin at the sides, from 9 mm behind the front edge, back by depth (22 mm by default). A preset with a wing makes the footprint an L, so a corner can be built.

The crest is the height of the top as you walk along the wall. It is graded, the same rule that draws the front band and a stone’s outline: a smooth wobble along the wall with a bumps control (2.5 a square, about one every 14 mm), ranked (sort by value, rank (i + 0.5) / N), and each rank mapped through a weighting’s quantile to a place in the band between the two ends of top. The crest therefore uses its whole band, ends included, whatever the seed (top 11 to 64 reaches 11.9 to 63.1 dmm on a 1×1; the value noise it replaces reached 28 to 55 on one seed and 43 to 56 on another), and the wobble only decides where the highs and lows fall. The weighting is flat by default (the band used evenly); low, high, bell, peak and ends share the heights as they do for the front band. The dirt is 3.4 mm thick; the crest is measured above it.

Takes in
top, depth, Run, Scale, a wing (J presets)
Hands on
a footprint polygon, the crest function, the tile size

Examples

Deep numbers: where the footprint sits

The front of the footprint is 9 mm behind the tile edge (preset front), the depth is 22 mm, the margin is 0.6 mm. On OpenLOCK every length is 0.7257 of its DungeonBlocks value; the 0.45 mm printer line does not shrink, so the same wall has thinner features relative to the line.

Printing

Nothing is printed yet. The margin is what makes tiles fit: the tile plane is the limit the gate and the printer both respect.

The target

The magenta target is drawn from the same functions the build uses. It is the footprint’s wall up to the crest; with the front band on (depth above 0), it also shows the front: each course’s own graded front edge between today’s front and the outer front, as a stepped loft from the ground up to the crest (a line and a faint face for every course). With the band off the target is the plain wall as before. The setback of each course is not drawn.

The whole band

Realized crest above the dirt, top 11 to 64 on a 1×1: 11.9 to 63.1 dmm at every seed (the value noise gave 28 to 55, 19 to 47 and 43 to 56). With the low end at 10 and the high end raised from 30 to 80, the realized low was 16.4, 19.7, 22.9, 26.1, 29.3 and 32.6 (it rose with the high end, as value noise does) and the realized high 26.5 to 67.8; now the low stays 10.4 to 11.2 and the high 29.7 to 78.8. There is no clamp in the build: the crest follows the dial to its maximum of 80. There was one in the picture: the viewer’s frame was fixed at 82 mm high, so a crest above about 62 dmm ran off the top of the canvas and the target seemed to stop changing; the frame now grows with the high end of top. Over the 114-build sweep the gate as it is passes 20 (22 before) and, with bridges left out, 76 (81 before); two builds (preset F, seed 3, and preset R, seed 1, both DungeonBlocks) are now two bodies; stones the rock drops, mean 0.62 (0.09 before), since the presets’ crests now use their whole bands.
