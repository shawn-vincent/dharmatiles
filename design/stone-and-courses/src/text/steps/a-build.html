Step 6 (L2) Building a stone

Each stone becomes a solid between the plane under it and the plane over it.

Goal

Turn every cell into a solid: a stone with a flat bed on the plane under it and a top on the plane over it.

How

A cell is a polygon. The stone is the prism of that polygon, from its bed to its top. The bed is the top of whatever is under it. The top is the bed plus the row's height (or the sum of the rows it rises through). wander changes each stone's height at random by up to its own share of the row; at 0 every stone ends exactly on its plane. A stone is never under 4 mm tall.

Every stone is given its top when it is built: its row’s top plane (or, for a stone that rises, the top plane of the last row it reaches) intersected with the cliff’s envelope, the footprint extruded up to the crest. The crest reaches the stone as planes it must lie under, one of three sets, whichever takes least off the stone: chords of the crest across its footprint (about every 2 mm), the one chord between its ends, or a flat plane at the lowest crest under it, each lowered by however far it stands above the crest anywhere on it (chords follow a hump closely but, in a steep valley, their extensions cut below the crest and can leave nothing of the stone); so the stone never rises above the crest (the sampled mesh is at most 0.5 mm over it, the sampling step) and follows it from below; a stone the planes would leave empty is not built; a part of a top lowered this way is a free surface, not a held joint. The stone is built inside that top and nothing is cut off it afterwards. A stone whose height, its top less its bed averaged along its footprint, is under the minimum stone height (4 mm times the scale, the least a row may be) is not built.

If the planes are tilted or waved, the prism is cut by the plane under it and the plane over it. A stone that stands on more than one 12 mm facet of a plane is made of one piece for each facet, each cut by that facet's own plane. The stone above is cut by the same facets, so its bed is exactly the top below it. There is no sink and no seal: stones meet exactly at the planes. The 0.15 mm overlap between neighbours at a joint (step 3) stays.

The cell

This prism (or its pieces on a tilted plane) is the stone’s region: its area (the Voronoi outline of its column on the ground, irregular in plan) carried up from its bed to its top, the plane where the stone stops. Step 8 hands that area and top to the rock, which works inside it as the Weathered Stone page works inside a box. Why the stones read square: every front face is the footprint’s one straight front line between two row planes, so from the front each stone is a rectangle; its irregular outline shows only in plan (cells fill 0.77 of their bounding box) and at the ends, and the rock then only breaks its corners. The editor draws the cells as soon as they are made (every stage of the rock at 0 is the cell, exactly), then each stone as its rock arrives.

Printing

A flat bed on the stone below is the best a printer can have. A held bed is also the one face the rock never rounds, cuts or chips. A bed that is not exactly the stone below it leaves a flat ceiling, which the gate counts as overhang past 65 degrees (see where the model breaks).

Filling to the crest

Preset L as a 2×1 wall with top 27 to 69 dmm, the target outline on, seen from the front. Before, the courses were laid up to the mean crest and a stone was raised to the crest only within 6 mm of it, judged at its centre alone, so the wall stopped flat and the high part of the arch stayed empty. Now the courses are laid up to the highest crest and scaled to fit it, every stone is built with its top already inside the envelope, and a stone too short under it is left out, so the courses form a stepped mound under the arch and the peaks fill.

Preset L, 1×1, seed 3, top 27 to 69: 32 stones in four courses before (top at 57.8 dmm), 36 stones in five after (top at 65.6 dmm, of a highest crest of 65.9); no mesh rises above the crest by more than 0.01 mm, and the wall’s top lies a mean 1.9 mm under the crest across its width. The gate over the 114-build sweep: as it is 22 (as before), with bridges left out 81 (72 before); every build is one body; stones the rock drops, mean 0.09 (0.22 before). Median draft rock 514 ms against 519, union and gate 599 against 615 (the mean number of stones went from 37.9 to 36.4: preset D −6, K −5, G −4, J2 −6, R +4, J +3). A bed with a bear outline is always cut to it, even with the cuts stage at 0, so presets with the cuts off (S, T, C, J, P, R) are cut to their support too.
