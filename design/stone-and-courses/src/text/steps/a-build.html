Step 6 (L2) Building a stone

Each stone becomes a solid between the plane under it and the plane over it.

A cell is a polygon and the stone is its prism, from the bed to the top. The bed is the top of whatever is under it; the top is the bed plus the row’s height, or the sum of the rows it rises through. wander changes each stone’s height at random by up to its share of the row; at 0 every stone ends on its plane. Every stone gets its top when it is built: its row’s top plane intersected with the cliff’s envelope, the footprint carried up to the crest, so no stone rises above the crest and none is cut afterwards. A stone the crest leaves under the minimum stone height ({{MINSTONE}} mm at the {{SQUARE}} mm square) is not built. On tilted or waved planes a stone that stands on several facets is one piece for each facet, each cut by that facet’s own plane. Wall on mirrors the wall onto the back.

The prism is the stone’s region: its area carried up from its bed to its top. Step 8 hands that area and top to the rock, which works inside it as the Weathered Stone page works inside a box. The editor draws the cells as soon as they are made, then each stone as its rock arrives.

Why. A bed that is exactly the stone below leaves no flat ceiling, which the gate would count as overhang. There is no seal: stones meet exactly at their planes.
