Step 8 (L4) The rock

Every stone becomes the rock: its cell, weathered by six stages, each of which does nothing at 0.

Each stone’s cell is handed to the rock with the rock’s settings (the six stages of Weathered Stone, each with a strength, zero meaning off) and a seed; the rock hands back the stone’s surface as a closed mesh. It is the same code as the Weathered Stone page, one copy. The region it takes is an area and a top: the stone’s outline in plan, a bed under it and a top over it, each a plane. A face is held when something outside the stone holds it (the seat, or a joint); a held face is never rounded when it leans down, never chipped, and grain and pockets fade out on any face that leans down.

The cuts stage has three limits, preserve top, preserve sides and preserve bottom: how much of the stone, measured in from that face, no cut may reach. A bed carries bear, the convex hull of the cut tops of the stones below, intersected with the stone’s area: a hull is convex, so it is one polygon, and the cuts bring the bed inside it. A gap inside the hull is bridged; the bridge’s underside is flat and faces down, so the gate counts it, and the viewer paints it blue. A stone with nothing worth standing on is not built. Quality sets the sampling: draft or fine.

Why. The groove between two stones is the rock’s wear, opening where a surface meets a face a neighbour holds, so the wall has no second way to bevel, facet, tilt or chip a stone.
