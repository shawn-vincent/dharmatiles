Step 7a (L4) The stone outline

Each stone's footprint lies between a core and its cell, drawn by the same graded rule round a circle; joints never move.

Goal

Draw each stone’s own footprint instead of its whole cell: the outline lies between a core (a share of the disc that fits inside the cell) and the cell itself, so the stone is no longer the Voronoi polygon with flat faces. Strength 0 hands on the cell exactly, as today.

How

The same rule as step 3a, round a circle. Points are placed round the stone (every vertex and the free edges cut into pieces, about six to a bump, at least 24 round the stone); each has a wobble value, a smooth periodic function of its angle with bumps whole bumps round the stone, from the stone’s own keyed seed. The values are ranked ((i + 0.5) / N over the points that can move), mapped through the weighting’s quantile to t, and each point goes to core + (cell − core) × (1 − strength × (1 − t)) along its ray from the stone’s centre. t = 1 is the cell, t = 0 the core. The weighting says how far, bumps says how many.

Only free edges move. A face a neighbour holds (a joint) is never moved: its end points stay where they are, and the outline eases back to the cell over the last stretch before one so that two neighbours meet at the same corner. The outline is the area the rock is built in. It can be non-convex: it is split into convex parts (ear clipping, then neighbours joined while they stay convex), the rock’s own form for a cell of several parts, the edge two parts share a crack inside the stone, and the stone is cut at its row planes as before.

Held by what holds it

A stone is cut to what holds it: its area is intersected, in plan, with the union of the final areas of the stones it rests on (computed course by course from the ground up), so a bed is always over material and no stone hangs over a smaller one. The shared edge of the two is then a free edge on both, and each gets the joint’s lower mouth (step 9’s facet) along it. Two small rules in the rock keep the parts of one stone one stone: the corner cut of the cuts stage is refused where it would meet a crack between parts (the cap rule, as it already is for a held top or bed).

Reading the weightings

Plan of course 0 on preset L at strength 1, 6 bumps, core 0.35. The dashed outline is the cell. On the right, where the points fall between the core (0) and the cell (1).

low “mostly small, with maybe a few big lurches out”
high “mostly large, with a few lurches in”
ends “in, out, in, out, not much in the middle”
bell “mostly in the middle of the range, not a lot of extremes”

How it reads. The mix of distances is exactly the weighting’s, and the shapes are what each weighting is meant to give: low mostly small stones with a few long spikes out to the cell; high mostly full stones with a few notches in; ends deep notches and full spikes with little between; bell an even ragged edge half way between core and cell. Two limits. Only a stone’s free edges move, and in course 0 those are the front, the back and the two ends: stones in the middle of the wall are all joints and do not change. And at strength 1 and 6 bumps the outline is spiky (about six points to a bump); a lower strength, fewer bumps or a larger core is calmer.
