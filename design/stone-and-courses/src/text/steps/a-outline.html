Step 7a (L4) The stone outline

Each stone’s footprint lies between a core and its cell, drawn by the same graded rule round a circle; joints never move.

The same rule as step 3a. Points are placed round the stone, each with a wobble value that is a smooth function of its angle with bumps whole bumps round it. The values are ranked, mapped through the weighting’s quantile, and each point goes to core plus (cell minus core) times (one minus strength times (one minus t)) along its ray from the stone’s centre: t of one is the cell, zero the core. Strength 0 hands on the cell exactly. Only free edges move; a joint keeps its end points and the outline eases back to the cell before one, so two neighbours meet at the same corner. The outline can be non-convex, so it is split into convex parts, which the rock takes; the edge two parts share is a crack inside the stone.

A stone is cut to what holds it: its area is intersected, in plan, with the final areas of the stones it rests on, course by course from the ground up, so a bed is always over material.

Why. The stone is no longer the Voronoi polygon with flat faces. Only free edges move, so a stone in the middle of a wall (all joints) does not change; a lower strength, fewer bumps or a larger core is calmer.
