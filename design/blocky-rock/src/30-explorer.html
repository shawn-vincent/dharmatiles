4 · How a rock is built

Lobes, rounded off, battered, cracked

Four stages of one rock. Then the cost, measured in this browser.

  1. Lobes. A rock is one to four lobes: boxes, or ramps, standing on the ground and touching along joints. They share an outline and, except for a step, a top level. Nothing is straight: each outer side has its own lean and its own twist in plan, the top its own slight slope, and every joint is angled a few degrees and unevenly spaced. Each lobe is a convex solid held as planes.
  2. Chamfers. Every convex edge of every lobe is rounded off by two or three planes, each tangent to a circle whose radius is set from the edge's own line, tilted so the width changes along the edge. The band is narrow on tops, so tops stay flat; joint edges get a fifth of the radius, so the crease between two lobes is a shallow V. A plane that would leave a sliver is left out. Inside corners are never rounded.
  3. Wobble and battered corners. A few planes at 0.5 to 3 degrees break the large faces. Then 0 to 3 chunks of one to three planes are knocked off outer corners and edges, 0.05 to 0.11 fh deep, after the chamfers so their edges stay broken; a chunk facing down is at most 50 degrees from vertical.
  4. Cracks. Some rocks are cut right through along one or two planes (a zigzag), tilted a few degrees and never parallel to a side, with no chamfer and no V. The far side is moved 0.008 to 0.02 fh apart and up to 0.03 along, so the crack shows. Every piece keeps its flat bottom on the ground. A crack that leaves a sliver is dropped.
  5. Mesh. Each piece is a closed convex mesh (welded, each edge in two triangles, steepest underside checked); the rock is its pieces together. Normals are smoothed across bends under 40 degrees for display only.

Nothing is a voxel or a field: every face is an exact plane. Where a crack and a lobe joint are both possible, cracks make the thin slits and lobe joints make the shallow creases and steps; the sheet has both (R1C8 is lobes, R3C3 and R4C1 are cracks).
