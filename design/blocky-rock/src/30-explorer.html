4 · How a rock is built

One block, every edge chamfered

Two stages of one rock. Then the cost, measured in this browser.

  1. The block. The base shape (slab, block, pillar or ramp) as six planes, each side tilted a little off square by its own amount, so no two sides are parallel, and the top with its own slight slope. The bottom is flat.
  2. A strip along every edge. Each edge becomes a ruled strip of 24 short segments between a line on one face and a line on the other. The width follows a smooth random curve along the edge, so it swells and narrows, and so does the tilt, so the strip twists; the strip also bows a little along its length, in or out. Top edges lie 8 to 27 degrees below horizontal with a short drop down the side, bottom edges rise 60 to 69 degrees, and side edges take any angle from 18 to 72 degrees, leaning into one face or the other. The chamfers on one face together never take more than a third of its width, so the rock stays the same block.
  3. Faces and corners. Every face is also curved a little both ways, a shallow dome or saddle, from slightly concave to slightly convex, and the chamfer lines lie on the curved faces. Each strip stops short of the corner by the neighbours' legs, and a small patch, bulged toward the old corner, joins the three strips' ends and the three faces' insets.
  4. Mesh. Faces, strips and corner patches share their vertices, so the mesh is closed by construction (every edge in two triangles). Normals are smoothed across bends under 40 degrees for display only. About 3,600 triangles, 1 to 3 ms.

Off for this round, kept in the code (makeRock(seed, { full: true })): lobes and steps, cracks, battered corners, kink planes, and the print rule (reported as "steepest underside", not enforced).
