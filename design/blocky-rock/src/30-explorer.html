4 · How a rock is built

One mass, cut

Four stages of one rock (found by scanning for one with a step, a split and a chip). Then the cost, measured in this browser.

  1. The mass. One convex solid held as planes: four sides that lean a little, a top that slopes only for a ramp, a flat bottom. Slab, block, pillar and ramp are the same solid with different proportions.
  2. Chamfers. Each edge is read off its two faces and cut by three to five planes whose widths are straight lines along the edge, so the widest wins and the width wanders with creases between facets.
  3. Facets, kinks and chips. More planes on the same solid: a few degrees off a side face (a ramp's slope may take one too, a flat top never), and skew corner flakes. Still convex.
  4. Cuts. Concave pieces are taken out of the solid by convex cutters: a V-groove (two planes, with a tapering end) scored into a face and sometimes on across the top, and a bite out of a top corner for a step. Faces are split at the cutters' planes, what lies inside is dropped, and the cutters' own faces close the cut. A groove never divides the rock; a bite is one corner shelf.
  5. Mesh. Weld the corners, repair T-junctions, fan the polygons into triangles, count the edges (each must have two) and find the steepest face facing down; a rock that fails is drawn again from the same seed.

Nothing is a voxel or a field: every face is an exact plane.
