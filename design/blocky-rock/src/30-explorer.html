4 · How a rock is built

Columns, cuts, joined

Four stages of one seed (found by scanning for a rock with a step, a facet and a chip). Then the cost, measured in this browser.

  1. Columns. The base box (or ramp) is cut into columns, each a convex solid held as six planes: four sides that may lean or move in, a top that may slope, a flat bottom.
  2. Chamfers. Each edge of each column is read off its faces, and one to three planes are cut along it, their widths set from the edge's own line in space, so two columns that share an edge cut it the same way.
  3. Facets and chips. A few more planes on one column each. Convex clips only, so a column stays convex.
  4. Join. Each polygon of each column is split at the other columns' planes; the pieces inside another column are dropped, and so is a pair of pieces that touch back to back (a joint). What is left is the surface of the union, with the V of a split and the riser of a step in it.
  5. Mesh. Weld the corners, repair the T-junctions where a split crosses a neighbour's edge, fan the polygons into triangles, count the edges (each must have two), and find the steepest face facing down.

Nothing is a voxel or a field: every face is an exact plane, so an edge is as crisp as the planes make it.
