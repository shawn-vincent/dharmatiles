4 · How a rock is built

One block, every edge chamfered

Two stages of one rock. Then the cost, measured in this browser.

  1. The block. The base shape (slab, block, pillar or ramp) as six planes, each side tilted a little off square by its own amount, so no two sides are parallel, and the top with its own slight slope. The bottom is flat.
  2. A strip along every edge. Each edge becomes a ruled strip of 24 short segments between a line on one face and a line on the other, and everything about it is one simple arc: the width is a little wider or narrower in the middle (up to 15 per cent), the tilt is constant or changes once along the edge, and the strip bows a little along its length (up to 0.15 of its width), in or out. Each edge has its own arcs. Top edges lie 8 to 27 degrees below horizontal with a short drop down the side, bottom edges rise 60 to 69 degrees, side edges take 32 to 58 degrees, leaning into either face, and narrow toward the foot. Widths are at least 0.06 fh where they show, and the chamfers on one face together never take more than a third of its width.
  3. Faces. Each face is nearly flat: tilted by up to about 1.5 degrees, and curved by one faint arc each way (0.04 to 0.16 per cent of its size), from slightly concave to slightly convex. Each face is an even 24 by 24 grid (a Coons patch between its four sides, whose corners are shared), so there is no seam or fan.
  4. Corners. On each face, the point where its two chamfer lines meet is found directly; the three such points, one per face, are the vertices of the corner facet, one triangle. Each strip runs between its two end points, so it never folds.
  5. Mesh. Faces, strips and corner patches share their vertices, so the mesh is closed by construction (every edge in two triangles). Normals are smoothed across bends under 6 degrees, so each chamfer shows as a band for display only. About 7,500 triangles, 3 ms.

Off for this round, kept in the code (makeRock(seed, { full: true })): lobes and steps, cracks, battered corners, kink planes, and the print rule (reported as "steepest underside", not enforced).
