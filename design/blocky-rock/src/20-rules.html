2 · The rules

The smallest set that explains the sheet

Two base shapes, two ways of cutting a concave piece out of it, and three ways of breaking a surface. Every number is proposed: taken from the study, then judged against the references by rendering. Lengths are in figure heights (fh); m is the shorter plan side of the rock. A rock is about 0.3 to 1.3 fh at its longest.

B1 Base: a box, or a ramp

Draw the class with the sheet's shares: slab .22, block .30, pillar .22, ramp .26. Plan side W (the long one) then D, height H, all against W:

  • slab: W 0.75–1.15 fh, D 0.55–0.85, H 0.28–0.42
  • block: W 0.5–0.85 fh, D 0.72–1, H 0.62–1
  • pillar: W 0.32–0.5 fh, D 0.6–0.9, H 1.7–2.5 (never above 1.3 fh)
  • ramp: W 0.6–1 fh, D 0.6–0.9, back H 0.62–0.95, front 0.25–0.45 of the back; the top rises along W

Lean: a pillar leans with chance .3, a ramp .2, a block .1: the whole rock sheared 0.012–0.035 along a plan side (under 2 degrees); the sides stay near upright. A face of a block, slab or ramp moves in as it rises with chance .3, by 1–2 per cent of its width over the height.

B2 One mass

A rock is one convex solid with chamfers, facets and chips cut in; the concave features below are taken out of it. It never has a second top or a second outline, so it reads as one block from every side.

F1 Split = a scored V-line

A V-groove 0.045–0.075 fh wide at the face and about two thirds as deep, scored into the left face (.65) or right face (.35) at 0.25–0.75 of its width, running up through the top edge. It starts at the ground with a taper (no notch in the base) or, with chance .6, at 0.2–0.7 of the height with a tapering end. With chance .3 (not on a ramp) the line goes on across the top and stops 0.4–0.9 of the way back. The groove is tilted up to 5 degrees. Number of grooves: slab 1 (.6) or 2 (.4); block 0 (.25), 1 (.55), 2 (.2); pillar 0 (.78) or 1 (.22); ramp 0 (.6) or 1 (.4). A groove is sharp at its bottom and does not change the outline.

F2 Step = a bite from a top corner

Chance .15 on a slab, .25 on a block, .45 on a pillar, .5 on a ramp. A box is cut out of a top corner: 0.28–0.5 of the length at the left end (.65) or the near end (.35), 0.4–0.75 of the depth (chance .6; otherwise all the way through), down to 0.5–0.75 of the height on a block or slab and 0.45–0.7 on a pillar. On a ramp the bite is a level shelf at 0.55–0.9 of the ramp's height there (the seat). Tread and riser meet at a sharp inside corner. The bite's own rim edges are not chamfered yet.

C1 Chamfers: every convex edge, 1 to 3 planes

Each edge (top, upright, bottom) is cut by 3, 4 or 5 planes (.35, .4, .25). The width of a plane is a straight line along the edge, drawn at random and changing by up to ±0.3 m per fh; the widest wins, so the planes cross and the width wanders along the edge with a crease at each change. Base widths: top 0.08–0.14 m, upright 0.08–0.14 m, bottom 0.07–0.12 m, No chamfer under 0.06 fh (0.05 at the bottom; on a rock narrower than 0.55 fh the floor gives way to 0.11 m so a pillar is not a prism) and none over 0.2 m. A top chamfer drops 0.45–0.95 of its width; an upright one is 45 degrees. A bend under 35 degrees (a ramp's top against its front) is left as a crease. Concave corners are never cut.

C2 Facets: a plane a few degrees off a face

0 to 3 a rock (.15, .4, .35, .1). A plane tilted 3–9 degrees from a side face (3–6 from a ramp's slope; a flat top takes none), toward a direction that is mostly up (.45), diagonal (.4) or along (.15); tilted up the face (the rock narrows toward the top) with chance .85, down .15; set 0.03–0.1 m below the face, so it meets the face along a crease and cuts a wedge off one side. It is refused if it takes more than 28 per cent of the box. One to three kinks as well: the same plane at 1.5–4 degrees and 0.012–0.035 m deep, which break a large face into planes that shade a little differently, as on the sheet. These are the oblique planes of R1C6, R2C1 and R4C3.

C3 Chips: a corner flake

0 to 2 a rock (.45, .35, .2). The plane through a point on each of the three edges at a corner, 0.16–0.4 of the shorter side out along each, so the three legs are unequal and the flake is a skew triangle. 45 per cent are at a lower corner. Refused if it takes more than 20 per cent of the box, or if one of its three faces is a joint. The V notch of R3C1 is read as a chip; a pocket in a face is not generated.

P1 Undersides print

No face is flatter than 35 degrees above horizontal, except the flat bottom. Bottom chamfers rise run over 1.4 (55 degrees from vertical). A plane facing down with its normal pointing more than sin 55° (0.819) downward is lengthened on its standing leg or dropped; the finished mesh is checked and a seed with a face over 55 degrees is drawn again from the same seed (the table in chapter 4 gives how often; every rock shown passes).

P2 Where a face must stay plain

For the cliff: bottomBevel: false leaves the bottom edges sharp (an edge on the ground, or on another stone). A top that carries a stone should take no step, facet, chip or chamfer; a face against a neighbour no split, facet or chip; the generator has no switch for these yet.

P3 One closed mesh

The cut solid is welded and its T-junctions repaired; the page checks that every edge has two triangles.
