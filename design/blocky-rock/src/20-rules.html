2 · The rules

The smallest set that explains the sheet

Two base shapes, one cut that is a split or a step, and three ways of breaking a surface. Every number is proposed: taken from the study, then judged against the references by rendering. Lengths are in figure heights (fh); m is the shorter plan side of the rock. A rock is about 0.3 to 1.3 fh at its longest.

B1 Base: a box, or a ramp

Draw the class with the sheet's shares: slab .22, block .30, pillar .22, ramp .26. Plan side W (the long one) then D, height H, all against W:

  • slab: W 0.75–1.15 fh, D 0.55–0.85, H 0.28–0.42
  • block: W 0.5–0.85 fh, D 0.72–1, H 0.62–1
  • pillar: W 0.32–0.5 fh, D 0.6–0.9, H 1.7–2.5 (never above 1.3 fh)
  • ramp: W 0.6–1 fh, D 0.6–0.9, back H 0.62–0.95, front 0.25–0.45 of the back; the top rises along W

Lean: a pillar leans with chance .6, a ramp .2, a block .1: the whole rock sheared 0.05–0.14 along a plan side (3 to 8 degrees). Each outer face of a block, slab or ramp moves in as it rises with chance .45, by 3–8 per cent of its width over the height (not on a pillar, which tapers only through its facets).

B2 Columns: the footprint is cut

The footprint is cut across its full depth into columns, and each column is its own box with its own top. A cut falls at 0.3–0.7 of the column it cuts; no column is narrower than 0.3 m, or than a fifth of its height. Number of cuts: slab 1 (.55) or 2 (.45); block 0 (.17), 1 (.5), 2 (.33); pillar 0 (.78) or 1 (.22); ramp 0 (.6) or 1 (.4).

F1 Split = a cut with level sides

Both sides keep the top (one side is 2–5 per cent lower, with chance .3 when there is a cut). The groove is nothing but the chamfers of the two columns where they meet, so it is a V in every face the cut crosses, sharp at the bottom of the V, and as wide as its two chamfers: 0.022 to 0.03 fh a side, so 0.045 to 0.06 across. It need not go through: a split that stops partway is made by a column behind it that joins the two (the bed, F3).

F2 Step = a cut with one side lower

Chance .15 on a slab, .25 on a block, .45 on a pillar, .5 on a ramp. The column at a corner is lowered by 0.25–0.5 of the height (0.3–0.55 on a pillar): at the left end of the left face with chance .65, at the near corner with .35, and with chance .6 it is also cut short in depth, so it is a corner and not a whole end. On a ramp the corner column is a level shelf at 0.55–0.9 of the ramp's height there, with the ramp rising behind it (the seat). The tread and riser meet at a sharp inside corner; their outer edges are chamfered like any other.

C1 Chamfers: every convex edge, 1 to 3 planes

Each edge of each column (top, upright, bottom) is cut by 1, 2 or 3 planes (.25, .45, .3). The width of a plane is a straight line along the edge, drawn at random and changing by up to ±0.45 m per fh; with two or three, the widest wins, so they cross and show creases. Base widths: top 0.08–0.14 m, upright 0.08–0.14 m, bottom 0.07–0.12 m, and a quarter of that on the two sides of a cut. No free chamfer under 0.06 fh (0.05 at the bottom, 0.022 for a groove side; on a rock narrower than 0.55 fh the floor gives way to 0.11 m so a pillar is not a prism) and none over 0.2 m. A top chamfer drops 0.45–0.95 of its width; an upright one is 45 degrees. A bend under 35 degrees (a ramp's top against its front) is left as a crease. Concave corners are never cut.

C2 Facets: a plane a few degrees off a face

0 to 3 a rock (.15, .35, .35, .15). A plane tilted 6–24 degrees from a side face (6–10 from a top), toward a direction that is mostly up (.45), diagonal (.4) or along (.15); a plane tilted up the face (so the rock narrows toward the top) is chosen with chance .85, one tilted down .15; set 0.05–0.2 m below the face at the middle of its place, so it meets the face along a crease and cuts a wedge off one side. It is refused if it takes more than 28 per cent of the column's box, and never goes in a face that is a joint (that would open the groove into a gap). One to four kinks as well: the same plane at 2.5–6 degrees and 0.015–0.05 m deep, which break a large face into planes that shade a little differently, as on the sheet. These are the oblique planes of R1C6, R2C1 and R4C3.

C3 Chips: a corner flake

0 to 2 a rock (.45, .35, .2). The plane through a point on each of the three edges at a corner, 0.16–0.4 of the shorter side out along each, so the three legs are unequal and the flake is a skew triangle. 45 per cent are at a lower corner. Refused if it takes more than 20 per cent of the box, or if one of its three faces is a joint. The V notch of R3C1 is read as a chip; a pocket in a face is not generated.

F3 Bed: a line across

Chance .12, on blocks and pillars. Every column that is tall enough is cut at 0.5–0.8 of the height into a lower and an upper box, joined by one that stands behind them at 0.28–0.5 m from the front, so the groove shows on the near faces and the top and ends at the sides. Five of the six beds in the sheet are a line across the top, which the split's V already gives; this is for the sixth.

P1 Undersides print

No face is flatter than 35 degrees above horizontal, except the flat bottom. Bottom chamfers rise run over 1.4 (55 degrees from vertical). A plane facing down with its normal pointing more than sin 55° (0.819) downward is lengthened on its standing leg or dropped; the finished mesh is checked and a seed with a face over 55 degrees is drawn again from the same seed (the table in chapter 4 gives how often; every rock shown passes).

P2 Where a face must stay plain

For the cliff: bottomBevel: false leaves the bottom edges sharp (an edge on the ground, or on another stone). A top that carries a stone should take no step, facet, chip or chamfer; a face against a neighbour no split, facet or chip; the generator has no switch for these yet, and says so in the verdict.

P3 One closed mesh

The columns are convex; the rock is their union, found by splitting each face at the others' planes and dropping what lies inside another column, and by dropping both of two faces that touch back to back. Faces are welded and the T-junctions of the splits repaired; the page checks that every edge has two triangles.
