2 · The rules

The smallest set that explains the sheet

A rock is lobes grown together, every edge rounded off by a chain of planes. Every number is proposed: taken from the study and from drawing four reference rocks by hand. Lengths are in figure heights (fh); m is the shorter plan side. A rock is about 0.3 to 1.3 fh at its longest.

B1 Base: a box, or a ramp

Class shares of the sheet: slab .22, block .30, pillar .22, ramp .26. Plan W, D and height H:

  • slab: W 0.75–1.1 fh, D 0.6–0.76 W, H 0.28–0.36 W
  • block: W 0.5–0.75 fh, D 0.75–0.95 W, H 0.68–0.95 W
  • pillar: W 0.42–0.56 fh, D 0.7–0.9 W, H 1.7–2.1 W (never above 1.3 fh)
  • ramp: W 0.5–0.7 fh, D 0.65–0.85, H 0.62–0.82; the top rises toward the back (y), from 0.22–0.32 of H at the front

Sides are near upright: a pillar leans with chance .4 by at most 0.03.

L1 A rock is lobes, nothing straight

1 to 4 lobes, each a box (or ramp) from the ground to its own top, touching along joints. Slab: 1 (.3), 2 (.35) or 3 (.35); block: 1 (.45), 2 (.3), 3 (.25). Joints are at uneven places (0.28–0.48 and 0.62–0.78 of the length) and each is angled up to 0.09 in plan. Every outer side leans by its own −0.01 to 0.045 (opposite sides differ), is twisted in plan by up to 0.07, and the top slopes by up to 0.025 each way. Tops are level within 0.012–0.03 except for a step.

L2 A step is a lower lobe

Chance .12 on a slab, .22 on a block (first lobe at 0.45–0.65 of the height), .5 on a pillar (an end lobe 0.38–0.52 of the width, at 0.35–0.5 of the height), .6 on a ramp: a seat 0.35–0.5 of the width by 0.3–0.4 of the depth at the front-left, a level shelf at 0.45–0.6 of the ramp's height there. A lower lobe is chamfered like any other.

C1 Chamfers: a rounded-off edge as a chain of planes

Each convex edge gets 2 (.4) or 3 (.6) tangent planes; radius is 0.08–0.13 m on a top (so tops stay flat), 0.09–0.15 on an upright, 0.07–0.11 at the bottom, never under 0.05 fh; ±15 per cent per plane, and tilted so the width changes by up to 0.4 r along the edge. A joint edge takes a fifth of the radius. A bend under 35 degrees is a crease. Concave corners are never cut. A plane that would leave a sliver is left out.

C2 Wobble

One to three planes a rock (one on a slab), 1.2–3.2 degrees off a side face or 0.5–1.3 off a top, 0.012–0.035 fh deep, never on a joint face.

C3 Battered corners and edges

2 to 5 a rock (at most 4 on a slab), visible on most rocks. Each is two to four planes knocked off an outer feature of a random lobe: a top corner (.3), a low corner (.2), a low upright edge (.2), a bottom edge (.15) or a top edge (.15). Low and bottom chunks use planes tilted downward, so each takes a cap off the foot of the edge. Depth 0.04–0.16 of the shorter side (0.1 of a lobe's narrowest side on a top, 0.17 elsewhere), so a bite reaches up to a third of an edge; each plane's normal mixes the corner's axes with none under 0.38, so no leg runs long. Cut after the chamfers. Downward faces stay within 55 degrees from vertical.

K1 Crack: sometimes, and wandering

About 1 rock in 5, never more than one crack. The crack is a surface of up to 8 facets whose normals scatter around a base direction and whose positions scatter across the rock, so its line is irregular on the top, the sides and the corners, never straight in any direction. It separates a convex piece from the rest, and the path varies: right through from one side to the other (.35), cutting a corner off (.3), running down from the top and out through a side (.2), or diagonal (.15). The convex piece is moved 0.01–0.022 fh away, and a little along; the gap varies along the crack with the facets. A piece that touches the ground moves only sideways; a piece above the ground may lift. No chamfer, no V. A crack that leaves a sliver or an underside past 55 degrees is dropped.

R1 A ramp seat

The ramp is one sloped back piece, with the seat a lower level lobe at its front-left corner, its top tilting up 0.22 toward the riser. The slanted riser that flows into the seat is not built: tilting the lobes' joint faces made the pieces separate, so the riser is vertical.

P1 Undersides print

No face is flatter than 35 degrees above horizontal, except the flat bottom. A bottom chamfer plane whose normal points more than sin 55° (0.819) down is dropped; the finished mesh is checked and a plane that would leave a face over 55 degrees or a sliver is not used, so nothing is drawn again.

P2 Where a face must stay plain

For the cliff: bottomBevel: false leaves the bottom edges sharp (an edge on the ground, or on another stone). A top that carries a stone should take no wobble or chamfer, and a face against a neighbour none; the generator has no switch for these yet.

P3 One closed mesh

Each piece is welded and checked: every edge has two triangles.
