5 · Against the references

What looks right, and what does not

Judged by rendering the same view as the sheet and putting the two side by side, over many seeds, not by the numbers.

Looks right

  • The vocabulary. A chunky mass on a flat bottom, every convex edge cut by a few planes of changing width, a V-groove that splits it, a corner step or a seat on a ramp, an oblique facet across a face, a skew corner flake. A seed reads as one of the sheet's families at a glance.
  • Proportions and mix. Slabs low and long, blocks near cubes, pillars two to three times as tall as wide, ramps rising to a high back, in the sheet's shares; steps on four in ten and splits on eight in ten.
  • Sharp inside corners. Where a step meets its riser and at the bottom of every groove the corner is sharp, as the rule says, because it comes from joining boxes.
  • It prints and it is fast. Every shown rock is one closed mesh, no face over 55 degrees from vertical except the flat bottom, and a rock builds in 2 to 6 ms on average and under 50 ms at the worst seed (chapter 4).

Does not look right yet

  • Softness. The sheet is a clay sculpt: faces are slightly wavy, creases soft. These are exact planes, shaded as one across bends under 18 degrees; a cut stone next to the clay, though far less crystalline than before.
  • One mass. Every rock is one solid with grooves and a bite cut into it; it no longer reads as two rocks. A groove ends in a flat-floored taper that looks more like a slot than the sheet's soft line.
  • The bite's rim. A step's tread and riser edges are sharp; the sheet chamfers them.
  • Chamfer wander. Widths change along an edge with creases, but only as a convex envelope, so they widen toward the ends or the middle and do not wander freely as on the sheet.
  • Not generated at all: the pocket or V notch cut into a face (R2C6, R3C1, R4C2; three rocks), and the wavy surface of the clay. A horizontal bed line is generated but is rare in the sheet (R5C3 only).
  • Cliff constraints are only partly in. The generator has the bottom switch (bottomBevel: false) but no switch yet for a top that carries a stone, or a face against a neighbour. I would add them as a list of faces to leave plain.

What I did not look at

  • The back and right-back of any reference rock; there are none. The faces of a generated rock that the camera never sees are not compared with anything.
  • A real phone: the page was checked in headless Chrome at 1280 and 390 px wide; touch orbit and pinch are OrbitControls' and were not tried by hand.
  • The feature positions against the sheet by statistics: the rules take shares and ranges from the study; I did not test that a thousand generated rocks match the table, only looked at several hundred.
  • How the rock sits on a cliff's plate, with neighbours and a figure; this level is one rock.
