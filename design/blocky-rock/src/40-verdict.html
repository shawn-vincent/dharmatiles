5 · Against the references

What looks right, and what does not

Round 7: one idea, single-arc chamfers. Compared beside R1C8, R2C4 and R1C5. Judged by rendering the same view beside the cell.

Looks right

  • Every chamfer is now one clean arc and shows as its own band with a crease to its face; round 6's wandering is gone.
  • The rock reads as a soft-edged block: top bands just off horizontal, a steep foot, upright bands, faces very slightly dished or domed. The base shape stays plain.
  • Faces are even grids, so the X creases are gone; a closed mesh by construction, 2 ms a rock.

Does not look right yet

  • It is a pillow, not a rock. Against R1C8, R2C4 and R1C5 it is rounder and softer: the reference chamfers are crisp planes at hard edges, and the rocks have steps, creases between lobes and chips, which are off here.
  • Bottom chamfers read as a skirt flaring out at the foot, and the bottom corners come to small points.
  • Slabs pinch in a waist, where the side chamfers meet the thin faces.
  • The steepest underside is up to about 90 degrees on some seeds, because printing is not enforced.

What I did not look at

  • The back of any reference rock; there are none.
  • A real phone: headless Chrome at 1280 and 390 px only.
  • Statistics of generated rocks against the study tables.
  • The rock in a cliff, with neighbours.
