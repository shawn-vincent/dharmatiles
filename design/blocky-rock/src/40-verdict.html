5 · Against the references

What looks right, and what does not

Four reference rocks were drawn by hand first (R1C8, R2C4, R1C7, R1C5), then a generator was written from them; round 4 added irregularity, battered corners and cracks. Judged by rendering the same view beside the cell.

Looks right

  • R1C8 (block of lobes) passes: one boxy mass, flat tops, shallow creases at uneven places.
  • R2C4 (pillar with a lower lobe) passes weakly: the right proportions and step, flatter top and narrower chamfers than before; the lower lobe is still a plain box.
  • R1C7 (ramp with a seat) passes weakly: one sloped piece with a level seat low at the front-left. The seat reads shallower than the clay's and the riser is vertical where the sheet's slants.
  • R1C5 (slab) marginal: three lobes give two creases, but the chip at the front-left is too big for so low a rock.
  • Irregularity. No two seeds are alike and no side is parallel to another; it stays blocky.
  • Fast, no redraw. Under 20 ms at the worst seed; a cutting plane that would leave a sliver is not used, so no seed is drawn twice.

Does not look right yet

  • Cracks read as thin slits with a small step; the sheet's wide grooves are not cracks and are not generated. A crack never changes a rock's outline from above much.
  • Battered corners are a few flat planes; on small rocks they look like cut corners more than beaten ones.
  • Softness. Still planes and not clay; faces do not wander in soft waves.
  • Not generated: V-notch pockets, a groove that stops partway, a slanted riser on a seat.
  • Cliff constraints: only bottomBevel exists; none yet for a loaded top or a face against a neighbour.

What I did not look at

  • The back of any reference rock; there are none.
  • A real phone: headless Chrome at 1280 and 390 px only.
  • Statistics of generated rocks against the study tables.
  • The rock in a cliff, with neighbours.
