5 · Against the references

What looks right, and what does not

Round 8: clean corners, near-flat faces. Compared beside R1C8, R2C4 and R1C5. Judged by rendering the same view beside the cell.

Looks right

  • It now reads as a block: near-flat faces, flat tops, clear chamfer bands on the top edges, no barrel sides, no waist on pillars or slabs.
  • Corners are one clean triangle each; the debris, slots and the striped face of round 7 are gone (the stripes came from the face grid meeting the strip's bow off the face plane; the grid now starts from the strip's own points).
  • A closed mesh by construction, 3 ms a rock.

Does not look right yet

  • The feet. At each bottom corner the upright chamfer comes down onto a small steep bottom chamfer, leaving a little downward-pointing triangle, and the foot reads as a faint plinth.
  • Too tame. Against R1C8, R2C4 and R1C5 it is a clean chamfered block, without the steps, creases between lobes and chips that make the references read as rocks; those are off this round.
  • Some seeds still show a thin dark line along a top edge (seed 12).
  • The steepest underside is reported up to about 89 degrees (the pointed feet); printing is not enforced.

What I did not look at

  • The back of any reference rock; there are none.
  • A real phone: headless Chrome at 1280 and 390 px only.
  • Statistics of generated rocks against the study tables.
  • The rock in a cliff, with neighbours.
