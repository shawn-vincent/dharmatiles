5 · Against the references

What looks right, and what does not

Round 10: half-width chamfers. Compared beside R1C8, R2C4 and R1C5. Judged by rendering the same view beside the cell.

Looks right

  • Chamfers are half the width of round 9's, so the rock reads as a crisp block with bands that show but do not round it; faces are near flat and the large faces no longer cushion.
  • Printability: 0 of 600 seeds per base shape pass 55 degrees from vertical (steepest 51 to 54), fixed at the source by holding the bottom chamfer at 40 to 43 degrees above horizontal.
  • A closed mesh by construction, 3.4 ms a rock.

Does not look right yet

  • Too tame. Against R1C8, R2C4 and R1C5 it is a clean chamfered block; the steps, lobe creases and chips that make the references read as rocks are off.
  • Each bottom corner is still a facet whose lowest point is the corner of the foot; it sits on the ground line, not below it (the lowest vertex of the mesh is z = 0), but from the side it can still read as a faint point.
  • Halving the chamfers makes the tops and bottoms read as slightly plain: the bands are thin against the large faces.

What I did not look at

  • The back of any reference rock; there are none.
  • A real phone: headless Chrome at 1280 and 390 px only.
  • Statistics of generated rocks against the study tables.
  • The rock in a cliff, with neighbours.
