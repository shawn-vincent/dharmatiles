5 · Against the references

What looks right, and what does not

Four reference rocks were drawn by hand first (R1C8, R2C4, R1C7, R1C5), then a generator was written from them; round 4 added irregularity, battered corners and cracks. Judged by rendering the same view beside the cell.

Looks right

  • R1C8 (block of lobes) passes: one boxy mass, flat tops, shallow creases, bolder damage.
  • R2C4 (pillar with a lower lobe) weak pass: right proportions, a step and a battered foot; the top still chamfered flat.
  • R1C5 (slab) weak pass: two creases and a battered corner.
  • Cracks are now rare (1 in 5) and wander on the top and the sides, take different paths (through, corner, from the top, diagonal), and the gap varies along them.
  • Damage is on most rocks, on corners and low edges, in sizes that read at sheet scale.
  • Fast, and no redraw: under 40 ms at the worst seed.

Does not look right yet

  • R1C7 fails: the ramp is one sloped piece with a level seat, but the seat's slanted back that flows into the slope is not made. Tilting the lobes' joint faces separated the pieces, so the riser is vertical and reads as a step.
  • Chunks on narrow rocks can still take too much: a pillar or a ramp corner is sometimes pointed, the sheet's never are.
  • Cracks are made of a handful of straight segments, so a close look shows short straight runs; and they read as thin slits more than as breaks.
  • Softness is out of scope: flats stay flat.
  • Cliff constraints: only bottomBevel exists.

What I did not look at

  • The back of any reference rock; there are none.
  • A real phone: headless Chrome at 1280 and 390 px only.
  • Statistics of generated rocks against the study tables.
  • The rock in a cliff, with neighbours.
