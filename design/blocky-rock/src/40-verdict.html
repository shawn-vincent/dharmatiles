5 · Against the references

What looks right, and what does not

Four reference rocks were drawn by hand first (R1C8, R2C4, R1C7, R1C5), then a generator was written from them. Judged by rendering the same view beside the cell.

Looks right

  • One block of lobes. A seed reads as a single boxy rock with creases between its lobes, not as pieces side by side. R1C8 (a block of three lobes) is the closest match; R2C4 (a pillar with a lower lobe) and the slab R1C5 read as the same kind of rock.
  • Edges. Every convex edge is a chain of planes whose width changes along it, soft at a distance, with the normals smoothed for display.
  • It prints and it is fast. One closed mesh per rock, no face over 55 degrees except the flat bottom, a few milliseconds a rock (chapter 4).

Does not look right yet

  • R1C7, the ramp with a seat, does not match. Its seat and slope come out as separate lobes with a valley between them; the sheet's slope runs unbroken down into the seat. Lobes with planar tops cannot make that without a crease.
  • Puffy. The chamfers are generous, and a rock reads rounder and a little taller than the clay it copies. The sheet's tops are flatter inside a narrow band.
  • Faces. The sheet's faces wander in a few soft waves; mine have a few gentle planes.
  • Not generated: V-notch pockets and chips (R2C6, R3C1, R4C2), a groove that ends partway down a face, a split that does not cross the whole rock.
  • Cliff constraints are only partly in: bottomBevel exists, but no switch for a loaded top or a face against a neighbour.

What I did not look at

  • The back of any reference rock; there are none.
  • A real phone: headless Chrome at 1280 and 390 px only.
  • Statistics of generated rocks against the study tables.
  • The rock in a cliff, with neighbours.
