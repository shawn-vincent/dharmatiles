5 · Against the references

What looks right, and what does not

Round 6: one idea only. Compared beside R1C8, R2C4 and R1C5. Judged by rendering the same view beside the cell.

Looks right

  • One idea, and it reads as one: a boxy rock with soft, wandering chamfers on every edge, nothing parallel, nothing straight.
  • It stays the base shape: slab, block, pillar and ramp are all plainly themselves.
  • A closed mesh by construction, 1 to 3 ms a rock.

Does not look right yet

  • It reads more like crumpled paper than the references. The sheet's chamfers are crisp planes with distinct edges; mine, at these widths, blend into the faces under the display smoothing, and the faces carry faint X-shaped creases from the way they are triangulated.
  • The steps, creases between lobes and damage that made the sheet's rocks read as rocks are off, so every seed is a single plain block; the sheet's variety is not there.
  • Some side chamfers swing wide and leave a notch-like dark band down an edge.
  • The steepest underside is up to about 90 degrees on a seed, because printing is not enforced.

What I did not look at

  • The back of any reference rock; there are none.
  • A real phone: headless Chrome at 1280 and 390 px only.
  • Statistics of generated rocks against the study tables.
  • The rock in a cliff, with neighbours.
