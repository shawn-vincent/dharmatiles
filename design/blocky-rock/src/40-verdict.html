5 · Against the references

What looks right, and what does not

Round 9: undercut feet. Compared beside R1C8, R2C4 and R1C5. Judged by rendering the same view beside the cell.

Looks right

  • The foot. Bottom chamfers tuck under at 37 to 41 degrees above horizontal, large enough to read; each bottom corner is one clean facet like a top corner. The rock sits on an undercut foot, not a tray; seen from slightly below, the chamfers and corner facets are clean.
  • Near-flat faces, flat tops with chamfer bands, no waist, no debris. The dark slit along a top edge in seed 12 is gone (the chamfer's bow was moving strip points off the face plane; it is smaller now and part of the face grid's own base).
  • A closed mesh by construction, 3.5 ms a rock; the underside check now reports the real angle.

Does not look right yet

  • Too tame. Against R1C8, R2C4 and R1C5 it is a clean chamfered block; the steps, lobe creases and chips that make the references read as rocks are off.
  • A few seeds still bulge slightly in the top or the sides (seeds 8, 10, 11).
  • About 3 in 100 seeds have a facet past 55 degrees (a few tilted corner facets), and a faint hatch shows on one top corner in seed 1.

What I did not look at

  • The back of any reference rock; there are none.
  • A real phone: headless Chrome at 1280 and 390 px only.
  • Statistics of generated rocks against the study tables.
  • The rock in a cliff, with neighbours.
