Soft areas

An area written as two outlines, inner and outer, takes odds exactly as a top does. Inside the inner outline things fill the space for certain. In the gap the weighting says how likely they are, so a scatter thins out, a cliff's sites thin out into a ragged front, and each site's own outline is a weighted area again.

Inside the inner outline the sites fill the space for certain. In the gap each candidate site rolls its own number, the rolls are ranked, and the weighting turns the rank into how deep a site may stand (rolled, as for a top). With weighted stone outline on, each gap stone gets its own outline: points around it (the points slider), each point placed at a depth the same weighting chooses, joined by straight lines, so it leans toward the inner area more often than away. Nothing smooths the polygon at this step. Grain only affects the dirt's edge.

The answer in short

  • An area band takes odds exactly as a top does. at="[inner .. outer]" is the plain band and at="low(inner .. outer, 4)" is a cloud, with the language's own weightings. The area holds the two outlines and the odds. It never rolls.
  • Each element rolls for itself, and the weighting places the roll. That is Band::rolled: the element makes its own roll at each node (or each candidate), the rolls are ranked, and the weighting turns each rank into how far out that node may go. Two columns below set the top and the area side by side.
  • Scatter places things at full density inside the inner area and with the weighting's density in the gap, so there are fewer where the odds are lower. Cliffs, walls, columns and bricks are sites with cells. Sites fill the inner area for certain and are placed in the gap by the weighting, which breaks the front up.
  • A site's outline in the gap is a weighted area again, drawn like a top. Pick points around the stone, place each at a depth the weighting chooses, join them with straight lines. That polygon is the stone's area, as a soil top is nodes joined up, and the rock's own cuts, chips and wear take off the corners, so nothing else smooths it. The form is closed by intersection with one added piece, a core every stone keeps.
  • A plain band has no odds, as a plain top has none. The element uses its own roll. Scatter and cliff sites roll white noise, so a plain band thins them in a straight line, and scatter needs no refusal. flat(a .. b) is the explicit ranked form. The value is a new kind, band, over places, so pins, except, within of= and parent() refuse it as a kind mistake. dt::band-outside stands.

How tops work today

read from the code at commit 870b0b18

Three things look alike in a document and are three different mechanisms in the program. Your idea matches the third.

The band is a constraint, not a draw

A Band holds a lo and a hi Field, one value per lattice node. solve turns the body's own band and its pins into the band the body fills: later pins win where places overlap, a pin eases back to the body's band in the body's way (dirt by slope, water over a fixed distance, cliffs and floors not at all), and several pins pull by odds o = w / (1 - w) that keep every pin exact at its place. The module says it outright: nothing in it draws a random number.

band.rs:1-34 solve, band.rs:663 guide/heights

A value range is a draw

A Weighting is a quantile map: one uniform u in, a position t in [0, 1] out, and Dist::at is lo + (hi - lo) * t. It never takes a second random number, so a flat range is the identity. Each thing draws its own u from a keyed seed, so every draw is independent.

Weighting, weighting.rs:217 unit, :517 Dist, :917 Dist::at, :1039

A cloud is both: a band, plus a field that is ranked through a weighting

The 9 Nov design said a weighting on a band is an error, and for a plain band that is still so. The clouds change (guide/clouds) gave it a meaning, so a top= or a pin may carry one today. A band with odds is a cloud, and the program makes its heights in six steps, in Band::rolled:

  1. The body makes its own smooth field of rolls over the nodes (hills for dirt, waves for water). How neighbours relate is the body's business.
  2. A node takes part if it has odds and the body's take accepts it (dirt: where there is room).
  3. The nodes are sorted by roll, least first. Ties go in node order, so no thread can change the result.
  4. A node's rank is (position + 0.5) / count, from 0 to 1.
  5. The weighting turns that rank into t.
  6. The height is lo + (hi - lo) * t.

Two properties come from ranking. The heights come up as often as the weighting says, whatever the field's own distribution, because ranks are evenly spread. And neighbours stay alike, because rank only reorders the roll and never scrambles it. The solver is untouched: the band it solves is the same one, and the cloud only decides where in the band the surface sits.

Where the program is already close. A within "[a .. b]" distance already draws a ragged edge. region_at reads each node's nearest source, and drawn_depth lays masses of 6 to 15 mm along the place, each as deep as rng.uniform(a, b) draws it. That is a ragged edge between a place and a distance, drawn by the place and not by the element, in blocks instead of smoothly, and keyed by region and place only, so another tile seed draws the same edge. It is an area that draws, which the rule in "Who rolls" retires.

The proposal

every example here is a proposal; nothing in the repo runs it

An area band, plain or cloud

An area band is a pair of regions, an inner one (min) and an outer one (max) with min inside max, written as a range of places. A plain one, [face .. faceMax], has no odds, as a plain top has none: the element uses its own roll. A weighted one, low(face .. faceMax, 4), uses the same weightings a size or a top uses. Where a top says "the surface may sit anywhere from low to high, and how often each height comes up is the weighting", an area says the same of how far out the edge or the sites go. A zero-width band, [pool .. pool], is the plain region pool (the band fits where a region is wanted), so nothing already written changes.

The top and the area, side by side

A top: Band::rolled, band.rs:424

  1. The body makes its own roll at each node: smooth noise for dirt and water.
  2. Nodes that have odds and that take accepts are ranked by roll, ties in node order.
  3. Rank → (position + 0.5) / count.
  4. The weighting turns the rank into t.
  5. Height = lo + (hi - lo) * t.
  6. fit keeps it between low and high.

An area: the same six steps

  1. The element makes its own roll at each node or candidate: white noise for scatter things and cliff sites, smooth noise for a body's edge and a stone's outline.
  2. Nodes or candidates in the gap that the element accepts are ranked by roll, ties in order.
  3. Rank → (position + 0.5) / count.
  4. The weighting turns the rank into t, how deep it may go.
  5. A node is inside where its depth u is at most t: u = dm / (dm + dM), 0 at the inner outline and 1 at the outer. A site stands there, or does not.
  6. fit keeps the result between the outlines.

Ranking is what makes the frequencies exact. The depths the weighting hands out come up as often as it says, whatever roll the element used, so the chance of standing at depth u follows the weighting's curve (this is about depth, not about how much of the gap's area is kept). With a white-noise roll it is an independent thinning with exact counts. With a smooth roll the kept parts come in patches. How neighbouring rolls relate is the element's own business, as it is for dirt and flagstones.

How it is written

// PROPOSAL: the same shape twice, a top cloud and an area cloud
cliff at="low(face .. faceMax, 4)" top="$groundHeight + low(0dmm .. 35dmm)"

// PROPOSAL: a named band, with the let that already exists
let frontBand "bell(face .. faceMax, 0.3)"
cliff at=frontBand top="$groundHeight + 35dmm"

The weighting words, at, let and the range exist; the one word added is the kind band. A range of places is not a range of an expression kind. Kind::Region in units.rs is the kind of $tile, $region and parent(…), and a declared region name reads as Kind::Any, which unifies with anything. So the decoder resolves both ends to places before the kind check and gives the range the kind Band, refusing a range whose ends are not places (dt::type, worded by units::misfit). A weighting wraps that range as it wraps any plain range, so low(low(a .. b)) and low(a) stay dt::weighting.

A let can hold a band because the area holds odds and never rolls: each element that reads the name draws its own roll, as each element reads a top's cloud. That is the 9 Nov rule unchanged, a name holds the distribution and each use rolls.

Who rolls

The area holds the two outlines and the odds, and never rolls. Each element draws its own roll from its own keyed seed, and the weighting places it, as Band::rolled ranks a top's rolls. No seed, no key and no random number belongs to the area itself. One caveat: the outlines are lines, and a line's wobble is drawn from the tile seed, so the two masks are fixed per seed, as a top's body is. The odds are the only part left unrolled. within "[a .. b]" is the one place an area draws today (drawn_depth, places.rs:152, in blocks and independent of the tile seed), so it goes in the ledger to retire.

How two elements stay consistent. A cliff and the grass beside it each roll their own. They agree on three things and are free on one:

  • Inside the inner area both are certain.
  • In the gap both follow the same curve of depth: the chance of standing at depth u is one minus the weighting's running total, for both. The counts differ, because the weighting acts on depth and the elements have different candidates.
  • Where the grass should follow the cliff exactly, the stack says so: grass listed after the cliff sees where it stands (at="foot(cliff)", near of=cliff), as it sees rocks listed before it.
  • What differs is the individual stone against the individual blade in the gap. That is what two cloud tops (dirt and water over one band) do today.

A shared roll key, so that every element in a band draws one field, was considered and not recommended. Tops do not do it, elements roll different kinds of noise, and it would add a key to the area, which breaks "the area never rolls". seed= already pins an entry's own randomness.

Scatter: refuse the plain band, or take it? Take it. A plain band has no odds, and a scatter's roll is white noise, so unranked and ranked-flat give the same result: a straight fall-off across the gap. The earlier refusal was needed only while a band gave a scatter nothing to do. Elements that roll smooth fields (a dirt edge, a stone outline) keep their own field unranked under a plain band, as a plain top keeps the body's own field. flat(a .. b) is the explicit ranked form for them.

A site's outline is a weighted area, drawn like a top

A site in the gap stands because its roll was high enough. Its cell needs an outline, and Shawn's rule is that the outline is another weighted area with the same weighting, so a stone's outline leans toward the inner area more often than away. The outline is drawn the way a top's surface is drawn, as rolled points joined up.

  1. Points. Choose points around the stone, evenly in angle from its centre with a keyed start.
  2. Rolls. Each point gets a roll from the stone's own keyed seed. The rolls are ranked and the weighting turns each rank into t, as in Band::rolled.
  3. Place. Each point goes on its ray, between the core and the edge of the stone's cell, at the position whose depth u (inherited from the parent band: 0 at the inner outline, 1 at the outer) is nearest t, ties toward the core. u need not be monotone along a ray and may never equal t, so this is a nearest-depth clamp and always has an answer. With low, t is mostly small. A ray toward the inner area runs through falling depth and reaches depth t or the cell edge. A ray away from it only rises, so it stops at the core.
  4. Join. Straight lines between the points. The polygon is the rock's at=, its area.
  5. Fit. The polygon is cut to the stone's cell and the parent's outer outline. The stone's own inner area, core ∪ (cell ∩ inner), is added. That is the same clamp as everywhere, so the outline stays between the stone's two outlines whatever the points do. The result is no longer star-shaped, so it is re-decomposed into convex parts, which the rock already takes.

Settled by this design

  • No smoothing at this step. The rock's stages already take off corners (cuts, chunks, chips, wear). A smooth curve here would be a second smoothing mechanism, and the first one is the rock's own, so there is one.
  • How many points. A spacing in mm at the reference square along the stone's footprint, not a count, so a small stone has few points and a big one has many (about one every 2 mm; a 4 mm stone gets around 6, a 10 mm stone around 16), with at least 3, which is geometry and not a limit. It is a named constant in the code, documented on the rock page, as drawn_depth's 6 to 15 mm blocks are, so it is not a hidden word, and it gets a control only when a tile needs one. The explorer's points slider only shows the effect.
  • Convexity. A non-convex outline is fine. The raw polygon is star-shaped about the stone's centre, so it is a fan of convex triangles, and after fit the result is re-decomposed into convex parts. The rock already takes an area as a list of convex parts, so no special case arises.
  • Neighbours. Gap stones share cells, so independently rolled outlines could leave gaps or overlaps. A point that clamps at a cell edge lies on it, and where both stones reach a shared edge the joint is the cell's own edge, taken by both and never rolled. Where a neighbour does not reach, the edge is free. That is the rock's joint rule (a joint is a face shared with a standing neighbour, held and never moved), and it is the mechanism for the held faces the rock interface already says never move.
  • Few points. Ranking over 3 to 16 points gives every stone the same mix of depths in a different order. The frequency across the gap is carried by the sites, which are many, and not by one stone's points.
  • Between the outlines. By construction: every point is in the cell and the outer outline, the core and the certain part are added, and fit cuts the polygon to the cell and the outer outline (a chord between two points of a non-convex outer outline can leave it, and fit removes that part).

Is it closed, the same form all the way down?

Yes, with one piece added. Intersecting a band with a region gives a band: both ends are cut by the same region, and the weighting and the depth carry over. A cell wholly inside the inner area becomes [C .. C], the plain region C. A cell wholly in the gap with no inner part has an empty inner area, which a band allows. Anything placed in a stone takes the band of its own cell the same way. The added piece is the core, and it is part of the stone's own band, not a constant: the stone's inner area is core ∪ (cell ∩ inner), so its outline is a band of the same form. Without the core a point could roll the whole outline away from its own centre. The core is the cell's inscribed disc (question 4). The rolled points use the same ranking function as Band::rolled, applied to a list of points instead of a field of nodes: one function, not a second path.

The rock's interface. The Stone and Courses round made a cell an area and a top: area (convex polygons with roles surface, joint, seam), a bed, a top and cuts, with the box as the same region in another form. Here the polygon above is the cell's area and its top is a top band that may be a cloud, each rolled by the rock from its own seed. The interface does not change shape. Joint faces a neighbour holds stay joints and only free edges move, so held faces never move. I read the two round-2 briefs and not the rock code.

The containment check and the refusals

  • Containment. The inner area must lie inside the outer one, node by node, checked in check at the seed it is run at, with dt::band-outside. It is a new code because dt::region means "cannot be made". It stands unchanged for clouds, since the cloud adds odds and not bounds. Cells below the document's band need no check (above).
  • Check equals build. Wobble is drawn from the tile seed, so a check at one seed would pass a document that fails at another. The default is therefore seed-free and conservative: the inner lines grown by their wobble must lie inside the outer lines shrunk by theirs. It can refuse a document that would have passed at its own seed, and the error says by how much. Checking at one named seed is the fallback, for a tile where the margin is wanted and tight.
  • Weightings. dt::weighting stands for a weighting wrapping something other than a plain range of places, and for a weighting on a within distance, which builds one mask and so has nothing to roll. dt::range stands for a strength outside its limits. dt::cloud is about a top's arithmetic; an area has none, so a cloud area where a plain region is needed is dt::type.
  • Not clipped. Intersecting the inner area with the outer one to make a document pass would change output silently, which the errors rule forbids.

Open questions for Shawn

each with a recommendation
  1. What does a plain band mean? Recommend no odds, as a plain top: the element uses its own roll, which for scatter and sites is uniform white noise, so it thins in a straight line. flat(a .. b) is the explicit ranked form. The alternative, a flat cloud for everything, would give [a .. b] a second meaning next to a plain top.
  2. Which roll for which element? Recommend white noise for things and sites (an independent thinning with exact counts) and smooth noise for a body's edge and a stone's outline (patches), each the element's own, as dirt and flagstones choose theirs today.
  3. How do a cliff and the grass beside it agree in the gap? Recommend the stack (foot(cliff)) and equal weightings, with no shared roll key.
  4. What is a stone's core? Recommend the cell's inscribed disc, as part of the stone's own inner area core ∪ (cell ∩ inner), with no control until a tile needs one. The alternatives are the stone's seat or a share of the cell.
  5. How many outline points, and is there a control? Recommend a spacing in mm along the footprint (about 2 mm at the reference square), internal, with no control until a tile needs one. The alternative is the rock's own setting, which would add a word.
  6. Straight lines or smoothing? Recommend straight lines, because wear, chips and cuts already smooth, and one mechanism is better than two. The polygon is star-shaped about the centre, so it is a fan of convex triangles.
  7. Is the depth inherited or recomputed per cell? Recommend inherited from the parent band, so outlines reach toward the inner area. Recomputed from the core would make the fringe uniform all round.
  8. Cliff, wall, columns and bricks. Recommend the free-standing cliff first, then the edge forms once the front is read from the area, then the wall. I did not read how the front, the wall's layouts, columns or bricks are made.
  9. How is containment checked, given wobble? Recommend seed-free and conservative by default (the inner lines grown by their wobble inside the outer lines shrunk by theirs), with a named seed as the fallback. This keeps check equal to build at every seed.
  10. The ranged within "[a .. b]". It breaks "the area never rolls". Recommend a ledger line to retire it, with a migration that dharmatiles fix performs: rewrite it as a band over two rings. Removing it breaks existing tiles, so the line is closed only after the migration exists.
  11. Is band one kind for areas and tops? Recommend yes: a top is a band over Length and an area a band over Region, one word and one set of rules. Doing it for tops is a cleanup and not needed for areas, so it can follow.
  12. Order of elements. Recommend cliff, then scatter, then dirt, then water and grass, each schema's "takes" column saying so.

What the reviews changed

Round 1 (the plain band): Orin's seven changes stand where this page does not say otherwise: a range of places is a new form, holes are stated, containment has its own code, the area never rolls, a band is named with let, containment names its seed, and each element's "takes" column decides. Round 2 (the cloud, the outline) follows.

  • The band is a kind (Band over Region), not a second-class value: it is named, passed and intersected, so the refusals become kind mistakes and the word band is registered.
  • A plain band has no odds, as a plain top has none. "A plain band is a flat cloud" gave [a .. b] a second meaning, so it is gone; flat(a .. b) is the ranked form.
  • The weighting acts on depth, not on the gap's area share, so the page says the density follows the depth curve and that two elements share the curve, not the counts. It also notes that the outlines are fixed per seed.
  • Containment is seed-free and conservative by default, so check equals build at every seed. A named seed is the fallback. Retiring the ranged within comes with a fix migration.
  • The outline polygon is defined properly: nearest-depth clamp along each ray, re-decomposed into convex parts after fit, joints shared with a standing neighbour taken whole by both, few points giving the same depth mix per stone, and one ranking function for fields and point lists.
  • The core is part of the stone's own inner area, not a constant, and the point spacing is a named constant.
