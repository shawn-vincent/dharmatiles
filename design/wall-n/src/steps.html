Volume

A box under a dip slope

The free face is one plane, 52 degrees, facing front-left, through a foot point beside the figure. It rises to the back right, and a top plane trims the crest to a straight line from 13 mm at the left to about 43 mm at the back right. The right end is a cut face. Nothing else is decided.

Beds

Beds parallel to the slope

Planes at the same dip as the face, about 5 mm apart along their normal, in the outer 30 mm only; behind them one core block. Every slab's outer face is part of the free face. A bed set parallel to the free face is the dip slope.

Edges

Each slab ends at its own line

Each slab is cut by a plane containing the crest direction, a distance drawn from 0 to 14 mm back from the foot line (the innermost slab runs the whole width, so no gap shows). So each slab's edge is a long line parallel to the crest, and its end face is short. Cross cuts (every 9 to 14 mm, leaning up to 12 degrees, carry 0.12) break the slabs along their length.

Push

Each slab moves a hair

Each slab's outer face moves up to 0.5 mm along its normal, so neighbours step against each other. The big steps come from the slab edges, not from this.

Chips

Bites and foot blocks

Zero to two bites per cell, 0.8 to 1.9 mm deep, their planes aimed a little upward so the chipped edge shows a bright upturned facet. Fourteen small blocks of the same rock, 3 to 6 mm, lie along the foot of the wall.

Hew

Flat facets on the broad faces

Hew on every face wider than 3.6 mm, its point spacing a third of the face's narrow side (1.7 to 5 mm). The thin edge faces stay plain. This is the generated wedge above.
