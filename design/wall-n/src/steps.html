Volume

A box under a dip slope

One plane, 52 degrees, facing front-left, with a flat cap and a sloped crest. Nothing else is decided.

Level 1

One mass cleaved into big ledges

The free face is partitioned into convex cells: large slab faces, about 12 mm across and 40 mm along, their long direction 20 degrees off the dip line so the ledges run diagonally up the slope (face 262, dip 52), with the cracks along the slab direction stepping one way (the slab on the up-strike side proud, like shingles), cross cracks rare and leaning about 5 degrees, and no piece narrower than a third of its level's spacing. Every cell is a prism from one common base to its own top plane, so the cells cover the whole face: nothing is a separate blade, and no gap shows the ground. Each side of a crack gets its own height (up to 2 mm), and one side may tilt along the crack, so a step dies out toward one end.

Level 2

The same families again, finer

Every cell is cleaved again by the same machinery at 8 by 18 mm and 0.4 of the offset. Cracks stop at their parent's edge: a crack at this level never crosses a level-1 joint, so it dies inside the mass.

Level 3

Facets from the same planes

A third pass at 5 by 9 mm on 50 per cent of the cells, a quarter of its cracks diagonal (rhombic facets); the rest of the ledge face stays flat. Each leaf's top is its own plane, so facets come from the family directions, not from even triangles. The crest and the right end are the same machinery on their own planes (a crest plane and the end face), with offsets about half as large, so no face of the block is plain. Joints have no width: steps are risers against the rock behind, never gaps.

Pockets and corner chips

Hollows of recessed facets, chipped corners

Pockets: level-3 cells that fall inside a stretched, clustered noise field are lowered by 0.2 to 1.5 mm, so a hollow is a few recessed facets bounded by the same cracks. Eased lips: on 85 per cent of top edges longer than 3 mm, a plane through the edge's middle, tilted 28 to 52 degrees out of the face, taking 0.5 to 1.1 mm. Grain: on 55 per cent of cells wider than 4 mm, one or two grooves 0.25 to 0.5 mm wide and 0.3 to 0.6 mm deep, parallel to the slab, each a strip cell between two parallel planes: a worn lip, one plane cut. Corner chips: at a corner of a cell's top, a plane along the averaged normal of the faces there, tilted up to 25 degrees, taking 0.12 to 0.3 of the cell's narrow side, at 20 per cent of corners.

Foot

Blocks of the same rock at the foot

Eleven small blocks of the same rock, flat-faced, each top edge chamfered; no longer hewn.
