Wall N

One generator, one target: the tilted rock wedge in reference N, a ramp of rock on a dip slope, built as one mass: the face is cleaved into convex cells at three scales, each level by the same families at a fraction of the spacing and offset, with corner chips and clustered pockets last.

How it is made

Volume

A box under a dip slope

One plane, 52 degrees, facing front-left, with a flat cap and a sloped crest. Nothing else is decided.

Level 1

One mass cleaved into big ledges

The free face is partitioned into convex cells: long thin cells, about 5.5 mm across and 24 mm along, their long direction 20 degrees off the dip line so the ledges run diagonally up the slope (face 240, dip 60), with cross cracks leaning up to 14 degrees. Every cell is a prism from one common base to its own top plane, so the cells cover the whole face: nothing is a separate blade, and no gap shows the ground. Each side of a crack gets its own height (up to 2 mm), and one side may tilt along the crack, so a step dies out toward one end.

Level 2

The same families again, finer

Every cell is cleaved again by the same machinery at 3.6 by 9 mm and 0.4 of the offset. Cracks stop at their parent's edge: a crack at this level never crosses a level-1 joint, so it dies inside the mass.

Level 3

Facets from the same planes

A third pass at 2.8 by 5 mm on 80 per cent of the cells, a quarter of its cracks diagonal (rhombic facets); the rest of the ledge face stays flat. Each leaf's top is its own plane, so facets come from the family directions, not from even triangles. The crest and the right end are the same machinery on their own planes (a crest plane and the end face), with offsets about half as large, so no face of the block is plain. Joints have no width: steps are risers against the rock behind, never gaps.

Pockets and corner chips

Hollows of recessed facets, chipped corners

Pockets: level-3 cells that fall inside a stretched, clustered noise field are lowered by 0.2 to 1.5 mm, so a hollow is a few recessed facets bounded by the same cracks. Corner chips: at a corner of a cell's top, a plane along the averaged normal of the faces there, tilted up to 25 degrees, taking 0.12 to 0.3 of the cell's narrow side, at 20 per cent of corners.

Foot

Blocks of the same rock at the foot

Fourteen small blocks, hewn as before. The wall itself is no longer hewn.

What is still off

A few dark risers still read as slits between ledges, the faces are plainer than the reference (no worn lips, no grain), the crest and end are still more regular than N, and the foot blocks are generic. No gravel, colour or weathering.
